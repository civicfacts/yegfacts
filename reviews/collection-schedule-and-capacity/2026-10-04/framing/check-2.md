### 4. Operationalization and instruments — [framing]

**The schedule source hierarchy is missing a source that fits its own first rung.** The brief says, “first, a dated contemporaneous City schedule whose stated period includes 2026-09-10,” but its schedule-source section moves from the City webpage to captures bracketing that date. The City’s [2023–2026 Waste Services Utility Budget and Plans](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2023-2026-Wastes-Services-Utility-Budget-and-Plans.pdf?cb=1755112702) states the stream service standards and the green-cart months; the [March 2026 Development Standards](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/documents/development-standards-for-waste-collection.pdf) also describes the seasonal schedule. These records deserve examination before the brief relies on an inference between webpage captures.

**Replace the schedule-source treatment with:** “The City’s 2023–2026 Waste Services Utility Budget and Plans states the curbside stream service standards, including the months of the green-cart seasonal schedule; its stated period includes 2026-09-10. The March 2026 Development Standards also describes that schedule. Check for a later change, then use these dated records under the first rung of the dating rule. Use the June and September webpage captures as bracketing evidence only where a dated record does not establish the point.”

**The green-cart reopening instrument is overstated.** Park 5 says “the trucks’ cart-tip records would hold” seasonal set-out rates. The City’s [2024 Waste Collections Audit](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/23515_Waste_Collections_Audit.pdf) describes tip records and collector-entered “not out” events, but finds the noncollection data incomplete and inaccurate. A tip count alone does not distinguish a cart left at home from one the crew did not collect.

**Replace that parenthesis and reopening condition with:** “The City records cart tips and collector-coded reasons for noncollection, including ‘not out’; its 2024 Waste Collections Audit found the noncollection data incomplete and inaccurate. These records do not, without validation and a scheduled-cart denominator, establish a seasonal set-out rate. This park reopens on a published, validated seasonal set-out rate or a measure of green-cart fill.”

**The dump-run reopening condition does not always test the stated cause.** Park 2 says the claim includes having to make “a dump run or two” because the bin is insufficient, then offers “counts or tonnes of household garbage dropped at Eco Stations … before and after the present schedule began” as a standalone reopening instrument. Those totals would not identify which households made repeat trips or why.

**Replace the reopening sentence with:** “It reopens if a representative survey measures how often households self-haul between collections and whether insufficient cart capacity is their reason, or if drop-off records can be linked to evidence that establishes both that frequency and reason.”

The main alternatives are visible: September 10 versus the September 10–13 comment window; curbside versus communal service; recurring frequency versus collection in each calendar-week block; and the immediately preceding schedule versus any earlier schedule. The [bylaw’s service definitions](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/assets/Bylaws/BL20363.pdf) and the City’s [apartment and condo description](https://www.edmonton.ca/programs_services/garbage_waste/apartment-condo-collection) support that distinction.

**Replace park 1’s reopening condition with:** “The general curbside adequacy claim reopens on a current measure linking household size, assigned cart capacity, collection interval and garbage produced or reported overflow. The post’s condo-class complaint reopens only on a measure that also identifies whether service is curbside or communal and the capacity assigned per household.
