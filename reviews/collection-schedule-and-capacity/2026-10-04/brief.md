## The question

On 2026-09-10 a post in a public Edmonton Facebook group said the City's
garbage service fails a large family: "1 bin every two weeks". The author
later says the household has more than five people and four young
children, and uses "the small black bins" at a condo. The thread that
followed argued about whether one black bin every two weeks holds a
household's garbage. Some said it does not, that garbage rots in summer,
or that they haul garbage away between pickups. Others said it holds
theirs, households of five and seven among them, if you recycle and
compost.

Seventeen commenters appear under this question in the accessible
capture. The register codes seven for and ten against, but that split
does not reliably count positions in the argument: one commenter coded
against defends the service, and people on both sides state the
collection frequencies. The capture omits six of the comments displayed
by the platform, so it cannot establish how the complete thread, or
Edmonton more widely, divided.

The question as registered asks whether Edmonton's garbage, green-bin and
recycling collection schedules are adequate for households. **This brief
does not answer that question.** The post's capacity claim and the
opposing general claim that a large household can manage with biweekly
collection are proposed for parking pending a suitable measure. The
records examined, now including the City's 2019 business case for the
cart system and its attachment, do not provide a measure of household
garbage against cart capacity; what they do hold is set out under park 1.
The historical change to biweekly garbage and the green-cart frequency
are separate schedule assertions in replies. They cannot answer either
side's capacity claim, nobody in the capture disputes them, and so they
are reported as dated context, not tested.

**No claim goes to the panel.** The brief proposes seven parks and four
context facts. Whatever the next report says, the adequacy question stays
unanswered, and a schedule fact is never an answer to it and never counts
toward one. What a report can and cannot settle about the parks is set
out in the closing section.

Topics: none on the register, which this brief does not change.
Proposed for the question's page if one is published: `climate-environment`.
A published page needs at least one existing topic, and the vocabulary
has no waste topic. The `climate-environment` topic's own description
names Edmonton's "waste and water systems", and household garbage and

## Who asks this

Residents ask whether a black bin holds two weeks of garbage for
households like theirs, including a large household assigned a small
bin. The records examined so far do not answer that question. Pending an
instrument that does, the adequacy claims are proposed for parking at
that stated level. Dated schedule facts are presented as context, and
the brief states plainly that they are not an answer to capacity.

Residents also ask what a household with too much garbage can do, what a
bigger cart costs and who qualifies, and how much sorting can shrink a
household's garbage. Those are separate registered questions, named under
"Scope".

## Terms fixed in this brief

**Garbage, the black bin.** The residual stream the City collects from a
home in the black garbage cart, and before carts in bags or cans. Not
recycling, food scraps or yard waste.

**The green bin.** The City's green food scraps cart.

**Curbside and communal service.** Curbside service is the City's
collection from a home assigned its own carts; communal service is
collection from shared bins, or shared bins and carts, at apartments,
condos, rowhouses and other multi-unit buildings. The bylaw defines both.
Condo status alone does not identify the service: the City's Apartment
and Condo Collection page
(`https://www.edmonton.ca/programs_services/garbage_waste/apartment-condo-collection`)
says some apartments and condos, mostly four- or five-unit complexes, are
moved to curbside carts. The post
author reports small bins at a condo; the capture does not establish
whether that household receives curbside or communal collection.
Schedule facts for curbside homes must not be presented as facts about
that household.

**Every week; every two weeks.** The City's published recurring
frequency for a stream, as the City states it. Weeks in which a holiday
or a seasonal switch moves a collection are reported separately and do
not change the stated frequency.

**The standard service, or schedule.** The collection frequency the City
publishes as applying to a class of homes generally. Not what happened at
one address on one day: a missed, late or skipped pickup belongs to the
registered question `collection-reliability`. Not a paid or by-request
extra collection.

## No claim for the panel

The first draft sent two schedule claims to the panel,
`collection-frequency-change` and `green-bin-frequency`. Framing check 1
found that neither would tell a holder or an opponent anything that
matters in this exchange, and the editor agrees. The test is the one in
the framing prompt: state a consequence of both Supported and
Contradicted that would matter to an actual holder and an opponent in
this thread.

- *The change from weekly to biweekly garbage.* Both sides state the
  present biweekly schedule as a premise. Nobody in the capture denies
  that garbage used to be collected weekly; the defenders of the service
  argue that sorting makes one bin enough, not that the schedule never
  changed. Supported would tell an opponent nothing they dispute.
  Contradicted would correct the history but would not touch whether the
  bin holds two weeks of garbage, which is what the holders complain
  about. The one disputed thing in the second wording, that the City
  "raised taxes" as it cut collection, is its own registered question,
  `taxes-and-collection-cut`.
- *The green bin goes every week.* The commenters use it in September
  as a comparison with the black bin. The fair reading of their words is
  the frequency when they wrote, and both sides share it. The full-year
  reading the first draft made primary is not what they asserted: the
  winter proposal asks for monthly pickup and does not assert that winter
  pickup is weekly. A verdict on the September frequency would surprise
  nobody, and a verdict on the full year would test a claim nobody made.

So both are carried below as context with `black-bin-frequency` and
`recycling-frequency`, and no claim is proposed for the panel. This does
not weaken any claim to make it checkable: the capacity and consequence
claims keep their magnitude in the parks below, and nothing about them
is graded.

The editor considered whether anything in CR_7173 or its business case
creates a disputed proposition the panel could test at the level people
assert it. It does not; the reasons are under park 1.

## Proposed for parking at framing

Seven claims are proposed for parking on the no-instrument ground, each
with the condition that would reopen it. They are the editor's
proposals. None is adopted and none is on the register; the closing
section sets out what could adopt them. (Revision 2 cited methodology
v1.24 here. That rule covers a claim the panel answered and could not
publish, so it is not the authority for a park proposed before any panel
run.) Four fall on the side
that says the service is inadequate and three on the side that says it
is adequate or justified. Nothing about any is deleted; the register
keeps every wording and its provenance.

For each, the brief names the weaker statement the record could have
answered and says why it is not tested: a proposition may be made more
precise than the claim as made, never weaker. Each park records what was
examined, including CR_7173 and its business case.

1. `biweekly-bin-adequacy` (seven people, for), and with it the opposite
   assertion that every two weeks "is not enough", which has no claim of
   its own under this question. "I have a large family and the large bin
   is more than adequate for our needs. We rarely fill it."; "Im a family
   of 5 adults. The big black bin is good for us every 2 weeks". Each
   wording is one household's report of its own cart, and no public
   record holds what is in a named household's cart. Offered in reply to
   the post, the reports assert a general pattern: one black cart every
   two weeks is enough for a large household that recycles and composts.
   The post asserts the reverse for a large household with a small bin.
   Testing either needs a measure of household garbage against cart
   capacity, or of how many homes have more garbage than the cart holds,
   by household size. The records examined do not provide one. The
   City's characterization studies measure composition; the rate filing
   counts customers by cart size; the Cart Counts dataset counts carts;
   CO00921 says contamination in garbage carts was not monitored during
   the rollout. CR_7173 and
   its business case hold the two resident-reported measures described
   under "What is measured": pre-cart survey answers from residents who
   had not used carts, and a canvass of demonstration neighbourhoods in
   the first three months of carts, in 2019, by cart size. Neither is
   broken down by household size, so neither can test a claim about large
   households; neither describes 2026 service; and both measure what
   residents said about a new cart, not what the cart held. Testing the
   2026 claims against them would test a cousin: what some residents of
   about 8,000 demonstration homes said in summer 2019. The City's own
   statements that the large cart suits households of three or more, and
   that most households seeking excess collection have enough space once
   they sort, are its description of its design and cite no measurement;
   grading the claim against them would grade the City by its own word.
   The weaker statements the record could answer are "some households
   find it adequate", which seven comments satisfy by existing, and "the
   City says the cart is suitable", which is not what either side
   asserts. Background a reader may be given without a verdict: the cart
   volumes and the City's suitability lines, the share of curbside
   customers on each cart size, the rule for who may have the larger
   cart, and the existence and limits of the 2019 measures. Adequacy is
   not defined by a cart-size cutoff, because no published source
   supplies the measurements needed to apply one. Two more City records
   were examined after check 2 and do not supply such a measure: the
   Development Standards gives communal homes a garbage volume
   allocated per dwelling per week, which is a design allowance and not
   what households produce; and the City Auditor's 2024 Waste Collections
   Audit found that collectors used the non-compliant button
   inconsistently, some counting an overflowing cart as non-compliant
   and others not, so collector-coded overflow is not a count of
   overflowing carts. The general curbside adequacy claim reopens on a
   current measure linking household size, assigned cart capacity,
   collection interval and garbage produced or reported overflow. The
   post's condo-class complaint reopens only on a measure that also
   identifies whether service is curbside or communal and the capacity
   assigned per household. Evidence confined to curbside carts does not
   reopen the condo-class complaint. First places to look, none located:
   the demonstration analysis CR_7173's business case promised, the
   resident research CO00921 planned for spring 2022, the public
   perception survey the 2026 rate filing mentions, and the results of
   the 2025-26 characterization study.

2. `between-collection-dump-runs` (one person, against). "we have to do
   usually a dump run or two in between garbage weeks. It’s crazy to me
   that it’s bi weekly." One household's account of itself, with a
   frequency and a necessity; the household's size, cart and sorting are
   not given. No public record holds one household's trips. The general
   pattern it is offered for is that every two weeks is not enough, which
   is park 1. The registered proposition adds a cause, "because the bin
   service is insufficient", that no record could attribute: a count of
   drop-offs would not say why people came. CR_7173 and its business case
   hold no self-haul counts. The weaker statement the record could
   answer is that the City lets residents drop garbage at an Eco Station,
   which is the registered question `eco-station-garbage-allowance`. It
   reopens if a representative survey measures how often households
   self-haul between collections and whether insufficient cart capacity
   is their reason, or if drop-off records can be linked to evidence that
   establishes both that frequency and reason.

3. `summer-garbage-condition` (two people, against). "black bin should
   be picked up every week, it's pretty bad in the summer, especially
   families with babies or multiple dogs"; "Does recycle rot in hot
   sun?, no, but the garbage does!!" The claim is one of degree and
   consequence: two weeks in summer heat makes garbage bad enough that
   garbage, not recycling, should be the weekly stream. That the black
   bin "should be picked up every week" is a proposal, which the site
   does not test. That garbage can rot is a truism, and the registered
   proposition's "can rot" is that truism and is not tested. How bad it
   gets has no measure in any identified record. CR_7173's business case
   gives the City's reasons for its frequencies, odour and insects for
   kitchen organics and the removal of organics for biweekly garbage;
   those are design reasons and measure nothing. The editor found no City
   document that reports odour, pests or complaints for garbage carts by
   season or by collection frequency, and did not search Alberta Health
   Services' publications. Background a reader may be given without a
   verdict: the City's stated reasons for its frequencies, which
   materials its sorting rules send to the garbage cart (diapers, cat
   litter and bagged dog waste among them), and its advice on odours. It
   reopens if a City or Alberta Health Services record measures odour,
   pest or complaint rates tied to garbage carts by season or by
   collection frequency, or if the research named under park 1 reports
   it.

4. `diaper-waste-volume` (one person, against). "diapers take a lot of
   space, so families with 2 kids in diapers have a lot more garbage". A
   concession from a commenter who otherwise defends the service, made
   to explain why she lets such a family use her bin. That diapers take
   space is a truism and is not tested. The claim's point is the amount:
   enough more garbage that a family with two children in diapers
   overflows a cart others do not fill. No identified record states how
   much of a household's cart diapers take; CR_7173's business case
   mentions diapers only as a contaminant of compost under the old
   system. A figure built here from a number of diapers a day and a
   volume apiece would be the brief's invention, with a cutoff nobody
   published. Background a reader may be given without a verdict: where
   the City's sorting rules put diapers, and whether the City counts baby
   diapers toward eligibility for its larger cart, which belongs to
   `cart-sizes-and-eligibility`. It reopens if a characterization study
   reports diapers' share of garbage-cart contents by volume for
   households with young children, or if the City publishes a capacity
   guideline for such households.

5. `green-bin-utilization` (two people, against). "when my green bin is
   picked up every week, I’m lucky if 1/8 of it is even filled. Such a
   waste of manpower going around picking that up."; "there's not much
   in there and often we don't even put it out on garbage day if the
   weather is cold". Two households' reports of their own green carts,
   the first offered as typical and as the ground for moving collection
   effort to the black cart. The general pattern asserted is that green
   carts go out mostly empty, at least in winter. Testing it needs how
   full green carts are, or how many are set out, by season. The editor
   found neither. CR_7173's business case plans on an assumed kitchen
   waste weight per household per week, a planning figure and not a
   measure of fill. Tonnes collected and customer counts would give an
   average weight per home; the brief does not make that the test,
   because the holder's claim is about how much of the cart is filled, a
   weight says nothing about volume without a density nobody published,
   and an average says nothing about carts not set out. The proposal and
   the saving it predicts were set aside at intake as not claims. The
   City records cart tips and collector-coded reasons for noncollection,
   including "not out"; its 2024 Waste Collections Audit found the
   noncollection data incomplete and inaccurate. These records do not,
   without validation and a scheduled-cart denominator, establish a
   seasonal set-out rate. This park reopens on a published, validated
   seasonal set-out rate or a measure of green-cart fill.

6. `past-weekly-bag-volume` (one person, for). "People would dump 20 bags
   a week when there was no limit. Renovation guys". Three assertions:
   there was once no limit; people then put out about 20 bags a week;
   they were renovators. The first is the City's earlier rule, which
   CR_7173's business case addresses, and it is reported as context
   under `collection-frequency-change`; it is not parked. The claim's
   point is the other two, the amount and who: that the unlimited
   service was used to dump renovation waste by the twenty bags.
   CR_7173 and its business case report no count of bags set out per
   home and nothing about renovation waste in residential collection.
   The weaker statement the record could answer, whether the earlier
   service set a limit, is the premise and not the claim. It reopens if
   a City record from before the cart system reports the distribution of
   bags set out per home, or renovation waste found in residential
   collection. First places to look, none read: the Office of the City
   Auditor's Waste Services Audit of February 2018 (report CR_5555) and
   the 2016 four-season waste characterization study the business case
   cites.

7. `blue-bag-capacity-offset` (one person, for). "I blue bags because
   they take them weekly. I never run out of room in my bins". One
   household's account of itself, which no public record holds. Its
   general form, that recycling and composting shrink a household's
   garbage enough to fit the cart, is the registered question
   `household-waste-diversion` and belongs to that question's brief, not
   this one. It reopens under that question if a later source states it
   as a general claim, and it has no instrument of its own.

## Stakes

No claim carries a verdict, so no verdict word has stakes here. What the
proposals change:

- For a holder who says biweekly service fails a large household, the
  brief keeps the magnitude and proposes to park it, with the record that
  would reopen it. It does not tell them they are wrong.
- For a defender who says sorting makes the bin sufficient, the brief
  keeps the general claim at its stated level and proposes to park it the
  same way. It does not tell them they are right.
- The schedule facts are context for both. They may be published as
  dated, sourced facts. Neither a Supported nor a Contradicted verdict on
  them would decide whether the black bin is sufficient, and neither is
  progress toward answering the registered adequacy question.

## What the next report can settle, and what it cannot

Three things are kept apart here: whether the framing is accepted, what
happens to the claim park proposals, and who has authority to carry out
either.

- **Accepted framing.** An admitted FRAME OK would accept the brief's
  framing: no claim for the panel, the four schedule claims as context,
  and the seven proposals as stated. It freezes the brief.
- **The park proposals.** A FRAME OK does not by itself adopt any park.
  The existing written rule ties a claim parked at framing to the third
  framing report and its park confirmation (methodology v1.35). Earlier
  briefs that sent at least one claim to the panel have recorded framing
  parks without that route, by practice, and no rule states that an
  ordinary FRAME OK adopts parks or authorizes publishing a page with no
  finding. This brief does not claim that authority.
- **Remaining authority.** The confirmation routes in the existing rules
  apply only if the actual third report's standing findings satisfy
  their conditions. If a FRAME OK arrives and no existing rule gives an
  empty brief an authorized disposition, the honest outcome is a
  recorded process block: no park adopted, no panel, no finding, no
  page, and the adequacy question unanswered. This brief does not decide
  in advance what the next report will say.

If a report identifies a fair proposition that a holder and an opponent
in this thread actually dispute and that an identified record can test
at the level asserted, the editor will frame it with a verdict ladder
and stakes for both sides. The check 1 replacement ladder for a
full-year green-bin claim is kept in `framing/author-response-1.md` for
that case.
