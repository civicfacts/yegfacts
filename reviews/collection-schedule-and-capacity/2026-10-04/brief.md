## The question

On 2026-09-10 a post in a public Edmonton Facebook group said the City's
garbage service fails a large family: "1 bin every two weeks". Seventeen
people argued about it in the comments over the next four days. Ten of
them sit on the side that says the schedule is not enough or is the wrong
way round: garbage waits two weeks while the green bin and the blue bags
go weekly, garbage rots in summer, one household hauls its own garbage
between pickups, and collection used to be weekly before it was cut. Seven
sit on the side that says one black bin every two weeks is enough, their
own households of five and seven included, if you recycle and compost.

The question as registered asks whether Edmonton's garbage, green-bin and
recycling collection schedules are adequate for households. **This brief
does not answer that question, and no verdict under it should be read as
answering it.** No record the editor could identify measures whether one
black cart every two weeks holds a household's garbage, for large
households or any others. What the City's records do state is how often
each stream is collected and what the schedule was before. So the two
claims sent to the panel are about the schedule and its history, and the
seven claims about capacity and its consequences are proposed for parking,
each with the record that would reopen it.

Two claims are under review:

- `collection-frequency-change`: whether the City used to collect
  household garbage from curbside homes every week and changed that to
  every two weeks.
- `green-bin-frequency`: whether the City collects the green food scraps
  cart from curbside homes every week.

Each carries its own verdict, panel agreement and evidence-basis label.
Reviewer confidence appears only beside the reviewer who gave it.

Two claims carry no verdict and are not parked, because nobody on either
side of the thread disputes them and a verdict would tell no reader
anything: that the black bin is collected every two weeks, and that
recycling is collected weekly. See "Carried without a verdict".

Seven claims are proposed for parking at framing: that one black bin every
two weeks is adequate for households including those of five or more; that
some households make a dump run or two between collections because the
bin is not enough; that two-week-old garbage rots or gets especially bad
in summer; that diapers take so much space that families with two
children in diapers have a lot more garbage; that weekly green bins go out
nearly empty; that people put out 20 bags a week before there was a
limit; and that one resident avoids running out of room by using weekly
blue bags. See "Proposed for parking at framing".

The question's page, if one is published, leads with the parks and says
in its first lines that the adequacy question is not answered. The
question does not count as answered for any purpose.

Topics: none on the register, which this brief does not change.

## Who asks this

A resident with a full black cart on day nine asks: is one bin every two
weeks supposed to be enough, and is it? A neighbour answers: it is enough
for my family of five, so why not yours? Both are asking about capacity:
whether the cart holds two weeks of a household's garbage.

The identified record cannot answer that at the level they ask it. It
holds no measure of how full carts are, by household size or at all. What
it does answer is the level underneath the argument, in the words a
resident would use: did the City used to pick up garbage every week, and
does it pick up the green bin every week while the black bin waits two?
The two claims under review are stated at that level and no higher. A
reader who wants to know whether the schedule is adequate will not find
the answer in either verdict, and the brief says so wherever a verdict is
stated.

Residents also ask what a household with too much garbage can do, what a
bigger cart costs and who qualifies, and how much sorting can shrink a
household's garbage. Those are separate registered questions, named under
"Scope".

## Dates fixed in this brief

**As-of date for the schedule:** 2026-09-10, the day of the post and of
the first comments. "Is collected" is read as the published schedule in
force on that day. The comments carrying the two claims under review are
dated 2026-09-10 to 2026-09-12, and every comment under this question
falls between 2026-09-10 and 2026-09-13; a reviewer who finds the
published schedule changed inside those four days says so.

**Evidence cut-off:** 2026-10-04, the date of drafting. A document
published up to that day may be used for what it shows about the schedule
on the as-of date and before it. A schedule or rule that took effect
after 2026-09-13 is reported as a later change and does not carry a
verdict.

**How the schedule on the as-of date is established (the dating rule).**
A web page carries the date it was captured or fetched, not the dates
its contents applied. So the schedule in force on 2026-09-10 is read
from records in this order.

1. *A dated contemporaneous City schedule*: a City document whose stated
   period includes 2026-09-10, such as a collection calendar for the
   collection year, or a dated City notice of a schedule or seasonal
   change that names the dates it applies to.
2. *A City retrospective account*: a City document published by the
   cut-off that states which schedule applied over a period including
   2026-09-10, or the date on which the schedule now in force, or a 2026
   seasonal schedule, took effect or ended. The WasteWise calendar
   viewed after the as-of date counts here when it shows dates in a
   period that includes 2026-09-10.
3. *The bracketing captures*, only where no record of kind 1 or 2 is
   found: the Curbside Collection page as captured on 2026-06-18 and on
   2026-09-19. The reviewer may rest the as-of schedule on them for a
   stream only if both state the same frequency for that stream and no
   City record identified shows a different frequency at any point
   between those dates. The reviewer then says that the as-of schedule
   is inferred from statements dated 2026-06-18 and 2026-09-19 and was
   not observed on 2026-09-10, names what was searched for a change in
   the gap, and carries that limitation beside the verdict. Byte
   identity between the two captures is not evidence that the page was
   unchanged between them, and no reviewer describes it that way.

The live page as fetched after 2026-09-13, the October 2026 resident
guide and any other later restatement never establish the as-of
schedule on their own; they may be cited as consistent with a record of
kind 1, 2 or 3. Where records of different kinds disagree, the earlier
kind controls and the disagreement is reported.

*Why the bracketing captures are admitted for the primary reading.* A
same-day record of a City web page rarely exists. A dating rule that
accepted nothing else would decide the as-of schedule in advance as
unknowable, whatever the City did, which is a framing defect and not
caution. A schedule the City stated in the same words 84 days before
and 9 days after the date, with no record of a change in between, is
the ordinary way a published schedule is read, and a holder and an
opponent can both accept that before seeing a result, provided the
inference is named as one. *Required alternative, the strict dating
rule*: only records of kind 1 or 2 count. Under it, an element that
rests on the bracketing captures alone is undetermined, and the rung is
Not established. Both are reported for each claim; the primary carries
the verdict. Where the two rules give different rungs, the finding is
definition-sensitive, and the question's page says the as-of schedule
was inferred.

**"Previously":** the City's standard service immediately before garbage
collection every two weeks began. If the frequency changed more than once
in the City's history, the claim is tested against the last schedule
before the present one, and earlier schedules are reported as context.
The earlier schedule is read the same way: from a City document dated to
the period before the change or a City account that states it. An
archived page is evidence of what it said on its capture date, and
the date of the change itself comes from a City record that states it,
not from the dates of captures either side of it.

## Terms fixed in this brief

**Garbage, the black bin.** The residual stream the City collects from a
home in the black garbage cart, and before carts in bags or cans. Not
recycling, food scraps or yard waste.

**The green bin.** The City's green food scraps cart assigned to a
curbside home.

**Curbside homes.** Homes whose waste the City collects in carts and bags
at the curb or in the alley, as distinct from homes served by shared bins
(the City's apartment and condo collection, which the bylaw calls
communal collection). The commenters describe their own bins at their own
homes, and the primary scope is curbside homes. The required alternative
scope is all homes the City collects from, curbside and communal
together.

**The standard service, or schedule.** The collection frequency the City
publishes as applying to curbside homes generally. Not what happened at
one address on one day: a missed, late or skipped pickup belongs to the
registered question `collection-reliability`. Not a paid or by-request
extra collection.

**Every week; every two weeks.** One scheduled collection of the stream
in each calendar week; one in each two-week period.

**In every part of the year.** The same frequency in the schedule's
winter as in its summer, as the City publishes it.

## Claims under review

### Claim 1, id: `collection-frequency-change`

**Normalized proposition:** Under its standard service for curbside
homes, the City of Edmonton used to collect household garbage every week,
and it changed that to every two weeks, which is the schedule in force on
2026-09-10.

**Reader-facing question:** Did Edmonton used to pick up household
garbage every week before it went to every two weeks?

**Why this reading.** Both holders state a sequence: weekly, then every
two weeks, by the City's doing ("the stupid planners made it", "cut
back"). Neither gives a date, so none is made part of the proposition;
the date is reported. Both are describing the service at a home like
their own, in a thread about one bin at one house, so the primary scope
is curbside homes; the wider scope, every home the City collects from, is
the required alternative, because one holder says "garbage" and "they"
without limit and a reader could fairly take it as the whole city. The
second wording's "then raised taxes" is a separate registered question,
`taxes-and-collection-cut`, and is not tested here. The proposition has
two elements and the verdict turns on both: the earlier weekly schedule
and the present two-week schedule. The present schedule is the thread's
shared premise and is registered as its own claim, `black-bin-frequency`;
it is established here as the second element and gets no separate
verdict.

**What this verdict establishes, stated beside it.** Whether the City
halved how often it collects garbage from curbside homes. It does not
establish whether the present schedule is enough for any household,
whether the change was wise, whether rates or taxes rose with it, or what
else changed at the same time; the last of those is reported as
qualifications.

**The instrument.** For the present schedule: records under the dating
rule, which are any dated contemporaneous City schedule or City
retrospective account the reviewer finds, and otherwise the Curbside
Collection page as captured on 2026-06-18 and 2026-09-19, read together
as bracketing statements. For the earlier schedule: the archived Garbage
Collection FAQ of 2020-12-31 and the archived Garbage Collection pages of
2018 to 2020. For the change itself: the archived Edmonton Cart Rollout
pages of 2019 to 2021 and report CO00921. Essential: enough to apply the
dating rule to E2, which is a record of kind 1 or 2, or both bracketing
captures; and at least one City document that states the earlier
frequency (the archived FAQ, an archived Garbage Collection or Cart
Rollout page, or CO00921). The live page and the 2026-09-19 capture alone
are not enough for E2. A reviewer that cannot retrieve the essential
records says so, and the run stops rather than producing a finding from
news reports or a current page.

**Order of classification.** Read two elements from City documents. E1:
before the change, the City's standard service collected garbage from
curbside homes every week. E2: on 2026-09-10 the City's standard service
collects garbage from curbside homes every two weeks, established under
the dating rule; the reviewer names the kind of record E2 rests on.
Apply the Not established condition first, then Contradicted, then
Supported; what remains is Partially supported.

**Verdict ladder.**

- **Not established** if no City document the reviewer can read states
  the earlier frequency, or the frequency in force on 2026-09-10 cannot
  be established under the dating rule.
- **Contradicted** if the City's documents show that garbage was not
  collected every week from curbside homes generally before the change,
  or that it is not collected every two weeks from curbside homes
  generally on 2026-09-10.
- **Supported** if E1 and E2 both hold for curbside homes generally and
  in every part of the year.
- **Partially supported** if E1 and E2 both hold, but one of them holds
  only for part of the curbside homes or only for part of the year. The
  reviewer says which element and which part.

**Required alternative, reported with its rung while the primary carries
the verdict:** the wider scope, all homes the City collects from,
curbside and communal. Under it, Supported requires E1 and E2 to hold for
homes with shared bins as well; if they hold for curbside homes only, the
rung is Partially supported. Also reported: the rung under the strict
dating rule, under which E2 resting on the bracketing captures alone is
undetermined and the rung is Not established. Where either alternative
changes the rung, the finding is definition-sensitive, the question's
page says so beside the verdict, and neither side can fairly cite the
verdict without that qualification.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. When the change took effect: the dates and phases on which curbside
   homes moved to the present schedule, and how many homes, from the
   City's documents, quoted.
2. Who decided it and under what authority: the Council or committee
   decision the City's documents name, the report number, and what
   Bylaw 20363 section 20 and its predecessor say about who sets
   collection frequency. If the decision record cannot be read, say so.
