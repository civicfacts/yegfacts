## 2. Does the proposition test what the post asserts?

**Finding.** The brief combines three distinct claims:

1. Districts currently restrict, or will restrict, leaving a zone.
2. The City will track and fine inter-district travel.
3. District planning is a precursor to later restrictions.

The normalized proposition then joins two different factual standards with "or":

> Edmonton's district plans restrict, or create a mechanism to restrict, where residents can travel within the city.

A weak finding about some enabling mechanism could therefore support the same proposition as an operative restriction. The prediction that district planning is a "first step" is not the same claim as either one.

Replace the single proposition with separate claim IDs:

> **Current-restriction proposition:** As of 2026-09-01, an in-scope Edmonton instrument contains or authorizes an operative rule that limits, conditions, charges for, or penalizes crossing a district boundary.
>
> **Enabling-mechanism proposition:** As of 2026-09-01, an in-scope Edmonton instrument establishes district-specific legal authority, an administrative process, or a technical system that could be used to monitor or restrict inter-district travel.

Each proposition needs its own reviewer verdict. Treat predictions about what a future council will do as outside the factual review unless a captured claim supplies a present factual premise that can be normalized separately.

## 3. Is it the strongest fair reading?

**Finding.** The brief calls one interpretation the claim's "weakest defensible form":

> Check both the claim's strongest form (explicit restrictions) and its weakest defensible form (creates a mechanism that could enable restrictions) and address each.

A holder of the view may mean that district planning creates authority or infrastructure that a later council could activate, even if the current instruments cannot impose restrictions without further council action. The brief's present test risks answering only the narrower question.

Replace with:

> Review the current-restriction and enabling-mechanism propositions separately. For the enabling-mechanism proposition, report under both readings: whether the mechanism can operate without a new council-approved instrument, and whether the current instruments create district-specific authority or infrastructure that would still require later activation. Do not treat generic planning powers or the mere existence of district boundaries as a mechanism without identifying how they connect to inter-district travel controls.

## 4. Operationalization and its alternatives

**Finding.** Several verdict-sensitive choices need definitions or dual reporting.

- **"Restrict."** The brief recognizes permits, penalties, and monitoring, but not charges, physical controls, or formally authorized closures. A direct legal-prohibition standard and a broader practical-control standard could produce different findings. Define the categories and report them separately.
- **"Create a mechanism."** This is undefined. One reasonable standard requires a currently operable system. Another includes district-specific authority or infrastructure requiring later activation. The result could change, so reviewers must report under both.
- **"Without further council action."** A mechanism might require administrative implementation but no new bylaw, or it might require another council-approved instrument. Those are different thresholds. State both.
- **"Monitoring of individuals' movements."** Person-identifiable tracking differs from aggregate traffic counts or mobility data. Define which is alleged and report the two separately if both appear.
- **"Connected instruments."** The phrase could mean documents expressly incorporated by the District Policy, any implementing instrument, or any citywide policy touching transportation. That choice could change the evidence set. Define the legal or textual connection required.
- **"Adopted" and "in force."** An approved instrument may not yet be effective. Reviewers should record adoption date, effective date, version, and amendment status.
- **As-of date.** A fixed date is reasonable for a current-policy claim, but findings could differ at adoption, effective date, or publication. Require reviewers to identify any relevant change across those dates.
- **Evidence threshold.** The sentence below creates a binary test despite the four permitted reviewer verdicts:

> The claim is established only if the adopted District Policy / District Plans [...] contain provisions that limit movement between districts, create permissions/permits for travel, penalize inter-district travel, or establish monitoring of individuals' movements.

Replace with:

> For each proposition, identify any operative rule, legal authority, administrative process, technical system, permit, charge, penalty, physical control, or monitoring provision tied specifically to crossing district boundaries. State its current legal effect, what further action it requires, and whether it concerns identifiable people or aggregate movement. Reviewers then apply Supported, Partially supported, Not established, or Contradicted to each proposition.

There are no denominators or calculations to assess.

## 6. Is the claim checkable at all?

**Finding.** Current restrictions, current authority, and existing administrative or technical systems are documentary claims and are checkable. These forms are predictions:

> "They'll track and fine you for driving to another district."

> "Edmonton's district plans are the first step to travel restrictions."

The brief recognizes the current-versus-future distinction but does not fully isolate it because the story still says the districts "are, or will become" restrictions.

Replace with:

> This story tests two present-tense documentary claims about instruments in force as of 2026-09-01: whether they impose a district-boundary travel restriction, and whether they establish a district-specific mechanism or authority for one. It does not test predictions about policies a future council may adopt or the policy preference that such measures should or should not exist.

## 7. Scope traps

**Finding.** The scope names only District Policy and Plans, while the operationalization also includes the City Plan and zoning provisions:

> Geography: Edmonton's adopted District Policy and Plans.

> or connected instruments: City Plan, zoning bylaw provisions tied to districting

"Connected" and "tied to districting" leave the evidence boundary to reviewer discretion.

Replace with:

> **In-scope instruments:** Edmonton's adopted District Policy and District Plans, plus provisions of the City Plan, Zoning Bylaw, or another instrument only where the District Policy or a District Plan expressly incorporates, implements, or relies on that provision for a district-boundary travel rule or mechanism.

The categorical exclusion below could remove evidence on the tracking-and-fining claim:

> Out of scope: [...] traffic-calming or road-pricing debates (different instruments).

Replace with:

> Traffic-calming and road-pricing policies are out of scope unless an in-scope instrument expressly incorporates, authorizes, or relies on them as a district-boundary travel control. If such a connection exists, review only the connected operative provisions. Other cities may explain the composite claim's context but cannot establish a finding about Edmonton's instruments.

## Holder and opponent reaction
