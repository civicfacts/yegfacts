# Review brief: 15-minute districts and freedom of movement

Status: PROVISIONAL — drafted by the orchestrator 2026-09-01; founder
ratifies at the gate. Frozen before round 1. Methodology: v1.0.

## Story

Edmonton's District Plans organize the city into planning districts and
use "15-minute" language about access to daily needs. A persistent claim
holds that these districts are, or will become, restrictions on where
people can travel — up to "climate lockdown" framings. This story checks
the restriction claim against what the adopted policy actually says.

## Why this story (selection rationale)

- Prevalence: recurring in Edmonton social media since district planning
  began; part of a wider international claim pattern.
- Civic importance: district plans are adopted policy shaping zoning and
  infrastructure decisions.
- Verifiability: very high — the claim is about the content of public
  policy documents, which are fully public.

## Claim under review

### Claim — id: districts-travel-restrictions

**Circulating forms:** "15-minute cities mean you can't leave your zone."
"They'll track and fine you for driving to another district." "Edmonton's
district plans are the first step to travel restrictions."

**Normalized proposition:** Edmonton's district plans restrict, or create
a mechanism to restrict, where residents can travel within the city.

**Operationalization:** The claim is established only if the adopted
District Policy / District Plans (or connected instruments: City Plan,
zoning bylaw provisions tied to districting) contain provisions that
limit movement between districts, create permissions/permits for
travel, penalize inter-district travel, or establish monitoring of
individuals' movements. Read the actual policy text. A land-use planning
instrument that guides where housing, services and infrastructure go does
not restrict travel. Distinguish: (a) what the policy text does, (b) what
the City says it intends, (c) what critics predict it could become —
predictions are not evidence about the current instruments, but reviewers
should note honestly what the documents could or could not enable without
further council action. If the City has published an explicit denial,
that is evidence of the City's position, not by itself proof; the policy
text is the primary evidence.

## Scope

Geography: Edmonton's adopted District Policy and Plans. Time: instruments
in force as of 2026-09-01. Out of scope: other cities' policies (except as
brief context for where the claim pattern originated), the merits of
district planning, traffic-calming or road-pricing debates (different
instruments).

## Required calculations

None expected; this is a documentary claim.
