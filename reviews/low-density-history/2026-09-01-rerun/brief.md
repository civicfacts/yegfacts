## Story

An Edmonton infill thread produced a historical premise: that the city has
had low-density housing for a hundred years, and that many of its
neighbourhoods were planned around it. Those are two separate historical
assertions, and this story tests each of them on its own against Edmonton's
planning record. Topics: growth-planning, housing-development.

Claims in this story, reviewed and reported separately:

- `ldh-existed-century`: low-density housing has existed in Edmonton since
  approximately 1926.
- `ldh-planned-around`: many neighbourhoods inside Edmonton's boundary at
  the time were planned around low-density housing during that period.

## Why this story (selection rationale)

- Prevalence: unknown from this intake. The intake contains one
  founder-supplied formulation, described by the founder as a reply in an
  Edmonton infill or zoning thread. Because no source sample was captured,
  this brief cannot establish how prevalent or representative that wording
  is, and reviewers should not treat prevalence as established.
- Civic importance: the zoning bylaw renewal puts the residential districts
  of the current bylaw in front of council, and accounts of Edmonton's
  planning history are used on several sides of that debate.
- Verifiability: land use bylaws, statutory plans, subdivision records and
  historical zoning maps are public, dated documents, so both propositions
  can be tested against primary text rather than impressions.
- Workflow: the first story whose evidence is legal and cartographic rather
  than measured, which exercises the source hierarchy at the legal and
  audited end.

## Circulating forms

**Prevalence:** Unknown from this intake. It contains one founder-supplied

**Site-written formulations, not captured posts.** The three sentences

- "Edmonton has been a low density city since the beginning."
- "People bought into a neighbourhood that was zoned single family, that is

## What this brief leaves out, and why

The "management and pet projects" sentence is a judgement about council
priorities. It has no truth condition a panel can test. Only the two
historical propositions are reviewed. The post's preference against
upzoning, and any assertion about whether current zoning is a departure
from historical rules or is good policy, are not tested in this brief. Any
claim that the zoning in force in 2026 is a historical departure requires
its own captured source and its own brief, with its own cutoff date and
comparison rules.

## Claims under review

### Claim A, id: ldh-existed-century

**Normalized proposition:** Low-density housing has existed in Edmonton
since approximately 1926.

Claim A tests whether documented built low-density housing existed in
Edmonton approximately a century before the claim was recorded. Calendar
year 1926 counts as "approximately 1926", so evidence of documented
occupied low-density housing anywhere in 1926 or earlier meets the date
test. Report the result at the exact-date cutoff as well, but a failure at
an exact day does not by itself decide the proposition stated as
"approximately 1926." Where the exact-date result and the calendar-year
result differ, report the difference in limitations or
interpretation_notes and not as a second verdict.

### Claim B, id: ldh-planned-around

**Normalized proposition:** Many neighbourhoods inside Edmonton's boundary
in force at the time were planned around low-density housing during that
period.

Claim B's proposition and its geography are limited to Edmonton's
historical boundaries in each era. Planning done for land before it was
annexed to Edmonton is reported as context only and enters no Claim B
calculation.

Review and report the two propositions separately, each with its own
verdict, its own confidence in that verdict, and an evidence-basis label;
the claim itself carries panel agreement, computed from the three
verdicts, not a confidence. The review schema carries one verdict per
claim, so each reviewer gives exactly one verdict for Claim A and exactly
one for Claim B, reached under the primary readings this brief fixes, and
reports every alternative reading's result in limitations or
interpretation_notes. Do not treat "low density" as synonymous with
"detached", or "many" as synonymous with "most". A finding about Claim A
is not by itself a finding about Claim B.

## Operationalization

### Dates fixed in this brief

**As-of date (freeze date):** 2026-09-01.

**Exact 100-year window:** 1926-09-01 through 2026-09-01, inclusive.

These two dates are fixed here, in the text reviewers receive. The run
manifest repeats them; it does not define them and does not replace them.

### "Has existed" (Claim A)

For Claim A, "has existed" refers to documented built low-density housing,
not merely a zoning permission or a planning label. Low-density housing has
existed in a given year when the record shows a documented, occupied
low-density residential lot or area inside the city boundary in force in
that year, evidenced by the instrument or record the reviewer cites, with that
record's year stated. Occupied means the record shows dwellings in use, for
example an assessment roll, a census enumeration, a street directory, a
building permit with an occupancy record, or a plan annotated as built out.
A registered plan with no evidence of occupation does not by itself satisfy
this test. Classify the lot or area as low density under the
classification rule below.

Test documented presence in calendar year 1926, at the 2026-09-01
endpoint, and at least once during each city-wide bylaw era defined below.
Name the published source for every observation and report the longest
interval between observations. This is the fixed operational test of
"since"; annual or day-by-day observations are not required.

Published sources for those observations include the 1926 Census of the
Prairie Provinces
(https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2017/statcan/CS98-1926.pdf)
and dated directories or historic-resource records for the first
observation, later federal or municipal census and neighbourhood records
for the intervening eras, and the current assessment, permit or occupancy
record for the endpoint.

Report legal permission and official planning terminology
separately from built existence. If continuous legal permission is also
tested, report it as a qualification in limitations or
interpretation_notes and do not substitute it for built existence.

### "Planned around"

Evaluate "planned around" using both public planning instruments and other
documented evidence of deliberate neighbourhood planning, including
subdivision design, developer plans, restrictive covenants, servicing
standards and street or lot layouts. Report these evidence classes
separately. Built form alone does not establish planning intent, but it may
show whether a documented plan was implemented.

Public planning instruments include zoning and land use bylaws and their
residential district schedules, statutory plans (general municipal plans,
area structure plans, neighbourhood structure plans, area redevelopment
plans), registered plans of subdivision and the subdivision rules that
governed them, and the dimensional rules (minimum lot area, frontage,
coverage, permitted unit counts) attached to residential districts.

Keep the instrument and the outcome separate. An instrument that permitted
more than one dwelling type in a district where one type was built, and a
district where one type predominates although no rule required it, are both
cases where instrument and outcome diverge. Reviewers report the instrument
and the outcome as separate findings and say which one each source
establishes.

"Planned around" does not require exclusive detached-house zoning unless
the source evidence establishes that narrower proposition.

### Accounting windows

Two windows are used. They are not applied the same way to the two claims;
the section on which alternative applies to which claim states how each is
used.

1. The exact 100-year window, 1926-09-01 through 2026-09-01, inclusive.
   This is the primary accounting window for Claim A and it carries the
   Claim A verdict.
2. The period beginning with Edmonton's first verified zoning bylaw and
   ending 2026-09-01. This window is a qualification. Report what it shows
   in limitations or interpretation_notes; it carries no verdict of its
   own.

Include earlier non-zoning planning instruments where available. Do not
describe the regulatory lineage as continuous across years when no
applicable instrument has been established. Report the actual date of every
instrument found, including any earlier than 1926, and any gap in which no
zoning instrument was in force.

Edmonton's first zoning bylaw is sometimes cited as a 1933 bylaw. Confirm
the year, the number and the text against the bylaw itself or against the
City's History of Zoning in Edmonton and its Historical Land Use Bylaws
resource guide, both cited under Eras below, or correct it; do not carry
the date forward on an unsourced secondary description. Where only part of
an instrument's text or maps is published, say so, and limit every
classification to the text actually obtained.

### "Low density"

**The classification rule, fixed here before any evidence is reviewed.**
One rule applies to both claims. A lot, an area, an instrument or a plan
counts as low density when either of the following holds:

(a) the era's own planning or zoning terminology calls it low density,
residential single-family, single-detached, or that era's equivalent term,
quoted from the instrument. Classify under branch (a) from the operative
instrument or plan text actually obtained and quoted, and limit the
classification to that text; or

(b) the record satisfies branch (b) as that branch is defined below for
the claim being tested.

**Branch (b) for Claim A.** For Claim A, branch (b) uses the occupied
residential lot as the classification unit, not an area. A lot qualifies
when a published record identifies one or two occupied dwellings on that
lot in 1926. State the address or legal lot, the dwelling count, the
occupation evidence, the date, and the source. Claim A requires one
qualifying occupied lot in 1926; it does not require reconstruction of an
area's predominant lot pattern.

**Branch (b) for Claim B.** For Claim B, branch (b) is met when the
operative instrument text limits the classified residential lots to no
more than two principal dwellings per lot. Report every other permitted or
discretionary residential form. If the text does not settle that question,
the instrument is borderline.

Either branch is sufficient on its own. Neither branch requires a computed
density figure, a sourced density threshold or a minimum-site-area
calculation, and no such figure, threshold or calculation is a condition
of classification.

The two claims apply the same rule to different objects. For Claim A it is
applied to the documented built outcome; for Claim B it is applied to the
intended or permitted development. Permission does not establish the
density of an occupied area, and built form does not establish what an
instrument allowed, so keep instrument and outcome separate and say which
one each source establishes.

Report, for every lot, every area and every instrument classified, the
housing forms observed or the housing types permitted, which branch of the
rule was met, and the source for that classification, with its date and
its operative text quoted.

**Dwellings per hectare, a sensitivity only.** Report a figure in
dwellings per hectare only where a published source with a defined
land-area denominator exists for that area and that era, and report it as a
sensitivity alongside the classification, never as the classification.
Where such a figure is reported, name the source and state whether the
denominator is site area, net residential area or gross area. No
dwellings-per-hectare figure is required for any area or any instrument.
Its absence is not a gap in the evidence, does not make a classification
borderline, and never counts against a claim.

**Era terminology, reported for both claims.** Quote each era's own terms
from the instrument in force at the date of that instrument. The labels
changed over time (residential districts, RF1 and the R-series, small
scale residential), and the permissions behind a label changed with them.
Do not apply the current category names backwards.

Report, for every instrument classified, the permitted housing types, the
permitted units per lot, and the minimum lot area, frontage and site
coverage where the instrument states them, so that the classification can
be checked against the instrument.

Report borderline instruments separately. A borderline instrument is one
whose classification cannot be settled under either branch without an
interpretation, for example where the schedule is silent on the permitted
unit count, where a discretionary or conditional use changes the permitted
forms, or where a secondary or garden suite is permitted in addition to
the permitted principal dwellings. Report the count of borderline
instruments and state how each was treated and why. An instrument
contributes to the primary calculation only when branch (a) or branch (b)
is satisfied without an unresolved interpretation. Exclude borderline
instruments from the primary result. Report the included result as a
qualification in limitations or interpretation_notes.

Do not equate "low density" with detached-only housing. Where a
residential district permitted duplexes, secondary suites, rowhousing or
small apartment buildings, those permissions are part of what the district
was, and must be reported even where the district label reads as low
density today. Where a district permitted more than two dwellings on a lot,
report that permission and state whether the district still meets branch
(a) or branch (b) of the rule.

The rules in this section are fixed before any evidence is reviewed and
may not be changed after the evidence is seen.

### The neighbourhood unit, coverage and "many"

**Eras, fixed here.** The common eras are the periods during which one
city-wide zoning or land use bylaw was in force, each ending on the date
that bylaw was replaced. Assign neighbourhood-specific plans and
subdivisions to those periods by their operative dates rather than creating
overlapping eras from them. List the eras used with their start and end
dates and the bylaw that defines each, and use the same era list for every
calculation. Take the era dates from the City's History of Zoning in
Edmonton
(https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/HistoryofZoninginEdmonton.pdf),
which identifies the city-wide instruments and their dates, and from the
bylaw texts themselves and the City's Historical Land Use Bylaws resource
guide
(https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/ResourceGuide-HistoricalLandUseBylaws.pdf),
which records that several historical texts and maps are held only in
physical form, are partial, or are missing.

**How era results become a Claim B result, fixed here.** The cumulative
reading is primary and carries the Claim B verdict: Claim B is tested
cumulatively across the exact 100-year window, counting every
neighbourhood planned around low-density housing at any point in it. The
end-of-window reading, which tests the position at 2026-09-01 alone, is a
qualification. Report its result and the era-by-era results in limitations
or interpretation_notes, not as a second verdict.

**The neighbourhood unit, fixed here.** Present-day City of Edmonton
neighbourhood polygons, applied to historical instruments, are the primary
unit and carry the verdict. Contemporaneous subdivisions or planning
units, wherever their boundaries are recoverable from the record, are a
qualification: report the result under that unit in limitations or
interpretation_notes rather than as a second verdict. Both units are
defined here:

1. Present-day City of Edmonton neighbourhood polygons, taken from the
   City's published neighbourhood polygon layer
   (https://gis.edmonton.ca/site1/rest/services/ZoningWebApp/Zoning_Map/FeatureServer/35),
   applied to historical instruments. Primary.
2. Contemporaneous subdivisions or planning units, wherever a published
   source supplies their boundaries. Qualification.

Splits, overlaps and many-to-one mappings, predeclared. Under the
present-day unit, a published plan or record that names or maps more than
one current neighbourhood counts each of those neighbourhoods once, and a
current neighbourhood named or mapped by several such sources is still
counted once. Under the contemporaneous unit, the historical planning unit
is the counting unit and each such unit is counted once. Covered shares
are reported only where a published source supplies the geometry and the
denominator. Where a historical plan or subdivision name does not
correspond to a current neighbourhood name, state the mapping used and its
source.

Where no published source supplies contemporaneous boundaries, identify
the affected periods and report that limitation. Do not treat the
present-day-polygon result as equivalent to a contemporaneous-unit result
for those periods. The absence of published contemporaneous boundaries
limits a qualification only; it is not an evidence gap and cannot affect
the verdict.

**The primary count, fixed here.** For Claim B's primary count, count a
present-day City neighbourhood once when a published neighbourhood-specific
plan, subdivision document, covenant, developer plan, or official
historical report names or maps that neighbourhood and states that
qualifying low-density housing was a principal or predominant planned
residential form. Do not require a GIS covered-area calculation. A
city-wide instrument without published neighbourhood-level geography may
establish historical context but does not add neighbourhoods to the count.

The cumulative count at 10 carries the verdict; report the result at 5 as
the stated alternative. Report exact covered-area shares, area weighting,
contemporaneous-unit results, era-by-era shares, and residential-land
shares only where a published source supplies the required geometry and
denominator. Their absence is not an evidence gap and cannot affect either
verdict.

**Which neighbourhoods count, and the removed denominators.** A
neighbourhood enters the Claim B count for a plan or an instrument only
where the published record shows that the land it covers lay inside
Edmonton's boundary in force at that plan's or instrument's date, and the
list of neighbourhoods counted is reported. State the source of the city
boundary used, from the City's annexation history map or the Province's
Municipal Boundary Document Search
(https://municipalaffairs.gov.ab.ca/mc_boundary_search), and note that
Alberta states its city records in that search are incomplete and that
legal descriptions require the Gazette or the original documents. Those
sources establish annexation dates and city extent; no exact historical
boundary intersection is required, and none may be demanded.

Both era-end denominators, the neighbourhoods with documented occupied
residential development at that date and the neighbourhood units planned
or approved by that date, are removed from the required Claim B verdict
calculation. Under the cumulative primary count, the denominator is not
needed. If a published source supplies a complete defined roster for a
particular era, report the corresponding share as a qualification and name
that source. Otherwise state that no share was calculated; the absence
does not count against Claim B.

**"Many", fixed here.** "Many" has two readings. The count reading is
primary and carries the verdict. The share reading is a qualification.
They are not combined, and the share reading does not control the count
reading:

1. **Count reading, primary.** The number of neighbourhoods counted under
   the primary count rule above, at a cutoff of 10. Report the result at
   the stated alternative cutoff of 5 as a qualification.
2. **Share reading, qualification, conditional on a published
   denominator.** The share of neighbourhoods counted, at a cutoff of 25
   percent and at the stated alternative of one third, reported only where
   a published source supplies a complete defined roster of neighbourhoods
   for the era or the window and that source is named. Where no such
   roster is published, state that no share was calculated. Neither cutoff
   carries a verdict, and the absence of a share is not an evidence gap.

Ten is the smallest count that cannot fairly be described as a handful of
exceptional cases, and one quarter is a share plainly larger than a token
minority and plainly smaller than "most", which would be more than half.
Neither cutoff is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard, so each
is stated here with one other reasonable cutoff and results are required
under both, which methodology v1.8 accepts as sufficient. No cutoff was
chosen with any expected result in view, and none may be changed after the
evidence is seen.

Report the exact count for every era and for each aggregation reading, and
report a share for an era only where a published complete roster supplies
its denominator. The verdict is the result under the primary count
reading at the cutoff of 10; every other reading and every other cutoff is
reported in limitations or interpretation_notes with its result named. The
two readings are not required to be met together: the proposition contains
no such conjunction, and requiring both would let whichever reading is
harder to meet decide the verdict. Do not convert "many" into "most", and
do not select a reading or a cutoff after seeing the results.

Report the share of residential land covered only where a published source
supplies both the covered geometry and the land denominator, and report
the conclusion it supports separately, naming that source and stating the
land denominator and the treatment of partial coverage. Its absence is not
an evidence gap and cannot affect the verdict. The land share is a
reported denominator, not one of the two "many" readings above, which are
counted in neighbourhoods.

Distinguish the historical city boundary from the present-day boundary in
every statement about coverage, and name the published source of each
boundary used.

### Pre-annexation instruments, context only

Claim B's geography is Edmonton's boundary in force in each era, so
planning done by another municipality or county before annexation is not
part of any Claim B calculation. It enters no numerator, no denominator, no
count, no share and no verdict. Where such planning helps a reader
understand a neighbourhood's history, report it as context, separately from
Edmonton instruments and labelled as context, with the annexation date
stated. Do not carry it into a result.

### The instrument in force at the end of the window

Where a finding refers to the zoning bylaw currently in force, identify the
bylaw and the official consolidation in force on 2026-09-01. The current
instrument is Zoning Bylaw 20001; take the amendments in force on
2026-09-01 from the City's official amendment register
(https://zoningbylaw.edmonton.ca/amendments). State that date and the
version reviewed. Consolidations state on their face that the
original bylaw governs; note which version you read.

### Which alternative applies to which claim

Not every alternative in this brief applies to both claims. Apply them as
follows, and do not apply a reading or a calculation to a claim it does
not fit.

**Claim A, ldh-existed-century.** Assess Claim A over the exact 100-year
window, 1926-09-01 through 2026-09-01, which is the primary accounting
window, using the built-housing meaning of "has existed" above. Calendar
year 1926 counts as "approximately 1926", so documented occupied
low-density housing anywhere in 1926 or earlier meets the date test.
Report the exact-date result as well, and put any difference between the
two in limitations or interpretation_notes. Report the first-zoning-bylaw
window separately, as context about the legal record and not as a test of
Claim A: a bylaw that began after 1926 cannot establish whether housing
existed in 1926. Classify the housing under the classification rule above,
reporting the observed housing forms and the source for each
classification. The neighbourhood count, the neighbourhood share, the
coverage calculations and the residential-land denominator do not apply to
Claim A. Claim A carries one verdict: the result over the exact 100-year
window under that classification rule.

**Claim B, ldh-planned-around.** Assess Claim B under the classification
rule above and each era's own terminology. Claim B carries one verdict,
and it is the result under the primary readings: the cumulative
aggregation across the window, present-day City of Edmonton neighbourhood
polygons, the primary count rule above, and the count reading of "many" at
a cutoff of 10. Report the result at the alternative cutoff of 5, the
end-of-window aggregation and the era-by-era counts in limitations or
interpretation_notes. Report the share reading, covered-area shares, area
weighting, the contemporaneous unit and the residential-land share only
where a published source supplies the geometry or the denominator each
needs, and name that source; where none does, state that the result was
not calculated. Their absence is not an evidence gap and cannot affect the
verdict. The neighbourhood and residential-land denominators apply to
Claim B only. Claim B's geography in every calculation is Edmonton's
boundary in force at the date of the plan or instrument being counted.

**When an alternative reading changes the result.** The review schema
carries one verdict per claim, so a reviewer reports one verdict for Claim
A and one for Claim B, each reached under the primary readings fixed
above. Where an equally reasonable alternative reading named in this brief
would give a different result, do not report a second verdict and do not
average the readings. Name the reading, state the result it gives, and
record it in the limitations or interpretation_notes fields, so the
qualification travels with the verdict.

### Evidence handling

Examine the relevant instruments, maps, subdivision records and outcome
data across the full period. For each source, report what it establishes
about planning intent, permitted housing forms, geographic coverage and
built outcomes. Apply the same search effort and evidentiary standards
across eras and residential districts.

### Comparators

No external comparator is required. Both propositions are about Edmonton's
own record, and another jurisdiction's record cannot establish them. If a
reviewer uses another jurisdiction to interpret an Edmonton instrument, for
example a model bylaw Edmonton adopted from elsewhere, treat it as a
comparator and give the transferability statement the methodology requires:
why relevant, how it differs, what can and cannot be inferred.

## Scope

Geography: Edmonton's own boundary as it stood at the date being tested.
Claim A tests documented built housing inside the city boundary in force in
the year cited. Claim B's proposition and geography are limited to
Edmonton's historical boundaries in each era, so a neighbourhood enters a
Claim B calculation only for the eras in which it lay inside the city. Land
annexed during the period belongs to the story from its annexation date
onward, and every annexation date is stated. Planning done for that land
before annexation is context only and enters no calculation.

Time: the two windows above.

Out of scope: subjective neighbourhood-character arguments and present-day
design preferences; whether infill is good policy; whether the zoning bylaw
in force should change; whether the zoning in force in 2026 departs from
historical rules; the effects of zoning on prices or supply (see the
infill-prices story); and the characterization of council priorities in the
source post.

In scope solely as evidence of historical planning intent: documented
street and lot layouts, servicing standards, subdivision design,
restrictive covenants and developer plans.

## Required calculations

- The neighbourhood count under the primary count rule, naming for each
  counted neighbourhood the published plan, subdivision document,
  covenant, developer plan or official historical report it was counted
  from. Neighbourhood shares, residential-land coverage shares and
  housing-mix figures are reported only where a published source supplies
  both the numerator and the denominator. For each calculation reported,
  state the numerator, denominator, geography, boundary year, source and
  treatment of missing or partially covered areas. No calculation is
  required that the published sources named in this brief cannot supply,
  and no such absence is an evidence gap.
- For every lot, area or instrument classified as low density, state which
  branch of the classification rule it met, branch (a) the era's own
  terminology quoted from the instrument, branch (b) for Claim A one or
  two occupied dwellings on the identified lot in 1926, or branch (b) for
  Claim B an operative instrument text limiting the classified residential
  lots to no more than two principal dwellings per lot, and give the
  source, its date and its operative text. Where a dwellings-per-hectare
  sensitivity is reported, name its published source and state its
  land-area denominator. No such figure is required.
- Every instrument cited carries its official name or bylaw number, its
  adoption date, and the date of the version read, with consolidations
  distinguished from the bylaw as adopted.
- Any statement about how much of the city a rule covered states the
  boundary source and the year.
- Any housing mix figure states source, year, denominator (dwellings,
  buildings or land area) and geography.

## Reviewer instructions of special note

- Quote operative text. A secondary description of a historic bylaw is a
  lead; the bylaw text or the zoning map is the source.
- Keep permitted and built separate, and report both where the record
  supports both.
- Do not apply current category names to earlier eras; use each era's own
  definitions and quote them.
- Distinguish city-wide instruments from neighbourhood-specific ones, and
  say which one a finding rests on.
- Answer Claim A and Claim B separately, applying the windows, the
  definitions and the denominators as the section on which alternative
  applies to which claim directs. Do not apply a reading or a calculation
  to a claim it does not fit.
- Report one verdict per claim, reached under the primary readings fixed
  in this brief, and record every alternative reading's result in
  limitations or interpretation_notes. The review schema carries one
  verdict per claim and cannot carry variant-specific verdicts.
- Record every source URL; you have no repo access, so cite fully.
- If the windows, the definitions or the split into two propositions change
  what the honest answer is, record a MATERIAL FRAMING CONCERN per
  prompts/reviewer.md and answer the claims as posed alongside it.
