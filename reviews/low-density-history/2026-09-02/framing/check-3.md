## 3. Is it the strongest fair reading?

### Finding: the approximate claim receives an exact-day canonical test

> **Normalized proposition:** Low-density housing has existed in Edmonton since approximately 1926.

and:

> Claim A's canonical reviewer verdict is the result over the exact 100-year window...

"Approximately 1926" is a fair normalization of the colloquial claim. Making 1926-09-02 the sole canonical cutoff is not. Evidence establishing occupation later in calendar year 1926 could fail the exact-day test even though a fair-minded reader would regard it as establishing "approximately 1926." The intake also records that the claim was supplied on 2026-09-01, while the brief derives its exact date from the following day's freeze date.

**Replacement wording:**

> Claim A tests whether documented built low-density housing existed in Edmonton approximately a century before the claim was recorded. Report the result at the exact-date cutoff and for calendar year 1926. If those results differ, give separate variant-specific verdicts. Do not let failure at an exact day by itself decide the proposition stated as "approximately 1926."

## 4. Operationalization and its alternatives

### Finding: the cross-era rule is an unusual proxy and does not classify Claim A's built outcome

> A district, plan or other instrument counts as low density when its permitted principal residential use is one or two dwellings per lot...

and:

> The source of the rule is the permitted principal use on the face of each instrument...

The cited schedules are sources for what an instrument permitted. They are not a source for the semantic threshold that one or two dwellings per lot is "low density." Lot sizes vary, so units per lot do not provide a consistent density measure.

Edmonton's current bylaw defines residential density in dwellings per hectare. Edmonton instruments also show that areas expressly called low-density can permit more than two dwellings per lot. The current Griesbach Low Density Residential Zone permits up to sixteen row-housing dwellings per lot, for example. These sources establish that the brief's proxy is not the City's standard density measure and does not consistently reproduce the City's own low-density classifications. [City density definition](https://zoningbylaw.edmonton.ca/density), [Griesbach Low Density Residential Zone](https://zoningbylaw.edmonton.ca/part-3-special-area-zones/griesbach-special-area/3172-gld-griesbach-low-density-residential-zone).

The rule also classifies instruments, while Claim A concerns documented built housing. Permission does not establish the density of the occupied area. This could change both verdicts.

**Replacement wording:**

> Use separate cross-era rules for the two claims. For Claim A, classify the documented built outcome using an actual built-density measure, stated in dwellings per hectare, and report the observed housing forms. For Claim B, classify the intended or permitted development using each era's terminology and a predeclared cross-era density measure. Identify the source for every threshold. Include all permitted residential forms, not only the principal use. If more than one threshold is reasonable, report separate results and verdicts under each.

### Finding: the two readings of "many" are collapsed into a stricter third rule

> Report the exact count and the exact share for every era, and give a separate result under each reading. "Many" is established for an era only when both readings are met.

The count and share were introduced as separate reasonable readings. Requiring both is not merely reporting both. It creates a conjunctive third definition under which whichever reading is harder to meet controls the verdict. That directly recreates the collapse check 2 prohibited.

The justifications for 10 and 25 percent also do not address other reasonable cutoffs. Five, twenty, one fifth, or one third could all change the result. Saying that ten is not a "handful" and one quarter is not "most" does not show that both sides would accept those exact boundaries before seeing the evidence.

**Replacement wording:**

> Treat the count and share readings as separate operational variants. Report a variant-specific verdict under each. Do not produce one unqualified Claim B verdict when they differ. Before freezing the brief, either justify each cutoff from an identified pre-existing standard or state at least one other reasonable cutoff for each reading and require results under both. Do not require the count and share readings to be met together unless the proposition itself contains that conjunction.

### Finding: the reasonable neighbourhood-unit alternative remains verdict-sensitive

> Count neighbourhoods using the City of Edmonton's current neighbourhood boundaries, applied to historical instruments.

and:

> An area covered by a historical instrument that predates those boundaries is mapped to the current neighbourhood that contains it.

Fixing one unit removes reviewer discretion, but it does not answer the alternative identified in check 2. A historical subdivision can cross several current neighbourhoods, and a current neighbourhood can contain several historical planning units. The singular rule, "the current neighbourhood that contains it," does not govern either case.

The response argues that contemporaneous units are inconsistent or unavailable. That may create missing data, but it does not make current polygons historically neutral. Because the unit can change both the count and the denominator, this remains verdict-sensitive.

**Replacement wording:**

> Report results using present-day City neighbourhood polygons and, where recoverable boundaries permit, contemporaneous subdivisions or planning units. Predeclare how splits, overlaps and many-to-one mappings are treated. Where contemporaneous boundaries cannot be recovered, identify the affected periods and report the limitation instead of treating the current-polygon result as equivalent. Give separate variant-specific verdicts if the unit changes the result.

### Finding: Claim B has no rule for combining era results into its canonical verdict

> For each era, "many" has two predeclared readings...

and:

> Claim B's canonical reviewer verdict is the result over the exact 100-year window...

The brief never says how era-by-era results become a result "over" the window. Reasonable readings include thresholds met in any era, at every era end, at the end of the window, or cumulatively across neighbourhoods planned during the window. Those readings can produce different verdicts.

The era definition adds another ambiguity. City-wide zoning eras are sequential, but neighbourhood plans and subdivision regimes can overlap. Calling each overlapping plan period an era while requiring one common era list does not produce a determinate calculation.

**Replacement wording:**

> Use city-wide bylaw periods as the common eras. Assign neighbourhood-specific plans and subdivisions to those periods by their operative dates rather than creating overlapping eras. State whether Claim B is tested at each era end, at the end of the window, cumulatively across the window, or under more than one of those readings. Report separate verdicts for every equally reasonable reading that changes the result.

### Finding: the coverage threshold and occupied-neighbourhood denominator lack alternatives

> A neighbourhood counts as covered by an instrument when the instrument applied to at least half of that neighbourhood's land area.

and:

> A current neighbourhood counts as existing at the end of an era when...the record shows documented occupied residential development inside it...

Fifty percent is a verdict-sensitive threshold with no justification or alternative. A plan covering 49 percent contributes nothing to the count, while one covering 50 percent contributes a whole neighbourhood.

Requiring occupation at the era end can also omit the very case Claim B tests: a neighbourhood deliberately planned during that era but not yet occupied. The denominator therefore mixes a built-existence rule into a planning-intent claim.

**Replacement wording:**

> Report each neighbourhood's exact covered-area share. Give results under the 50 percent rule and at least one predeclared reasonable alternative, or use an area-weighted calculation that does not turn partial coverage into a whole-neighbourhood binary. For Claim B, report planned or approved neighbourhood units separately from occupied neighbourhoods at the era end. State which denominator supplies each result and give separate verdicts where the choice changes the finding.
