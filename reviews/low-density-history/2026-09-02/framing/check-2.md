## 4. Operationalization and its alternatives

### Finding: "has existed" is undefined

> **Normalized proposition:** Low-density housing has existed in Edmonton since approximately 1926.

The brief distinguishes planning instruments from built outcomes but never says which one establishes Claim A. Reasonable readings include actual built housing, continuous legal permission, or recognition as a planning category. These could produce different findings.

**Replacement wording:**

> For Claim A, "has existed" refers to documented built low-density housing, not merely a zoning permission or planning label. Test whether such housing was present at the beginning of the exact 100-year window and remained part of Edmonton's housing stock throughout it. Report legal permission and official planning terminology separately. If continuous legal permission is also tested, identify it as a separate operational variant and do not substitute it for built existence.

### Finding: the cross-era definition has measures but no classification rule

> Consistent cross-era measures, including permitted housing types, permitted units per lot, any available unit-density measure (units per hectare or per acre), minimum lot area and frontage, and site coverage.

Those are measurements, not a definition of "low density." The later instruction to use "reasonable alternative definitions" leaves reviewers to choose the alternatives after entering the evidence. The classification can therefore still move with the result.

**Replacement wording:**

> Before the brief is frozen, state the cross-era rule that converts permitted housing types, units per lot or area, lot dimensions and site coverage into a low-density classification. Give the source and threshold for that rule. Retain contemporaneous official terminology as the other operationalization. If more than one cross-era threshold is equally reasonable, name each threshold in this brief and require a separate result under each. Raw measurements alone do not classify an area as low density.

### Finding: "many" remains undefined

> Report conclusions under both denominators. Do not convert "many" into "most"...

Avoiding "most" does not define "many." An absolute number and a share of neighbourhoods are both reasonable readings, and they can point in different directions.

**Replacement wording:**

> Before research begins, define two explicit readings of "many": an absolute neighbourhood-count threshold and a share-of-neighbourhoods threshold. Justify both without reference to the expected evidence. Report the exact count and share and give a separate result under each reading. If the two readings produce different reviewer verdicts, do not collapse them into one unqualified verdict for Claim B.

### Finding: the neighbourhood unit can change the count

> For each era, report both the number and share of then-existing City-defined neighbourhoods covered...

A reasonable alternative is to count contemporaneous subdivisions, plan areas, or other neighbourhood units rather than overlay later City neighbourhood definitions. Boundary consolidation and partial coverage could materially change the result.

**Replacement wording:**

> Report neighbourhood counts under both contemporaneous named subdivisions or planning units and present-day City neighbourhood polygons overlaid on the historical city boundary. State the rule for matching historical plans to each unit and the treatment of partial coverage. Do not count an entire neighbourhood as covered unless the predeclared coverage rule is met.

### Finding: the exact as-of date is still outside the brief

> The exact 100-year period ending on the freeze date recorded in run.yaml.

and

> identify the bylaw and the official consolidation in force on the freeze date recorded in run.yaml.

The brief does not state the freeze date or the resulting start date. Reviewers receive the brief but are told they have no repository access. Referring to another file does not freeze the accounting window in the text they review.

**Replacement wording:**

> **Freeze date:** YYYY-MM-DD.  
> **Exact 100-year window:** YYYY-MM-DD through YYYY-MM-DD, inclusive.  
> The run manifest must repeat these dates, but it does not define or replace them.

Fill in the dates before the next framing check.

### Finding: the alternatives are applied to claims for which they do not fit

> Answer Claim A and Claim B separately, under both windows, both definitions of low density and both denominators.

The first-zoning-bylaw window cannot establish whether housing existed around 1926 if the bylaw began later. Neighbourhood-count and land-share denominators do not apply to Claim A. Applying every choice to every claim will create irrelevant results and leaves no rule for choosing the single reviewer verdict required for each claim.

**Replacement wording:**

> Assess Claim A over the exact 100-year window using the built-housing definition. Report the first-zoning-bylaw window separately as context about the legal record.  
>  
> Assess Claim B under both stated low-density definitions and the predeclared neighbourhood-count and share readings. Denominators apply only to Claim B. For every equally reasonable operational variant that changes the reviewer verdict, require a separate variant-specific verdict or create a separate proposition. State before research which result, if any, supplies the claim's canonical reviewer verdict.
