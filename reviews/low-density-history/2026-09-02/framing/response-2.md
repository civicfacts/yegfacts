framing check 2. Every finding in framing/check-2.md that is WEAKENED, OPEN
or new is answered below in the checker's order. The findings marked

## Earlier finding, WEAKENED: definition of "low density" has measures but no classification rule

Met. The measures stay, and a classification rule is now fixed in the text
before any evidence is reviewed:

> 2. A cross-era classification rule, fixed here before any evidence is
>    reviewed. A district, plan or other instrument counts as low density
>    when its permitted principal residential use is one or two dwellings
>    per lot, or the era's own term for that use. An instrument whose
>    permitted principal residential use is three or more dwellings per lot
>    is not low density under this rule. Report, for every instrument
>    classified, the permitted housing types, permitted units per lot, any
>    available unit-density measure (units per hectare or per acre),
>    minimum lot area and frontage, and site coverage, so that the
>    classification can be checked against the measurements.

The source of the threshold, and the ban on late-arriving alternatives, are
stated with it:

> The source of the rule is the permitted principal use on the face of each
> instrument, which the residential schedules of every era state, so one
> threshold can be applied across eras without translating one era's labels
> into another's. Raw measurements alone do not classify an area as low
> density; the rule above does. This is the only cross-era threshold used
> in this brief, and it may not be changed after the evidence is seen.

The sentence the checker objected to, "If no neutral threshold can be
justified before reviewing the results, report findings under the
reasonable alternative definitions", is deleted. One threshold is declared
instead, so there is nothing left for reviewers to choose after entering
the evidence. Borderline instruments are reported separately, as the
founder's direction required, and every threshold calculation is reported
with them in and with them out:

> Report borderline instruments separately. A borderline instrument is one
> whose permitted principal use cannot be read as one or two dwellings per
> lot without an interpretation, for example where the schedule is silent
> on unit count, where a discretionary or conditional use changes the
> count, or where a secondary or garden suite is permitted in addition to
> the permitted principal dwellings. Report the count of borderline
> instruments, state how each was treated and why, and report every
> threshold calculation with borderline instruments included and excluded.

The era-terminology operationalization is kept, and the brief says which
claims both apply to:

> Both operationalizations, the era's own terminology and the cross-era
> rule, apply to Claim A and to Claim B, and every finding is reported
> under both.

## New finding: "has existed" is undefined

Met, with the checker's replacement wording and a definition of the
evidence that satisfies it:

> For Claim A, "has existed" refers to documented built low-density
> housing, not merely a zoning permission or a planning label. Low-density
> housing has existed in a given year when the record shows a documented,
> occupied low-density residential area inside the city boundary in force
> in that year, evidenced by the instrument or record the reviewer cites,
> with that record's year stated. Occupied means the record shows dwellings
> in use, for example an assessment roll, a census enumeration, a street
> directory, a building permit with an occupancy record, or a plan
> annotated as built out. A registered plan with no evidence of occupation
> does not by itself satisfy this test.

and:

> Test whether such housing was present at the start of the exact 100-year
> window and remained part of Edmonton's housing stock throughout it.
> Report legal permission and official planning terminology separately from
> built existence. If continuous legal permission is also tested, identify
> it as a separate operational variant and do not substitute it for built
> existence.

## Earlier finding, WEAKENED, and new finding: "many" remains undefined

Met, with a predeclared threshold stated both as a count and as a share:

> **"Many", fixed here.** For each era, "many" has two predeclared
> readings:
>
> 1. **Count reading:** at least 10 then-existing neighbourhoods are
>    covered.
> 2. **Share reading:** at least 25 percent of then-existing neighbourhoods
>    are covered.
>
> Ten is the smallest count that cannot fairly be described as a handful of
> exceptional cases. One quarter is a share plainly larger than a token
> minority and plainly smaller than "most", which would be more than half.
> Neither threshold was chosen with any expected result in view, and
> neither may be changed after the evidence is seen.
>
> Report the exact count and the exact share for every era, and give a
> separate result under each reading. "Many" is established for an era only
> when both readings are met. Where the two readings point in different
> directions, say so and report each result under its own reading; do not
> collapse them into one unqualified statement.

Two things go further than the replacement wording asked, and neither
weakens it. First, the brief requires both readings to be met before "many"
is established, rather than allowing either one to carry it. Second, "era"
was itself undefined once thresholds were attached to era ends, so it is
now fixed:

> **Eras, fixed here.** An era is the period during which one city-wide
> zoning or land use bylaw was in force, ending on the date that bylaw was
> replaced. Where a finding rests on a non-zoning instrument, the era is
> the period of the statutory plan or subdivision regime that instrument
> belongs to. List the eras used with their start and end dates and the
> instrument that defines each, and use the same era list for every
> calculation.

The residential-land denominator required by check 1 is retained and still
carries a reported conclusion. It is no longer one of the two "many"
readings, because the readings the checker specified are counted in
neighbourhoods:

> Report also the share of residential land covered, where the necessary
> boundary data exists, and report the conclusion it supports separately,
> stating the land denominator, its source and the treatment of partial
> coverage. The land share is a reported denominator, not one of the two
> "many" readings above, which are counted in neighbourhoods.

## New finding: the neighbourhood unit can change the count

Met by fixing one unit, stated once, and by adding the coverage rule the
checker required:

> **The neighbourhood unit, fixed here.** Count neighbourhoods using the
> City of Edmonton's current neighbourhood boundaries, applied to
> historical instruments. An area covered by a historical instrument that
> predates those boundaries is mapped to the current neighbourhood that
> contains it. This is the only neighbourhood unit used in this brief, for
> every era and for both claims. Where a historical plan or subdivision
> name does not correspond to a current neighbourhood name, state the
> mapping used and its source.

> **Which neighbourhoods count as then-existing.** A current neighbourhood
> counts as existing at the end of an era when its boundary lay within the
> city boundary in force at that date and the record shows documented
> occupied residential development inside it at that date. State the source
> of the city boundary used for each era end, and list the neighbourhoods
> counted as then-existing.

> **Coverage rule, fixed here.** A neighbourhood counts as covered by an
> instrument when the instrument applied to at least half of that
> neighbourhood's land area. Do not count a whole neighbourhood as covered
> unless that rule is met. Report separately the count of neighbourhoods
> with some coverage that falls below the rule.

**Where this departs from the replacement wording, and why.** The checker
asked for counts under two units, contemporaneous subdivisions or planning
units and present-day polygons. The brief fixes one unit instead. Running
two units alongside two low-density operationalizations, two "many"
readings and two windows leaves reviewers with a menu and no rule for
choosing among the results, which is the defect the checker's own last
finding names. Historical subdivision and plan names are not a stable unit
across a century: they overlap, they are renamed, and they have no
published boundary set that can be counted the same way twice. The current
City boundaries are published, stable and countable, the mapping rule for
pre-boundary areas is stated, and the coverage rule and the partial-coverage
count are reported so the mapping can be audited rather than assumed. The
contemporaneous names are not lost: the brief requires the mapping used for
each of them to be stated with its source.
