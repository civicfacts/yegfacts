### 2. Does the proposition test what the post asserts?

**Finding.** The normalized proposition changes and expands the captured claim:

> Edmonton's residential neighbourhoods have been planned around low-density, detached housing for roughly a century, so the residential zoning in force in 2026 is a departure from the rules under which most of the city was built.

The source says "low density," not "detached." It says "many neighbourhoods," not "most of the city." It does not state that 2026 zoning is a departure. The brief has folded together four distinct assertions:

1. Low-density housing has existed in Edmonton for about 100 years.
2. Many neighbourhoods were planned around low-density housing.
3. Those plans centred specifically on detached housing.
4. The zoning in force in 2026 departs from the historical rules governing most of Edmonton.

The last two come from the brief, not the captured wording. Folding the claims could produce an opaque "Partially supported" verdict when the evidence supports one assertion but not another.

**Replacement wording:**

> **Normalized propositions:**  
> A. Low-density housing has existed in Edmonton since approximately 1926.  
> B. Many Edmonton neighbourhoods were planned around low-density housing during that period.  
>  
> Review and report the two propositions separately. Do not treat "low density" as synonymous with "detached," or "many" as synonymous with "most." Any claim that the zoning in force in 2026 is a departure requires its own captured source and brief.

### 3. Is it the strongest fair reading?

**Finding.** This is not the strongest fair reading. It is a stronger and more brittle claim than the supplied wording:

> planned around low-density, detached housing

and

> the rules under which most of the city was built

A fair-minded holder could reasonably say they claimed that low-density housing shaped many neighbourhood plans, not that most of Edmonton was governed continuously by detached-only rules.

**Replacement wording:**

> For roughly the past century, low-density housing has existed in Edmonton, and many Edmonton neighbourhoods were planned around low-density housing. "Planned around" does not require exclusive detached-house zoning unless the source evidence establishes that narrower proposition.

### 4. Operationalization and its alternatives

**Finding: meaning of "planned around."**

> "Planned around" means the instruments of public planning, not market outcomes.

A reasonable alternative includes developer plans, subdivision design, restrictive covenants, servicing standards, street layouts and other evidence of deliberate physical planning. Limiting the test to public instruments could change the finding.

**Replacement wording:**

> Evaluate "planned around" using both public planning instruments and other documented evidence of deliberate neighbourhood planning, including subdivision design, developer plans, restrictive covenants, servicing standards and street or lot layouts. Report these evidence classes separately. Built form alone does not establish planning intent, but it may show whether a documented plan was implemented.

**Finding: accounting window.**

> "Roughly a century" means, as of 2026, roughly 1926 onward.

Reasonable alternatives include an exact 100-year window ending on the brief's cutoff date and a window beginning with Edmonton's first verified zoning bylaw. The brief itself suggests that zoning may not have begun until 1933. The choice could change any finding about continuity.

**Replacement wording:**

> Use two reported windows: the exact 100-year period ending on the brief's stated cutoff date, and the period beginning with Edmonton's first verified zoning bylaw. Include earlier non-zoning planning instruments where available. Do not describe the regulatory lineage as continuous across years when no applicable instrument has been established.

**Finding: definition of low density.**

> "Low density" means what Edmonton's own planning vocabulary meant by it in each era.

A contemporaneous label is one reasonable definition. A stable cross-era measure based on permitted housing types, units per parcel or hectare, lot dimensions and site coverage is another. A detached-only definition is narrower again. These choices could change the finding.

**Replacement wording:**

> Report both each era's own terminology and consistent cross-era measures, including permitted housing types, permitted units per lot, any available unit-density measure, minimum lot dimensions and site coverage. Do not equate "low density" with detached-only housing. If no neutral threshold can be justified before reviewing the results, report findings under the reasonable alternative definitions.

**Finding: the departure comparison.**

> "A departure" is tested by comparing the residential districts of the zoning bylaw in force in 2026 with the residential districts of earlier eras...

This operationalizes an added claim rather than the supplied claim.

**Replacement wording:**

> Remove the departure comparison from this brief. If a captured source later asserts that current zoning is a historical departure, review that as a separate proposition with its own cutoff date and comparison rules.

**Finding: denominator and "many."**

> For every instrument, reviewers state what share of Edmonton's residential land or how many neighbourhoods it applied to...

Neighbourhood count and residential-land share are different denominators and may yield different impressions. The brief also leaves "many" undefined.

**Replacement wording:**

> For each era, report both the number and share of then-existing City-defined neighbourhoods covered, plus the share of residential land where the necessary boundary data exists. State the neighbourhood and land denominators, boundary year and treatment of partial coverage. Report conclusions under both denominators. Do not convert "many" into "most" or select one denominator after seeing the results.

**Finding: as-of date.**

> the zoning bylaw in force in 2026

The brief does not specify a date or version. Different 2026 amendment dates could produce different comparisons.

**Replacement wording:**

> Identify the zoning bylaw and official consolidation in force on the brief's exact freeze date. State that date and the version reviewed.

**Finding: calculations.**

> None beyond sources and years.

The brief nevertheless requires coverage shares and permits housing-mix figures. Those are calculations whose denominators can affect the finding.

**Replacement wording:**

> **Required calculations:** neighbourhood counts and shares, residential-land coverage shares where data permits, and any housing-mix figures used. For each calculation, state the numerator, denominator, geography, boundary year, source and treatment of missing or partially covered areas.

An external-jurisdiction comparator is a reasonable contextual alternative, but it would not determine whether Edmonton's own record establishes these propositions. No external comparator is required.

### 7. Scope traps

**Finding.** The brief excludes evidence that may be needed to determine whether neighbourhoods were "planned around" low-density housing:

> Out of scope: ... neighbourhood character and design disputes...

Subjective character disputes can remain out of scope, but objective evidence about subdivision and physical design cannot.

**Replacement wording:**

> Out of scope: subjective neighbourhood-character arguments and present-day design preferences. In scope solely as evidence of historical planning intent: documented street and lot layouts, servicing standards, subdivision design, restrictive covenants and developer plans.

**Finding.** The annexation scope does not clearly include planning by the municipality or county that governed an area before Edmonton annexed it:

> Geography: Edmonton only, including areas annexed into the city during the period, with annexation dates stated.

Excluding pre-annexation instruments could misdescribe how present-day Edmonton neighbourhoods were planned.

**Replacement wording:**

> For land now within Edmonton, include applicable planning instruments from the municipality or county that governed it before annexation. Report those separately from Edmonton instruments and state the annexation date. Distinguish the historical city boundary from the present-day boundary in every coverage calculation.
