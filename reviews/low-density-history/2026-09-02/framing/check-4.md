## 4. Operationalization and its alternatives

### New finding introduced by revision 3: Claim A’s land-area denominator is undefined

> The measure is dwellings in use divided by the land area of the occupied residential area being classified.

The revision requires a boundary source and land-area figure, but it never says what land that boundary includes. Residential density can use site area, net residential area, or gross area. Edmonton’s zoning definition divides dwellings by the site’s area, while the City’s neighbourhood-density material distinguishes gross and net calculations. Those denominators include or exclude roads and other non-residential land differently. [City zoning density definition](https://zoningbylaw.edmonton.ca/density), [City dwelling-unit density report](https://www.edmonton.ca/public-files/assets/document?path=2014+Residential+Density+by+Neighbourhood.pdf).

This matters because the brief requires reviewers to compare the resulting figure with a sourced low-density threshold. A density calculated using one denominator cannot fairly be compared with a threshold defined using another. Including or excluding roads, parks, undeveloped land or non-residential parcels can change Claim A’s classification and verdict.

**Replacement wording:**

> For Claim A, use the land-area denominator defined by the identified source for the low-density threshold. State whether that denominator is site area, net residential area or gross area, and list the land classes it includes and excludes. Do not compare a density calculated using one denominator with a threshold defined using another. Do not use a threshold whose denominator is not defined.

This relies on the identified pre-existing standard and therefore satisfies the bound on check 4 without requiring another alternative.
