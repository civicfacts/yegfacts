## Freshness audit — 24 September 2026

The material gaps I found predate the 23–24 September verification. The main finding, **“Not established,” still holds**, but the draft’s count of documented districts and its account of 132 Avenue need correction.

### lc-lanes-taken-citywide

1. **Correction required — the documented minimum is at least 3 districts, not 2.** The City’s [Active Transportation Network Expansion route table](https://www.edmonton.ca/projects_plans/roads/active-transportation-network-improvements-project) lists the protected contraflow lane on **96 Street, 119–124 Avenue**, as built in **2025** and open for use. It says 96 Street became one way southbound to accommodate that lane. The City’s [district plans, updated 27 August 2026](https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/urban_planning_and_design/plans-in-effect), place Delton in **North Central**. Add it to Central and Scona; update the story, claim, and calculation. Three remains below both proposed thresholds of five and eight districts.

2. **Correction required — 132 Avenue has a final design and completed sections.** The City’s [final-design package, September–October 2022](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/132AveRenewal_FinalDesignInformationPackage.pdf?cb=1771352812) specifies one driving lane each way and raised bike paths. Its [renewal page, updated 1 September 2026](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/on_your_streets/132-avenue-renewal) says new active transportation infrastructure was completed between **101 and 107 Street in 2023**. The statements that only a draft exists and that finished sections are unknown should be revised. The City’s [final-design FAQ, 2022](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/132AveRenewal-Phase5-FAQ.pdf?cb=1770738873) says some sections previously had four driving lanes and that reconfiguration made room for several uses, including biking. That does **not**, by itself, isolate which former driving-lane space became a bike path, so it does not justify adding a district to this claim’s count.

3. **Update and source upgrade — the City documents two more Scona examples.** A [City plan dated April 2015](https://www.edmonton.ca/public-files/assets/document?path=RoadsTraffic%2FBikeFactSheet_83ave_APR2015.pdf) says **83 Avenue, 105–104 Street**, would change from two way to one way eastbound with a protected cycle track; a [March 2017 project sheet](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/documents/RoadsTraffic/2017_83_ave_bike_route_facts.pdf) schedules that section for construction in 2017. The City’s [current route table](https://www.edmonton.ca/projects_plans/roads/active-transportation-network-improvements-project) also marks the protected lane on **100 Street, 83–80 Avenue**, open for use in **2025**, and says the corridor’s one way conversion accommodates the bike facilities. These replace the draft’s broken-link and media-source leads. They strengthen the street evidence without adding a district.

4. **Update/acknowledgement — an imminent project milestone is published.** The [132 Avenue renewal page, updated 1 September 2026](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/on_your_streets/132-avenue-renewal) expects remaining work between **121 and 127 Street** to finish in **mid-October 2026**. It is a useful date for rechecking the corridor; it does not settle the citywide claim.

### lane-removal-increases-congestion

**NOTHING MATERIAL that reopens the claim.** The strongest additional primary source located, the City’s [Hermitage Road evaluation, September 2025](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/Hermitage-Road-40-Street-Towards40-Evaluation-Report.pdf?cb=1770267659), reports before-and-after traffic volumes, speeds and some queues. It does not provide the travel-time or delay study needed to attribute congestion to a bike-lane conversion. **Classification: immaterial to the finding.**

### bike-infra-reduces-congestion

**NOTHING MATERIAL that reopens the claim.** The same [September 2025 Hermitage Road evaluation](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/Hermitage-Road-40-Street-Towards40-Evaluation-Report.pdf?cb=1770267659) reports a 29% increase in observed cycling, but does not establish that those riders switched from cars or estimate a citywide delay effect. **Classification: immaterial to the finding.**

### lanes-removed-for-traffic-calming

**NOTHING MATERIAL that reopens the claim.** The City’s [132 Avenue final-design report, 2022](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/132AveRenewal_WhatWeDecided.pdf?cb=1770159081) describes traffic calming and bike paths within the same renewal. It does not say a driving lane would have been removed without the bike facility, or that combining the work saved money. **Classification: immaterial to the finding.**
