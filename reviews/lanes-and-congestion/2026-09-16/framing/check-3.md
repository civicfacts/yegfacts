## 1. Provenance

OK. The intake identifies the source, capture limitation, extraction process, quotation gate and participation counts. The forms are representative of the 621 accessible captured records, not Edmonton generally, and the brief says so.

## 2. Does the proposition test what the post asserts?

[framing] Claim 2 changes a causal claim into a claim about whether the effect “has been shown.”

Quoted brief text:

> “Edmonton’s cycling infrastructure has been shown to reduce motor-vehicle congestion across the city by moving trips out of cars.”

The posts assert that infrastructure reduces congestion. They do not merely assert that somebody has demonstrated it. “Has been shown” makes the proposition about the state of the literature, while the verdict vocabulary already asks whether evidence establishes the underlying proposition.

The proposition also omits the magnitude that its D10/D5 ladder applies.

Replacement wording:

> **Normalized proposition:** Edmonton’s cycling infrastructure causes peak-period motor-vehicle travel time or delay across the city to be at least 10 per cent lower than it would be without that infrastructure by moving trips out of cars, with the result at 5 per cent reported as the alternative.

## 3. Is it the strongest fair reading?

[framing] Claim 2’s consequence and predeclared magnitude appear only in the operationalization, not in the proposition that receives the verdict.

Quoted brief text:

> “has been shown to reduce motor-vehicle congestion”

but:

> “D10 counts … a … reduction of at least 10 per cent”

A Supported verdict would formally attach to an unquantified “reduce” proposition even though the ladder requires 10 per cent. Use the replacement proposition under check 2.

Claims 1 and 3 otherwise retain the causality and geographical generality asserted by their holders. Claim 4 now retains both the traffic-calming counterfactual and the combined-project/savings component.

## 4. Operationalization and its alternatives

The named public instruments exist as of 2026-09-16: the [Bike Routes – On Street feature layer](https://gis.edmonton.ca/site1/rest/services/Overlay_Public/Common_Layers/MapServer/236), [Bike Plan and 2021–2026 Implementation Guide](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/cycling_walking/bicycle-transportation-plan), [Downtown Bike Network evaluation material](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/cycling_walking/bike-maps-and-routes), [Transportation Data resources](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/transportation-data), [Eco-Counter data](https://data.edmonton.ca/w/tq23-qn4m/depj-dfck), [Navigating Tomorrow survey](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/traffic_reports/household-travel-survey), and the district plans. Charter Bylaw 24000 is correctly described as the [District Policy](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=235995), while separate plans provide the 15 boundaries. The AAWDT/short-term ADT distinction is also accurate: the City expressly says survey-period ADT is not seasonally adjusted on its [Traffic Volumes and Turning Movements page](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/traffic_reports/traffic-volumes-turning-movements).

[defect] Claim 1 lets non-attribution-capable measurements populate M and thereby produce Contradicted.

Quoted brief text:

> “Let M be the number of those Q corridors with a qualifying published measurement: the City’s published before-and-after peak-period motor-vehicle travel time or delay…”

but:

> “A corridor counts toward U only where the City evaluation expressly attributes the change…”

Thus three uncontrolled before-and-after measurements can produce M ≥ 3 and U = 0, triggering Contradicted even though none can answer the causal proposition. The unclassified causal remainder decides the verdict.

Exact replacement wording:

> Let M be the number of the Q corridors with both (a) published before-and-after peak-period motor-vehicle travel time or delay and (b) an evaluation that expressly attributes the change to the conversion or uses a comparison, counterfactual or other design capable of supporting that attribution. Let U be the number of those M corridors where attributed travel time or delay rose by at least the applicable cutoff. A corridor with only an unadjusted before-and-after difference is not in M; report it as a qualification only. Apply M/Q coverage and the verdict ladder using this definition of M.

[defect] Claim 4 excludes every corridor without a contemporaneous reason record from its denominator, allowing a small documented subset to produce Supported for the general proposition.

Quoted brief text:

> “Let C be the qualifying corridors with a contemporaneous reason record…”

and:

> “Supported if C is at least 3 and J is more than half of C.”

Two qualifying records out of three could therefore support a proposition covering a much larger set of conversions while every undocumented conversion disappears. The unclassified remainder can decide the verdict.

Exact replacement wording:

> Let B be the total number of qualifying converted corridors established under claim 3. Let E be the number of those B corridors with contemporaneous records sufficient to decide both the traffic-calming counterfactual and the combined-project or savings component. Let O and K count positive evidence for their respective components among all B corridors, and let J count corridors satisfying both.
>
> Apply endpoint coverage first and independently for each coverage threshold. For the primary result, if E is less than one-half of B, Supported and Contradicted are unavailable: return Partially supported if O or K is non-zero, and Not established otherwise. If E is at least one-half of B, apply this ladder:
>
> - **Supported** if E is at least 3 and J is more than half of E.
> - **Partially supported** if O or K is non-zero and Supported does not apply.
> - **Contradicted** if E is at least 3, O and K are both zero, and every one of the E records affirmatively gives an account incompatible with both components.
> - **Not established** otherwise.
>
> Separately, apply the same rule using one-third of B as the alternative coverage threshold and report that result only in `interpretation_notes` or `limitations`. Retain the required minimum-five alternative.

[framing] Claim 4’s majority cutoff lacks the required reasonable alternative.

Quoted brief text:

> “No alternative cutoff on the share: the verdict turns on a majority of documented corridors, and there is no second way to count a majority.”

That explains the arithmetic meaning of “majority,” not why more than half is the fair threshold for this general claim. Two-thirds is another reasonable standard and could change the finding. Reviewers can compute it from the same J and E records.

Replacement wording:

> Alternative share threshold, results required under both: J is at least two-thirds of E. The primary result remains more than half. Both are judgment thresholds rather than pre-existing standards.

[framing] The brief still identifies no published instrument capable of meeting the decisive definitions for Claims 1, 2 or 4.

The named Downtown evaluation describes traffic counts and monitoring, not an attribution-capable before-and-after travel-time study. Navigating Tomorrow measures regional travel behaviour and is expressly excluded by the brief from S. The identified [Hermitage Road decision report](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/40-Street-Hermitage-Road-What-We-Decided-Report.pdf) documents the design and traffic-calming program, but the brief identifies no passage establishing both that lane removal would have proceeded without the bike lane and that the bike lane was added to existing work to combine projects or save money.

The definitions therefore presently predetermine Not established or, for Claim 4, possibly Partially supported. Naming general classes of documents is not identifying a source from which the required calculation can plausibly be made.

Replacement wording:

> Before freeze, identify by title, date and URL at least one published source plausibly capable of supplying each required instrument:
>
> 1. for Claim 1, attributed before-and-after peak-period travel time or delay on a converted Edmonton corridor;
> 2. for Claim 2, Edmonton infrastructure-attributable substitution linked to a citywide delay estimate; and
> 3. for Claim 4, a contemporaneous record addressing both the without-the-bike-lane counterfactual and combined-project or savings component.
>
> If no qualifying source can be identified for a claim as of 2026-09-16, remove that claim from the panel as uncheckable rather than freeze a ladder whose endpoint evidence has no plausible published source.

[framing] The asserted GIS snapshot is not identified.

Quoted brief text:

> “Use the City’s Bike Routes – On Street GIS layer as the inventory, snapshotted on the as-of date.”

The official endpoint is a mutable live feature layer with created and last-edited fields; the brief gives no immutable 2026-09-16 snapshot or checksum. Reviewers have no repository access, so they cannot reproduce an unidentified private snapshot.

Replacement wording:

> Use the City’s Bike Routes – On Street GIS layer. Before freeze, provide reviewers an accessible immutable export captured on 2026-09-16, with its URL, capture time and checksum. The live layer URL is recorded as provenance but does not substitute for the dated snapshot.

[framing] Claim 4’s definition of “contemporaneous” has no alternative accounting boundary.

Quoted brief text:

> “‘Contemporaneous’ means a document published before or at the approval of the lane-removal design…”

A revised rationale published after design approval but before physical installation is another reasonable contemporaneous boundary and could change E, O, K and J.

Replacement wording:

> Primary boundary: documents published no later than approval of the lane-removal design. Alternative boundary: documents published no later than the start of physical installation. Report whether the alternative changes any classification; later retrospective explanations remain qualifications only.

## 8. Stakes

[framing] The written stakes now describe both sides and every verdict, but the endpoint stakes for Claims 1, 2 and 4 are not presently reachable through an identified published instrument.

Without such an instrument, Supported or Contradicted cannot surprise either side; the operative result is predetermined by source absence. That fails the stakes test even though the prose describing hypothetical endpoint consequences is sound.

Replacement wording:

> Retain these stakes only for claims for which the source table required under check 4 identifies a plausible qualifying instrument. If no such instrument exists, remove the claim as uncheckable and state that the published Edmonton record identified at intake cannot answer it at the asserted causal level.

## 9. Who asks this?

[framing] Claim 2 still answers an evidentiary cousin of the resident’s question.

Quoted brief text:

> “A person who rides asks: is it true that we’re making traffic worse, or better?”

but the proposition asks whether the effect:

> “has been shown”

Replacement wording:

> A resident asks: does Edmonton’s bike network actually move enough trips out of cars to make citywide traffic meaningfully faster than it would otherwise be? For this brief, “meaningfully” is tested at 10 per cent, with 5 per cent required as the alternative. The proposition tests that causal question, not merely whether a publication has used supportive language.
