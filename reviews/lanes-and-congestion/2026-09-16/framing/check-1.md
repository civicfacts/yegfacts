## 1. Provenance

OK. The intake identifies the source, capture date, complete-thread capture, extraction process, quotation gate, and participation counts. The forms appear representative of this captured thread because the intake says every materially factual claim was extracted and coverage-tested. The brief correctly limits that representation to “one source”; it must not later describe these forms as representative of Edmonton-wide circulation.

## 2. Does the proposition test what the post asserts?

[framing] Claim 4 changes the asserted reason.

Quoted brief text:

> “its stated reason for removing the lane was traffic calming or street renewal rather than the bike lane”

and:

> “traffic calming, neighbourhood renewal, safety, transit priority, LRT construction or any reason other than the bike lane counts for”

The post says the lanes were being removed for traffic calming anyway and bike lanes were added opportunistically to save money. It does not say every non-bike purpose—including transit priority, LRT construction or generic safety—supports that account. The operationalization can therefore support the proposition with reasons the holder never asserted.

Replacement wording:

> **Normalized proposition:** Where the City removed a motor-vehicle through lane and installed a bike lane, its contemporaneous record says the lane would have been removed for traffic calming even without the bike lane, and that adding the bike lane during the work combined projects or saved money.
>
> Traffic calming is the primary reading. Street renewal, transit priority, LRT construction, generic safety and other reasons are reported separately as qualifications and do not count as traffic calming unless the City document expressly links them to a traffic-calming lane removal.

[framing] Claim 5 tests a different proposition.

Quoted brief text:

> “79 Street … carries through motor-vehicle traffic at the level of a collector road and has a school on or beside it.”

The post asserts two things: 79 Street diverts traffic that would otherwise use 75 Street, and parents use it for school trips. Road classification, volume and the mere presence of a school establish neither diversion from 75 Street nor use by parents driving children. A holder could accept all three proposed conditions while saying the brief never tested the claim.

Replacement wording:

> **Normalized proposition:** On the segment at issue, 79 Street carries through traffic that would otherwise use 75 Street, and parents use that segment to drive children to school.
>
> Test the first component only from an identified published traffic-assignment, origin–destination, corridor or diversion study capable of comparing 79 Street with 75 Street. Test the second only from an identified school travel plan, traffic assessment or comparable published record of school-trip routes. Road classification, AAWDT and school proximity are qualifications, not substitutes. If no such record plausibly exists, drop this claim as uncheckable rather than issue a verdict on the proxy.

## 3. Is it the strongest fair reading?

[framing] Claim 3 drains the geographical generality from “all throughout the city.”

Quoted brief text:

> “‘All throughout’ is fixed as more than one part of the city”

and:

> “Supported if C is at least 3 and A is at least 2.”

Three corridors in two of Edmonton’s 15 district-plan areas is evidence of more than one location, but not a fair test of “all throughout the city.” Supported under this rule would tell an opponent almost nothing.

Replacement wording:

> **Normalized proposition:** The City has removed motor-vehicle through lanes for bike lanes across a geographically broad share of Edmonton.
>
> Primary threshold: Supported requires at least one qualifying corridor in at least 8 of the 15 district-plan areas. Alternative threshold, reported under both: at least one in 5 district-plan areas. These are judgment thresholds rather than pre-existing standards; reporting both is therefore required. Individual examples and a smaller geographical spread may support only the examples or a partial finding, not “all throughout.”

[framing] Claim 1 allows three measured corridors to carry a general verdict regardless of how many converted corridors remain unmeasured.

Quoted brief text:

> “Supported if M is at least 3 and U is more than half of M.”

The proposition covers converted corridors generally, but the denominator contains only corridors the City chose to measure. If three of a much larger set were measured, their result cannot establish the program-wide generalization without a coverage rule.

Replacement wording:

> Let T be the total number of qualifying converted corridors established under claim 3. In addition to the existing M and U conditions, Supported requires measurements for at least half of T. Report the result again using one-third of T as the alternative coverage threshold. If neither coverage threshold is met, the general proposition is Not established; the measured-corridor results remain qualifications.

## 4. Operationalization and its alternatives

[framing] Delay or travel time is a reasonable primary definition of congestion, but the brief does not fully handle other standard congestion dimensions. Transportation practice also uses queues, level of service and travel-time reliability; FHWA describes congestion through slower speeds, longer travel times and queues, and treats reliability as a related but distinct dimension. [FHWA congestion and reliability primer](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/fhwahop19062/whatis.htm)

Quoted brief text:

> “‘Congestion’ is fixed … as motor-vehicle delay on a corridor”

Replacement wording:

> Primary definition: change in peak-period average travel time or delay. Alternative definition, reported where the City publishes the inputs: change in peak-period queue/level of service or travel-time reliability. State whether the alternative changes the claim’s classification. Vehicle volume remains contextual and is not itself congestion.

[framing] Claim 2’s proposed evidence does not establish its causal chain.

Quoted brief text:

> “before-and-after counts on a corridor showing bicycle volume up and motor-vehicle volume down together”

and:

> “Supported if S is at least 1 and D is at least 1. Whether the two are the same corridor is reported, not required.”

Coincident counts do not establish that bike infrastructure moved those drivers to bicycles. Allowing S and D to come from unrelated places or studies also does not show that mode substitution caused congestion to fall. One local example cannot establish the citywide proposition.

Replacement wording:

> S counts only studies that directly estimate infrastructure-attributable substitution from driving to cycling through a stated counterfactual method. D counts only delay reductions that the same study or a linked network analysis attributes to that substitution. Supported requires evidence connecting both links in the same evaluated population or network. Evidence for only one link is Partially supported. Unlinked bicycle and vehicle counts are qualifications. Contradicted requires comparable causal or network evidence pointing against the claimed reduction; absence of S and D is Not established.

The official household travel survey exists, but it measures regional travel patterns and modes; the brief has not identified a published route-user counterfactual instrument matching S. [City household travel survey](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/traffic_reports/household-travel-survey)

[framing] Several instruments exist, but two are described inaccurately.

The named bike plan, implementation guide, Downtown Bike Network evaluation, cycling counters, household survey, active-transportation project page, AAWDT dataset and 2026 committee material all exist as of the date checked. [Edmonton Bike Plan](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/cycling_walking/bicycle-transportation-plan), [Downtown Bike Network interim report](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/The_Downtown_Bike_Network_Interim_Report.pdf), [Transportation Data](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/transportation-data), [AAWDT 2011–2022 dataset](https://data.edmonton.ca/Transportation/Average-Annual-Weekday-Traffic-Volumes-2011-2022-/b58q-nxjr).

However:

- Charter Bylaw 24000 is the District Policy. The 15 district plans and their boundaries were adopted through separate district-plan bylaws. [Official Charter Bylaw 24000 record](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/Meeting.aspx?Agenda=Agenda&Id=fd2f249c-4d39-4fbf-86ca-8209589f45b3&Item=32&Tab=attachments&lang=English)

  Replacement wording:

  > “an area being one of the 15 district-plan areas shown in the City’s plans in effect and adopted through the applicable district-plan bylaw; Charter Bylaw 24000 supplies the District Policy, not the individual area boundaries.”

- The current Complete Streets standard does not identify 3,000 vehicles per day as a collector-road threshold. It says typical two-lane collectors carry up to 5,000–10,000 vehicles per day, while 3,000 is associated with enhanced locals and bikeway-selection guidance. [Current Complete Streets standard](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/COE-IM-GUIDE-0046-CSDCS-2-01-standards.pdf)

  Replacement wording:

  > “The 3,000 threshold is a judgment cutoff, not a collector-road standard. Report the result again at 5,000 as the predeclared alternative. Do not replace either threshold after evidence review. Road classification and AAWDT are separate conditions.”

[framing] The traffic-volume sources must not be conflated.

Quoted brief text:

> “The City publishes corridor vehicle volumes on the Open Data Portal … and on its Traffic Volumes and Turning Movements page.”

The Open Data/Transportation Data resources publish AAWDT, while the separate Traffic Volumes and Turning Movements page describes site ADT as an unadjusted survey-period count.

Replacement wording:

> “Use the Open Data AAWDT dataset and the AAWDT map/spreadsheet on the Transportation Data page for seasonally adjusted weekday volumes. Treat files on the Traffic Volumes and Turning Movements page as survey-period ADT or turning-movement counts unless the file itself identifies an AAWDT measure.”

[defect] Claim 3’s verdict bands leave a coverage gap.

Quoted brief text:

> “Not established if, for every candidate corridor, the City’s record does not show what the lane was before”

and:

> “Contradicted if C is zero on a record that does show prior lane use”

If one candidate’s prior use is known and another’s is unknown, C can be zero while neither row applies.

Exact replacement wording:

> Let T be the number of on-street bike-lane corridors in the City’s inventory that were built by the as-of date, and let R be the number of those corridors for which the City’s project record establishes prior lane use.
>
> - **Not established** if C is zero and R is less than T. Read first.
> - **Contradicted** if C is zero and R equals T.
> - The Supported and Partially supported rows apply when C is at least 1.

[defect] Claim 5 treats missing evidence as evidence that a condition is false.

Quoted brief text:

> “Where only one of the two is published, the unpublished condition counts as not holding”

An unpublished AAWDT or classification can thereby determine Partially supported or Contradicted.

Exact replacement wording:

> Let K be the number of the three conditions for which published evidence is sufficient to decide whether the condition holds, and let H be the number of those decided conditions that hold.
>
> - **Supported** if K is 3 and H is 3.
> - **Partially supported** if H is at least 1 and the condition for Supported is not met.
> - **Contradicted** if K is 3 and H is zero.
> - **Not established** if K is less than 3 and H is zero.
>
> An unpublished or undecidable condition is unclassified and never counts as not holding.

## 8. Stakes

[framing] The stakes section does not state every verdict’s consequence for both sides, and Claim 2’s Contradicted gloss asserts evidence its rule does not require.

Quoted brief text:

> “Contradicted requires them to accept that where the City measured, more bikes came with slower cars.”

The rule does not require bicycle use to have increased on those corridors. Claims 3–5 also omit Not established, and several Partially supported entries describe only what the holder retains.

Replacement stakes:

- **Claim 1:** Supported means holders may say an adequately covered set of City measurements shows the claimed general increase; opponents must concede that measured result. Contradicted means holders must abandon that general measured claim at the fixed cutoff; opponents may say the adequately covered record points against it. Partially supported means both sides must accept heterogeneous corridor results. Not established means neither side gains an effect conclusion; only the measurement gap is established.

- **Claim 2:** Supported means holders gain Edmonton evidence connecting infrastructure, car-trip substitution and lower motor-vehicle delay; opponents must concede that causal chain. Contradicted means holders must abandon that Edmonton causal claim and opponents gain comparable evidence pointing against it. Partially supported means only one link or a local effect is established. Not established means neither the arithmetic argument nor its rejection has been demonstrated in Edmonton.

- **Claim 3:** Supported means holders establish broad geographic occurrence and opponents must withdraw a blanket denial. Contradicted means holders must withdraw the claim that any through lane was removed and opponents may maintain the denial. Partially supported means specific removals are established but “all throughout” is not. Not established means the prior-use record is too incomplete for either side’s generalization.

- **Claim 4:** Supported means the holder establishes that the documented counterfactual reason was traffic calming on the required share of corridors; opponents must answer those records. Contradicted means the holder must withdraw that general account and opponents may say the documented reasons point against it. Partially supported means the reasons vary by corridor. Not established means neither side may infer a reason from undocumented projects.

- **Claim 5, if retained after correction:** Supported means the holder establishes both diversion from 75 Street and school-trip use; the opponent must concede both. Contradicted means the holder must withdraw both factual assertions and the opponent gains evidence against them. Partially supported means only one component is established. Not established means the proxy facts do not answer either side’s actual dispute.

## 9. Who asks this?

[framing] The main “Who asks this” section is good for the two congestion claims, but Claim 5 answers a measurable cousin.

Quoted brief text:

> “what the record can answer is whether 79 Street does the work of a collector … and whether a school sits on it.”

A resident or reporter would ask: “Does 79 Street actually take traffic off 75 Street, and do parents use it to drive children to school?” Nobody in the record asks whether its AAWDT exceeds 3,000 or whether a school merely abuts it.

Replacement wording:

> “A resident asks: does 79 Street actually take traffic that would otherwise use 75 Street, and is it used for school drop-off trips? If no published record answers those questions, the site cannot answer them from road classification, volume and school proximity alone.”

A holder of the anti-lane view would see Claim 1 as recognizable but under-covered, Claim 3 as weakened from “all throughout,” and Claim 5 as a substitution of easier facts for the claim made. A holder of the pro-lane view would see Claim 2 as too easy to support through unrelated observations while being much harder to contradict, and Claim 4 as improperly crediting every non-bike project rationale to the traffic-calming account. An opponent on either side could therefore reject a favourable verdict without disputing the evidence; the brief is not yet a fair test.
