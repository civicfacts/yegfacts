## The question

Ten people in one Facebook thread, writing on 2026-08-27, the day after
the Infrastructure Committee met on the bike-lane programme and the post
reported its decision, made eight factual claims between them about cycling safety and
where cyclists ride. One says riders on roads without lanes are more
likely to be injured; one says it is a myth that lanes make cycling
safer. One bets that ninety percent of bicycle collisions are the
cyclist's fault. Three say cyclists ride on sidewalks instead of the lanes
built for them, naming downtown, the Victoria Promenade and a street in
the northeast. One says cyclists have the same legal right to the road as
drivers, lane or no lane; another describes riding lawfully on roads
without lanes. One says the City's Vision Zero Street
Labs hinder traffic and have no safety value for pedestrians. One rider
says they are honked at and buzzed when riding lawfully without a lane.
One parent says the downtown network will let the family ride safely from
104 Street to 124 Street.

**The question as registered has two halves, and this brief answers
neither at the level people ask it.** Whether lanes make cycling safer is
a comparison of injury risk with and without a lane. The Edmonton records
read for this draft hold counts of cycling collisions by facility type,
and the City's own two analyses of those counts say the same thing:
without a count of riders on each facility the comparison cannot be made.
No record read for this draft supplies that count; the search is
described under "What is measured" and in `source-note.md`. Where
cyclists actually ride, as the three sidewalk commenters mean it, needs
an observational count of riders on sidewalks beside lanes. No record
read for this draft holds one; the one City document found that mentions
sidewalk riding on the downtown grid records it as a 311 complaint theme,
not a count. Both halves are proposed for parking, with the instrument
that would reopen each and the bounds of the search stated.

What the record can answer is narrower and still argued about in the
thread: what the law says about where a cyclist may ride, and what the
City's own evaluations of its Street Labs measured. Two claims are
proposed for the panel:

- `cyclists-have-right-to-the-road`: whether, under the provincial law and
  City bylaws in force on 2026-08-27, a person riding a bicycle in
  Edmonton may ride on the roadway of a public road, with or without a
  bike lane, with the rights and duties the rules of the road give a
  driver. All four essential texts are archived and read, the Public
  Spaces Bylaw in the City's consolidation of 2026-05-19; the chronology
  limit on that consolidation is stated under "What is measured".
- `street-labs-no-safety-value`: whether the City's own published
  evaluations of its Street Labs, taken together, report no measured
  safety effect. The instrument exists and has been read: the 24
  evaluation and assessment documents linked from the City's Street Labs
  page on 2026-10-21, described under "What is measured".

Six claims are proposed for parking at framing on the no-instrument
ground, each with its reopening condition: `lanes-keep-riders-out-of-
traffic` and `bike-lanes-dont-improve-safety` (the risk comparison from
each side), `cyclists-ride-on-sidewalks`, `downtown-network-safe-family-
route` (its safety judgement; its route-continuity component belongs to

## How this question got here

Whole-source extraction produced this question through grouping and
two-reader triage. The two readers split: Gemini 3.1 Pro returned GO on
the strength of the collision records and the traffic bylaw, and GPT-5.6
Sol returned PARK "when collision records can be linked to route design
and cyclist exposure, because raw crash totals cannot show whether bike
lanes change risk." Under the combining rule the question went ahead. The
register's public reason records the split and the missing exposure link.
Before the questions were formed, the same two readers had ruled on each
proposition: two of the eight were parked at that stage
(`cyclists-ride-on-sidewalks`, `lanes-keep-riders-out-of-traffic`) and one
was declined by both (`cyclists-harassed-on-roads-without-lanes`); the
register preserves the decline as a `prior_triage` note. The capture holds
621 accessible records where the platform displayed 669; we cannot tell
whether the comments the platform did not expose framed the issue
differently. The source was read end to end under methodology v1.15 to
the limit the platform exposed, every materially factual claim in it was
extracted and merged, and `scripts/intake-coverage.ts` proved nothing
raised was lost. Ten distinct commenters took part, four for the lanes
and six against. Those figures describe the accessible capture of one
thread, not how the argument circulates in Edmonton. Cost decides when a
question is checked, never whether.

The editor agrees with the Sol reader's reason, and this brief acts on
it: the risk comparison is parked until collisions and riders are counted
on the same facilities. The editor does not agree that nothing in the
group is checkable; the legal claim and the fault claim name instruments
the City and the Province publish.

The triage reason assumed two records. The traffic bylaw exists, and it
has changed: sections 49 to 52 of Traffic Bylaw 5590 (headed Bicycles,
Bicycle Conduct, Other Vehicles and Other Vehicle Conduct) are marked
Deleted in the consolidation of 2025-05-12, by section 96(1)(b) of Bylaw
20700, the Public Spaces Bylaw, in force from 2025-05-12 under its
section 95. The City's own Bike Routes page, as archived on 2026-10-21,
still links "Traffic Bylaw 5590 - Part III Sidewalks and Boulevards -
Section 50" for the bell rule and gives two different sidewalk rules in
two places (one by wheel size, one by age). The instrument for where a
cyclist may ride on a sidewalk is therefore Part XIV of Bylaw 20700,
archived and read in the City's consolidation of 2026-05-19. The
collision records exist as annual totals by mode and by main cause, and
as the City's published analyses and annual reports; none of those read
links a collision to a facility together with a rider count, which is why
the risk claims are proposed for parking, and none of those read states
fault by party for bicycle collisions, which is why the fault claim is
proposed for parking.

## Who asks this

A resident asks two plain questions. Are cyclists allowed on the road in
Edmonton, and do they have to use the bike lane, or the sidewalk, when
there is one? The statute, the regulation and the bylaws in force answer
that, and the first claim under review is stated at that level. And did
the City's Street Labs do anything measurable? The City's own evaluation
and assessment summaries, one per lab where published, are the record,
and the second claim under review is stated at that level.

Residents also ask, when a cyclist is hit, whose fault the City's record
says it usually was. The City's collision publications answer that only
if one states fault by party for bicycle collisions; none read for this
draft does, the search is bounded under park 5, and the claim is
proposed for parking until such a publication is located. Residents ask
whether the lanes have made cycling safer. The records read cannot answer
that at the level people ask it, for the reason the City itself gives: it
counts crashes by facility but not riders. The brief parks that question
on both sides and names the record that would reopen it. Residents ask
whether cyclists really ride on the sidewalk anyway. The record read
answers what the rule is, not what people do; the rule is reported under
the first claim and the behaviour is parked.

**Evidence cut-off:** 2026-10-21, the date of drafting. A publication or
amendment dated after 2026-08-27 and by 2026-10-21 is reported as a later
change, never as the as-of record.

**How the law on the as-of date is dated.** A published consolidation
carries the date it is current to, not the date a reader looks at it. The
law on 2026-08-27 is read from the latest consolidation current on or
before that date, checked against the publisher's amendment record for
any amendment in force between the consolidation date and 2026-08-27.
Where an amendment in that gap touches a provision relied on, the
reviewer quotes it from the amending instrument. A consolidation current
after the as-of date (the Traffic Safety Act's, current as of 2026-07-02,
is before it; a later City consolidation of either bylaw would be after
it) is cited only as consistent with the earlier record. The reviewer
names, for each text, the consolidation date it used.

## Terms fixed in this brief

**Bicycle, cycle.** A bicycle is a cycle propelled by human muscular
power, as the regulation and the bylaw define it. "Cycle" in the
regulation is the wider class, which the reviewer reports where the two
differ. E-bikes, e-scooters and mobility aids are outside every claim in
this brief except where a qualification names them.

**Highway, roadway, sidewalk.** As the Traffic Safety Act and Bylaw 5590
define them: a highway is the whole public way, including the sidewalk
and the boulevard; the roadway is the part of a highway intended for
vehicular traffic; the sidewalk is the part especially adapted to or
ordinarily used by pedestrians. "The road", as the holders use it, is the
roadway.

**Bike lane.** A lane on the roadway marked or separated for bicycles,
whether painted or protected by a curb or barrier, as the City's Bike
Routes page describes its facility types. A shared pathway and a shared
sidewalk are off the roadway and are not bike lanes.

**Reported collision involving a bicycle.** A collision in the City's
collision database, which the City's analysis describes as drawn from
police collision reports and, from September 2022, Collision Reporting
Centre reports, entered only when a motor vehicle was involved and the
report threshold was met, in which a person riding a bicycle was a party.
A fall or a collision between a bicycle and a pedestrian with no motor
vehicle is not in the database and not in this claim.

**At fault.** The party the City's published report records as at fault,
in the City's own words ("deemed at fault", "at fault", or the report's
equivalent). Fault is reported as the City attributed it; the panel does
not attribute fault.

**Street Lab.** A project under the City's Vision Zero Street Labs
programme, as the City names it, in which adaptable traffic-calming
measures (curb extensions, centre medians, speed humps or tables,
two-stage crossings, diverters) are installed on neighbourhood streets,
community-led before 2023 and City-led with public engagement since.

**Published evaluation.** An evaluation summary, assessment summary,
evaluation report or project update for a named lab that the City links
from its Street Labs page. A survey notice or an engagement report is not
an evaluation.

**Measured safety outcome.** A before-and-after figure the evaluation
itself reports for vehicle speed (average or 85th percentile),
speed-limit compliance, or pedestrian collisions, on the lab's streets.
The City's evaluations treat lower speed and higher compliance as the
mechanism by which the measures protect people on foot, and the primary
reading takes them at that word; pedestrian collisions are the direct
measure and the required alternative. A finding stated in words without
a figure, a post-only figure, a traffic volume figure and resident
feedback are each reported but are not a measured safety outcome under
this definition. The brief does not invent a measure the evaluations do
not report.

## Claims under review

### Claim 1, id: `cyclists-have-right-to-the-road`

**Normalized proposition:** Under the provincial law and the City of
Edmonton bylaws in force on 2026-08-27, a person riding a bicycle may
ride on the roadway of a public road in Edmonton, whether or not the road
has a bike lane, with the rights and duties the rules of the road give a
person driving a motor vehicle, subject only to prohibitions on
particular highways or classes of highway made by sign, bylaw or
regulation.

**Reader-facing question:** Are cyclists allowed to ride on the road in
Edmonton, and do they have to use the bike lane or the sidewalk when there
is one?

**Why this reading.** The holder says cyclists have "the same legal right
to be on the road as you do" and, if lanes are removed, "will be cycling
right down the middle of your lane". The first sentence is the claim; the
second is its consequence and is partly a prediction about behaviour,
which the site does not test, and partly a question of what position the
rules allow, which is reported as a qualification. "The same legal right"
is read as the entitlement to use the roadway on the terms the rules of
the road give a driver, because that is the entitlement a driver has and
the comparison the holder draws. The stricter reading, that every right a
driver has is a right a cyclist has, is the required alternative, under
which any difference the texts make (highways closed to bicycles, position
rules, licensing) is weighed. "With or without a bike lane" is carried as
the second element: a rule that required a cyclist to use a bike lane,
path or sidewalk where one exists would make the right conditional, which
is what the holder denies. The other holder's "riding lawfully on roads
without bike lanes" is the same proposition from the rider's side.

**What this verdict establishes, stated beside it.** This proposition
checks what the statute, the regulation and the bylaws provide. It does
not establish whether riding on a given road is safe, whether cyclists
should be on arterial roads, or what cyclists do.

**The instrument.** The Traffic Safety Act as consolidated to 2026-07-02;
the Use of Highway and Rules of the Road Regulation as consolidated to
2025-08-08, with the King's Printer's regulations index to 2026-09-30
confirming no later amendment, in particular Part 2 Division 4 (sections
75 to 79); Traffic Bylaw 5590 as consolidated 2025-05-12 with the City's
bylaw index checked for a later consolidation, in particular Part III,
section 84 (restricted highways), section 107(cc) and (dd) (designated
portions where bicycles are prohibited or bicycles only), and any other
provision in Parts IV to VIII that prohibits or restricts bicycles; and
Public Spaces Bylaw 20700 as consolidated 2026-05-19, Part XIV sections
77 to 82, with the City's bylaw index checked for any amendment in force
between 2026-05-19 and 2026-08-27 and the result reported. All four are
essential: a reviewer that cannot retrieve any of them as in force on
the as-of date says so, and the run stops on it rather than guessing
from the City's web page or from a later consolidation.

**Order of classification.** Apply the Not established condition first.
Then determine, from the texts, element E1: whether a person operating a
bicycle on a highway is permitted to ride on the roadway with the rights
and duties of a person driving a motor vehicle, quoting the provision.
Then E2: whether any provincial or City rule in force on the as-of date
requires a cyclist to use a bike lane, pathway or sidewalk where one
exists, or prohibits cyclists from the roadway generally or from a class
of road wider than particular designated highways, quoting any such
provision. Then E3: every provision that prohibits or restricts bicycles
on a particular highway or class of highway, listed with its source,
including section 84 of Bylaw 5590 and any designation under its section
107(cc) the reviewer can identify, saying where the existence of a
designation could not be checked.

**Verdict ladder.**

- **Supported** if E1 holds and no rule under E2 is found. Prohibitions
  under E3 are reported as qualifications and do not move the rung,
  because the holder's "the road" is the ordinary street.
- **Partially supported** if E1 holds but a rule under E2 is found: the
  cyclist may use the roadway but must use a bike lane, pathway or
  sidewalk where one is provided, or is excluded from a class of road
  wider than signed highways, so "with or without a bike lane" fails
  where lanes exist.
- **Contradicted** if E1 fails: the texts in force require a cyclist to
  ride off the roadway where a sidewalk or path exists, or do not permit
  bicycles on roadways generally.
- **Not established** if any essential text cannot be retrieved as in
  force on the as-of date, or if the texts conflict in a way the reviewer
  cannot resolve from their own terms.

**Required alternatives, each reported with its result while every other
choice stays primary:** the strict reading of "the same legal right",
under which the reviewer lists every right or duty the texts give a driver
and not a cyclist, or a cyclist and not a driver, and reports whether a
fair holder would accept "the same" with those differences (the rung is
reported under both readings; where they differ the finding is
definition-sensitive and the story says so beside the verdict); and the
alternative as-of date of 2026-10-21, under which any amendment or
consolidation published between the two dates is applied, reported so a
reader knows whether the law changed between the comment and the check.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The position rules for a cycle on a highway (where the regulation
   requires the operator to ride, one-way streets, shoulders, single
   file), quoted, in plain words, as the answer to "right down the middle
   of your lane": what the rules permit and require, not what cyclists
   do.
2. Every highway or class of highway on which bicycles are prohibited by
   bylaw, regulation or sign, as the texts list them.
3. The rule on sidewalks for bicycles, e-bicycles and e-scooters as
   Bylaw 20700 section 78 states it, quoted, with its exception for
   shared pathways and persons aged 16 years and under, and what the
   City's Bike Routes page says in its two places (one by wheel size, one
   by age); the deletion of Bylaw 5590's bicycle sections by Bylaw 20700
   section 96(1)(b) while that page still cites section 50; and the
   result of the chronology check named under "What is measured".
4. The minimum passing distances the City's Charter Bylaw sets for
   drivers passing cyclists, with the bylaw's number and effective date
   as the reviewer finds them; the City's page gives 2021-09-30.
5. Equipment and helmet rules for bicycles under the Vehicle Equipment
   Regulation, Division 5, in one sentence.
6. Whether a bicycle must be licensed or registered, from the texts,
   because the thread argues about it.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether cyclists obey the rules,
whether drivers give the passing distance, whether riding on arterial
roads is safe or wise, and whether lanes should be removed.

### Claim 2, id: `bike-collision-fault-share` (proposed for parking; ladder held)

**Disposition.** This claim is proposed for parking at framing as park 5,
because the bounded search described there and in `source-note.md`
located no City publication stating which party was recorded at fault in
reported bicycle collisions. The park does not assert that no such
publication exists. The proposition, ladder, alternatives and
qualifications below are held unchanged so that, if park 5 reopens on a
located publication, the claim can run without redrafting; none of them
carries a verdict now, and the panel does not grade this claim in this

**Normalized proposition:** In the most recent years for which the City of
Edmonton has published, by 2026-08-27, which party it recorded at fault in
reported collisions involving a bicycle, the cyclist was recorded at fault
in about ninety percent of those collisions.

**Reader-facing question:** When a cyclist is hit in Edmonton, whose fault
does the City's record say it usually was?

**Why this reading.** The holder bets that "90 percent are because the
biker's were in the wrong", about "how many accident's there has been with
bike's", which they heard counted on the news. The count on the news is
not identified and is not the claim; the share is. "In the wrong" is read
as the party recorded at fault, because that is the only sense in which a
public record can say who was in the wrong. "About 90 percent" is carried
as a magnitude with a predeclared threshold: a holder who found the record

said 55 would. No period is named; the fair reading of "has been" is the
recent record, and the window is the latest years the City published,
reported year by year.

**What this verdict establishes, stated beside it.** This proposition
checks what the City's own reports attribute. It does not establish who
was in fact at fault in any collision, what happens in collisions the
database does not hold, or whether cyclists run red lights.

**The instrument.** The City's annual collision publications that state,
for a calendar year, the share of reported collisions involving a bicycle
(or of cyclist injury collisions, where that is the unit the City uses) in
which the cyclist was recorded at fault, found at the addresses named

collisions where that is the unit, in which the cyclist was recorded at
fault; and U, the unit the publication uses. Report each year. Read the
rung from the pooled share across those years where the publication gives
counts, and otherwise from the latest year, saying which.

**Verdict ladder.**

- **Supported** if the share is 80 percent or more.
- **Partially supported** if the share is more than 50 percent and under
  80 percent: the cyclist was recorded at fault in most such collisions,
  but not in nearly all of them.
- **Contradicted** if the share is 50 percent or less.
- **Not established** if no City publication available by the as-of date

**Required alternatives, each reported with its result while every other

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

2. From the Collision Cause dataset, for each year 2018 to 2023, the count

3. The share of persons at fault across all collision types who are

4. The database's reporting limits, in the City's words: no motor vehicle,
   no record; and the September 2022 reporting change.

### Claim 3, id: `street-labs-no-safety-value`

**What the record holds.** The 24 evaluation and assessment documents
linked from the City's Street Labs page on 2026-10-21 are archived and
read (`source-note.md` lists each). Several report before-and-after
vehicle speed or speed-limit compliance figures; some report resident
feedback only; two are project updates stating findings without figures;
one is a survey notice; none reports pedestrian collisions before and
after. Which of them were published by 2026-08-27 is not established from
the page as retrieved, so the window rule below fixes it from the
documents' own dates and has the reviewer report the rest beside the
primary count.

**Normalized proposition:** The City of Edmonton's published evaluations
of its Vision Zero Street Labs available by 2026-08-27, taken together,
report no measured before-and-after reduction in vehicle speed, no
measured increase in speed-limit compliance, and no measured reduction in
pedestrian collisions on the streets where the labs' measures were
installed.

**Reader-facing question:** Did the City's Street Labs measurably slow
traffic or make crossings safer, according to the City's own checks?

**Why this reading.** The holder says the labs "hinder normal street
traffic and have no safety value for pedestrians what so ever". That
traffic calming slows or diverts traffic is its stated purpose and not
disputed; it is reported as context. The claim's point is the second
half, and its force is in "no ... what so ever": not a small effect but
none. The fair test is the City's own measurements, because the holder's
complaint is about a City programme and the City publishes an evaluation
for many of its labs. "Safety value for pedestrians" is read as a
measured safety outcome under the terms fixed above: the City's
evaluations treat lower speed and higher compliance as the way the
measures protect people on foot, and the Safe Mobility Strategy states
the speed-and-injury relationship that reading rests on. A measured
reduction in either contradicts "no value whatsoever"; the threshold for
"measured" is the evaluation's own before-and-after figure, and the brief
sets no magnitude the evaluations do not use. Because a holder may say a
speed change is not safety value for pedestrians, the required
alternative is the direct measure alone, pedestrian collisions before and
after, and both are reported.

**What this verdict establishes, stated beside it.** This proposition
checks what the City's own evaluations report. It does not establish
whether the labs were worth their cost, whether residents liked them,
whether the City's measurements are sound, or what the measures' effect
would be if made permanent.

**The instrument.** The City's Street Labs page and every evaluation
summary, assessment summary, evaluation report or project update it
links, each read whole; the Vision Zero annual reports for 2020 and 2021
for the programme's description and first-year counts; the Safe Mobility
Strategy 2027-2030 for the programme's place in the 2021-2025 strategy.
The evaluations are essential. The page as the reviewer retrieves it may
link more or fewer documents than the 24 archived; the reviewer lists
every one it finds with the dates it carries.

**Window.** Primary: every published evaluation whose own survey window,
report date or stated evaluation period falls on or before 2026-08-27. An
evaluation whose only date is a 2026 index label that is a season or a
month, or whose document dates fall after 2026-08-27, is excluded from the
primary count and reported under the alternative window. Alternative:
every published evaluation the reviewer finds by the evidence cut-off,
2026-10-21, reported beside the primary with each document's dates.

**Order of classification.** Apply the Not established conditions first.
Then, for each published evaluation in the window, record L, the lab; T,
the document's dates; M, each before-and-after figure it reports for
speed, compliance or pedestrian collisions, with its values; and F, any
finding it states in words without a figure. Count N, the evaluations in
the window; R, those reporting at least one before-and-after figure
showing lower speed, higher compliance or fewer pedestrian collisions;
and Z, those reporting no such figure, whether because they report
feedback only, post-only figures, volume only, or a stated finding
without a figure.

**Verdict ladder.**

- **Supported** if N is one or more and R is zero: no evaluation in the
  window reports a measured reduction.
- **Partially supported** if R is one or more and Z is one or more: some
  evaluations report a measured reduction and others report none.
- **Contradicted** if R is one or more and Z is zero.
- **Not established** if N is zero, or the evaluations cannot be
  retrieved, or the reviewer cannot date enough of them to form the
  primary window.

**Required alternatives, each reported with its result while every other
choice stays primary:** the direct measure alone (pedestrian collisions
before and after; where no evaluation in the window reports one, Not
established under this alternative, reported beside the primary); the
alternative window above; and the count rule under which an evaluation
stating a reduction in words without a figure joins R, reported so a
reader sees whether the City's stated findings and its figures point the
same way. Where an alternative changes the rung the finding is
definition-sensitive and the story says so beside the verdict.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. Each evaluation in either window, its lab, its dates, its measures,
   and each before-and-after figure quoted with its location; evaluations
   that report feedback only, named.
2. Traffic volume changes the evaluations report, and measures the City
   removed or converted to permanent and why, as the context for "hinder
   normal street traffic", without a verdict on that half.
3. The number of labs the City says were installed and the number with a
   published evaluation, from the annual reports and the Street Labs
   page, with the page's own note that labs before 2023 did not follow
   the current documentation.
4. Whether any evaluation in either window reports a pedestrian
   collision count before and after, stated either way.

## Stakes

What each verdict would change, for the person making the claim and for
the person arguing against it. Stated without any view of which way it
comes out, and no wider than the rules above establish. Stakes are stated
for the claims proposed for the panel; for the parked claims the record
cannot reach both directions, and no verdict on another claim settles
them.

**The same legal right to the road.** Supported would establish that the
law puts a cyclist on the roadway on a driver's terms whether or not a
lane exists; a commenter who wants cyclists on the sidewalk, or who
expects removing a lane to take cyclists off the road, would learn that
the law does the opposite, and the holder's warning follows from the
rules as a matter of position, with the position rules reported beside
it. Partially supported would establish the right with a condition the
holder denies: where a lane or path is provided the cyclist must use it,
or a class of road is closed, so "with or without a bike lane" fails
where it matters most. Contradicted would establish that the rider in
[70] was not "riding lawfully" on a road without a lane, and that
"Remove bike lanes and they will be cycling right down the middle of your
lane" describes an offence rather than a right. Not established would
mean an essential text could not be read. Under the strict reading of
"the same", reported beside the verdict, a difference the texts make
between drivers and cyclists is weighed, and the finding names it.

**Ninety percent the cyclist's fault.** No stakes are stated for this
run: the claim is proposed for parking (park 5) and no verdict is
reachable in either direction on the records located. If the park
reopens, the held ladder's stakes are these: Supported would establish
that the City's own record attributes nearly all bicycle collisions to
the cyclist; Partially supported, most but not nearly all; Contradicted,
half or more to the other party; and every statement of a finding names
the years it covers.

**Street Labs and pedestrian safety.** Supported would establish that
none of the City's own evaluations available by the as-of date reports a
measured speed, compliance or collision improvement, which is what the
holder asserts and the opposite of what the City's description of the
programme implies. Contradicted would establish that every evaluation
with a before-and-after figure reports an improvement, and the holder's
"what so ever" fails on the City's own numbers. Partially supported would
mean the evaluations divide, some measuring an improvement and others
reporting none. Not established would mean the evaluations could not be
retrieved or dated. Under the direct-measure alternative, reported beside
the verdict, whether any evaluation reports a pedestrian collision figure
is stated, so a holder who means collisions and not speed can see
whether the City's evaluations measure what they mean.

**Definition sensitivity.** Each verdict applies under the primary
readings fixed above. Where a required alternative produces a different
rung, the finding is definition-sensitive, the story says so beside the
verdict, and neither side can fairly cite the verdict without that
qualification.
