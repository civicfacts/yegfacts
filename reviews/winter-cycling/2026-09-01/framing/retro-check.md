## 2. Does the proposition test what the post asserts?

Finding.

> **Normalized proposition:** Edmonton's winter climate makes cycling unworkable as a meaningful transportation mode.

The composites contain at least three distinct claims:

1. Cycling stops at an extreme temperature.
2. Edmonton bike infrastructure has useful demand during only four months of the year.
3. Climate prevents cycling from becoming a viable transportation mode.

Folding them together changes the test. A cold comparator city could bear on the third claim without answering whether Edmontonians cycle at -30 or whether local lanes receive meaningful use outside four months. Edmonton counts could answer the first two without showing that climate is the binding cause.

Replace with:

> The three composite forms are related but not interchangeable. This brief tests only the climate-feasibility claim:
>

>
> The extreme-temperature and four-month claims require separate propositions and measurements. They are out of scope for this review unless separately operationalized.

## 4. Operationalization and its alternatives

Finding.

> "Unworkable as a meaningful transportation mode"
>
> "non-trivial share of trips year-round"
>
> "comparably cold, snowy cities"
>
> "adequate maintenance"
>
> "mature networks"
>
> "current policies and most recent published counts"

The finding depends on choices the brief leaves undefined:

- "Meaningful" and "non-trivial" need a numeric threshold. Reasonable choices include a fixed mode share, a share relative to summer use, or a minimum number of utilitarian trips. They could produce different findings.
- "Winter" could mean December through February, November through March, days below freezing, or days with snow cover. "Year-round" could require continuous monthly use or only some use in every season. These choices could change the finding.
- "Share of trips" needs a denominator. All person-trips, commute trips, trips below a specified distance, and counts on instrumented corridors are not interchangeable.
- "-30" needs a measure if retained. Hourly temperature, daily minimum, daily mean, and wind chill identify different observations.
- "Prevents" and "binding constraint" could mean climate is necessary for low uptake, the largest cause, or merely a substantial cause. Those are different causal propositions.
- Comparator eligibility needs fixed climate measures and cutoffs. Mean winter temperature, snowfall, snow-cover days, freeze-thaw cycles, and extreme-cold days can select different cities.
- "Adequate maintenance," "mature network," and "despite investment" need measurable service, connectivity, and investment criteria. Selecting them after seeing outcomes invites cherry-picking.
- A warm city with low cycling is not, by itself, a negative control that isolates climate. Reasonable alternatives include matched cold and warm comparator sets or within-city seasonal comparisons.
- "Most recent" is not reproducible. The brief needs a fixed evidence cutoff, such as material published by 2026-09-01.
- The required calculations do not specify formulas, seasonal weighting, treatment of missing counter data, or how counts relate to mode share.

Replace the operationalization with a precommitted table or equivalent text specifying:

> Evidence cutoff: 2026-09-01.  
> Winter period: [fixed dates or weather rule].  
> Transportation denominator: [defined trip population].  
> Meaningful-use threshold: [numeric value].  
> Extreme-cold measure, if retained: [temperature variable and interval].  
> Edmonton scenarios: [current network and service standard] and [defined connected-network and service standard].  
> Comparator rule: [fixed climate, urban-form, network, and maintenance criteria applied without reference to cycling outcomes].  
> Calculations: [formulas, seasonal weighting, missing-data treatment, and limits on converting facility counts into mode share].

For every verdict-sensitive choice, require results under at least one reasonable alternative unless the brief gives a result-blind justification acceptable to both sides.

## 6. Is the claim checkable at all?

Finding.

> "Edmonton is too cold for cycling to ever work as transportation."

"Ever" is an unbounded prediction. "Real transportation" may be a value judgment. The climate proposition becomes checkable only when bounded to defined scenarios, measurements, and a time window. The brief excludes the policy question but does not identify the predictive and rhetorical parts it is leaving out.

Replace with:

> **Checkable factual question:** Under each defined Edmonton network and winter-service scenario, what transportation share does cycling reach during the defined winter period, and what evidence attributes any seasonal difference to climate rather than other causes?
>
> This review does not test whether cycling is "real" transportation, whether Edmonton should build bike infrastructure, or whether cycling will "ever" work. Those are value judgments, policy questions, or unbounded predictions.

## 7. Scope traps

Finding.

> Out of scope: whether Edmonton SHOULD build more bike infrastructure (policy question), cost-benefit of the network (separate story), safety statistics (separate story).

The four-month composite commonly implies that bike lanes lack enough annual utility to justify their cost. Excluding cost-benefit while retaining that composite strips away part of its likely meaning. Safety may also be part of what a speaker means by "work as transportation." With no captured posts, the brief cannot establish otherwise.

Infrastructure and maintenance are necessary causal controls, yet the brief treats the claim as applying "regardless" of them. Current uptake is also descriptive evidence, not the same question as feasibility under a different network or service standard.

Replace with:

> **Scope:** This claim concerns winter cycling use and climate causation under the two predeclared network and winter-service scenarios. Infrastructure and maintenance are in scope only as causal controls. Current Edmonton uptake is descriptive context and must not be treated as equivalent to feasibility under another scenario.
>
> Economic justification, policy desirability, safety, and the prediction that cycling will or will not "ever" work are out of scope. No finding about them may be inferred. Remove the four-month composite unless annual utilization receives its own operationalized claim.

A holder of the view would object that the brief replaces a practical concern about actual or plausible Edmonton conditions with an absolute claim about climate defeating cycling under any infrastructure and maintenance regime. An opponent would object that the brief announces strong contrary evidence, then uses an undefined comparator process that can be shaped around successful winter-cycling cities. Both sides have reason to reject the frame until the composites are disclosed as site-written, the proposition is narrowed to one claim, and the thresholds, scenarios, denominator, winter period, calculations, and comparator rule are fixed before research begins.
