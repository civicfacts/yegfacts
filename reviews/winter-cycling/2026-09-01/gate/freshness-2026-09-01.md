### wc-too-cold

- **(b) Update/acknowledgement.** Oulu's [2025 Audit Committee report](https://www.ouka.fi/media/18849/download), published May 18, 2026, confirms that the newest available modal-share result still comes from the **2021** national travel survey: cycling was 18% of trips in Oulu and 17% regionally. The 2026 survey finished collecting data in March, with results expected late in 2026. Date the 18% figure explicitly as 2021 and acknowledge the scheduled refresh. The report does not provide a newer winter-only share.

- **(c) Stronger primary evidence.** Oulu's [2026 budget and 2027-2028 plan](https://www.ouka.fi/media/18727/download?inline=), adopted December 8, 2025, records 168.1 kilometres of highest-class winter-maintained routes in 2022-2024 and a 2026 target of 250 kilometres. Oulu's [current cycling page](https://www.ouka.fi/pyoraily), checked September 1, reports 930 kilometres of cycleways and 165 kilometres receiving "Super" winter maintenance, with around-the-clock cyclability and live maintenance information. These are stronger sources for the maintenance mechanism than the cited advocacy explainers.

- **(c) Missed Edmonton primary document.** The City's [Bike Plan Implementation Guide 2021-2026](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/BikePlan-ImplementationGuide.pdf) specifies bare pavement within 24 hours for Level 1 routes, bare pavement or no more than two centimetres of snowpack within 48 hours for Level 2, and roadway-equivalent maintenance for Level 3. It also documents how mismatched maintenance can make connected segments impassable. The [current service-level page](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/on_your_streets/neighbourhood-roads-winter) now says the Winter Priority Bike Loop reaches bare pavement within one day, while other machine-maintained pathways must be safe and passable within three days.

- **(c) Strengthens the mechanism, not the modal-share estimate.** Tampere University's [FinnCycle final report](https://trepo.tuni.fi/handle/10024/234089), published February 2026, finds recurring winter-maintenance delivery and monitoring gaps across Finnish cities. It supports the distinction between climate and maintenance as possible binding constraints. It is not peer-reviewed and should not replace Oulu-specific counts.

- **(b) Data-source acknowledgement.** Edmonton's derived [Daily Pedestrian and Bike Counts view](https://data.edmonton.ca/w/sw7k-ptx8/depj-dfck?cur=8RGR4qZeAtP), checked September 1, currently warns that its query may be broken or its underlying dataset unavailable. The [base Eco Counter dataset](https://data.edmonton.ca/w/tq23-qn4m/depj-dfck?cur=uLerbx_W750) remains listed. Recalculate from an export of the base dataset and record the extraction date rather than relying on the derived view.

- **(d) Immaterial to the normalized claim, but an editorial guardrail.** The already-cited [June 2026 Trondheim study](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12544-026-00803-7) finds that cold, darkness, and snowfall reduce cycling propensity. That would contradict wording such as "weather does not matter." It does not establish that winter makes meaningful cycling impossible regardless of infrastructure and maintenance.

No post-verification evidence was found establishing Edmonton's climate as a categorical barrier.
