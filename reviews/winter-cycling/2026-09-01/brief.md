# Review brief: Winter cycling in Edmonton

Status: PROVISIONAL — drafted by the orchestrator 2026-09-01; founder
ratifies at the gate. Frozen before round 1. Methodology: v1.0.

## Story

Whenever Edmonton debates bike infrastructure, the same objection appears:
this is a winter city, so cycling can't be real transportation here. This
story tests that claim. It is deliberately the second story YEGFacts
produces because Edmonton-only evidence cannot settle it: the methodology's
comparative-evidence and transferability rules carry the weight.

## Why this story (selection rationale)

- Prevalence: the single most common reply in Edmonton bike-lane threads.
- Civic importance: recurring council decisions on the bike network budget.
- Verifiability: strong comparative evidence exists (winter cities with
  measured year-round cycling); Edmonton counter and maintenance data exist.
- Workflow gate: validates the comparative-evidence path of the methodology.

## Claim under review

### Claim — id: wc-too-cold

**Circulating forms:** "Nobody bikes at -30." "We're a winter city, bike
lanes are for 4 months a year." "Edmonton is too cold for cycling to ever
work as transportation."

**Normalized proposition:** Edmonton's winter climate makes cycling
unworkable as a meaningful transportation mode.

**Operationalization:** "Unworkable as a meaningful transportation mode"
means that winter conditions themselves prevent cycling from serving a
non-trivial share of trips year-round, regardless of infrastructure and
maintenance. The claim is about climate as the binding constraint. Evidence
that Edmonton's current cycling share is low does NOT establish the claim
(low uptake has multiple candidate causes); evidence that comparably cold,
snowy cities sustain meaningful year-round cycling under adequate
maintenance would contradict it. Reviewers must:
- separate feasibility (can it work) from current Edmonton uptake (does it
  currently happen at scale);
- use comparator cities chosen to isolate the climate variable (e.g.
  comparably cold/snowy cities with mature networks; warm cities with low
  cycling as the negative control for "climate determines cycling");
- state transferability explicitly for every comparator: why relevant,
  differences from Edmonton (density, trip distances, temperatures,
  maintenance standards), what can and cannot be inferred;
- report Edmonton-specific winter data where it exists (counter data,
  winter maintenance service standards for bike routes).

## Scope

Geography: Edmonton, with comparators explicitly in scope. Time: current
policies and most recent published counts. Out of scope: whether Edmonton
SHOULD build more bike infrastructure (policy question), cost-benefit of
the network (separate story), safety statistics (separate story).

## Required calculations

Any mode-share or count comparison must state source, year and
denominator; no silent aggregation across seasons.

## Reviewer instructions of special note

- Do not cherry-pick successful cycling cities; search for cold cities
  where winter cycling stayed marginal despite investment, and report them.
- Distinguish temperature from snowfall from maintenance quality.
- Record every source URL; you have no repo access — cite fully.
