Verdict: REVISE

## 1. Provenance

**Finding**

> **Circulating forms:** "Edmonton is a climate leader." "The City is meeting its climate goals." Conversely: "the targets are fake, nothing is on track."

No intake record exists. These are site-written composite paraphrases, not captured posts. The brief does not disclose that fact. Without sources, dates, context, or verbatim claims, there is no basis for saying these forms represent how the claim circulates.

**Replacement wording**

> **Circulating forms:** The site wrote the following composite paraphrases; no individual posts were captured. They must not be presented as quotations or treated as proven representative examples: "Edmonton is a climate leader"; "The City is meeting its climate goals"; "The targets are fake; nothing is on track."

## 2. Does the proposition test what the post asserts?

**Finding**

There is no raw post whose intended meaning can be checked. The three composite forms also contain distinct claims:

- "Climate leader" is a comparative and partly evaluative claim.
- "Meeting its climate goals" could mean meeting current milestones, remaining on a future pathway, or progressing on climate policy generally.
- "The targets are fake" concerns sincerity or credibility.
- "Nothing is on track" is a broad claim that may cover corporate emissions, community emissions, adaptation, spending, or other programs.

The normalized proposition tests only community-wide emissions performance against adopted targets. It neither tests "climate leader" nor whether targets are "fake." Folding all three forms together makes a result about one emissions measure appear to answer broader claims.

**Replacement wording**

> **Claim represented by this brief:** "Edmonton's community-wide greenhouse-gas emissions are on track to meet the City's adopted emissions targets." This is a site-written composite paraphrase, not a captured quotation.
>
> **Claims not tested here:** Whether Edmonton is a "climate leader"; whether its targets are sincere or "fake"; whether every City climate program is on track; and whether corporate City-operations emissions are on track.

## 3. Is it the strongest fair reading?

**Finding**

> **Normalized proposition:** Edmonton is on track to meet its adopted community greenhouse-gas emissions targets.

The proposition does not say whether "targets" means every milestone, the next milestone, or the long-term target. It also changes "meeting its climate goals" into the weaker and more prospective "on track to meet."

**Replacement wording**

> **Normalized proposition:** As of 2026-09-01, Edmonton's latest reported community-wide greenhouse-gas emissions were on the path required by the City's adopted accounting framework to meet each applicable adopted emissions milestone.

If the site intends to test only the nearest milestone, it must say so in both the circulating form and the proposition rather than imply a conclusion about all targets.

## 4. Operationalization and its alternatives

**Finding**

> "On track" means the most recent published community emissions inventory is at or below the trajectory required by the City's adopted targets [...] OR the City's own published assessment states it is on track.

Several verdict-sensitive choices remain unresolved:

- **Meaning of "on track."** The brief uses either a same-period numerical comparison or an attributed City assessment. A reasonable alternative is to require the numerical measure and formal assessment to agree, or to report them separately. The current `OR` rule could establish the proposition solely from the institution's own characterization even when its measurements point elsewhere.
- **Trajectory.** The brief does not define whether the trajectory is an official annual pathway, linear interpolation between milestones, or compliance with a cumulative carbon budget. Those choices can produce different findings.
- **Inventory comparator.** Comparing the latest inventory with the "nearest milestone year" compares different years unless the inventory year happens to equal that milestone. A reasonable alternative is to compare the inventory with the pathway value for the same year.
- **Carbon-budget accounting.** A single-year inventory can be within an annual path while cumulative emissions have exceeded the remaining budget, or the reverse. Both are reasonable measures of progress and could change the finding.
- **Which target.** The brief names 2025, 2030, and 2050 without saying whether every target must be on track. Reporting only the nearest milestone could conceal a different status for another milestone.
- **"Most recent published."** This could mean the document published most recently, the inventory with the latest emissions year, or the latest restated series using the current method. These are not necessarily the same.
- **City assessment.** "The City's own published assessment" could include a formal performance report, a dashboard, a staff report, or promotional communications. The source class could change the finding.
- **As-of date.** "As of 2026" is not a fixed date. A document published later in the year could change the evidence set.
- **Denominator and boundary.** The brief identifies community emissions but does not state whether the target uses absolute emissions or per-capita emissions, which geographic boundary applies, or how inventory-method changes and restatements are handled. The adopted target's own basis should control.
- **Gross versus net emissions.** Treatment of offsets, removals, and credited reductions is unstated. Gross and net results could differ.
- **No independent modelling.** This is reasonable only if City documents publish a comparator for the inventory year. Otherwise the required calculation cannot be performed without inventing a trajectory.

**Replacement wording**

> **Operationalization:** Fix the evidence cutoff at 2026-09-01. Use the absolute community-wide emissions measure, geographic boundary, sector coverage, accounting treatment, units, and restated historical series specified by the adopted target framework. For the latest inventory year available by the cutoff, report:
>
> 1. the inventory value for that year;
> 2. the official pathway value for the same year, if published;
> 3. cumulative emissions against the adopted carbon budget, if the framework supplies that comparison;
> 4. the latest formal City status assessment available by the cutoff, with its publication date, data year, target, and method.
>
> Do not substitute a milestone-year value for a same-year comparator. Do not construct an unpublished pathway. If the documents provide no same-year or cumulative comparator, state that the numerical on-track test cannot be completed. If the numerical comparison and formal City assessment differ, report both and explain the difference; neither automatically overrides the other. Assess each adopted milestone separately before giving a verdict on the plural proposition.

## 5. Does the brief leak an expected finding?

**Finding**

> Editorial balance: expected finding cuts against City messaging, which demonstrates the site is not an administration mouthpiece.

This states the expected result and treats that result as evidence of editorial independence.

**Replacement wording**

> Editorial balance: No expected finding is assumed. Reviewers must apply the frozen test without regard to whether the result agrees with City communications.

**Finding**

> If the City's own documents state it is not on track, that is strong direct evidence.

This tells reviewers how evidence on one side should affect the verdict without giving equivalent treatment to the opposite statement.

**Replacement wording**

> Record any formal City status assessment verbatim, with its date, reporting period, target, metric, and stated basis. Treat it as attributed evidence and compare it with the numerical measures.

**Finding**

> The strongest evidence here is the City contradicting its own goal; quote it precisely and date it.

This both predicts the result and directs reviewers to build the case for it.

**Replacement wording**

> Quote and date the City's latest formal status assessment, whether it says on track, off track, or gives no status. Identify the exact target and metric addressed.

## 6. Is the claim checkable at all?

**Finding**

The time-bounded emissions-status proposition is checkable once the missing definitions are fixed. The other circulating forms are not all checkable as written. "Climate leader" lacks a comparator and criteria. "Fake" concerns intent or legitimacy. "Nothing" is undefined and effectively universal.

The brief does not clearly state that it has isolated only the factual emissions-status claim.

**Replacement wording**

> **Checkability:** This brief tests only a factual, time-bounded claim about community-wide emissions relative to the City's adopted target framework. It does not test whether Edmonton is a "climate leader," whether the targets are sincere or "fake," whether climate policy is desirable, or whether the targets will ultimately be met.

## 7. Scope traps

**Finding**

> Out of scope: [...] future projections beyond what City documents state.

"On track to meet" is prospective. A lagged inventory alone establishes past position against a path, not whether a future target will be achieved. The brief must either narrow the proposition to position against the adopted path as of the latest inventory year or place a defined forecast test in scope.

**Replacement wording**

> The review does not predict whether Edmonton will ultimately meet future targets. It tests whether the latest reported inventory was within the City's adopted pathway for the same reporting year and records any formal City forecast separately as an attributed assessment.

**Finding**

> Corporate (City-operations) emissions are a different measure from community emissions [...] report both if sources allow

Corporate emissions do not answer the normalized proposition. Requiring reviewers to report them invites conflation and adds a separate story.

**Replacement wording**

> Corporate City-operations emissions are outside this claim and must not affect its verdict. Review them only under a separate proposition.

**Finding**

> Out of scope: [...] federal/provincial policy

The merits and causal effects of those policies can remain out of scope. Any assumptions about them embedded in the City's formal forecast cannot be excluded if that forecast is used as evidence.

**Replacement wording**

> The merits of federal and provincial policy, and attribution of responsibility for Edmonton's emissions, are out of scope. Identify any federal or provincial policy assumptions used by a City forecast that is cited as evidence.

A holder of the view would object that the brief mixes their emissions-status claim with broader claims about leadership and sincerity, then changes "meeting" into an undefined version of "on track." An opponent would object that the `OR` rule lets a City self-assessment establish the claim despite contrary measurements. Both should reject the expected-finding language, the nearest-milestone comparison, and the missing fixed cutoff. Both can accept a test that discloses the composite provenance, compares the same accounting periods, and reports numerical performance separately from the City's characterization.
