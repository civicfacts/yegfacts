# Review brief: Who pays for Edmonton's roads

Status: DRAFTED 2026-09-03 by Stew under D-0019, framing check pending,
NOT frozen. Methodology: v1.19. Question id `who-pays-for-roads`,
registered 2026-09-03 from the whole-source intake of
`yegscoop-2026-08-26`. Topics: transportation, bike-lanes, city-finances.

## Question

Who pays for Edmonton's roads, drivers or everybody?

Under a Facebook thread about council's bike-lane decision of 2026-08-26,
fifteen people argued about this and split almost down the middle: eight
said drivers already pay for the roads through the taxes and fees they pay
to drive, seven said the roads come out of taxes everybody pays. Neither
side offered a figure. Both sides treat their answer as settled fact and
build a fairness argument on top of it.

Claims in this question, reviewed and reported separately:

- `drivers-pay-for-roads-via-fuel-taxes`: whether the charges people pay
  because they drive cover what Edmonton spends on its roads.
- `roads-funded-by-property-taxes`: whether property taxes and the City's
  other general revenue supply most of what Edmonton spends on its roads.

Each claim carries its own verdict, panel agreement and evidence-basis
label. Reviewer confidence appears only beside the reviewer who gave it. A
finding on one claim is not a finding on the other. The two claims share a
denominator, fixed once below, and have different numerators; where an
amount enters both numerators, reviewers say so.

**What the register holds and this brief does not carry.** The register
lists four claims under this question. The two above carry verdicts. The
other two, `cyclists-pay-property-taxes` ("Cyclists pay the property and
general taxes that fund roads") and `taxes-fund-services-you-dont-use`
("Edmontonians pay taxes for services and infrastructure they do not
personally use"), do not. Both intake triage seats independently declined
them as undisputed features of the tax system, and a finding on either
would tell no reader anything. They keep their register entries. Neither
this brief nor the article it leads to states a finding on them.

## Why this question (selection rationale)

- **Prevalence.** Fifteen distinct accounts in one captured source, eight
  against and seven for. It is the most evenly split question in that
  source and the second largest by headcount.
- **Civic importance.** Both sides use their answer to decide who has
  standing to object to street spending. If the premise is wrong on either
  side, the fairness argument built on it does not stand up, and the
  argument is live: the Infrastructure Committee heard the bike-route
  program on 2026-08-26 and the Province has said it will legislate on
  municipal bike-lane authority this fall.
- **Verifiability.** Where municipal money comes from and what it is spent
  on is reported annually, on a standardized basis, by every municipality
  in Alberta, and separately by the City in its own audited statements.
  Where fuel-tax and vehicle-registration revenue goes is set out in
  statute and in published grant programs. The named documents are listed
  against each claim below.

## Circulating forms

The captured forms, with comment ids and dates, are in `intake.md`, which
reviewers do not receive. In summary for the panel, all from the same
captured thread, 2026-08-26 to 2026-09-01:

**The claim that drivers pay** was made by eight accounts. Representative
wordings: "there is already a road tax on fuel for that very purpose";
"car drivers pay taxes like vehicle registration and fuel tax to pay for
those roads"; "drivers pay the fuel tax to maintain the roads"; "I do pay
gas taxes, tire taxes and various consumption taxes on vehicles, repairs
and parts that help pay for the road"; "Right now it's a free ride". One
of the eight says drivers pay "partially via the fuel tax".

**The claim that general taxes pay** was made by four accounts.
Representative wordings: "municipal roads are predominantly funded through
property tax"; "The majority of municipal roads are funded through
property tax"; "most of the funds for building and maintaining roads come
from general taxes, property taxes in the case of municipal spending on
road maintenance"; "Your car payment, insurance and gas doesn't pay for
roads: taxes do".

**Prevalence.** The intake establishes that both claims circulate in this
argument and how many distinct accounts made each in this source. It does
not establish how common either is in Edmonton generally, and no claim in
this brief turns on that.

**Magnitude is part of both claims.** Neither side is saying a dollar
exists somewhere. "Pay for the roads" and "predominantly funded" are
claims about who covers the cost, and the propositions carry that.

## Who asks this

A resident would ask: "When the City rebuilds my street, whose money is
that? The tax on my gas and my registration, or the property taxes
everybody pays?"

The propositions answer that at the level the public record answers it:
the City of Edmonton's own road spending, and where the money that funds
it comes from. The record does not answer the narrower question "how much
of the fuel tax I personally pay comes back to Edmonton's roads", because
no published source apportions Alberta's fuel tax revenue geographically
or by taxpayer. This brief says so rather than testing a measurable cousin
of the question in its place.

## Dates fixed in this brief

**As-of date (freeze date):** 2026-09-03. Anything published or decided
after that date is out of scope.

**Reference window:** the City of Edmonton's fiscal years 2022, 2023 and
2024, which are calendar years. These are three consecutive complete
fiscal years whose filed municipal financial data is published on the
as-of date.

**Why three years and why these three.** One year would let a single large
road project or a single grant instalment decide both verdicts, and road
capital work is lumpy. Three consecutive years is the shortest window that
is not dominated by one project. 2022 to 2024 is the most recent such
window that is certainly filed and published. The choice is made before
any figure is seen and neither side gains from it.

**Required alternative window.** If the City of Edmonton's 2025 filing is
published on the as-of date, reviewers repeat both calculations over 2022
to 2025 and report the classification under the four-year window beside
the three-year one. If it is not published, reviewers say so.

## The denominator, fixed once for both claims

Both claims are about a share of what Edmonton spends on its roads. That
figure is fixed here, once, so that neither claim's verdict depends on a
boundary a reviewer drew.

**Primary basis.** The City of Edmonton's Financial Information Return
(FIR), filed annually with Alberta Municipal Affairs under the Municipal
Government Act, Schedule 9C "Financial Activities by Function", the
function **"Roads, streets, walks and lighting"**. Total expenses reported
against that function, summed over the reference window. Published in the
**Municipal Financial and Statistical Data** open dataset
(https://open.alberta.ca/opendata/municipal-financial-and-statistical-data),
which holds one spreadsheet per schedule per reporting year for every
Alberta municipality. Function definitions are in the **Financial
Information Return Manual** (https://open.alberta.ca/publications/6026340).

**Why this boundary and not a City one, stated before any result is
seen.** The category is defined by the Province for every Alberta
municipality, not by Edmonton and not by this brief. It separates roads
from public transit by definition, so a transit total cannot leak into a
roads figure. It is compiled from Edmonton's own audited financial
statements. And the reviewer does not construct the set: it reads a
published row. This site has already published one comparison whose
"roads" boundary was assembled out of capital-profile labels the adopted
budget does not itself publish, and two readers assembled different sets
from the same brief. That is the failure this definition exists to
prevent.

**Every share in this brief is an accounting attribution, not a trace of
dollars.** Money is fungible and no government publishes which dollar paid
for which road. What the record supports is which revenue sources cover
which spending in a set of accounts, and that is what both claims are
tested against. Reviewers state this limit in `limitations`.

**The FIR is an accrual basis.** Expenses reported against a function
include amortization of assets built in earlier years and exclude this
year's capital additions. That means no ratio in this brief can mix
several years of capital spending with one year of operating spending,
which is the arithmetic defect that took the site's `at-100m-vs-snow`
claim off the findings board (methodology v1.19). It also means the
denominator includes a non-cash charge that no revenue source pays in the
year it is charged. Both readings are reported; see the required
alternatives on each claim.

**Required alternative basis.** The transportation or roadway expense
figure the City of Edmonton itself prints in the audited consolidated
financial statements of its Annual Report
(https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/documents/financial-annual-report-2025-compressed_0.pdf
for 2025, with earlier years from
https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/budget-and-finances). Reviewers
state the exact line name, the schedule or note it sits in, and in
particular whether it includes public transit; and report each claim's
classification under it. A figure that includes transit is never called a
roads figure.

**If a named field is absent.** If Schedule 9C, the named function, or
Edmonton's filing for a window year is not published under these names on
the as-of date, reviewers report the name the field carries, or its
absence, and proceed on the required alternative basis as primary, saying
so. A claim is Not established where neither basis yields a figure for the
window; that is a finding about the record, not a failure. If the road
spending figure for the window is zero or absent on both bases, no share
can be computed and both claims are Not established on that ground.

**Provincial roads are not in either claim.** Alberta's provincial
highways in and around Edmonton, including Anthony Henday Drive, are the
Province's roads and the Province's spending. They are outside the City's
FIR function and outside the City's statements, and no claim here covers
them. Reviewers state this where a source mixes the two.

## Claims under review

### Claim 1, id: `drivers-pay-for-roads-via-fuel-taxes`

**Normalized proposition:** The charges people pay because they drive
cover what the City of Edmonton spends on its roads.

**Why this reading.** Eight accounts assert that drivers already pay for
the roads and rest a fairness argument on it, in one case that other road
users get "a free ride". A fair-minded holder would not accept a
proposition that merely asks whether any driver-related dollar exists; the
claim as made is that these charges pay for the roads. So the proposition
carries the coverage. The holder who said drivers pay "partially" is not
failed by default: the ladder below has a band for a substantial
contribution that does not cover the cost, and that band is what a
partial-coverage reading lands in.

**"Charges people pay because they drive", fixed here.** Two components,
both enumerated. Reviewers do not add a source on a judgement that it feels
driver-related; a source enters only through one of these two doors, and
every included amount is listed with its figure and its source.

*(a) Money reaching the City that is tied to fuel or vehicle charges.* A
transfer to the City of Edmonton counts only where the statute, regulation
or published program agreement governing it ties the amount, or the
source of the amount, to fuel tax, fuel sales, or vehicle registration
revenue. Reviewers work this list, and for each one report whether it is
in force on the as-of date, whether its governing instrument ties funding
in that way, and what Edmonton received under it in each window year:

  1. The Local Government Fiscal Framework Act and the LGFF capital
     funding program, with its published allocation formula and program
     guidelines (https://www.alberta.ca/local-government-fiscal-framework-capital-funding).
  2. The City Charters Fiscal Framework Act, SA 2018, c C-13.3
     (https://www.canlii.org/en/ab/laws/stat/sa-2018-c-c-13.3/latest/sa-2018-c-c-13.3.html),
     including whether it was in force in any window year and when it
     ceased to be, if it did.
  3. The Municipal Sustainability Initiative, including any Basic
     Municipal Transportation Grant component
     (https://www.alberta.ca/municipal-sustainability-initiative).
  4. The Canada Community-Building Fund as administered in Alberta,
     including the source of its money and its eligible project
     categories.
  5. Alberta's Fuel Tax Act and the Government of Alberta's own annual
     reporting of fuel tax revenue, to establish where that revenue is
     directed.
  6. Alberta's vehicle registration fees under the Traffic Safety Act and
     its fee regulation, to establish where that revenue is directed.

  Any further transfer whose governing instrument ties it to fuel tax,
  fuel sales or vehicle registration revenue is added, named, with the
  instrument quoted. No transfer is added without one.

*(b) Charges on driving that the City itself collects.* The revenue the
City reports for on-street and City-operated parking, and the revenue it
reports from traffic fines and automated enforcement, in each window year,
from the City's own audited statements or the year-end operating financial
updates (2023: https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=215334;
2024: https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=252517;
2025: https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=291514).
Reviewers name each line as printed. Including these is the reading most
favourable to the claim's holders, since these are charges paid by drivers
and not by other road users. The classification with (b) excluded is
required beside the primary one.

**The verdict figure and its thresholds.** The coverage ratio is the sum
of (a) and (b) over the reference window, divided by the road spending
figure over the same window on the primary basis, in nominal Canadian
dollars.

- **Supported** if the ratio is 1.0 or more.
- **Partially supported** if the ratio is at least 0.25 and below 1.0:
  driver charges make a substantial contribution to road spending, and the
  part that fails is "pay for".
- **Contradicted** if the ratio is below 0.25.
- **Not established** if no road spending figure can be obtained for the
  window on either basis, or if the amount under (a) cannot be determined
  because the governing instruments do not say what they direct revenue
  to.

The bands are contiguous and exhaustive over every ratio the arithmetic
can produce, including zero, which falls in Contradicted, and including a
ratio above 1.0, which falls in Supported. If any component figure is
printed as a negative, reviewers report it as printed and state its effect
on the ratio rather than dropping it.

Alternative cutoffs, results required under both sets: Supported at 0.9 or
above; Partially supported from 0.5 to below 0.9; Contradicted below 0.5.
Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard. 1.0 is
where "pay for" is literally true; 0.9 allows for rounding across a
three-year window; 0.25 is the floor of a contribution a reasonable person
would call substantial, and 0.5 is where driver charges would be the
larger part rather than a contribution. No cutoff may be changed after the
figures are seen.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The ratio and classification with component (b) excluded.
2. The ratio and classification on the required alternative denominator
   (the City's own printed figure), with that figure's contents stated.
3. The ratio restated with amortization removed from the denominator,
   where the filing reports amortization for the function; where it does
   not, reviewers say so and state what source would carry it.
4. The classification over the four-year window, where the 2025 filing is
   published.
5. What the Government of Alberta's own reporting says fuel tax and
   vehicle registration revenue are directed to, whatever that turns out
   to be, with the instrument named.
6. Federal and provincial consumption taxes on vehicles, parts and repairs
   are named in one captured wording. Reviewers state which government
   collects each and what its governing statute directs it to, and state
   whether any part reaches Edmonton's roads. This is reported, not
   counted in the ratio, unless an instrument ties it under door (a).
7. Whether the City reports any revenue in these years from a road toll,
   congestion charge, bicycle registration or similar user charge.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether drivers should pay more or
less; whether anyone is paying a fair share; whether bicycles should be
licensed or registered; whether provincial fuel tax and registration
revenue is adequate to Alberta's own road spending. Whether cyclists pay
taxes is not tested here or anywhere in this question.

### Claim 2, id: `roads-funded-by-property-taxes`

**Normalized proposition:** Property taxes and the City of Edmonton's
other general revenue supply most of what the City spends on its roads.

**Why this reading, and what was split off.** As it appears on the
register the claim is two assertions in one sentence: that property and
general taxes predominantly fund municipal roads, and that fuel taxes and
vehicle registration do not. The second is Claim 1's territory, and two
assertions under one finding would state neither and would tag everybody
quoted beneath it with a verdict on a claim they did not make. So the
proposition here is the positive assertion only. Its holders' own wording
covers "building and maintaining", so the denominator covers both and is
the same one Claim 1 uses.

**"Property taxes and other general revenue", fixed here.** The share is
computed from published fields, not from a set the reviewer assembles.

*Primary share.* From Schedule 9C for the "Roads, streets, walks and
lighting" function, summed over the reference window:

    S = (total expenses − total revenues) ÷ total expenses

Total revenues for a function on that schedule are the revenues the filing
attributes to it. What is left is met from the municipality's general
revenue, of which property taxation is one component. Reviewers state the
schedule's own label for each figure used and quote the FIR Manual's
definition of the function.

*Required alternative share.* Government transfers directed at road work
may or may not be attributed to the function on Schedule 9C. Reviewers
establish from the FIR Manual which it is and say so. Where road-directed
transfers are not already netted off inside the function's revenues,
reviewers compute

    S' = (total expenses − total revenues − road-directed transfers)
         ÷ total expenses

where road-directed transfers are the amounts the City reports as received
and applied to road projects in the window under the programs enumerated
in Claim 1 door (a), each named with its figure and source, and report the
classification under S' beside the one under S. Where the treatment cannot
be established from the manual, reviewers report both S and S' and say
which the schedule supports.

*Required companion figure.* So that "general revenue" is not left as a
word, reviewers report net municipal property taxation as a share of the
City's total revenue in each window year, from the City's audited
statements, and state it beside the verdict. This figure is reported; it
does not carry the verdict.

**The verdict figure and its thresholds.** The verdict figure is S over
the reference window on the primary basis.

- **Supported** if S is greater than 0.5.
- **Partially supported** if S is at least 0.35 and no greater than 0.5:
  general revenue supplies a substantial part of road spending, and the
  part that fails is "most".
- **Contradicted** if S is below 0.35.
- **Not established** if Schedule 9C does not publish revenues and
  expenses for the function for the window, and the required alternative
  basis does not yield the equivalent figures either.

The bands are contiguous and exhaustive. S above 1.0, which happens only
if the function's attributed revenues are negative as printed, falls in
Supported. S at or below zero, which happens if attributed revenues equal
or exceed expenses, falls in Contradicted. S exactly 0.5 falls in
Partially supported, because "most" and "predominantly" mean more than
half.

Alternative cutoffs, results required under both sets: Supported above
0.6; Partially supported from 0.4 to 0.6 inclusive; Contradicted below
0.4. Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard. More
than half is what "most" and "predominantly" mean in ordinary speech; 0.6
is where a majority is not arguable on rounding; 0.35 and 0.4 are two
readings of where a share stops being a substantial part.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The classification under S', and whether it differs from the one under
   S.
2. The classification on the required alternative denominator, with that
   figure's contents stated.
3. The share restated with amortization removed from the denominator
   where the filing reports amortization for the function.
4. The classification over the four-year window, where the 2025 filing is
   published.
5. The split of the residual by revenue type, as far as the City's
   statements publish it: property taxation, franchise fees, investment
   earnings, and anything else the statements name as general revenue.
6. The place of tax-supported debt. The City funds road capital partly by
   borrowing, and its capital budget labels that funding "tax-supported
   debt". Reviewers state how the FIR treats debt-funded capital under an
   accrual basis, what the City's debt servicing for road-related debt
   costs in the window, and where that servicing is funded from. This is
   reported, not folded into S.
7. The City's own budget documents for the 2023-2026 cycle
   (https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2023-2026CapitalBudget.pdf,
   https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2023-2026OperatingBudget.pdf):
   what funding sources they name for road capital and for road operating
   programs, and in what proportions, as the City itself presents them.
   Budget documents establish what was planned, never what was spent.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether property taxes are too high or
have risen (a separate claim, parked on the register); whether the City
spends its road money well; whether renters pay property tax; whether
cyclists pay property tax.

## Stakes

**Claim 1.** *Supported* would mean the eight accounts are right on the
arithmetic: driving charges cover Edmonton's road bill, and the argument
that other road users are subsidised has a factual base that the other
side would have to answer. *Partially supported* would mean driving
charges are a real contribution but not the source of the money, so the
"free ride" framing overstates it while the underlying point survives in
part. *Contradicted* would mean the premise eight people built a fairness
argument on does not hold, and that argument would have to be made some
other way. *Not established* would mean neither an Edmontonian nor a
councillor can find out from the public record where the money for their
street comes from, which would be a finding worth publishing on its own.
Each is reachable before any evidence is read: the instruments enumerated
under door (a) include Alberta statutes that tie municipal capital
transfers to provincial revenue measures, and whether any of them ties to
fuel or vehicle revenue, and in what amount in the window, is what the
panel establishes.

**Claim 2.** *Supported* would mean the four accounts are right that
general revenue carries the roads, and the people who object to paying for
infrastructure they do not use would be objecting to how municipal
finance works rather than to a bike lane. *Partially supported* would mean
general revenue is a substantial part but not most, and something else,
grants or debt or fees, is doing more of the work than either side in the
thread said. *Contradicted* would mean the money mostly comes from
somewhere other than Edmonton's own general revenue, which would surprise
the people who made this claim most confidently. *Not established* has the
same significance as for Claim 1.

**Both claims can fail together.** The two claims are not complements. If
most road money comes from provincial or federal transfers that are not
tied to fuel or vehicle charges, from developer levies, or from borrowing,
both propositions are wrong at once and neither side of the thread has the
right answer. That outcome is available on the evidence and reviewers
should not treat it as a contradiction to be resolved.

**Definition sensitivity.** Each verdict applies under the primary
denominator and the primary numerator set named above. Where a required
alternative produces a different classification, the finding is
definition-sensitive, the article says so beside the verdict, and neither
side can fairly cite the verdict without that qualification.

## Scope

**Geography.** The City of Edmonton's own roads and the City's own
finances. Provincial highways and provincial road spending are outside
both claims. No comparator municipality is required; if a reviewer uses
one to show how the standardized reporting works, it is context and
carries no verdict.

**Time.** As fixed above.

**Out of scope.** Whether anyone pays a fair share; whether bicycles
should be licensed; whether property taxes are too high or have risen;
whether Edmonton's roads and alleys are in poor condition (registered
separately as `infrastructure-deficit`); what share of the road capital
budget the bike-lane program is (registered separately under
`active-transportation`); whether free on-street parking is a subsidy
(registered separately under `on-street-parking`); whether most cyclists
also drive (registered separately under `who-cycles`); the cost or value
of any particular road or bike route; anything published after the as-of
date.

## Required calculations

Shared by both claims:

- Road spending on the primary basis: the "Roads, streets, walks and
  lighting" function's total expenses and total revenues, per year, for
  each year in the window, from Schedule 9C of Edmonton's Financial
  Information Return, with the reporting year of each spreadsheet stated
  and the units as printed. Show the sum.
- The same on the required alternative basis, with the City's own line
  name and its contents stated, including whether transit is in it.
- Amortization charged to the function per year where the filing reports
  it, and the restated denominator with it removed.
- The four-year window figures where the 2025 filing is published.

Claim 1:

- Every transfer worked through door (a), with the instrument, whether it
  is in force on the as-of date, whether it ties funding to fuel or
  vehicle revenue, and Edmonton's receipt in each window year.
- Every City-collected driving charge under door (b), by line name and
  year.
- The coverage ratio and classification under both cutoff sets, with and
  without door (b), and under both denominators.

Claim 2:

- S per year and over the window, with numerator and denominator shown.
- S' per year and over the window, with the road-directed transfers listed
  and named.
- Net municipal property taxation as a share of the City's total revenue,
  per window year.
- The classification under both cutoff sets and under both denominators.

All figures in nominal Canadian dollars unless a constant-dollar
restatement is reported as a qualification with its index named.

## Reviewer instructions of special note

- Name the schedule, the spreadsheet, the reporting year, the row label
  and the units for every figure taken from the filed municipal data, and
  the document, report number, page and date for every figure taken from a
  City publication. A news report is a lead to the document behind it,
  never the source of a figure in a verdict.
- Keep the levels of government apart. Fuel tax and vehicle registration
  in Alberta are provincial charges; property tax is municipal; several
  capital programs are provincial or federal transfers. A dollar collected
  by one government does not reach another's roads unless an instrument
  sends it there. Say which government collects each amount you name.
- Keep capital and operating money apart, and never set a multi-year
  capital total against a single year of operating spending. Where you
  report the City's own capital and operating figures as a qualification,
  match the years and label each.
- Keep budgeted apart from spent. An approved or planned amount is not
  money out the door, and no verdict figure in this brief is a budget
  figure.
- Do not construct a roads boundary of your own. The denominator is a
  published row. If you think the published row is the wrong container,
  say so in `interpretation_notes` and answer the claims as posed.
- Report one verdict per claim, under the primary readings and cutoffs
  fixed here. Every alternative reading's result goes in
  `interpretation_notes` or `limitations`. The schema carries one verdict
  per claim.
- Record every source URL in full; you have no repo access. Where a
  council document is reached through the City's meeting portal, give the
  meeting agenda page as well as the attachment URL.
- If a definition, cutoff, window or basis in this brief changes what the
  honest answer is, record a MATERIAL FRAMING CONCERN per
  `prompts/reviewer.md` and answer the claims as posed alongside it.
