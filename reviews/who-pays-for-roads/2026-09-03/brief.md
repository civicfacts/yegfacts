## Question

Who pays for Edmonton's roads, drivers or everybody?

Under a Facebook thread about council's bike-lane decision of 2026-08-26,
fifteen people argued about this and split almost down the middle. Eight
accounts advanced the driver-charge premise. Four accounts explicitly said
property or general taxes provide most municipal road funding. Three
further accounts made adjacent taxpayer or shared-service arguments. Those
adjacent claims do not carry verdicts. Neither side offered a figure. Both
sides treat their answer as settled fact and build a fairness argument on
top of it.

**One claim in this question carries a verdict:**

- `drivers-pay-for-roads-via-fuel-taxes`: whether the charges people pay
  because they drive cover what Edmonton's roads function costs the City.

**Three do not, for two different reasons.**

`roads-funded-by-property-taxes` ("Edmonton's municipal roads are paid for
predominantly out of property and general taxes, not fuel taxes or vehicle
registration") is **parked for want of a record**, not declined as
uninteresting. No published source quantifies what share of a municipal
function is financed by property or other general taxes. Alberta's
Financial Information Return puts all property taxation into an
unallocated pool that also holds every federal and provincial transfer,
and nothing published separates the two at the level of a function. The
brief requires the nearest computable figure to be reported, with no
verdict attached, and states below what the article may and may not say
about it. The reasoning is in `framing/resolution.md`. The claim reopens if
a published source attributes municipal own-source revenue, or property
taxation, to a reporting function.

`cyclists-pay-property-taxes` ("Cyclists pay the property and general taxes
that fund roads") and `taxes-fund-services-you-dont-use` ("Edmontonians pay
taxes for services and infrastructure they do not personally use") carry no
verdict for a different reason: both intake triage seats independently
declined them as undisputed features of the tax system, and a finding on
either would tell no reader anything. All three keep their register
entries. Neither this brief nor the article it leads to states a finding on
any of them.

## Why this question (selection rationale)

- **Prevalence.** Fifteen distinct accounts in one captured source. It is
  the most evenly split question in that source and the second largest by
  headcount.
- **Civic importance.** Both sides use their answer to decide who has
  standing to object to street spending. If the premise is wrong on either
  side, the fairness argument built on it does not stand up, and the
  argument is live: the Infrastructure Committee heard the bike-route
  program on 2026-08-26 and the Province has said it will legislate on
  municipal bike-lane authority this fall.
- **Verifiability.** What municipal functions cost and what revenue is
  attributed to them is reported annually, on a standardized basis, by
  every municipality in Alberta, and separately by the City in its own
  audited statements. Where fuel-tax and vehicle-registration revenue goes
  is set out in statute and in published grant programs. What the record
  does *not* publish is set out under "Who asks this" below, and it is the
  reason one registered claim is parked.

## Circulating forms

The captured forms, with comment ids and dates, are in `intake.md`, which
reviewers do not receive. In summary for the panel, all from the same
captured thread, 2026-08-26 to 2026-09-01:

**The claim that drivers pay** was made by eight accounts, and those eight
wordings are not all the same claim. Some assert coverage: "there is
already a road tax on fuel for that very purpose"; "car drivers pay taxes
like vehicle registration and fuel tax to pay for those roads"; "drivers
pay the fuel tax to maintain the roads"; "Right now it's a free ride".
Others assert only a contribution: "people that drive cars pay for roads
partially via the fuel tax"; "I do pay gas taxes, tire taxes and various
consumption taxes on vehicles, repairs and parts that help pay for the
road"; "at least vehicles and rail pay many different kinds of taxes". The
distinction decides what the verdict is about and is fixed in Claim 1
below.

**The claim that general taxes pay** was made by four accounts.
Representative wordings: "municipal roads are predominantly funded through
property tax"; "The majority of municipal roads are funded through
property tax"; "most of the funds for building and maintaining roads come
from general taxes, property taxes in the case of municipal spending on
road maintenance"; "Your car payment, insurance and gas doesn't pay for
roads: taxes do". No verdict is reported on these wordings.

**Prevalence.** The intake establishes that both claims circulate in this
argument and how many distinct accounts made each in this source. It does
not establish how common either is in Edmonton generally, and nothing in
this brief turns on that.

**Magnitude is part of the claim under review.** Nobody is saying a dollar
exists somewhere. "Pay for the roads" is a claim about who covers the
cost, and the proposition carries that.

## Who asks this, and what the record can answer

A resident would ask: "Do fuel taxes and vehicle fees pay Edmonton's road
costs, or do property and other general taxes pay most of them?"

The record answers the first half and not the second.

For the first half, the available record answers a narrower accounting
question about a published municipal function called "Roads, Streets,
Walks, Lighting". It does not trace the financing of a particular street
rebuild. Claim 1 names that narrower level, and the published article must
name it too, in the reader's own words.

For the second half, **the available record cannot answer whether property
and other general taxes pay most of the function's cost.** The article
reports that limitation as the answer to that part of the resident's
question. It may separately report how much expense remains after
function-attributed revenue and identified road transfers, but it gives
that calculation no claim verdict.

Three things the record cannot do, stated here so that no proposition
pretends otherwise:

1. **No published source apportions Alberta's fuel tax or vehicle
   registration revenue geographically or by taxpayer.** So "how much of
   the fuel tax I personally pay comes back to Edmonton's roads" is not
   testable, and no claim here asks it.
2. **No published source attributes property tax revenue to a municipal
   function.** The Financial Information Return puts all property taxation
   into Schedule 9C's unallocated Total General Revenue, and the City's
   audited statements report revenue by type and expenses by segment
   without joining them.
3. **The unallocated pool cannot be separated into the City's own revenue
   and government transfers at the level of a function.** Schedule 9C's
   general pool holds all federal and provincial operating and capital
   transfers alongside taxation, and the only published attribution of any
   transfer to the roads function is Schedule 9E column 2, which covers
   provincial capital transfers only. So a residual computed after
   function-attributed revenue is not a municipal own-revenue share, and
   nothing in this brief may call it one.

## Dates, dollars and bases fixed in this brief

**As-of date (freeze date):** 2026-09-03, the date the question was fixed
on the register. The capture date, 2026-09-02, is the reasonable
alternative freeze date; it is not expected to change which annual records
exist, and reviewers identify any verdict evidence first published during
the intervening day.

**Reference window:** the City of Edmonton's fiscal years 2023, 2024 and
2025, which are calendar years. These are the three most recent complete
fiscal years published on the as-of date. Alberta's Municipal Financial
and Statistical Data record carries a 2025 financial-year workbook and was
last updated 2026-07-08; the City publishes its 2025 audited annual report.

**Why three years.** One year would let a single large road project or a
single grant instalment decide the verdict, and road capital work is
lumpy. Three consecutive years is the shortest window that is not
dominated by one project. The choice of length is made before any figure
is seen and neither side gains from it.

**Required alternative window.** Reviewers repeat every calculation over
2022 to 2025 and report the classification under the four-year window
beside the three-year one. If any window year's filing turns out not to be
published on the as-of date, reviewers say which and proceed on the years
that are, stating what they used.

**Dollars.** Figures are stated in nominal Canadian dollars, and every
window total is **also** restated in constant 2025 dollars using Statistics
Canada Table 18-10-0005-01, Consumer Price Index, annual average, not
seasonally adjusted, Edmonton, all-items
(https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1810000501).
Reviewers report whether the classification changes under the
constant-dollar restatement. This is required, not optional: nominal
aggregation over a multi-year window gives later, higher-price years more
weight and could move a classification near a cutoff.

## The denominator, fixed once

**Primary basis.** The City of Edmonton's Financial Information Return
(FIR), filed annually with Alberta Municipal Affairs under the Municipal
Government Act, Schedule 9C "Financial Activities by Function", the
function **"Roads, Streets, Walks, Lighting"**. Total expenses reported
against that function, summed over the reference window. Published in the
**Municipal Financial and Statistical Data** open dataset
(https://open.alberta.ca/opendata/municipal-financial-and-statistical-data),
which holds one workbook per reporting financial year containing the
published FIR schedules and municipal records for every Alberta
municipality. Function definitions are in the **Financial Information
Return Manual** (https://open.alberta.ca/publications/6026340; the 2024
edition is at
https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/123503ed-54a6-4dce-a382-1d0992ad06f6/resource/96618c96-0f04-4995-bee0-9752dee204dc/download/ma-financial-information-return-manual-2024.pdf).

**What that function contains, named rather than assumed.** The manual
defines it as activities which provide for roads, streets, walks and
lighting, including roads and streets, medians, boulevards, sidewalks,
street lighting, street signs, traffic signals, railway crossing signals,
and public parking facilities. It is therefore not roads alone. Every
verdict in this brief applies to that published function, and the article
says so in the reader's words. Reviewers quote the manual's definition.
The manual reports public transit as a separate function, so a transit
total cannot leak into this figure.

**Primary basis measures accounting cost, not cash spent.** The FIR is an
accrual basis. Expenses reported against a function include amortization of
assets built in earlier years and exclude the year's capital additions. So
the primary calculation measures the annual accounting cost reported for
the function. It does not measure the cash spent building roads during the
window, and it does not identify the financing of any particular street
rebuild. Reviewers state this in `limitations` and never call the primary
figure "what the City spent". The accrual basis is also what makes it
impossible to set several years of capital against one year of operating
spending, which is the arithmetic defect that took the site's
`at-100m-vs-snow` claim off the findings board (methodology v1.19).

**Required alternative basis: the City's own audited line.** The
**"Roadway and parking"** expense line the City of Edmonton reports within
Transportation services in the segment disclosure of its audited
consolidated financial statements (2024 annual report:
https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2024FinancialAnnualReport.pdf;
2023 statements:
https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/FinancialAnnualReportConsolidatedFinancialStatements2023.pdf;
other years from
https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/budget_finances/financial-reports).
Reviewers state the exact line name, the schedule or note it sits in, and
report the classification under it. **The broader "Transportation
services" total must not be used as a road denominator**: it includes bus
and light rail transit, which the City reports on its own line beside
"Roadway and parking". A figure that includes transit is never called a
roads figure.

**Required alternative basis: the capital side, so "building" is not
dropped.** The claims speak of building as well as maintaining, and the
accrual primary excludes the year's capital additions. For the "Roads,
Streets, Walks, Lighting" function, over the same window:

- The capital denominator is **purchased tangible capital asset additions,
  Schedule 9F column 1**. That is what the roads function's capital
  additions cost the City.
- **Donated or contributed additions (Schedule 9F column 2) are reported
  separately and are not in that denominator**, because an asset the City
  did not pay for is not part of what its roads cost it.
- The capital numerator contains **only driver-linked amounts that a
  published source identifies as financing those purchased additions.**
- Capital long-term debt principal additions and reductions (Schedule 9F
  columns 3 and 4) and interest on long-term debt (Schedule 9E column 4)
  are reported separately: principal additions are financing, repayments
  are not negative capital cost, and neither is revenue.

This basis is an alternative and not the primary, because the primary must
remain a single published row that two reviewers reading this brief will
read identically.

**Every share in this brief is an accounting attribution, not a trace of
dollars.** Money is fungible and no government publishes which dollar paid
for which road. What the record supports is which revenue sources are
attributed to which function in a set of accounts. Reviewers state this
limit in `limitations`.

**How the FIR treats revenue, established from the manual rather than
assumed.** Schedule 9C separates *Total General Revenue* (line 0700), which
the manual defines as revenues not allocated to specific reporting
functions, from *Function Revenue* (lines 0730 to 1130). The manual states
that Total General Revenue should normally include all property, business
and special taxation, all penalties and costs on taxes, all franchise and
concession contract revenue, and **all federal and provincial government
operating and capital transfers**. Reviewers quote the manual on this and
state whether Edmonton's own filings follow the normal treatment it
describes. The one published attribution of transfers to the function is
Schedule 9E column 2, provincial capital transfers by function.

**Amortization is published by function.** Schedule 9E column 3 reports
annual amortization expense broken down by function, tying to Schedule 9D
line 2110. Reviewers take the amortization charged to "Roads, Streets,
Walks, Lighting" from that column for each window year and report every
ratio restated with it removed from the denominator. This is a required
calculation, not a conditional one.

**If a named field is absent.** If Schedule 9C, 9E, 9F, the named function,
or Edmonton's filing for a window year is not published under these names
on the as-of date, reviewers report the name the field carries, or its
absence, and proceed on the required alternative basis as primary, saying
so. The claim is Not established where no basis yields a figure for the
window; that is a finding about the record, not a failure.

**Provincial roads are not in the claim.** Alberta's provincial highways in
and around Edmonton, including Anthony Henday Drive, are the Province's
roads and the Province's spending. They are outside the City's FIR function
and outside the City's statements. Reviewers state this where a source
mixes the two.

## The claim under review

### Claim 1, id: `drivers-pay-for-roads-via-fuel-taxes`

**Normalized proposition, which carries the verdict:** The fuel taxes,
vehicle-registration fees and other vehicle charges named in the captured
claims cover what Edmonton's "Roads, Streets, Walks, Lighting" function
costs the City.

**Which captured wordings the verdict is about.** The intake contains
strong coverage readings and weaker contribution readings. The coverage
proposition carries the verdict only for wordings that fairly assert
coverage. Contribution-only wordings, including "partially via the fuel
tax", "help pay for the road" and "pay many different kinds of taxes",
carry no verdict. **Do not state that all eight accounts asserted full
coverage.** Reviewers report the amount and the share as a qualification
for the contribution readings, and the article identifies exactly which
captured wordings the verdict covers and which it does not.

**"Charges people pay because they drive", fixed here.** Two components,
both enumerated, and both subject to the same attribution test: an amount
enters the numerator only where a published record attributes it to the
roads function or to named Edmonton road projects in the window. Reviewers
do not add a source on a judgement that it feels driver-related, and every
included amount is listed with its figure and its source.

*(a) Money reaching the City that is tied to fuel or vehicle charges, and
attributed to roads.* Two tests, both of which must pass.

  *The instrument test.* The statute, regulation or published program
  agreement governing the transfer ties the amount, or the source of the
  amount, to fuel tax, fuel sales, or vehicle registration revenue.
  Reviewers work this list, and for each one report whether it is in force
  on the as-of date, whether its governing instrument ties funding in that
  way, and what Edmonton received under it in each window year:

  1. The Local Government Fiscal Framework Act and the LGFF capital
     funding program, with its published allocation formula and program
     guidelines (https://www.alberta.ca/local-government-fiscal-framework-capital-funding).
  2. The City Charters Fiscal Framework Act, SA 2018, c C-13.3
     (https://www.canlii.org/en/ab/laws/stat/sa-2018-c-c-13.3/latest/sa-2018-c-c-13.3.html).
     Reviewers establish whether the Act was in force during any window
     year and whether any amount governed by it was received or attributed
     during the window.
  3. The Municipal Sustainability Initiative, including any Basic
     Municipal Transportation Grant component
     (https://www.alberta.ca/municipal-sustainability-initiative).
  4. The Canada Community-Building Fund as administered in Alberta
     (https://www.alberta.ca/canada-community-building-fund), including
     the source of its money and its eligible project categories.
  5. Alberta's Fuel Tax Act and the Government of Alberta's own annual
     reporting of fuel tax revenue, to establish where that revenue is
     directed (https://www.alberta.ca/about-fuel-tax).
  6. Alberta vehicle-registration fees under sections 52 and 64 of the
     Traffic Safety Act
     (https://www.canlii.org/en/ab/laws/stat/rsa-2000-c-t-6/) and the
     Registry Agent Product Catalogue, to establish where that revenue is
     directed. If a regulation prescribes the government fee, reviewers
     identify that regulation by title and citation.
  7. The Alberta tire recycling environmental fee, which one captured
     wording names as a "tire tax" that helps pay for the road
     (https://files.albertarecycling.ca/Tires_Products-Definitions-and-Fees.pdf).
     Reviewers identify its legal basis, collector, statutory or program
     purpose, and whether any published record sends any part of it to
     Edmonton's road function. It enters no ratio without both a
     vehicle-charge connection and a published road attribution.

  Any further transfer whose governing instrument ties it to fuel tax,
  fuel sales or vehicle registration revenue is added, named, with the
  instrument quoted. No transfer is added without one.

  *The attribution test.* Only the portion of such a transfer that a
  published City or provincial record attributes to Edmonton's roads
  function, or identifies as applied to named Edmonton road projects
  during the window, enters the numerator. **A whole allocation is not
  counted merely because roads were an eligible use.** These are
  broad-purpose capital programs whose eligible uses include transit,
  water, wastewater, emergency facilities and other infrastructure, and
  counting the full allocation would put money spent on other functions
  into a roads ratio. The published attribution to use first is Schedule
  9E column 2, provincial capital transfers by function, for the "Roads,
  Streets, Walks, Lighting" row. Where an amount passes the instrument
  test but no published record attributes any of it to roads, reviewers
  report the amount as unattributable, state that it is excluded from the
  ratio, and give the ratio's value if it were wholly included as a stated
  upper bound.

*(b) Charges on driving that the City itself collects and attributes to
the function.* Schedule 9E column 1 reports sales and user charges
attributed to the "Roads, Streets, Walks, Lighting" function. **That line
does not enter the primary numerator as an undifferentiated total.**
Include only a component that a published source identifies both as a
charge paid because of driving and as revenue attributed to the function.
If the total cannot be disaggregated, exclude it from the primary
numerator and report the whole line as an upper-bound sensitivity.
Reviewers state what the City reports on that line and how they
disaggregated it, or that they could not.

*Not in the numerator: fines.* Traffic fines and automated enforcement
revenue are not counted. The FIR does not break fines out by function, a
fine arises from a violation rather than from road use, and no captured
wording rests on fines. Reviewers report the City's fine and automated
enforcement revenue, and any City parking revenue the FIR does not
attribute to the function, as a **non-verdict sensitivity calculation**:
the ratio restated with those amounts added, stated as such, from the
City's own audited statements or the year-end operating financial updates
reported to Council. For each such report, reviewers record the report
name and number, the meeting date, the agenda item and the agenda-page URL
alongside the attachment link. These attachment links are starting points
recorded at drafting and were not independently opened; reviewers verify
each against the agenda page and say so if a link does not resolve:
2023, https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=215334;
2024, https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=252517;
2025, https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=291514.

**The verdict figure and its thresholds.** The coverage ratio is the sum
of (a) and (b) over the reference window, divided by the function's total
expenses over the same window on the primary basis, in nominal Canadian
dollars.

- **Supported** if the ratio is 1.0 or more.
- **Partially supported** if the ratio is at least 0.25 and below 1.0:
  driver charges make a substantial contribution to road cost, and the
  part that fails is "pay for".
- **Contradicted** if the ratio is below 0.25.
- **Not established** if no road cost figure can be obtained for the
  window on any basis, or if the amount under (a) cannot be determined
  because the governing instruments do not say what they direct revenue
  to.

The bands are contiguous and exhaustive over every ratio the arithmetic
can produce, including zero, which falls in Contradicted, and including a
ratio above 1.0, which falls in Supported. If any component figure is
printed as a negative, reviewers report it as printed and state its effect
on the ratio rather than dropping it.

Alternative cutoffs, results required under both sets: Supported at 0.9 or
above; Partially supported from 0.5 to below 0.9; Contradicted below 0.5.
Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard. 1.0 is
where "pay for" is literally true; 0.9 allows for rounding across a
three-year window; 0.25 is the floor of a contribution a reasonable person
would call substantial, and 0.5 is where driver charges would be the
larger part rather than a contribution. No cutoff may be changed after the
figures are seen.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The ratio and classification with component (b) excluded, and the
   upper-bound sensitivity with the whole Schedule 9E column 1 line
   included.
2. The ratio and classification on the required alternative denominator
   (the City's audited "Roadway and parking" line), with that figure's
   contents stated.
3. The ratio and classification on the capital basis.
4. The ratio restated with amortization removed from the denominator,
   from Schedule 9E column 3.
5. The classification over the four-year window.
6. The classification under the constant-dollar restatement.
7. The upper-bound ratio with unattributable driver-tied transfers wholly
   included, labelled as an upper bound.
8. The non-verdict sensitivity with fines and non-attributed parking
   revenue added.
9. What the Government of Alberta's own reporting says fuel tax and
   vehicle registration revenue are directed to, whatever that turns out
   to be, with the instrument named.
10. Federal and provincial consumption taxes on vehicles, parts and
    repairs are named in one captured wording. Reviewers state which
    government collects each and what its governing statute directs it to,
    and whether any part reaches Edmonton's roads. Reported, not counted
    in the ratio, unless an instrument ties it and a published record
    attributes it under door (a).
11. Whether the City reports any revenue in these years from a road toll,
    congestion charge, bicycle registration or similar user charge.
12. The amount and share attributable to the contribution-only wordings,
    reported so that those wordings are answered without being tagged with
    the coverage verdict.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether drivers should pay more or
less; whether anyone is paying a fair share; whether bicycles should be
licensed or registered; whether provincial fuel tax and registration
revenue is adequate to Alberta's own road spending. Whether cyclists pay
taxes is not tested here or anywhere in this question.

## Reported without a verdict: the accounting gap

`roads-funded-by-property-taxes` carries no verdict, for the reason in the
Question section and in `framing/resolution.md`. The nearest computable
figure is still worth publishing, and is computed and reported as follows.

From Schedules 9C and 9E for the "Roads, Streets, Walks, Lighting"
function, summed over the reference window:

    Gap = (total expenses − function revenue − road-attributed transfers)
          ÷ total expenses

where *function revenue* is the revenue Schedule 9C attributes to the
function (line 0850), and *road-attributed transfers* are the provincial
capital transfers Schedule 9E column 2 attributes to the function, plus
any further government transfer a published City record identifies as
applied to named Edmonton road projects in the window, each named with its
figure and source.

**What this figure is and is not.** It is the share of the function's
reported cost not offset by revenue the record attributes to the function
or by separately identified road transfers. **It is not a property-tax
share, and it is not a municipal own-revenue share**, because the
unallocated pool it is met from also holds federal capital transfers and
all operating transfers, which nothing published separates from the City's
own money at the level of a function. No verdict word attaches to it.
Reviewers report it in `interpretation_notes`, state this limitation
beside it, and do not classify it. The article states that this residual is
not a property-tax or municipal-own-revenue share and does not tag the
captured property-tax wordings with it.

Reported beside it, from the City's audited statements for each window
year, again with no verdict: net municipal property taxation as a share of
the City's total revenue; and the other named components of revenue not
attributed to functions, including franchise and concession contract
revenue, penalties and costs on taxes, investment earnings, and federal and
provincial transfers. Reviewers also state how the FIR treats debt-funded
capital under an accrual basis, and report the City's own budget documents
for the 2023-2026 cycle
(https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2023-2026CapitalBudget.pdf,
https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2023-2026OperatingBudget.pdf):
what funding sources they name for road capital and for road operating
programs, and in what proportions, as the City itself presents them.
Budget documents establish what was planned, never what was spent.

## Stakes

**Claim 1.** *Supported* would require holders to accept the defined
accounting boundary but would establish their full-coverage premise;
opponents would have to abandon the claim that driver-linked charges do
not cover that cost. *Contradicted* would require holders to abandon full
coverage; opponents would gain evidence against that premise, without
settling who ought to pay. *Partially supported* would preserve a measured
contribution while rejecting full coverage. *Not established* would leave
both sides without a defensible public-record answer, which would be a
finding worth publishing on its own. **All four verdicts are reachable on
Claim 1** under the numerator and denominator definitions above, before
any figure is read.

**The parked claim.** `roads-funded-by-property-taxes` carries no verdict
because the public record does not quantify the property or general-tax
share, or distinguish municipal own-source general revenue from
unallocated transfers at the road-function level. Holders would learn that
the public record does not establish their claimed majority. Opponents
would gain no evidence that driver charges cover the roads. The adjusted
net-cost figure may describe the accounting gap, but it changes neither
side's factual claim.

**Definition sensitivity.** The verdict applies under the primary
denominator and the primary numerator set named above. Where a required
alternative produces a different classification, the finding is
definition-sensitive, the article says so beside the verdict, and neither
side can fairly cite the verdict without that qualification.

## Scope

**Geography.** The City of Edmonton's own roads function and the City's own
finances. Provincial highways and provincial road spending are outside the
claim. No comparator municipality is required; if a reviewer uses one to
show how the standardized reporting works, it is context and carries no
verdict.

**Time and dollars.** As fixed above.

**Boundary.** The verdict covers only the published function named in the
proposition, whose contents are listed in the denominator section. A
transfer enters the numerator only to the extent a published record
attributes it to that function or to named road projects.

**Out of scope.** Whether anyone pays a fair share; whether bicycles
should be licensed; whether property taxes are too high or have risen;
whether Edmonton's roads and alleys are in poor condition (registered
separately as `infrastructure-deficit`); what share of the road capital
