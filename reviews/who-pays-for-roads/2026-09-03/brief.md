# Review brief: Who pays for Edmonton's roads

Status: REVISED 2026-09-03 by Stew after framing check 1 returned REVISE.
Framing check 2 pending, NOT frozen. Methodology: v1.19. Question id
`who-pays-for-roads`, registered 2026-09-03 from the whole-source intake
of `yegscoop-2026-08-26`. Topics: transportation, bike-lanes,
city-finances.

## Question

Who pays for Edmonton's roads, drivers or everybody?

Under a Facebook thread about council's bike-lane decision of 2026-08-26,
fifteen people argued about this and split almost down the middle. Eight
accounts advanced the driver-charge premise. Four accounts explicitly said
property or general taxes provide most municipal road funding. Three
further accounts made adjacent taxpayer or shared-service arguments. Those
adjacent claims do not carry verdicts. Neither side offered a figure. Both
sides treat their answer as settled fact and build a fairness argument on
top of it.

Claims in this question, reviewed and reported separately:

- `drivers-pay-for-roads-via-fuel-taxes`: whether the charges people pay
  because they drive cover what Edmonton's roads cost the City.
- `roads-funded-by-property-taxes`: whether the City's own general revenue,
  rather than road-specific charges or government transfers, meets most of
  what Edmonton's roads cost it.

Each claim carries its own verdict, panel agreement and evidence-basis
label. Reviewer confidence appears only beside the reviewer who gave it. A
finding on one claim is not a finding on the other. The two claims share a
denominator, fixed once below, and have different numerators; where an
amount enters both numerators, reviewers say so.

**What the register holds and this brief does not carry.** The register
lists four claims under this question. The two above carry verdicts. The
other two, `cyclists-pay-property-taxes` ("Cyclists pay the property and
general taxes that fund roads") and `taxes-fund-services-you-dont-use`
("Edmontonians pay taxes for services and infrastructure they do not
personally use"), do not. Both intake triage seats independently declined
them as undisputed features of the tax system, and a finding on either
would tell no reader anything. They keep their register entries. Neither
this brief nor the article it leads to states a finding on them.

## Why this question (selection rationale)

- **Prevalence.** Fifteen distinct accounts in one captured source. It is
  the most evenly split question in that source and the second largest by
  headcount.
- **Civic importance.** Both sides use their answer to decide who has
  standing to object to street spending. If the premise is wrong on either
  side, the fairness argument built on it does not stand up, and the
  argument is live: the Infrastructure Committee heard the bike-route
  program on 2026-08-26 and the Province has said it will legislate on
  municipal bike-lane authority this fall.
- **Verifiability.** What municipal functions cost and what revenue is
  attributed to them is reported annually, on a standardized basis, by
  every municipality in Alberta, and separately by the City in its own
  audited statements. Where fuel-tax and vehicle-registration revenue goes
  is set out in statute and in published grant programs. The named
  documents are listed against each claim below.

## Circulating forms

The captured forms, with comment ids and dates, are in `intake.md`, which
reviewers do not receive. In summary for the panel, all from the same
captured thread, 2026-08-26 to 2026-09-01:

**The claim that drivers pay** was made by eight accounts. Representative
wordings: "there is already a road tax on fuel for that very purpose";
"car drivers pay taxes like vehicle registration and fuel tax to pay for
those roads"; "drivers pay the fuel tax to maintain the roads"; "I do pay
gas taxes, tire taxes and various consumption taxes on vehicles, repairs
and parts that help pay for the road"; "Right now it's a free ride". One
of the eight says drivers pay "partially via the fuel tax", and one says
vehicles "pay many different kinds of taxes" without saying they cover the
cost.

**The claim that general taxes pay** was made by four accounts.
Representative wordings: "municipal roads are predominantly funded through
property tax"; "The majority of municipal roads are funded through
property tax"; "most of the funds for building and maintaining roads come
from general taxes, property taxes in the case of municipal spending on
road maintenance"; "Your car payment, insurance and gas doesn't pay for
roads: taxes do".

**Prevalence.** The intake establishes that both claims circulate in this
argument and how many distinct accounts made each in this source. It does
not establish how common either is in Edmonton generally, and no claim in
this brief turns on that.

**Magnitude is part of both claims.** Neither side is saying a dollar
exists somewhere. "Pay for the roads" and "predominantly funded" are
claims about who covers the cost, and the propositions carry that.

## Who asks this, and what the record can answer

A resident would ask: "Do fuel taxes and vehicle fees pay Edmonton's road
costs, or do property and other general taxes pay most of them?"

The available record answers a narrower accounting question about a
published municipal function called "Roads, Streets, Walks, Lighting". It
does not trace the financing of a particular street rebuild, and it does
not, by subtraction, identify property taxes as the source of anything.
The propositions below name that narrower level, and the published article
must name it too, in the reader's own words.

Two things the record cannot do, stated here so that no proposition
pretends otherwise:

1. **No published source apportions Alberta's fuel tax or vehicle
   registration revenue geographically or by taxpayer.** So "how much of
   the fuel tax I personally pay comes back to Edmonton's roads" is not
   testable, and no claim here asks it.
2. **No published source attributes property tax revenue to a municipal
   function.** Alberta's Financial Information Return puts all property
   taxation into an unallocated general pool (see the denominator section),
   and the City's audited statements report revenue by type and expenses by
   segment without joining them. So "property tax pays X per cent of
   Edmonton's roads" is not testable either, and Claim 2 does not ask it.
   What is testable is the share of road cost that falls to the City's
   general revenue at all, rather than being met by road-attributed charges
   or government transfers. Claim 2 tests that and says so in its own
   wording, and the composition of the general pool is reported beside the
   verdict rather than folded into it.

## Dates fixed in this brief

**As-of date (freeze date):** 2026-09-03. Anything published or decided
after that date is out of scope.

**Reference window:** the City of Edmonton's fiscal years 2023, 2024 and
2025, which are calendar years. These are the three most recent complete
fiscal years published on the as-of date. Alberta's Municipal Financial
and Statistical Data record carries a 2025 financial-year workbook and was
last updated 2026-07-08; the City publishes its 2025 audited annual report.

**Why three years and why these three.** One year would let a single large
road project or a single grant instalment decide both verdicts, and road
capital work is lumpy. Three consecutive years is the shortest window that
is not dominated by one project. The choice of length is made before any
figure is seen and neither side gains from it.

**Required alternative window.** Reviewers repeat both calculations over
2022 to 2025 and report the classification under the four-year window
beside the three-year one. If any window year's filing turns out not to be
published on the as-of date, reviewers say which and proceed on the years
that are, stating what they used.

## The denominator, fixed once for both claims

Both claims are about a share of what Edmonton's roads cost the City. That
figure is fixed here, once, so that neither claim's verdict depends on a
boundary a reviewer drew.

**Primary basis.** The City of Edmonton's Financial Information Return
(FIR), filed annually with Alberta Municipal Affairs under the Municipal
Government Act, Schedule 9C "Financial Activities by Function", the
function **"Roads, Streets, Walks, Lighting"**. Total expenses reported
against that function, summed over the reference window. Published in the
**Municipal Financial and Statistical Data** open dataset
(https://open.alberta.ca/opendata/municipal-financial-and-statistical-data),
which holds one workbook per reporting financial year containing the
published FIR schedules and municipal records for every Alberta
municipality. Function definitions are in the **Financial Information
Return Manual** (https://open.alberta.ca/publications/6026340; the 2024
edition is at
https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/123503ed-54a6-4dce-a382-1d0992ad06f6/resource/96618c96-0f04-4995-bee0-9752dee204dc/download/ma-financial-information-return-manual-2024.pdf).

**What that function contains, named rather than assumed.** The manual
defines it as activities which provide for roads, streets, walks and
lighting, including roads and streets, medians, boulevards, sidewalks,
street lighting, street signs, traffic signals, railway crossing signals,
and public parking facilities. It is therefore not roads alone. Every
verdict in this brief applies to that published function, and the article
says so in the reader's words. Reviewers quote the manual's definition.
The manual reports public transit as a separate function, so a transit
total cannot leak into this figure.

**Primary basis measures accounting cost, not cash spent.** The FIR is an
accrual basis. Expenses reported against a function include amortization of
assets built in earlier years and exclude the year's capital additions. So
the primary calculation measures the annual accounting cost reported for
the function. It does not measure the cash spent building roads during the
window, and it does not identify the financing of any particular street
rebuild. Reviewers state this in `limitations` and never call the primary
figure "what the City spent". The accrual basis is also what makes it
impossible to set several years of capital against one year of operating
spending, which is the arithmetic defect that took the site's
`at-100m-vs-snow` claim off the findings board (methodology v1.19).

**Required alternative basis: the City's own audited line.** The
**"Roadway and parking"** expense line the City of Edmonton reports within
Transportation services in the segment disclosure of its audited
consolidated financial statements (2024 annual report:
https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2024FinancialAnnualReport.pdf;
2023 statements:
https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/FinancialAnnualReportConsolidatedFinancialStatements2023.pdf;
other years from
https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/budget_finances/financial-reports).
Reviewers state the exact line name, the schedule or note it sits in, and
report each claim's classification under it. **The broader "Transportation
services" total must not be used as a road denominator**: it includes bus
and light rail transit, which the City reports on its own line beside
"Roadway and parking". A figure that includes transit is never called a
roads figure.

**Required alternative basis: the capital side, so "building" is not
dropped.** The claims speak of building as well as maintaining, and the
accrual primary excludes the year's capital additions. The FIR publishes
the capital side by function, so this is computed rather than waved at.
For the "Roads, Streets, Walks, Lighting" function, over the same window:

- Schedule 9F, purchased tangible capital asset additions (column 1) and
  donated or contributed additions (column 2).
- Schedule 9E, provincial capital transfers attributed to the function
  (column 2).
- Schedule 9F, capital long-term debt principal additions (column 3) and
  reductions (column 4).

Reviewers report each claim's classification on a capital denominator of
9F columns 1 and 2, with the financing sources above named, and state
plainly which parts of that financing the record attributes to the
function and which it does not. This basis is an alternative and not the
primary, because the primary must remain a single published row that two
reviewers reading this brief will read identically.

**Every share in this brief is an accounting attribution, not a trace of
dollars.** Money is fungible and no government publishes which dollar paid
for which road. What the record supports is which revenue sources are
attributed to which function in a set of accounts, and that is what both
claims are tested against. Reviewers state this limit in `limitations`.

**How the FIR treats revenue, established from the manual rather than
assumed.** Schedule 9C separates *Total General Revenue* (line 0700), which
the manual defines as revenues not allocated to specific reporting
functions, from *Function Revenue* (lines 0730 to 1130). The manual states
that Total General Revenue should normally include all property, business
and special taxation, all penalties and costs on taxes, all franchise and
concession contract revenue, and **all federal and provincial government
operating and capital transfers**. Two consequences bind both claims and
neither is left to the reviewer to guess:

1. **Government transfers are not attributed to the roads function on
   Schedule 9C.** They sit in the unallocated general pool.
2. **Property taxation is not attributed to the roads function either**,
   for the same reason.

The one published attribution of transfers to the function is Schedule 9E
column 2, provincial capital transfers by function. Reviewers quote the
manual for each of these points and state whether Edmonton's own filings
follow the normal treatment the manual describes.

**Amortization is published by function.** Schedule 9E column 3 reports
annual amortization expense broken down by function, tying to Schedule 9D
line 2110. Reviewers take the amortization charged to "Roads, Streets,
Walks, Lighting" from that column for each window year and report every
ratio restated with it removed from the denominator. This is a required
calculation, not a conditional one.

**If a named field is absent.** If Schedule 9C, 9E, 9F, the named function,
or Edmonton's filing for a window year is not published under these names
on the as-of date, reviewers report the name the field carries, or its
absence, and proceed on the required alternative basis as primary, saying
so. A claim is Not established where no basis yields a figure for the
window; that is a finding about the record, not a failure. If the road
cost figure for the window is zero or absent on every basis, no share can
be computed and both claims are Not established on that ground.

**Provincial roads are not in either claim.** Alberta's provincial
highways in and around Edmonton, including Anthony Henday Drive, are the
Province's roads and the Province's spending. They are outside the City's
FIR function and outside the City's statements, and no claim here covers
them. Reviewers state this where a source mixes the two.

## Claims under review

### Claim 1, id: `drivers-pay-for-roads-via-fuel-taxes`

**Normalized proposition, which carries the verdict:** The fuel taxes,
vehicle-registration fees and other vehicle charges named in the captured
claims cover what Edmonton's "Roads, Streets, Walks, Lighting" function
costs the City.

**The weaker circulating form carries no verdict.** The intake holds two
materially different assertions: that driver charges *cover* the road
bill, and that driver charges *contribute* to it. Eight accounts assert
the first and rest a fairness argument on it, in one case that other road
users get "a free ride"; that is the strong form and it is what the
verdict tests. The weaker contribution form, made in the wordings
"partially via the fuel tax" and "pay many different kinds of taxes", is
not assigned a separate verdict, because the schema carries one verdict
per claim. Reviewers report the amount and the share as a qualification,
and **the article does not tag the weaker wordings with the primary
verdict** and says which wordings the verdict is and is not about.

**"Charges people pay because they drive", fixed here.** Two components,
both enumerated, and both subject to the same attribution test: an amount
enters the numerator only where a published record attributes it to the
roads function or to named Edmonton road projects in the window.
Reviewers do not add a source on a judgement that it feels driver-related,
and every included amount is listed with its figure and its source.

*(a) Money reaching the City that is tied to fuel or vehicle charges, and
attributed to roads.* Two tests, both of which must pass.

  *The instrument test.* The statute, regulation or published program
  agreement governing the transfer ties the amount, or the source of the
  amount, to fuel tax, fuel sales, or vehicle registration revenue.
  Reviewers work this list, and for each one report whether it is in force
  on the as-of date, whether its governing instrument ties funding in that
  way, and what Edmonton received under it in each window year:

  1. The Local Government Fiscal Framework Act and the LGFF capital
     funding program, with its published allocation formula and program
     guidelines (https://www.alberta.ca/local-government-fiscal-framework-capital-funding).
  2. The City Charters Fiscal Framework Act, SA 2018, c C-13.3
     (https://www.canlii.org/en/ab/laws/stat/sa-2018-c-c-13.3/latest/sa-2018-c-c-13.3.html),
     including when it ceased to be in force. It is listed because a
     holder of the claim may cite it, not because it is expected to carry
     an amount in this window.
  3. The Municipal Sustainability Initiative, including any Basic
     Municipal Transportation Grant component
     (https://www.alberta.ca/municipal-sustainability-initiative).
  4. The Canada Community-Building Fund as administered in Alberta
     (https://www.alberta.ca/canada-community-building-fund), including
     the source of its money and its eligible project categories.
  5. Alberta's Fuel Tax Act and the Government of Alberta's own annual
     reporting of fuel tax revenue, to establish where that revenue is
     directed.
  6. Alberta's vehicle registration fees under the Traffic Safety Act and
     its fee regulation, to establish where that revenue is directed.

  Any further transfer whose governing instrument ties it to fuel tax,
  fuel sales or vehicle registration revenue is added, named, with the
  instrument quoted. No transfer is added without one.

  *The attribution test.* Only the portion of such a transfer that a
  published City or provincial record attributes to Edmonton's roads
  function, or identifies as applied to named Edmonton road projects
  during the window, enters the numerator. **A whole allocation is not
  counted merely because roads were an eligible use.** These are
  broad-purpose capital programs whose eligible uses include transit,
  water, wastewater, emergency facilities and other infrastructure, and
  counting the full allocation would put money spent on other functions
  into a roads ratio. The published attribution to use first is Schedule
  9E column 2, provincial capital transfers by function, for the "Roads,
  Streets, Walks, Lighting" row. Where an amount passes the instrument
  test but no published record attributes any of it to roads, reviewers
  report the amount as unattributable, state that it is excluded from the
  ratio, and give the ratio's value if it were wholly included as a stated
  upper bound.

*(b) Charges on driving that the City itself collects and attributes to
the function.* Sales and user charges attributed to the "Roads, Streets,
Walks, Lighting" function on Schedule 9E column 1, for each window year.
Reviewers state what the City reports on that line. This component is in
the primary numerator because it is a published attribution of a charge to
the function, not because it favours either side; the classification with
(b) excluded is required beside the primary one.

*Not in the numerator: fines.* Traffic fines and automated enforcement
revenue are not counted. The FIR does not break fines out by function, a
fine arises from a violation rather than from road use, and no captured
wording rests on fines. Reviewers report the City's fine and automated
enforcement revenue, and any City parking revenue the FIR does not
attribute to the function, as a **non-verdict sensitivity calculation**:
the ratio restated with those amounts added, stated as such, from the
City's own audited statements or year-end operating financial updates
(2023: https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=215334;
2024: https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=252517;
2025: https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=291514).

**The verdict figure and its thresholds.** The coverage ratio is the sum
of (a) and (b) over the reference window, divided by the function's total
expenses over the same window on the primary basis, in nominal Canadian
dollars.

- **Supported** if the ratio is 1.0 or more.
- **Partially supported** if the ratio is at least 0.25 and below 1.0:
  driver charges make a substantial contribution to road cost, and the
  part that fails is "pay for".
- **Contradicted** if the ratio is below 0.25.
- **Not established** if no road cost figure can be obtained for the
  window on any basis, or if the amount under (a) cannot be determined
  because the governing instruments do not say what they direct revenue
  to.

The bands are contiguous and exhaustive over every ratio the arithmetic
can produce, including zero, which falls in Contradicted, and including a
ratio above 1.0, which falls in Supported. If any component figure is
printed as a negative, reviewers report it as printed and state its effect
on the ratio rather than dropping it.

Alternative cutoffs, results required under both sets: Supported at 0.9 or
above; Partially supported from 0.5 to below 0.9; Contradicted below 0.5.
Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard. 1.0 is
where "pay for" is literally true; 0.9 allows for rounding across a
three-year window; 0.25 is the floor of a contribution a reasonable person
would call substantial, and 0.5 is where driver charges would be the
larger part rather than a contribution. No cutoff may be changed after the
figures are seen.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The ratio and classification with component (b) excluded.
2. The ratio and classification on the required alternative denominator
   (the City's audited "Roadway and parking" line), with that figure's
   contents stated.
3. The ratio and classification on the capital basis.
4. The ratio restated with amortization removed from the denominator,
   from Schedule 9E column 3.
5. The classification over the four-year window.
6. The upper-bound ratio with unattributable driver-tied transfers wholly
   included, labelled as an upper bound.
7. The non-verdict sensitivity with fines and non-attributed parking
   revenue added.
8. What the Government of Alberta's own reporting says fuel tax and
   vehicle registration revenue are directed to, whatever that turns out
   to be, with the instrument named.
9. Federal and provincial consumption taxes on vehicles, parts and repairs
   are named in one captured wording. Reviewers state which government
   collects each and what its governing statute directs it to, and state
   whether any part reaches Edmonton's roads. This is reported, not
   counted in the ratio, unless an instrument ties it and a published
   record attributes it under door (a).
10. Whether the City reports any revenue in these years from a road toll,
    congestion charge, bicycle registration or similar user charge.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether drivers should pay more or
less; whether anyone is paying a fair share; whether bicycles should be
licensed or registered; whether provincial fuel tax and registration
revenue is adequate to Alberta's own road spending. Whether cyclists pay
taxes is not tested here or anywhere in this question.

### Claim 2, id: `roads-funded-by-property-taxes`

**Normalized proposition, which carries the verdict:** Most of what
Edmonton's "Roads, Streets, Walks, Lighting" function costs the City is
met from the City's own general revenue — the unallocated pool that
property taxation is part of — rather than from revenue the record
attributes to the function or from government transfers.

**Why this reading, and what the record forced.** As it appears on the
register the claim is two assertions in one sentence: that property and
general taxes predominantly fund municipal roads, and that fuel taxes and
vehicle registration do not. The second is Claim 1's territory, and two
assertions under one finding would state neither and would tag everybody
quoted beneath it with a verdict on a claim they did not make. So the
proposition here is the positive assertion only.

The positive assertion as its holders word it — "predominantly funded
through property tax" — cannot be tested at the level of property tax,
and the brief says so rather than pretending. Alberta's FIR puts all
property taxation into Schedule 9C's unallocated Total General Revenue,
and the City's audited statements report revenue by type and expenses by
segment without joining the two. No published source attributes property
tax revenue to a municipal function, so a share of road cost paid by
property tax is not a computable quantity, and the residual after
function-attributed revenue is **not** that share: the same unallocated
pool also holds franchise fees, penalties on taxes, investment income and
every government transfer.

What the record does answer, and what the proposition therefore tests, is
the level immediately above: whether the roads bill falls mostly on the
City's own general revenue at all, or is instead met by charges attributed
to roads and by transfers from other governments. That is the substance
the two sides were arguing about — a general tax base against road-specific
money — and it is stated in the proposition in words a reader would use.
The composition of the general pool, including property taxation's place
in it, is a required companion figure below and is reported beside the
verdict rather than folded into it. The article must carry that
distinction in the reader's own words and may not report this verdict as a
finding about property tax specifically.

Its holders' own wording covers "building and maintaining", so the capital
basis in the denominator section is a required alternative here as well as
for Claim 1.

**The verdict figure, fixed here.** From Schedules 9C and 9E for the
"Roads, Streets, Walks, Lighting" function, summed over the reference
window:

    G = (total expenses − function revenue − road-attributed transfers)
        ÷ total expenses

where *function revenue* is the revenue Schedule 9C attributes to the
function (line 0850), and *road-attributed transfers* are the provincial
capital transfers Schedule 9E column 2 attributes to the function, plus
any further government transfer a published City record identifies as
applied to named Edmonton road projects in the window, each named with its
figure and source. Reviewers state the schedule's own label for each
figure used, quote the FIR Manual's definition of the function, and quote
the manual's treatment of Total General Revenue.

**Naming it honestly.** G is the share of the function's reported cost met
from the City's unallocated general revenue. It is not the property-tax
share and reviewers must not call it one. Where an amount enters both this
numerator and Claim 1's, reviewers say so.

*Required companion figures.* So that "general revenue" is not left as a
word, reviewers report, from the City's audited statements for each window
year: net municipal property taxation as a share of the City's total
revenue; and the other named components of revenue not attributed to
functions, including franchise and concession contract revenue, penalties
and costs on taxes, investment earnings, and federal and provincial
transfers. These are reported; they do not carry the verdict.

**Thresholds.**

- **Supported** if G is greater than 0.5.
- **Partially supported** if G is at least 0.35 and no greater than 0.5:
  general revenue meets a substantial part of road cost, and the part that
  fails is "most".
- **Contradicted** if G is below 0.35.
- **Not established** if Schedule 9C does not publish revenues and
  expenses for the function for the window, and the required alternative
  bases do not yield the equivalent figures either.

The bands are contiguous and exhaustive. G above 1.0, which happens only
if the function's attributed revenues are negative as printed, falls in
Supported. G at or below zero, which happens if attributed revenues and
road-attributed transfers together meet or exceed expenses, falls in
Contradicted. G exactly 0.5 falls in Partially supported, because "most"
and "predominantly" mean more than half.

Alternative cutoffs, results required under both sets: Supported above
0.6; Partially supported from 0.4 to 0.6 inclusive; Contradicted below
0.4. Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard. More
than half is what "most" and "predominantly" mean in ordinary speech; 0.6
is where a majority is not arguable on rounding; 0.35 and 0.4 are two
readings of where a share stops being a substantial part.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The share and classification computed without the transfer deduction,
   labelled as the net-expense share and not as a general-revenue share,
   and whether it differs from the classification under G.
2. The classification on the required alternative denominator, with that
   figure's contents stated.
3. The classification on the capital basis.
4. The share restated with amortization removed from the denominator,
   from Schedule 9E column 3.
5. The classification over the four-year window.
6. The companion composition figures named above.
7. The place of tax-supported debt. The City funds road capital partly by
   borrowing, and its capital budget labels that funding "tax-supported
   debt". Reviewers state how the FIR treats debt-funded capital under an
   accrual basis, report capital long-term debt principal additions and
   reductions for the function from Schedule 9F columns 3 and 4 and the
   interest on long-term debt for the function from Schedule 9E column 4,
   and say where that servicing is funded from. This is reported, not
   folded into G.
8. The City's own budget documents for the 2023-2026 cycle
   (https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2023-2026CapitalBudget.pdf,
   https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2023-2026OperatingBudget.pdf):
   what funding sources they name for road capital and for road operating
   programs, and in what proportions, as the City itself presents them.
   Budget documents establish what was planned, never what was spent.

**What this claim does not test.** What share of road cost property tax
specifically pays, which the record does not publish; whether property
taxes are too high or have risen (a separate claim, parked on the
register); whether the City spends its road money well; whether renters
pay property tax; whether cyclists pay property tax.

## Stakes

**Claim 1.** *Supported* would require holders to accept the defined
accounting boundary but would establish their full-coverage premise;
opponents would have to abandon the claim that driver-linked charges do
not cover that cost. *Contradicted* would require holders to abandon full
coverage; opponents would gain evidence against that premise, without
settling who ought to pay. *Partially supported* would preserve a measured
contribution while rejecting full coverage. *Not established* would leave
both sides without a defensible public-record answer, which would be a
finding worth publishing on its own.

**Claim 2.** *Supported* would establish, for holders, that the roads bill
falls mostly on the City's own general revenue under the defined boundary;
opponents would have to abandon the claim that road-specific charges and
transfers are the main source. *Contradicted* would require holders to
abandon the majority claim; opponents would still need to identify the
actual leading source. *Partially supported* would establish a material
general-revenue contribution but not predominance. *Not established* would
mean the public record does not provide the needed function-level
attribution.

**Both claims can fail together.** The two claims are not complements. If
most road cost is met by government transfers that are not tied to fuel or
vehicle charges, both propositions are wrong at once and neither side of
the thread has the right answer. That outcome is available on the evidence
and reviewers should not treat it as a contradiction to be resolved.

**All four verdicts are reachable on each claim** under the numerator and
denominator definitions above, before any figure is read.

**Definition sensitivity.** Each verdict applies under the primary
denominator and the primary numerator set named above. Where a required
alternative produces a different classification, the finding is
definition-sensitive, the article says so beside the verdict, and neither
side can fairly cite the verdict without that qualification.

## Scope

**Geography.** The City of Edmonton's own roads function and the City's own
finances. Provincial highways and provincial road spending are outside
both claims. No comparator municipality is required; if a reviewer uses one
to show how the standardized reporting works, it is context and carries no
verdict.

**Time.** As fixed above.

**Boundary.** The verdict covers only the published function named in the
propositions, whose contents are listed in the denominator section. A
transfer enters a numerator only to the extent a published record
attributes it to that function or to named road projects.

**Out of scope.** Whether anyone pays a fair share; whether bicycles
should be licensed; whether property taxes are too high or have risen;
whether Edmonton's roads and alleys are in poor condition (registered
separately as `infrastructure-deficit`); what share of the road capital
budget the bike-lane program is (registered separately under
`active-transportation`); whether free on-street parking is a subsidy
(registered separately under `on-street-parking`); whether most cyclists
also drive (registered separately under `who-cycles`); the cost or value
of any particular road or bike route; anything published after the as-of
date.

## Reviewer instructions of special note

- Name the schedule, the workbook, the reporting year, the row or column
  label and the units for every figure taken from the filed municipal
  data, and the document, report number, page and date for every figure
  taken from a City publication. A news report is a lead to the document
  behind it, never the source of a figure in a verdict.
- Call the primary figure what it is: the reported accounting cost of a
  published municipal function that includes sidewalks, lighting, signs,
  signals and public parking facilities as well as roads. Do not call it
  road spending, and do not present it as the cost of building any street.
- Do not call any figure in this brief a property-tax share. The record
  does not attribute property tax to a function, and Claim 2's verdict
  figure is a general-revenue share.
- Keep the levels of government apart. Fuel tax and vehicle registration
  in Alberta are provincial charges; property tax is municipal; several
  capital programs are provincial or federal transfers. A dollar collected
  by one government does not reach another's roads unless an instrument
  sends it there. Say which government collects each amount you name.
- Keep capital and operating money apart, and never set a multi-year
  capital total against a single year of operating cost. The capital basis
  is computed and reported on its own terms, matched year for year.
- Keep budgeted apart from spent. An approved or planned amount is not
  money out the door, and no verdict figure in this brief is a budget
  figure.
- Do not construct a roads boundary of your own. The denominator is a
  published row. If you think the published row is the wrong container,
  say so in `interpretation_notes` and answer the claims as posed.
- Report one verdict per claim, under the primary readings and cutoffs
  fixed here. Every alternative reading's result goes in
  `interpretation_notes` or `limitations`. The schema carries one verdict
  per claim.
- Record every source URL in full; you have no repo access. Where a
  council document is reached through the City's meeting portal, give the
  meeting agenda page as well as the attachment URL.
- If a definition, cutoff, window or basis in this brief changes what the
  honest answer is, record a MATERIAL FRAMING CONCERN per
  `prompts/reviewer.md` and answer the claims as posed alongside it.
