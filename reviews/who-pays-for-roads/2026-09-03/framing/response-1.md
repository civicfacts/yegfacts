# Author's response to framing check 1 (Stew, 2026-09-03)

The check is right on the two findings that decide this brief — the
Claim 1 numerator counted whole grants, and Claim 2's residual was not
the quantity its holders were arguing about — so the brief is rewritten
rather than patched. Every finding is adopted except one, and the refusal
is argued below rather than left implicit.

Before revising I opened the source the check and the brief both rest on,
the 2024 Financial Information Return Manual, and read the definitions
rather than assuming them. That settled three things the draft had left
open and changed what the record can be asked for. They are set out here
because several of the adoptions below are stronger than the wording the
check proposed, and the reason is the manual, not my preference.

**What the manual establishes.**

1. The function "Roads, Streets, Walks, Lighting" is defined as roads and
   streets, medians, boulevards, sidewalks, street lighting, street signs,
   traffic signals, railway crossing signals, and public parking
   facilities. Your finding is confirmed word for word.
2. Schedule 9C's Total General Revenue (line 0700) is defined as revenue
   not allocated to specific functions, and the manual says it should
   normally include all property, business and special taxation, all
   penalties and costs on taxes, all franchise and concession revenue, and
   **all federal and provincial operating and capital transfers**. So
   transfers are not attributed to the roads function on 9C, and neither
   is property tax. The draft's open question about transfer treatment is
   answered, and its conditional S/S' pair is gone.
3. The FIR does publish by function what the draft thought it might not:
   Schedule 9E column 1 sales and user charges, column 2 provincial
   capital transfers, column 3 annual amortization expense, column 4
   interest on long-term debt; Schedule 9F columns 1 and 2 capital asset
   additions and columns 3 and 4 capital debt principal movements.

Finding by finding:

**1. Provenance, the eight-to-seven split.** Adopted. Your count is right:
seven accounts took the "everybody pays" side, but only four made the
property or general tax claim, and three made adjacent taxpayer or
shared-service arguments that carry no verdict. Your replacement wording
is used almost verbatim in the Question section.

**2. Claim 1, folding coverage and contribution.** Adopted. The strong
form carries the verdict; the weaker "partially" and "many different kinds
of taxes" wordings are expressly not assigned it, are reported as a
qualification, and the brief now binds the article to say which wordings
the verdict is and is not about.

**2, 3 and 6. Claim 2 is not a property-tax test — adopted; the remedy is
not.** This is the one place I do not do what you asked, and it is the
most important finding in your report, so here is the argument.

You are right on the defect, and the manual makes it worse than you put
it. `S = (expenses − revenues) ÷ expenses` was never a property-tax share,
and the residual it measures is met from a pool that holds franchise fees,
penalties, investment income **and every federal and provincial transfer**.
A Supported verdict on that figure would have told a reader that property
taxes carry the roads when the true driver might have been a capital
grant. That is not a qualification problem, it is a wrong quantity, and
the draft is at fault for it.

You then gave two remedies: use a published attribution of property and
general taxes to the road function, or carry no verdict. The first does
not exist — I looked, and the manual's design forbids it: the FIR puts all
property taxation in the unallocated pool by construction, and the City's
audited statements report revenue by type and expenses by segment without
joining them. So your first remedy is unavailable and your second is what
stands.

I am not taking it, and I am not taking it on the authority of your own
check 9, which says that where the record cannot answer the question
people ask at the level they ask it, the brief says so and tests the
nearest level the record does answer, naming that level in words a reader
would use. That is what the brief now does. The proposition is no longer
about property tax. It asks whether most of what the roads function costs
falls on the City's own general revenue at all, rather than being met by
revenue attributed to the function or by transfers from other governments.
The verdict figure deducts road-attributed transfers, which is the
deduction that made your objection bite, and the brief forbids reviewers
and the article from calling the result a property-tax share.

Three reasons this is not the truism your check 3 warns against. It can
fail: if provincial capital transfers and function-attributed charges meet
more than half the cost, it is Contradicted, and the LGFF and CCBF amounts
are large enough that this is a live outcome rather than a formality. It
keeps the magnitude the holders asserted, "most" and "predominantly",
rather than draining it. And it is the substance the thread was actually
arguing about — a general tax base against road-specific money — with the
part the record cannot reach declared in the brief and reported beside the
verdict as a composition figure instead of being smuggled into it.

What a holder loses is real and I am not hiding it: someone who said
"predominantly funded through property tax" gets a verdict about general
revenue, not about property tax. The brief says so in the proposition, in
the "who asks this" section, in the reviewer instructions and in the
article's obligations. Parking the claim instead would have left the
larger side of an evenly split argument with a verdict and the other side
with silence, which is a worse answer to the same reader.

**4. Reference window.** Adopted, and the draft was simply wrong. I
checked the dataset record: the 2025 financial-year workbook is published
and the record was last updated 2026-07-08. The claim that 2022 to 2024
was "the most recent window certainly filed and published" was false on
the as-of date. The primary window is now 2023 to 2025 and 2022 to 2025 is
the required alternative, exactly as you wrote it.

**4. Dataset description.** Adopted: one workbook per reporting financial
year, not one spreadsheet per schedule.

**4 and 9. The road boundary is not roads.** Adopted, from the manual's
own list. The function's contents are now named in the denominator
section, the propositions name the function, and the reviewer instructions
forbid calling the primary figure road spending.

**4. The audited alternative.** Adopted, and tightened with the line name
you identified: the alternative denominator is the City's "Roadway and
parking" expense line within Transportation services, and the brief now
states explicitly that the Transportation services total must not be used,
because it includes bus and light rail transit.

**4. Accrual expense is not spending.** Adopted. The brief says the
primary calculation measures annual accounting cost, not cash spent
building roads, and never calls it spending.

You added a condition before requiring a matched-period capital
calculation: name a published source that supplies both actual road
capital additions and their financing by source. The manual supplies most
of it by function — Schedule 9F columns 1 and 2 for additions, 9E column 2
for provincial capital transfers, 9F columns 3 and 4 for capital debt
principal. So the capital basis is now a required alternative with those
sources named, and the brief says plainly which parts of the financing the
record attributes to the function and which it does not. It is an
alternative rather than the primary because the primary has to stay a
single published row that two reviewers will read identically, which is
the failure this brief exists to avoid.

**4. Claim 1's whole-transfer numerator.** Adopted, and this is the
correction that most changes the arithmetic. An amount now has to pass two
tests: the instrument test the draft had, and an attribution test — only
the portion a published record attributes to the roads function or to
named road projects counts, and a whole allocation is never counted
because roads were an eligible use. Schedule 9E column 2 is named as the
published attribution to use first. Where an amount passes the instrument
test with no published attribution, it is reported as unattributable and
the fully-included ratio is given as a stated upper bound, so the reader
sees the range rather than a silent exclusion.

**4. Parking and fines.** Adopted in substance, with one component kept on
a different justification than the draft's. Fines are out of the primary
numerator entirely, for your reason and one more: the FIR does not break
fines out by function at all. They are now a non-verdict sensitivity, as
you asked. Sales and user charges attributed to the function on Schedule
9E column 1 stay in the primary numerator, but the draft's justification
for them — "the reading most favourable to the claim's holders" — is
deleted. You were right that it was a biased justification, and the brief
now says the component is in because it is a published attribution of a
charge to the function, with the classification excluding it required
beside the primary.

**4. Claim 2's formula.** Adopted. The old S is demoted to a reported
qualification and is labelled the net-expense share, never a
general-revenue or property-tax share.

**4. Instrument check.** Noted, and the City Charters Fiscal Framework
Act's repeal on 2019-12-05 puts it before every window year under the new
window too. It stays in the enumeration, with the brief now saying it is
listed because a holder may cite it, not because it is expected to carry
an amount.

**5. Leaks.** Adopted; the "most favourable to the claim's holders"
sentence is gone, as above.

**7. Scope traps.** Adopted, all four. Parking and enforcement revenue
handled as above; broad grants handled by the attribution test; "building
and maintaining" handled by the required capital basis rather than by
narrowing the proposition; the function's contents named in the
proposition and in the scope section.

**8. Stakes.** Adopted, close to verbatim, including the removal of the
sentence about people objecting to how municipal finance works, which was
a value judgement about an argument this brief excludes.

**9. Who asks this.** Adopted, and extended. The section now states the
resident's question in your wording, names the narrower level the record
answers, and lists the two things the record cannot do — no geographic
apportionment of fuel tax, and no attribution of property tax to a
function — before any proposition is stated.
