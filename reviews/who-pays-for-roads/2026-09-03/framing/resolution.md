# Editor's resolution after framing check 2 (Stew, 2026-09-03)

Under the cap (methodology v1.12), after the second report the editor
resolves in writing every finding still OPEN or WEAKENED, the brief is
revised once more, and the third report confirms or parks.

Check 2 (`check-2.md`) left one OPEN finding of substance and seven
WEAKENED ones. Every one is adopted. The first is adopted against my own
argument in `response-1.md`, and that is the decision this file exists to
record.

## The finding I lost: Claim 2 carries no verdict

In `response-1.md` I refused the checker's remedy on Claim 2 and reframed
the proposition instead, from "property taxes pay for most of the roads"
to "the City's own general revenue meets most of the roads bill", on the
authority of the framing prompt's own check 9. Check 2 says the reframe
fails too, and it is right, for a reason my revision missed.

Schedule 9C puts **all** federal and provincial transfers, operating and
capital, into the unallocated general pool. The only published attribution
of any transfer to the roads function is Schedule 9E column 2, and that
column covers provincial **capital** transfers only. So deducting 9E
column 2, as my `G` did, still leaves federal capital transfers and all
operating transfers sitting inside the residual, indistinguishable from
the City's own money. `G` therefore cannot be called a share met from the
City's own general revenue. It is an adjusted residual and nothing more.
I checked this against the manual before conceding it, and the manual is
against me.

So the reframe did not move the claim to the nearest level the record
answers. It moved it to a level the record still does not answer, and
attached a verdict word to it. That is the same failure as the original,
one step further from the claim people actually made, which is worse.

**Resolved: `roads-funded-by-property-taxes` carries no verdict in this
brief.** The checker's replacement wording is adopted. The brief will:

- state that no published source quantifies what share of the roads
  function is financed by property or other general taxes, and that the
  unallocated pool cannot separate those taxes from unallocated government
  transfers;
- require the residual after function-attributed revenue and identified
  road transfers to be computed and reported **without a verdict**, named
  as the accounting gap it is;
- forbid the article from tagging the captured property-tax wordings with
  that figure, or from presenting it as a property-tax or own-revenue
  share;
- record the claim on the register as parked, with this reason, so the
  decision is on the public surface and not only in this run directory.

The consequence is that this brief carries one verdict claim where it set
out to carry two, and the larger side of an evenly split argument gets an
answer while the other side gets a documented silence. I argued in
`response-1.md` that this was the worse outcome for a reader. I no longer
think so. A documented silence is a true statement about the public
record; a verdict on a quantity that does not measure the claim is a false
one dressed as an answer, and this site has already taken four claims off
its findings board for less. The article can still answer the resident's
question in both halves: what the record establishes about driver charges,
and the fact that it does not publish what property taxes pay for. That
second half is worth publishing on its own.

The reopening condition is a published source that attributes municipal
own-source revenue, or property taxation, to a reporting function. If one
appears, the claim comes back with a brief.

## Claim 1, the findings still standing

**Check 2 and 3, contribution wordings treated as coverage. Adopted.** The
sentence saying eight accounts assert full coverage is wrong on the
intake's own face: "partially via the fuel tax", "help pay for the road"
and "pay many different kinds of taxes" are contribution wordings, and the
first revision named only two of the three. The checker's replacement is
adopted, including its instruction that the article name exactly which
captured wordings the verdict covers.

**Check 4 and 7, Schedule 9E column 1 is overinclusive. Adopted
verbatim.** Attribution to the function is not proof that every dollar on
the line is a charge paid because of driving. The line enters the primary
numerator only as a component a published source identifies both as a
driving charge and as revenue attributed to the function; if the total
cannot be disaggregated it is excluded from the primary numerator and the
whole line is reported as an upper-bound sensitivity.

**Check 4 and 7, the captured tire charge has no route through either
door. Adopted.** A holder said "tire taxes ... help pay for the road" and
neither door reached it, which drained part of a captured wording. The
Alberta tire recycling environmental fee is now tested on its own terms:
legal basis, collector, stated purpose, and whether any published record
sends any part of it to Edmonton's roads. It enters no ratio without both
a vehicle-charge connection and a published road attribution. I confirmed
the administrator's published fee schedule exists before adopting the
citation.

**Check 4 and 7, the capital alternative had no defined numerator.
Adopted verbatim.** Purchased capital additions (Schedule 9F column 1) are
the City-cost denominator; donated and contributed additions (column 2)
are reported separately, because an asset the City did not pay for is not
what its roads cost it; debt principal movements are financing and
repayment, not capital cost. The numerator holds only driver-linked
amounts a published source identifies as financing those purchased
additions. The Claim 2 capital classification is dropped, which the
resolution above makes moot in any case.

**Check 4, the as-of date had no stated alternative. Adopted.** The
capture date, 2026-09-02, is named as the reasonable alternative to the
2026-09-03 registration date, the registration date stays primary, and
reviewers identify any verdict evidence first published in between.

**Check 4, constant dollars. Adopted.** Nominal aggregation over a
three-year window gives later years more weight and could move a
classification near a cutoff, so the constant-dollar restatement stops
being optional. Statistics Canada Table 18-10-0005-01, Edmonton all-items
annual-average CPI, is named. I confirmed the table is the annual-average
CPI series and that it publishes Edmonton as a geography.

**Check 4, the vehicle-registration fee source. Adopted.** The brief named
a "fee regulation" it could not identify. It now cites the Traffic Safety
Act sections the checker named and the Registry Agent Product Catalogue,
and asks reviewers to identify a prescribing regulation by title and
citation if one exists.

**Check 4, the eScribe attachment URLs. Adopted.** Each is now given with
the report name, meeting date, agenda item and agenda-page URL beside the
attachment link, so a reviewer who cannot open an attachment can still
reach the document.

**Check 5, a new leak. Adopted verbatim.** Saying the City Charters Fiscal
Framework Act is listed "not because it is expected to carry an amount in
this window" tells reviewers the answer. I introduced that sentence in the
first revision while fixing something else, which is exactly how leaks get
in. It is replaced with the neutral instruction to establish whether the
Act was in force in any window year and whether any amount under it was
received or attributed.

**Check 8, stakes. Adopted.** Claim 2's stakes are replaced with the
checker's wording. The sentence "All four verdicts are reachable on each
claim" is corrected to speak of Claim 1 only, since Claim 2 no longer
carries verdicts.

**Check 9. Adopted verbatim** for the Claim 2 portion.
