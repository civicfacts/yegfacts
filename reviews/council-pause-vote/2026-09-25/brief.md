## The question

Six people in one Facebook thread, writing the day after the Infrastructure
Committee met on the bike-lane programme on 2026-08-26, made four factual
claims between them about what the City's administration had recommended
and what the City's elected members did with it. All six are on the same
side of the argument; nobody in that source answered any of the four.

The four claims are:

- `council-rejected-pause`: whether the Infrastructure Committee rejected
  administration's proposed hold on the remaining routes, and whether City
  Council later decided it.
- `administration-recommended-freezing-14-routes`: whether administration
  recommended holding 14 unbuilt routes back from construction pending a
  reassessment.
- `motions-to-cut-budget-to-50-million-failed`: whether Councillors
  Principe and Rice twice moved to leave the programme about $50 million,
  directing the released funds elsewhere, and were defeated.
- `same-seven-councillors-vote-together`: whether the same seven members
  of council, serving before and after the October 2025 election, have
  voted on the programme's side at every recorded vote on it while
  serving.

Each carries its own verdict, panel agreement and evidence-basis label.
Reviewer confidence appears only beside the reviewer who gave it. Every
claim is about what the record of motions and votes shows; none is about
why anyone voted as they did, and the brief says so where the wording
invites it.

Topics: transportation, bike-lanes, city-hall.

## Who asks this

A resident asks: administration told council to hit pause on the rest of
the bike lanes; did council say no? The record answers that at the level of
the bodies that sat, so the question the first claim answers is: did
Infrastructure Committee reject administration's proposed hold on the
remaining routes, and did full City Council later decide it? The claim
answers it about a hold, not about a recommendation of any kind: it
establishes first whether what administration proposed was a hold, and
only then what the committee did with it. Where the record supports only
a committee-level answer, the finding says so beside the verdict. A reporter asks: what exactly did administration
recommend, which body received it, what motions were put, who voted which
way, and did the recommendation ever reach City Council? Another resident
asks: have Councillors Rice and Principe really tried twice to cut the
bike-lane money to $50 million, and lost both times? And another: is it
the same seven who vote to keep the bike lanes coming, every time?

The four claims below answer those questions from the City's minutes and
its own report, at the level people ask them: a body, a motion, a vote, a
name. Whether council was right, and why anyone voted as they did, are not
tested and the brief says so.

## What is measured, and by whom

**The City's meeting record.** The City of Edmonton publishes the agenda
and the post-meeting minutes of every City Council and committee meeting
on its eScribe portal, `https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com`. The
minutes record motions, votes and results, including movers and, for
Council motions, seconders where recorded. A standing-committee motion
does not require a seconder. The minutes record the names voting in
favour and opposed; they do not record debate. The minutes are the controlling source for every claim in this
brief about what a body did and how its members voted. The City also
publishes a term-specific voting-record dataset on its open data portal,
"2025-2029 Council And Committee Meetings - Voting Record",
`https://data.edmonton.ca/City-Administration/2025-2029-Council-And-Committee-Meetings-Voting-Re/abcm-eai5`,
one row per voter per motion, which the City itself describes as
informational, with the minutes as the official record; reviewers may use
it as a cross-check for the current term and say where it and the minutes
differ, and the minutes decide.

**The report.** Infrastructure Services report IS03688, "Active
Transportation Implementation Acceleration Program Update", Infrastructure
Committee, 2026-08-26, and its published attachments, are the instrument
for what administration recommended. The report's Recommendation section
is on its first page. Attachment 5, "Modified Approach for the Active
Transportation Network Expansion Program", is where the proposed treatment
of the remaining routes is set out, and it exists in three versions, all
listed on the committee's agenda: the original, a replacement labelled
Version 1 and a replacement labelled Version 2.

**The programme's budget.** The approved capital budget of the programme
at any date is read from the City's published capital profile for
CM-20-0330 and the capital budget adjustments Council approved before
that date, not from the minutes of the meeting at which a motion was made,
because a motion's minutes need not reproduce the running total. The
capital profile is published as an attachment to each borrowing-bylaw
report on the programme and in the capital budget and supplemental
capital budget adjustment reports. Capital budget and borrowing authority
are different figures and reviewers keep them apart.

**Where the files are.** Meeting pages load at
`https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/Meeting.aspx?Agenda=PostMinutes&Id=<meeting id>&lang=English`
(minutes) and `Agenda=Agenda` (agenda). The report and its attachments are
served from `https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=<id>`,
which answers scripts and AI tools with a browser check. The site holds
archived copies of every file this brief relies on, identified by SHA-256
in its evidence registry; the editor checked each DocumentId named below
against the archived committee agenda page, which lists every file under
item 7.6 with its DocumentId, and each meeting's date against its archived
minutes. A reviewer that cannot retrieve an essential file says so; a
missing essential source stops a run rather than becoming a finding.

**As-of date:** 2026-09-25, the date of drafting. Decisions made by
2026-09-25 are in scope. Minutes published later may document those
decisions, but not a later decision; reviewers state the meeting date and,
where material, the minutes' publication date. The as-of date is later than the 2026-08-26 meeting so that a later Council decision
on the recommendation, if any, is inside the window.

**Reference period for votes:** from 2026-08-26 for the first two claims
(the meeting the commenters were writing about), and from 2022-11-30, the

describes motions made "twice" over an unstated period and the fourth
describes a pattern that needs more than one vote to exist.

**Route status date for claim 2:** primary, the status Attachment 3 to
IS03688 states, which the attachment dates "as of August 10, 2026";
required alternative, status on 2026-08-26 from any dated City record that
establishes it. Reviewers report both and say which routes differ.

## Terms fixed in this brief

**The programme.** The Active Transportation Network Expansion Program,
funded through capital profile CM-20-0330, "Active Transportation
Implementation Acceleration - Approach 3", which City Council approved
during the 2023-2026 capital budget deliberations that began on
2022-11-30. In the source it is "the bike lanes", "the bike lane
expansion" and "rapid bike lane expansion". Every claim in this brief is
about this programme and not about any other cycling project.

**The recommendation, R.** Recommendation 1 of report IS03688: "That
Infrastructure Committee recommend to City Council: That the updated
approach for the remaining routes under the Active Transportation Network
Expansion Program, as outlined in Attachment 5 of the August 26, 2026,
Infrastructure Services report IS03688, be approved." The report's own
label for what it asks is "Council decision required".

**Adopting R.** A motion carried by the Infrastructure Committee that
recommends R to City Council, or a motion carried by City Council that
approves the approach in Attachment 5, in whole or with amendments that
keep the re-evaluation of the routes listed apart.

**Rejecting R.** A motion to adopt R put to a vote and defeated, or a
motion carried whose effect is incompatible with R: continuing the
programme at its previously approved scope, or advancing the routes that
Attachment 5 lists apart.

**Leaving R without a decision.** Neither adopting nor rejecting R: the
item concludes with no motion adopting R moved or carried and no
incompatible motion carried, whether because no member moved it, because a
motion was moved and not put, or because every substantive motion failed.

**The bodies.** The Infrastructure Committee is a standing committee of
City Council; its members are members of Council, but a committee decision
is not a Council decision, and a committee recommendation reaches Council
only if the committee carries it. Claim 1's verdict concerns the
Infrastructure Committee at its meeting of 2026-08-26, the body to which R
was addressed and the meeting the commenters were writing about the next
day, and the published short answer says so in those words. City Council
as a body of thirteen is the required alternative body, reported beside
the verdict at every meeting up to the as-of date. Reviewers report
separately what Infrastructure Committee decided and what, if anything,
City Council later decided. They do not describe a committee vote as a
City Council vote, or an override, without a later, incompatible Council
decision.

**An override.** A decision of City Council, after 2026-08-26 and by the
as-of date, that is incompatible with a decision the Infrastructure
Committee made on item 7.6. It requires a committee decision to override
and a later Council decision that contradicts it; an absence of a later
Council item is not an override.

**The pro-programme side of a vote.** On any motion or amendment in the
vote set defined for the fourth claim, the side whose success would keep
or increase the programme's funding or scope or keep its routes
proceeding: "in favour" on a motion to approve or increase funding, to
authorise or amend its borrowing, or to continue or advance the programme;
"opposed" on a motion to reduce or remove funding, or to pause, suspend,
cease or re-evaluate its routes. The side is read off the motion's text
and, where the motion bundles this programme with other profiles or
items, off the motion's net effect on this programme established from the
motion text and the relevant approved budget text, not off a phrase alone
and not off anyone's intention. A vote whose programme-specific effect is
genuinely inseparable from the rest of a bundled motion is unclassifiable
and is handled by the completeness rule in claim 4.

## Claims under review

### Claim 1, id: `council-rejected-pause`

**Normalized proposition:** At its meeting of 2026-08-26 the
Infrastructure Committee rejected administration's recommended hold on the
remaining bike routes rather than adopting it, and City Council had not
adopted the hold by 2026-09-25. "Hold" here means a recommendation that
meets claim 2's primary deferral definition; this claim tests the
disposition of R and whether R proposed a hold.

**Reader-facing question:** Did Infrastructure Committee reject
administration's proposed hold on the remaining routes, and did full City
Council later decide it?

**Why this reading.** Five people say council did not support, ignored or
went around a recommended pause; two of them say council overrode a
committee's decision. Their word is "council"; the body that sat on
2026-08-26 was a committee of Council, so the proposition names the
committee for the verdict, names City Council for the second clause, and
the bodies are kept apart as fixed above. "Rejected" rather than "did not
support" because "ignore", "go their own route" and "rejected the
opportunity" assert a choice made, and the ladder gives non-adoption
without a choice its own row. This claim tests the disposition of R and
whether R proposed a hold under claim 2's primary deferral definition.
Reviewers establish that treatment from the operative Attachment 5 before
describing any vote on R as a vote on a hold. If R did not propose a hold,
the finding says what treatment was proposed and does not call rejection
of R rejection of a hold. The published short answer names the body, the
treatment R proposed and R's disposition. Whether declining R was right is
not tested.

**The instrument.** The post-meeting minutes of the Infrastructure
Committee of 2026-08-26, item 7.6, for every motion and amendment put, its
mover, the recorded vote and the result; the agenda and minutes of every
City Council meeting from 2026-08-26 to the as-of date for whether R, or
the report, came before Council and what was done with it; and report
IS03688 for the text of R.

**Verdict ladder.** First establish, from the operative version of
Attachment 5 under claim 2's rules, whether R proposed a hold: whether the
recommended approach expressly defers construction or advancement of the
routes listed apart until a further evaluation, report or decision. Then
read the minutes of the committee and of every Council meeting to the
as-of date, and classify.

**Order of classification.** If the Infrastructure Committee or City
Council adopted R by the as-of date, report Contradicted, whether or not
the operative version can be read. Otherwise, if the operative version
cannot be read and R's treatment cannot be established, report Not
established. In all other cases, apply the hold or no-hold ladder below.

Where R proposed a hold:

- **Supported** if the committee rejected R as defined above, and no body
  adopted R by the as-of date.
- **Partially supported** if the committee left R without a decision as
  defined above and no body adopted R by the as-of date. What is
  established is that the recommendation was not taken up; what is not
  established is that members voted it down or voted to proceed against
  it, which is what "ignore" and "go their own route" assert.
- **Contradicted** if the committee or City Council adopted R by the as-of
  date.
- **Not established** if the committee minutes are unavailable, do not
  record item 7.6's disposition, or postpone or refer it beyond the as-of
  date, and no adoption by City Council by the as-of date is documented.

Where R did not propose a hold, Supported is not available, because no
hold was there to reject, and the finding states the treatment R did
propose in the attachment's words:

- **Partially supported** if the committee rejected R or left it without a
  decision and no body adopted R by the as-of date. What is established is
  the disposition of the recommendation; what is not established is that
  what was declined was a hold.
- **Contradicted** if the committee or City Council adopted R by the as-of
  date.
- **Not established** on the same conditions as above, or if the operative
  version cannot be read so that the treatment R proposed cannot be
  established.

*Required alternative reading of "did not support".* Under the alternative,
non-adoption alone suffices: Supported if no body adopted R by the as-of
date, whatever else happened to it; Contradicted if a body adopted it; Not
established as above. Reviewers report the classification under this
reading beside the primary. The verdict uses the primary reading, because
"ignore" and "go their own route" describe a choice made, and a body that
neither adopts nor rejects has not been shown to have made one.

*Required alternative body.* City Council as a body of thirteen, classified
on the same ladder over its own minutes. If the report or R had not come
before Council by the as-of date, reviewers say so and report Not
established for Council as a body, because a body that has not received a
recommendation cannot have supported or rejected it. The result goes in
`interpretation_notes`, and the distinction between the two bodies is
stated beside the published verdict.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. Every motion and amendment put at item 7.6, in the minutes' words, with
   mover, the names voting each way, absentees among the committee's
   members, and the result.
2. Which members of Council were present at the meeting and which of them
   were members of the committee entitled to vote on item 7.6.
3. Whether the report or R appears on the agenda or in the minutes of any
   City Council meeting from 2026-08-26 to the as-of date, and in what
   form, including any notice of motion or motion pending that names it.
4. The override strand, stated separately: what the committee decided on
   item 7.6, in the minutes' words; whether City Council later made a
   decision incompatible with it, and if so which; and whether the minutes
   therefore show an override as defined above. An absence of a later
   Council item is reported as an absence, not as an override and not as
   agreement.
5. What the record says governed the programme after 2026-08-26: the
   previously approved scope of CM-20-0330 and any later Council decision,
   quoted with its date. Administration's own statements about route
   status and timing, where they exist in a City document, are reported
   under that description and are not treated as a decision of Council.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether the recommendation was a pause;
that is claim 2. Whether declining it was right. Why any member voted as
they did.

### Claim 2, id: `administration-recommended-freezing-14-routes`

**Normalized proposition:** In report IS03688 for the Infrastructure
Committee of 2026-08-26, City administration recommended an approach that
expressly defers construction or advancement of 14 named, unbuilt routes
of the programme until a further evaluation, report or decision.

**Why this reading.** The holder said "recommended freezing 14 future
routes to reassess". "Freezing" is read as an express deferral of
construction or advancement pending a further evaluation, report or
decision, not as re-evaluation alone, because a holder asserted a freeze
and an opponent could accept further study while denying that anything was
held; "future" as not yet constructed; "14" as the count of routes the
recommended approach lists for that treatment. The proposition keeps all
three elements because the holder gave all three, and it is tested on the
report's own words. Whether the approach was in fact adopted is claim 1.

**The instrument.** Report IS03688, Recommendation 1 and the "Next Step"
paragraph, and Attachment 5 in its three versions, on the committee's
agenda as DocumentId 304032 (original), 304031 (REPLACEMENT, Version 1)
and 304030 (REPLACEMENT, Version 2); Attachment 3, "Locations & Status
Updates", at 304029 (original) and 304028 (REPLACEMENT), for the
construction status of each listed route.

**The operative version, fixed here.** Use the version the agenda or
minutes identify as before the committee when item 7.6 was considered. If
they do not distinguish the replacements, state that the operative version
is uncertain, use Version 2 as the declared primary reading, and report
the results under Version 1 and the original. If the classification
differs between versions the finding is version-sensitive, the story says
so beside the verdict, and neither side can cite the verdict without that
qualification.

**"Expressly defers construction or advancement", fixed here.** Primary
reading: the recommended approach, in the operative version, expressly
defers construction or advancement of the listed routes until a further
evaluation, report or decision. Re-evaluation, evaluation for trade-offs
or a report back, on their own, do not meet it unless the text says the
routes wait for that step; a hold with no end date meets it, and no stated
end date is required. Required alternative, stricter: the version states
in terms that the listed routes are removed from the programme's scope, or
will not proceed, whether or not a period is stated. Reviewers report the
classification under both readings for each version.

**"Unbuilt", fixed here.** A listed route is unbuilt if the City's status
record shows that construction on it had not started at the status date
fixed above. A route recorded as under construction or complete is not
unbuilt. A route whose status cannot be established from a dated City
record is unresolved, and an unresolved route never counts as unbuilt.

**The count.** The number of routes the operative version lists for the
deferral treatment, as distinct from the routes it says will continue as
planned, counted by the attachment's named route entries; an entry that
joins two street segments under one bullet is one entry, and reviewers
report any entry they would split or combine and what the count would be
if they did. Reviewers transcribe both lists from the attachment by the
name the attachment gives each route and report the counts. This brief
states no count.

**Verdict ladder.**

- **Supported** if Recommendation 1 asks for approval of the approach in
  Attachment 5, the operative version meets the primary reading of
  "expressly defers construction or advancement", the routes it lists for
  that treatment number exactly 14, and every one of those 14 is
  established as unbuilt at the primary status date.
- **Partially supported** if Recommendation 1 asks for approval of the
  approach, the operative version lists at least one route for
  re-evaluation, none of the Contradicted conditions holds, and Supported
  does not apply because at least one of the following is established:
  the count is not 14; the version does not expressly defer construction
  or advancement under the primary reading; or a listed route was under
  construction or complete at the primary status date. An unresolved
  status alone does not satisfy this row.
- **Contradicted** if the report recommends no change to the remaining
  routes, recommends proceeding with all of them, or Attachment 5 lists
  no routes for re-evaluation.
- **Not established** if none of the Supported, Partially supported or
  Contradicted conditions can be established. This includes an unreadable
  report or version selected under "The operative version" when the
  readable material establishes no Contradicted condition; an unresolved
  route status when it is the only obstacle to Supported; and a readable
  Recommendation 1 that does not ask for approval of the approach in
  Attachment 5 when no other row applies. Uncertainty about which version
  was before the committee does not by itself trigger this row: use
  Version 2 and report the other versions as required.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The exact words of Recommendation 1 and of the passage in each version
   of Attachment 5 that states what happens to the routes listed apart.
2. For each listed route, its status in Attachment 3 at the primary status
   date and, under the alternative, on 2026-08-26 from any dated City
   record, with the record named.
3. Whether the report says the recommendation would change the
   programme's budget or borrowing, in its own words.
4. To whom the recommendation was addressed and what decision it said was
   required.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether the recommendation was wise,
whether it was adopted, or whether "freezing" is what administration
would call it. The report's reasons for the recommendation are reported
only as the report states them.

### Claim 3, id: `motions-to-cut-budget-to-50-million-failed`

**Normalized proposition:** Between the programme's approval in the
2022-11-30 budget deliberations and 2026-09-25, Councillors Karen Principe
and Jennifer Rice brought two motions at City Council that sought to leave
about $50 million in the programme and directed the released funds to
named destinations, and Council defeated both.

**Why this reading.** The holder is one of the two councillors and her
words are "two motions — twice — to cut it to $50M and redirect the rest
to core priorities. Council didn't support them", where "it" is "$100M
for rapid bike lane expansion". The proposition tests whether the two
motions sought to leave about $50 million in the programme, directed the
released funds to named destinations, and were defeated. Reviewers report
the destinations in the motions' words. They do not decide whether those
destinations are "core priorities". The claim names two office-holders
for motions moved and votes cast in office; the minutes settle those, and
the site names office-holders when it reports them.

**"Brought", fixed here.** Count a motion if either Principe or Rice moved
or seconded it. Report each person's role, whether both participated in
the same motion, and whether the two attempts occurred on separate
occasions. Required alternative: count a motion only if one of them moved
it and the other seconded it, or both are recorded as movers; reviewers
report the classification under this co-sponsorship reading beside the
primary.

**"To leave about $50 million", fixed here.** Primary reading: the
motion's text would leave the programme's approved capital budget at
between $45 million and $55 million, taking the approved total at the
time of the motion, read from the capital profile and the adjustments
approved before that date as fixed under "What is measured", as the base
and the reduction the motion names as the cut. Required alternative: the
motion would reduce the programme's approved capital budget by at least
half, whatever the remainder. Reviewers report the classification under
both and show the arithmetic for each motion, naming the document the
base comes from. Neither band comes from a pre-existing standard; the
primary holds the claim to the figure the holder gave, the alternative to
its plain sense of "cut in half or more".

**"Directed the released funds to named destinations", fixed here.** The
motion's text names where the reduction goes: to the tax levy, to a
reduction in borrowing, to another capital profile or programme, or to a
named reserve. A reduction stated with no destination does not meet it.

**"Council defeated them", fixed here.** Each motion was put to a recorded
vote at City Council and lost, or was put and lost as an amendment to a
budget motion. A motion withdrawn, ruled out of order or never put is not a
defeat and is reported as what it was.

**The instrument.** The post-meeting minutes of every City Council meeting
from 2022-11-30 to the as-of date, searched for motions and amendments
naming capital profile CM-20-0330, "Active Transportation Implementation
Acceleration" or "Bike Plan Implementation", with attention to the
capital budget and supplemental capital budget adjustment meetings of
each autumn and spring, for each motion and its vote; the capital profile
and approved adjustments for the contemporaneous base. Reviewers list
every such motion they find and name the meetings they searched.

**Verdict ladder.**

- **Supported** if at least two jointly brought motions each meet the
  primary amount rule, each directed the released funds to a named
  destination, and were defeated.
- **Partially supported** if at least one jointly brought motion to reduce
  the programme was defeated but Supported does not apply.
- **Contradicted** if complete minutes show no jointly brought motion to
  reduce the programme was defeated, including where candidate motions
  carried, were withdrawn, were ruled out of order, or were never put.
- **Not established** if none of the Supported, Partially supported or
  Contradicted conditions can be established from the available record,
  including because relevant minutes cannot be obtained or a
  contemporaneous budget amount needed to classify a candidate motion
  cannot be established.

"Jointly brought" in this ladder means brought under the primary reading
of "Brought" fixed above.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. Each motion's full text as minuted, its meeting and date, mover and
   seconder, the names voting each way, and the result.
2. The destination each motion's text names for the released funds, in
   the motion's words, or a statement that it names none.
3. The programme's approved capital budget at the date of each motion,
   with the document it comes from, and the remainder each motion would
   have left, as the reviewer's calculation.
4. Any other recorded motion by either councillor to reduce, defund,
   pause or suspend the programme in the period, listed the same way.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether the cuts should have carried.
Whether the named destinations deserved the money is not tested; whether
a motion named destinations is tested.

### Claim 4, id: `same-seven-councillors-vote-together`

**Normalized proposition:** Across the recorded votes of City Council on
funding or continuing the programme from 2022-11-30 to 2026-09-25, there
is a fixed set of at least seven members of Council who served before and
after the October 2025 election, each of whom cast at least one such vote
in each term and voted on the programme's side at every such vote held
while they served.

**Why this reading.** One person, in two comments, asserts a fixed bloc of
seven that "voted for it" and "never had an intention of pausing it", the
"same old 7". The factual content is a voting pattern, and that is what
is tested: whether the same people, at least seven of them, were on the
programme's side every time. Everything about intention, cliques and
corruption is excluded; the triage reason excluded it, and a recorded
vote is evidence of how a member voted and of nothing else. "Same old"
asserts a continuing, fixed bloc, not a collection assembled across
different councils, so the primary rule requires each member of the bloc
to have served and voted on both sides of the October 2025 election, and
the period runs from the programme's first vote; the while-serving
reading without that requirement is a required alternative. "Councillors" is read as
members of Council, the mayor included, with the alternative reported.
Membership of Council in each term is established from the City's
published election results and the minutes.

**The vote set V, fixed here.** Every recorded vote of City Council, from
2022-11-30 to the as-of date, on a motion or amendment whose text would
(a) approve, increase, reduce or remove funding for capital profile
CM-20-0330, including first, second and third readings of its borrowing
bylaw and any amendment to that bylaw, or (b) pause, suspend, cease,
continue or re-evaluate the programme's routes as a whole. Excluded:
procedural votes (adopting an agenda, meeting in private, postponing),
votes only to request a report or memo, and votes on the design or scope
of a single route. List every candidate vote meeting (a) or (b),
including votes whose programme direction is unclassifiable. Reviewers
give for every vote its meeting, date, item, motion text, the names each
way, absentees and result, and say which meetings they searched.

*Required alternative vote set V+.* V together with recorded votes of
Council committees on motions meeting (a) or (b), and with Council votes
on the scope of a single programme route where the motion's text says
which way it cuts. Committee votes cannot show seven members but can break
a member's pattern. Reviewers report the bloc under V+ beside V.

**The pro-programme side** is as fixed under "Terms fixed in this brief".

**The bloc B, fixed here.** Primary rule: a member qualifies only if they
served on Council before and after the October 2025 election, cast at
least one vote in V in each term, and voted on the pro-programme side at
every vote in V held while they served; an absence breaks the pattern.
Count only members meeting all of those conditions in B. B counts the
mayor as a member. Required alternatives, results under each:

- the while-serving rule, under which a member is in the bloc if they
  voted, and voted on the pro-programme side, at every vote in V held
  while they were a member of Council, whether or not they served in both
  terms, an absence breaking the pattern, and a member being assessable
  only if at least two votes in V were held while they served;
- the less strict absence rule, under which a member is in the bloc if
  they voted at not fewer than half of the votes in V held while they
  served and were on the pro-programme side at every vote they cast,
  applied both with and without the both-terms condition;
- B counted without the mayor.

**When the both-terms condition cannot be met by anyone.** If V holds no
vote in one of the two terms, no member can cast a vote in each term, and
B under the primary rule is zero for want of a vote rather than because
anyone voted against the programme. In that case the primary rule reports
Not established, not Contradicted, the finding says which term holds no
vote in V, and the alternatives above are reported as usual; the same
applies to V+ if it too holds no vote in one term.

**Completeness rule for unclassifiable votes.** For each bloc rule,
calculate a lower bloc count by treating each member's cast,
unclassifiable vote as contrary and an upper count by treating it as
pro-programme; absences retain the rule stated for that calculation. If
both counts fall in the same verdict band, use that band and report both
counts. If they fall in different bands, report Not established. Apply the
same rule to V+.

**Assessability.** A pattern needs votes to show in. Primary: V holds at
least three votes; required alternative: V holds at least two. Neither
minimum comes from a pre-existing standard; reviewers report whether the
alternative changes the classification.

**Order of classification.** For each vote set and bloc rule, apply its
Not established conditions before its count bands. A count band applies
only when none of those conditions holds. This order also applies under
the alternative band set. The no-vote-in-one-term condition applies only
to a bloc rule that requires a vote in both terms.

**Verdict ladder.**

- **Supported** if V holds at least three votes and B is at least 7 under
  the primary rule.
- **Partially supported** if V holds at least three votes and B is 4, 5 or
  6: a fixed set exists but is smaller than the seven claimed.
- **Contradicted** if V holds at least three votes and B is 3 or fewer.
- **Not established** if V holds fewer than three votes, V holds no vote
  in one of the two terms so that the both-terms condition cannot be met
  by anyone, the minutes of the Council meetings in the period cannot be
  obtained, or the completeness rule above leaves the lower and upper
  counts in different bands.

Alternative bands, results required under both: Partially supported if B
is 5 or 6, Contradicted if B is 4 or fewer. Neither set comes from a
pre-existing standard: the primary treats a fixed set of a simple
majority of the councillors' seats as within reach of "seven", the
alternative asks for more than half the claimed size before crediting a
pattern at all.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The full vote table for V and V+, including unclassifiable candidate
   votes marked as such with the reason.
2. The names of the members in B under each rule, and for each member of
   Council over the period the number of votes in V held while they
   served at which they voted pro-programme, voted against, or were
   absent, by term.
3. Which members of B sit on the Council elected in October 2025, and
   which do not, so a reader can see how much of the set is "same" and
   how much is "old".
4. The largest number of members who voted on the pro-programme side in
   any single vote in V, and the smallest, so the bloc is read beside the
   margins.

**What this claim does not test.** Why any member voted as they did;
whether members coordinate; whether the bloc is a "clique"; whether
anyone is corrupt. No sentence in the finding may attribute an intention
or a motive to any member, and the story may not either.

## Stakes

What each verdict would change, for the person making the claim and for
the person arguing against it. Stated without any view of which way any
of them comes out, and stated no wider than the rules above establish.

**Pause decision.** For claim 1, Supported establishes rejection of an
administration-proposed hold by Infrastructure Committee, not a full
Council rejection; Council's action is stated separately. An opponent
could no longer say the committee merely took no decision, or that
nothing resembling a hold was on the table. Contradicted would establish
that the committee or Council adopted R, and the holder would have to
withdraw the non-support claim. Partially supported would establish
either that the committee neither adopted nor rejected a hold, so the
holder keeps "not supported" under the alternative reading and loses
"ignored" and "went their own way", or that what the committee declined
was not a hold under the deferral definition, so the holder loses the
word "pause" while keeping the disposition. Not established would leave
neither side able to cite the meeting for anything until the record shows
a disposition and a treatment.

**Administration's recommendation.** Supported would establish that
administration recommended an actual hold on 14 unbuilt routes, not only a
plan to study them, and an opponent who calls the recommendation something
milder than a freeze must answer the report's own words. Contradicted
would establish that no such hold was recommended; the holder would lose
the asserted treatment, even if some re-evaluation was proposed. Partially
supported would establish that administration recommended re-evaluating a
set of routes but the count, the word "freezing" or "unbuilt" overstates
it, which both sides would have to carry into the argument about what the
committee declined. Not established would mean the version before the
committee, or the status of a listed route, cannot be established from
the record.

**Budget motions.** Supported would establish two attempts by the named
councillors to leave about $50 million, direct the remainder as stated in
the motions, and lose both votes. Contradicted would establish that the
recorded attempts did not occur or were not defeated. Partially supported
would establish at least one defeated attempt by them to reduce the
programme, without the two, the amount or the stated destination all
holding; the holder keeps the record of trying and loses what does not
hold. A result against the co-sponsorship reading alone would not refute
what the holder said, which is why that reading is an alternative. Not
established would mean the minutes or the contemporaneous budget base
cannot be had.

**Same seven.** For claim 4, Supported establishes at least seven
continuing members who voted on the programme's side at every eligible
vote while serving in both terms; the alternative results show how the
finding changes if service in both terms is not required. An opponent
could no longer describe the pattern as shifting or as assembled from
members who never served together. Contradicted would establish that no
such continuing fixed set of more than three exists; the holder would lose
the claimed number and pattern. Partially supported would establish a
continuing fixed set smaller than seven, so the holder's number is wrong
while a pattern is real. Seven people each favourable in only two votes,
or drawn from different councils, would not surprise an opponent, which
is why absence breaks the pattern and both terms are required under the
primary rule. Not established would mean too few votes exist to show a
pattern either way, no vote in V falls in one of the two terms, or
unclassifiable votes leave the count straddling two bands.

**Definition sensitivity.** Each verdict applies under the primary body,
version, status date, amount band, destination rule, vote set, bloc rule
and band set fixed above. Where a required alternative produces a
different classification, the finding is definition-sensitive, the story
says so beside the verdict, and neither side can fairly cite the verdict
without that qualification.
