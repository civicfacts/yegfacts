Replace with: `"register-note.md records the drop beside this brief. /considered does not yet display claim-level drops."`

2. **Does the proposition test what the post asserts?: FINDING.** Claim 3 folds together three different numerators, then quietly excludes two: `"the '500 people' and '.001%' forms, which no instrument measures and which are reported as the range of the circulating figures, not tested."` The forms are a population participation share, a share of the population using bike lanes, and an absolute number of seasonal lane users. Replace with: `"This claim covers only the 1%, 1-2%, 2% and 2.3% population-rider forms. The '.001% of the population' lane-use form and the '500 people using the bike lanes 4 months of the year' form are distinct propositions. Record and dispose of each separately, with a source-availability reason if it cannot be checked."` Claim 4 has the same mismatch. Its proposition says people `"use the bike lanes year-round"`, but its measure is residents who `"ride through the winter"`. Replace the measure with actual bike-lane use by residents, if a published representative instrument measures it. Otherwise drop this claim as uncheckable rather than substituting all cycling for lane use.

3. **Is it the strongest fair reading?: FINDING.** The lower bounds reverse the meaning of "only." Claim 3 says `"Contradicted if P is below 0.5 percent"`, and claim 4 requires the year-round share to be `"at least 0.5 percent"` before that part can hold. A holder arguing that only a very small minority rides would not treat an even smaller share as contradictory. Replace claim 3's primary reading with: `"No more than about 2.3 percent of Edmonton's population rides a bicycle during the fixed reference period."` Use one-sided classifications: Supported at or below 2.5 percent, Partially supported above 2.5 through 5 percent, and Contradicted above 5 percent, with the existing 3 and 6 percent alternative. For claim 4, remove the lower bound: Part A holds at or below 2 percent, with 3 percent as the required alternative; Part B remains below the holder's stated 15 percent ceiling.

4. **Operationalization and its alternatives: FINDING.** Four repairs are required.

   - Claim 3 uses `"the reference period of the best available published measure"`. A day, month, season and year produce different participation rates, so this leaves a verdict-sensitive accounting window to the reviewers. Replace with: `"The primary reference period is the past 12 months. A shorter published period is reported as an alternative and cannot carry the verdict."` Before freezing, identify a published Edmonton source containing that exact measure and question wording. The identified household travel surveys measure trips, not whether a resident rode at least once during a year. The identified Bike Plan and Insight reports do not establish the required representative population share. If no such source is identified, drop claim 3.
   - Claim 4 relies on `"a published measure with a seasonal split"`, but the located City winter-cycling instruments are Insight or engagement-panel products and do not publish representative shares of all Edmonton residents using bike lanes in winter and outside winter. Replace with: `"This claim is sent to the panel only if the brief names a published representative Edmonton instrument that asks all sampled residents about bike-lane use in both seasons. Otherwise it is dropped as uncheckable."`
   - Claim 1 permits automatic Not established if data do not cover all of January through July 2026. The live [Eco-Counter metadata](https://dev.socrata.com/foundry/data.edmonton.ca/tq23-qn4m) available for this check reports a last update of 2026-07-22, which does not verify the full window. Replace with: `"This claim is sent to the panel only if a frozen source audit confirms published timestamps through 2026-07-31. If the window is incomplete on the as-of date, park the claim rather than predesign its verdict as Not established."`
   - The brief says the Eco-Counter dataset has `"a locations map and a monthly-by-location view published beside it."` The dataset and its referenced location dataset exist, but the named monthly view does not resolve on the as-of date. Replace with: `"The City publishes the Bike and Pedestrian Counts (Eco Counter) dataset, identifier tq23-qn4m, and references a separate counter-location dataset. The former Monthly Bike Counts by Location visualization is not relied on."` Also replace the summary statement that claim 2 uses `"the most recent complete calendar year plus that same window"` with `"the 2025 calendar year, including the July and January measures specified below"`, because its section requires no January-to-July 2026 calculation.

5. **Does the brief leak an expected finding?: FINDING.** The stakes headings label which side each pair is expected to surprise: `"The pair that could surprise the 'nobody rides them' side"` and `"The pair that could surprise the 'loads of people ride' side."` The sentence `"the people citing the counter total would have to accept that a large raw count and a small share are both true at once"` also assumes magnitudes not yet established. Replace the headings with `"Stakes of the counter claims"` and `"Stakes of the participation, trip-share and commuting claims."` Replace the sentence with: `"A counter total and a population, trip or commuting share measure different things. 6. **Is the claim checkable at all?: FINDING.** Claim 2 tests a citywide proposition using named counters or `"the full set of City automated bicycle counters sited on on-street bike lanes."` Those counters are monitoring locations, not a representative sample of Edmonton's bike lanes. They can describe measured locations but cannot establish that Edmonton's lanes generally carry little or no traffic. Replace with: `"The available counters cannot carry a verdict on the citywide generalization because their sites are not a representative sample of Edmonton bike lanes. Report the measured corridors as context, and do not send the general claim to the panel unless a representative network-level instrument is identified."` Claims 3 and 4 are likewise uncheckable under their current source requirements. Claims 5 through 8 are checkable in principle: the published 2015 travel report contains mode and mode-by-purpose tables, and the Census publishes the required City and CMA commuting universes. Dropping the Heritage Days claim is justified.

7. **Scope traps: FINDING.** The brief repeatedly moves between riding anywhere and using bike lanes. Claim 3 defines its numerator as residents who `"rode a bicycle at all"`, although some grouped forms concern people `"using the bike lanes"`. Claim 4 makes the same substitution explicitly. Replace with: `"Cycling participation and bike-lane use are separate measures. A participation survey may test only forms about residents who ride. Forms about distinct bike-lane users require a lane-use instrument and are dropped if none exists."` The remaining out-of-scope list is appropriate.

8. **Stakes: FINDING.** The brief says `"Every verdict is reachable on every claim"`, but it does not state what every verdict would mean to both sides for each claim, and current source constraints make that reachability assertion false for claims 2 through 4. Replace the stakes section with claim-specific consequences:

   - Claim 1: Supported lets the holder cite a counter-passage total near the stated figure and requires the opponent to stop denying that total; Contradicted requires the holder to withdraw the figure and lets the opponent say the published total is not close to it.
   - Claim 2, if made checkable: Supported lets the holder say the representative lane measure meets the predeclared low-use threshold; Contradicted requires the holder to drop the citywide low-use generalization and lets the opponent say the representative measure points against it.
   - Claim 3: Supported lets the holder cite a participation share no higher than the fixed ceiling; Contradicted requires the holder to drop that share and lets the opponent say substantially more residents ride.
   - Claim 4: Supported lets the holder cite both seasonal ceilings; Contradicted requires the holder to withdraw both and lets the opponent say neither seasonal assertion holds.
   - Claim 5: Supported lets the holder cite an all-trip bicycle share near 2 percent; Contradicted requires that figure to be withdrawn.
   - Claim 6: Supported lets the holder cite a bicycle commuting share below 1 percent; Contradicted requires that commuting figure to be withdrawn.
   - Claim 7: Supported lets the holder cite a car commuting share near 87 percent; Contradicted requires that figure to be withdrawn.
   - Claim 8: Supported lets the holder say a majority of bicycle trips are recreational under the source's categories; Contradicted requires that majority claim to be withdrawn and lets the opponent say the purpose breakdown points against it.
   - For every claim, Partially supported permits only the established portion to be repeated. Not established means neither the proposition nor its negation has been established. None of the four verdicts decides whether bike-lane spending or policy is justified.

9. **Who asks this: FINDING.** The resident question is good, but the answer overpromises: `"The record can answer the question"` and `"The reader's question is answered by the set of eight."` Counters do not count distinct people, and the brief has not identified a representative source for the one participation proposition. Replace with: `"A resident would ask: 'How many Edmontonians actually ride, and are the bike lanes being used?' No identified counter or travel dataset supplies a current count of distinct Edmonton bike-lane users, so the record cannot answer that question directly. This brief tests the separately circulating counter, trip-share, commuting and trip-purpose figures at the levels the published instruments support. It states plainly that those measures do not reveal how many distinct people use Edmonton's bike lanes."`

A holder of the low-use view would object that the draft substitutes riding anywhere for lane use and could label an even smaller participation share contradictory. An opponent would object that selected counter sites cannot refute a citywide generalization and that the stakes language hints at expected winners. Both should accept the separation of passages, people, trips and commuters, and the Census and household-travel instruments are sound for the measures they actually publish. Until the conflated claims, unavailable instruments, expected-direction language and incomplete stakes are repaired, neither side should regard the frame as fair.
