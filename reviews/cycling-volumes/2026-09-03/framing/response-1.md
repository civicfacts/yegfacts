# Author's response to framing check 1 (Stew, 2026-09-03)

Nine findings. Six adopted in full, two adopted in a different form with
the reason given, one rejected with the reason given. The brief in this
directory is the revised version; this file is the record of what moved
and what did not.

## Adopted in full

**1. Provenance.** Two fixes. The register note says `/considered`
cannot display a claim-level drop yet, and the brief said the note made
the drop "visible on /considered". That was wrong and it is corrected to
say the drop is recorded beside the brief and what the register would
need before the page could show it.

The filename mismatch the checker found is a packaging artifact, not a
brief error: the committed intake record is `intake.md` in this
directory, and the package handed to the checker renamed it
`intake-record.md`. The brief keeps `intake.md`, which is the true path,
and the package for check 2 uses the real filename so the checker is not
misled twice.

**3. The lower bounds reverse "only".** This is the best finding in the
report and it is taken whole. Every holder of claims 3 and 4 says
"only", and the point being argued is that the share is small. A band
with a floor let those claims fail in the direction their holders are
arguing for: a measured 0.4 percent would have been Contradicted, which
no holder would accept and no opponent would claim. Both claims are now
one-sided ceilings. Claim 3 becomes "No more than about 2 percent of
Edmonton's population rides a bicycle", Supported at or below 2.5
percent, Partially supported above that to 5 percent, Contradicted above
5 percent, with 3 and 6 percent as the required alternative. Claim 4's
Part A loses its floor and holds at or below 2 percent, with 3 percent as
the alternative. Part B keeps the holder's own 15 percent ceiling.

**4a. Claim 3's reference period.** Adopted. The window is now fixed at
the twelve months before the instrument's reference date, stated before
any figure is read, with a shorter published period reported as an
alternative that cannot carry the verdict.

**4c and 4d. Claim 1's instruments and window.** Adopted. The
monthly-by-location view is removed; the brief now names the base dataset
`tq23-qn4m` and the separate counter-location dataset, and requires
reviewers to name whatever view they used and relate it to the base
dataset. The summary line describing claim 2's period is corrected to the
2025 calendar year.

**5. Leaked expected finding.** Adopted whole, and the checker was
right that this was the worst defect in the draft. The two stakes
headings named which side each pair would surprise, which tells the panel
where the brief expects each pair to land. Both headings are gone, the
stakes are now stated per claim, and the sentence assuming a large raw
count and a small share is replaced with the neutral statement that a
count and a share measure different things and neither predicts the
other.

**7. Riding against lane use.** Adopted. The denominators section now
carries a rule of its own: riding a bicycle and using a bike lane are
different measures, no Edmonton instrument measures distinct lane users,
and where a claim's holders said "use the lanes" and only a riding
measure exists, the substitution is named in the proposition, stated in
`limitations` and printed beside the verdict. Claim 4's proposition is
rewritten to say "rides a bicycle" rather than "uses the bike lanes",
with the substitution declared in the section.

**9. Who asks this overpromised.** Adopted. The section now opens by
saying the record does not answer the reader's question directly, that no
published instrument counts distinct bike-lane users, and that the brief
tests the nearest levels the record does answer.

## Adopted in a different form

**2 and 4b. Claim 3's folded forms, and the demand to drop claim 3 and
claim 4 if no instrument is identified.**

On the folding: the checker asked for the ".001%" and "500 people" forms
to be split out as separate propositions. They are not split, because the
merge that produced this claim is a completed intake stage with its own
coverage proof, and re-splitting a registered claim inside a brief would
undo it without the check that made it trustworthy. What was genuinely
wrong was the draft's sentence saying those two forms were "not tested",
which quietly excluded two of the claim's own wordings. That sentence is
gone. The brief now lists all eight forms, says they are forms of one
assertion that the share is small, and requires reviewers to report the
measured share against every one of them, converting "500 people" to a
share of the City's published population with the arithmetic shown.
Nothing is dropped and nothing is smuggled.

On the demand to drop: rejected as an instruction, adopted as a
requirement to name sources. The framing check's own bound says a
definition is a defect when no source plausibly exists for the period or
place. Sources plausibly exist, and the brief now names them: Statistics
Canada's Canadian Community Health Survey, which asks about cycling in
the past twelve months and is published for census metropolitan areas
including Edmonton; any other Statistics Canada survey with an
Edmonton-level participation estimate; the Edmonton region household
travel surveys, admissible only if their published reports state the
share of respondents who made a bicycle trip; and any City survey drawn
from a representative sample. The Insight Community is named as an
opt-in panel that cannot carry a verdict.

The brief also states what happens if none of those publishes an
Edmonton figure: the verdict is Not established, the search is bounded
and stated, and the panel figures are reported under their own names.
That is not a rigged outcome. Eight people in one thread quoted a
population share at each other, and finding that no published Edmonton
measure of it exists is a real result a reader cannot get anywhere else.
Dropping the claim instead would delete the most-argued figure in the
question because the answer might be inconvenient to give.

**4c, the second half. Parking claim 1 if the counter window is
short.** Adopted as a rule, rejected as a park. The checker is right
that an automatic Not established on an incomplete window predesigns a
verdict around a dataset refresh lag. So the brief now says: if the
published data covers at least five of the seven months, the verdict is
computed on the months published, the exact first and last day is named,
the shortfall is stated wherever the figure appears, and the direction of
the bias is named, because a short window can only lower a total. Not
established is reserved for a record that cannot answer at all.

**8. Stakes.** Adopted in substance. The stakes section is rewritten
claim by claim, saying what Supported, Partially supported, Contradicted
and Not established would each change for the person making the claim and
for the person arguing against it, and adding that no verdict here
decides whether any lane or any spending was worth it. The checker's
assertion that reachability is false for claims 2 through 4 is answered
rather than adopted: the brief now states reachability per claim, says
that on claims 3, 4 and 8 Not established is a genuine fourth outcome
rather than an accident, and says what would have to be missing for it.

## Rejected

**6. Do not send claim 2's general statement to the panel.**

The checker's premise is correct and is now written into the brief: the
City's counters sit where the City chose to put them, they are not a
representative sample of Edmonton's bike lanes, and no representative
sample is published. What follows from that is not that the claim goes
unchecked.

Two standing rules point the other way. The framing check's own override
says a claim is tested as its holders assert it, and that when a post
offers an example as evidence for a general pattern, the general pattern
is the claim. Ten people did exactly that here. And check 9 says that
where the record cannot answer at the level people ask, the brief tests
the nearest level the record does answer and names that level in words a
reader would use.

So the proposition is narrowed to the level the record answers and says
so on its face: "The Edmonton bike lanes that are metered, including the
lanes residents named as empty, carry little or no bicycle traffic." The
verdict set is every City automated counter on an on-street bike lane,
fixed before any data is read. Reviewers must state how many counters
exist, how many are on on-street lanes, and what share of the City's
on-street bike-lane length they sit on where that is published. Every
statement of the finding says it is about the lanes the City counts, and
the story tells a holder who meant every lane that the unmetered lanes
were not measured and that nobody publishes a measure of them.

Leaving the claim out would take the observation ten of the twenty-five
people in this question are making and return nothing on it, while the
one measurement that bears on it sits in the City's open data. That is
the failure v1.13 and v1.11 were both written against.
