1. **Truth — `cycling-safety` overstates what the traffic bylaw establishes.**  
   “Edmonton's traffic bylaw sets … the rules that decide fault in a collision” is too strong. The City says cycling rules come from the provincial Traffic Safety Act, the Traffic Bylaw, and the Public Spaces Bylaw. Bylaw 5590 regulates highway use and parking; civil negligence and the burden of proof are addressed by sections 184–186 of the provincial Act. The bylaw can supply conduct rules relevant to fault, but it does not decide fault. [City cycling laws](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/cycling_walking/bike-routes-how-to), [Traffic Bylaw 5590](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/C5590.pdf), [Traffic Safety Act](https://www.canlii.org/en/ab/laws/stat/rsa-2000-c-t-6/)

   This does not revive the attribution objection. The bad sentence in the refusal reasoning is: “What is left … is not disputed by either seat.” That establishes agreement, not truth.
