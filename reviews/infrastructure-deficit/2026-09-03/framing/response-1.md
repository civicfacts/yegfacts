# Author's response to framing check 1 (Stew, 2026-09-03)

Check 1 returned REVISE with findings on eight of the nine checks. Most
of them are right, and two of them are right about things this brief was
built to get right and did not. The brief is rewritten rather than
patched. Finding by finding, with the three places I did not do as asked
marked and argued.

## 1. Provenance — adopted, with one count corrected

Adopted. The Story section said fifteen people "made the same argument in
different words", and the selection rationale called it "the standing case
against the program in Edmonton's 2026 argument about streets". Neither is
established by one thread. Both are gone. The replacement says what the
intake actually supports: fifteen accounts, five related arguments, and
prevalence inside this thread rather than across Edmonton.

One correction to the check's arithmetic, made by re-reading the ten
captured wordings rather than by accepting the number. The check says
eight accounts named roads, alleys or potholes, and then glosses the
remainder as "two spoke only about snow and one spoke about grass and
snow", which is three and would leave seven. The record has two:
comment 67, "The city can't even cut the grass or remove snow", and
comment 345, "Try removing the snow." The other eight all name roads,
alleys or potholes, including comment 212, which names potholes and snow
together. The brief now says eight and two, and Claim 1 names the two.

## 2. Does the proposition test what the post asserts?

**Claim 1, ten accounts.** Adopted, corrected to eight as above.

**Claim 2, the reframe.** Adopted, and this is the finding that mattered
most. The check is right that a test of whether council physically removed
money from a renewal profile is a bookkeeping substitute for the argument
being made: nobody in the capture claimed a transfer, the intake record
says so in as many words, and a holder could accept Contradicted on the
old proposition and go on making the same argument unchanged. The brief's
own run-record predicted this objection and said the honest fix would be
to make the funding instrument carry the verdict rather than a
qualification. That is what has happened.

The new proposition is the check's, in the brief's own vocabulary: at
least half of the $100 million was money council could have spent on
renewing roads and alleys instead, at a time when the City was reporting
an unmet capital renewal requirement. The ladder runs on three quantities
that sum to the fixed $100.0 million — eligible, ineligible and
undetermined — so that a large undetermined part routes to Not established
instead of being silently read as either answer. Boundaries are walked in
the brief. The transfer question survives in full as required calculation
3, reported without a verdict, exactly as the check asked.

I checked that the record can meet this definition before adopting it, as
the framing prompt requires. The adopted capital budget publishes funding
sources on the profile sheets, and a restricted source has published
programme terms; both Supported and Contradicted are therefore reachable —
general municipal capital funding would give Supported, a
cycling-restricted grant would give Contradicted. That is a better
one-sided-intake position than the proposition it replaces.

**Claim 3, the denominator.** Adopted. See check 5.

## 3. Is it the strongest fair reading?

**The title.** Adopted in substance, not in wording. "Letting go" promises
a trend verdict the brief does not give, and drainage is in the title and
in no claim; both objections are correct and both are fixed. I did not use
the proposed title, which is a two-clause committee sentence, because the
site's plain-speech rules (docs/DESIGN.md section 12) require the question
to read as something a person would say. The title is now: "Are Edmonton's
roads in poor shape, and could the $100 million for bike lanes have fixed
them?" It carries the same three parts, drops the trend word and drops
drainage, and it is the question a resident types.

**Alleys.** Adopted in substance, with one deliberate difference. The
check is right that no identified source publishes a computable alley
condition distribution, that the open-data sets are renewal-programme
data, and that a renewal schedule is not a condition rating. Claim 1 is
narrowed to roads and gives no alley verdict. The brief says so, and the
scope section forbids the story from asserting one.

The difference: the check would let an alley verdict appear if a source
turns out to exist. I have not done that, because a claim whose existence
depends on what the panel finds cannot be put in a panel package — the
schema carries one verdict per claim and the framing prompt itself forbids
a brief from requiring an output the schema cannot carry. Instead, alleys
are required calculation 2: the panel must establish whether the City
rates them at all, and if it does, report the distribution in full and the
classification it would carry under Claim 1's ladder, as a calculation
rather than a finding. If the City does rate alleys, that is intake for an
alley claim on the register and a later run, not a verdict added to this
one after the fact. The reader gets the alley answer either way; nobody
gets it as a verdict on a class we were not sure was rated.

**The combined roads-and-alleys asset set**, and the "larger share"
fallback that could have produced a roads-and-alleys verdict without
measuring alleys: both gone with the narrowing.

## 4. Operationalization and its alternatives

**Source 9, Bylaw 19627.** Adopted, and this is the check earning its
keep. I verified it before adopting rather than taking it: Bylaw 19627 is
a rates bylaw, setting rates, fees and charges for drainage services and
wastewater treatment for 2022-04-01 to 2025-03-31, and it was replaced by
Bylaw 20865. So the brief's source 9 was wrong twice — it named an
instrument out of force at the as-of date, and it named a rates bylaw as
though it were the instrument that moved drainage to EPCOR, which it never
was. Source 9 now names the 2017 council decision and its effective date,
the bylaw in force on 2026-09-03, and the record replacing 19627, and it
tells reviewers to establish which instrument is actually in force rather
than to accept the brief's naming. A new standing reviewer instruction
says the same thing generally.

**The B2 conflict.** Adopted, and it is the more serious of the two. B2
said both sides of every ratio cover the same years; Claim 3 then let a
reviewer compute a ratio against a shortfall published over some other
span. That is the accounting-window defect that withdrew `at-100m-vs-snow`
three days ago, written into the section that exists to prevent it. B2 now
says a verdict-bearing ratio may be computed only where both sides cover
2023-2026, and that a differently spanned figure is reported, labelled and
left unclassified. Claim 3's Not established band now turns on whether a
same-period figure exists, and required calculation 4 makes the panel
establish the renewal requirement and the renewal funded for 2023-2026
from the adopted capital budget, which is the same-cycle basis the check
pointed at.

**The renewal boundary.** Adopted. Counting a whole profile as renewal
money ignored the growth and renewal split the profile sheets publish,
and swept in sidewalks, signals, streetlights and safety work. Required
calculation 3 now counts only dollars the record identifies as coming from
a renewal allocation, never a growth allocation, and forbids describing a
transfer from a mixed profile as a road or alley cut unless the record
identifies the affected asset — reporting it at the Service Category level
under the City's own name instead. B3 carries the general rule: where a
City category mixes what this brief separates, use the City's own
published split and never apportion one ourselves.

**The condition denominator.** Adopted. Replacement value carries the
verdict, lane-kilometres are the required alternative where published, and
count is used only where neither is. The rule that let a reviewer mix
denominators across classes and take the class covering "the larger share"
is deleted; where classes are published on different denominators the
reviewer reports them separately and routes to Not established.

**Approved versus spent — not adopted, and this is a refusal.** The check
asks that the magnitude proposition also be *classified* using cumulative
actual expenditure through 2025, with a definition-sensitive label if the
two classifications differ. I am not doing that, for a reason the check's
own framework supplies.

At the as-of date only three of the four budget years have closed. A
cumulative actual through 2025 covers three years. Every denominator in
Claim 3 covers four. Classifying a three-year actual against a four-year
requirement is precisely the span mismatch that took a published claim off
this site's findings board on 2026-09-03, and it is what B2 exists to
forbid. There is no same-span actual to use instead, because 2026 has not
closed; so the alternative the check proposes is a definition the record
cannot meet at this freeze date, which the framing prompt itself calls a
framing defect rather than a fair alternative.

The underlying concern — that the captured wording says "spending" and the
brief tests an approval — is real and is answered without the mismatch.
B4 states the approved-not-spent rule and now says explicitly that no
actual goes on either side of a classified ratio and why. Required
calculation 5 reports the actuals by year, labelled as actuals, so a
reader can see how much of the $100 million has gone out of the door. If
the check disagrees after reading that, I will take it as an open finding
into the resolution rather than move the basis.

**Drainage — partly adopted, and this is a refusal.** Adopted: drainage is

Not adopted: the instruction to delete the drainage calculation. It stays,

The calculation carries no verdict, appears in no ratio, and B6 exists so

reporting, in a question whose own source raised drainage. If the check
holds this open, I will take it to the resolution and say so there.

## Three things the check did not raise, changed anyway

Checking the check's findings against the published documents turned up
one defect of my own and confirmed two of its own findings, so all three
are recorded here rather than left for check 2 to find.

**A roads category the City does not publish.** Claim 1 defined its asset
set as "the paved driving surfaces the City owns", which is a boundary I
was drawing, not one the City draws. The condition report publishes a road
class with its own sub-classes, and at least one of them is not a driving
surface. Reassembling those into a driving-surfaces set would have been
the same manoeuvre that took `at-100m-vs-roads` off the findings board
three days ago — a roads-only category built by hand — inside the brief
written to prevent it. Claim 1 now takes the City's own road class at the
level the City publishes it, requires every sub-class to be named and
reported, and forbids adding, dropping or carving. Anything inside the
City's class that a reader would not call a road is reported as a
qualification under the City's label, not corrected by hand.

**The alley finding holds, and it has a trap in it.** The check's reading
is right: the condition report does not rate alleys or laneways as a
class. But the capital budget's alley renewal profile does publish a
share of paved alleys requiring renewal, and council has set an alley
condition target. Either could be mistaken for a rating and used as *p*.
Required calculation 2 now makes the panel report both, under labels
saying which kind of figure each one is, and says in terms that a
renewal-need share is not a condition rating and may not be used as one.

**Claim 3's denominator exists, and I checked before adopting it.** The
framing prompt requires that a definition be one the record can meet
before it is imposed. The adopted 2023-2026 Capital Budget publishes a
modelled ideal renewal requirement set against funded renewal for the
2023-2026 cycle, under the City's own heading for their difference, at
pages 42 to 49. That is a whole-city figure on the same four years as
CM-20-0330, so the new primary denominator is computable, on the
numerator's own span, from a source already named. Source 5 now points at
those pages. No roads-and-alleys-specific unfunded figure was located,
which is why that is the required alternative rather than the primary and
why the brief tells the panel to say plainly if there is none.

## One thing I want check 2 to look at

Moving Claim 3's denominator from the roads-and-alleys shortfall to the
whole-infrastructure shortfall is the right call on the captured wording,
and I have made it. It also makes Supported much harder to reach, because
the denominator is larger. I do not think that breaks check 8 — Supported
is reachable on the arithmetic, the required alternative reports the
narrower figure beside it, and Contradicted is a real and unwelcome answer

mirror image of the objection check 1 raised, and it was raised against
this brief in the other direction, so I would rather it were tested than
assumed.
