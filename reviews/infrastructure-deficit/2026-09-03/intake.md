## How the claims got here

Nobody chose them. The source is one Facebook thread on the 2026-08-26
Infrastructure Committee decision, captured whole by the founder on
2026-09-02 as 621 comments
(intake/captures/yegscoop-2026-08-26/comments.jsonl; the capture's own
README describes the fields and the pseudonym scheme). Three extractor
seats listed every factual claim in it, one strong seat merged the three
lists, a script checked that every quoted wording is one unbroken run of
the comment it cites, and two triage readers ruled on the resulting
questions. Fifteen distinct people in that thread argued this question;
they produced five claims, all on the same side.

Commenters carry stable pseudonyms of the form adjective, Edmonton
animal, initial. Office-holders keep their names. Every wording quoted
below has been re-checked in this session against the capture and is an
exact substring of the comment attributed to it.

## Which side of the argument the thread is on

All five registered claims are labelled `side: against` — against the
bike-lane spending. No commenter in this source was captured arguing the
other half of this question, that Edmonton is not letting the basics go.
The people who argued the pro-bike side of the thread argued different
questions: who pays for roads, how many people cycle, whether lanes ease
congestion. Those are separate register questions with their own
accounts.

That matters for the brief in one specific way, and it is written into
it: the counter-case here has to come from the record rather than from a
captured opponent, so every claim's ladder has to be able to reach
Contradicted on the City's own documents. A one-sided intake is not a
reason to test a one-sided proposition.

## What this record does not establish

- **No captured wording asserts deterioration over time.** The claims say
  the basics are bad now. "crumbling infrastructure" (284) is the closest
  thing to a trend assertion and it is not a registered claim. So the
  brief tests condition at a level, not a trend, and the trend enters as a
  required calculation reported without a verdict.
- **No captured wording names a budget line, a document or a figure on
  the basics side.** Nobody says how much road renewal was funded, what
  the deficit is, or where the $100 million came from. Every figure in
  this question therefore comes from the City's own documents, not from
  the source.
- **No captured wording asserts that the bike money was physically taken
  out of a road budget.** The claims put the two things side by side
  ("while claiming a huge infestructure deficit", "while whining about a
  deficit") and leave the reader to draw the link. The brief says how it
  reads that juxtaposition and tests the reading at the level the council
  record can answer.
- **Snow clearing is a separate registered question.** `snow-clearing`
  ("How does Edmonton clear snow from its streets and bike lanes?") is on
  the register with its own triage GO. Three of the ten wordings under
  claim 1 are about snow. They stay counted here and are not tested here.

## Context on 2026-09-03

- Council approved the $100 million on 2022-12-09 as part of the
  2023-2026 capital budget. It is capital profile CM-20-0330, "Active
  Transportation Implementation Acceleration - Approach 3", approved in
  thousands at 5,950 / 26,750 / 33,650 / 33,650 for 2023 to 2026
  (evidence/registry/YF-EV-0114.yaml, the adopted capital budget,
  Appendix A).
- On 2026-08-26 the Infrastructure Committee heard report IS03688, in
  which administration recommended re-evaluating 14 routes not yet under
  construction; the committee adopted no recommendation. That is the
  decision the captured thread is reacting to.
- On 2026-09-03 two published claims came off the findings board under
  methodology v1.19: `at-100m-vs-snow`, because it set four years of
  capital spending against one year of operating spending, and
  `at-100m-vs-roads`, because it depended on a roads-only category the
  adopted budget does not publish. Both dated notes say the same thing:
  those figures are the arithmetic under an argument the site has not
  checked, and this question is that argument.

## What was left out of the brief, and why

Recorded here so that the exclusions are on the record before the framing
check reads them, and so the story cannot quietly claim any of them later.

- **`rec-centre-money-diverted`** is not in the brief. Its target is a
  named facility, not roads, alleys or drainage, and settling it needs
  that facility's own project and budget history, which is a different
  body of evidence from the one this question turns on. It stays on the
  register under this question and is a candidate for a separate brief.
- **`drainage-damage-to-homeowners`** is not in the brief. As captured it
  asserts that drainage problems cause millions of dollars of damage,
  which no party to this argument disputes, so no verdict on it would
  surprise anyone on either side. The part of the drainage question that
  is contested here is whether bike-lane money and drainage money come
  out of the same budget, and that is a required calculation in the
  brief, reported without a verdict.
- **`new-streetlights-added-costs`** is not in the brief. As captured it
  asserts only that a cost was added, which is true of any new asset and
  is not in dispute. A contested version — whether the streetlight
  programme raised net cost against what it replaced — is not the wording
  anybody was captured saying, and under v1.15 a wording nobody said does
  not run.
- **Snow clearing and grass mowing**, both inside the wording of claim 1,
  are not tested. Snow is its own registered question. For grass mowing
  no City-published condition or service-level measure was identified in
  drafting, so a proposition about it would be a definition the record
  cannot meet, which is itself a framing defect.
- **Property taxes** are a separate registered question
  (`property-taxes`) and are not tested here, although two of this
  thread's commenters raise taxes in the same breath as the basics.
