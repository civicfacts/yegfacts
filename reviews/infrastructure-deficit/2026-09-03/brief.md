## Story

Fifteen accounts in one captured Facebook thread argued against the
bike-lane programme in five related ways. Eight of them named the
condition of roads, alleys or potholes. Two put the City's infrastructure
deficit beside the $100 million approval. The other five raised a
recreation centre, drainage damage to homeowners, streetlights, and snow
and grass. This brief tests the captured factual claims the public record
can carry. The intake establishes how often this argument was made inside
that thread, not how often it is made across Edmonton. Topics:
transportation, bike-lanes, city-finances.

Claims in this question, reviewed and reported separately:

- `infra-roads-condition`: whether Edmonton's roads are in poor condition
  on the City's own condition ratings.
- `infra-bike-money-renewal-eligible`: how much of the $100 million
  approved for bike lanes was money council could have spent on renewing
  roads and alleys instead.
- `infra-hundred-million-vs-shortfall`: whether $100 million equalled a
  serious share of the renewal shortfall the City itself publishes.

Each claim carries its own verdict, panel agreement and evidence-basis
label. A finding on one is not a finding on another. In particular, a
finding on the third is not a finding on the second: money can be big
enough to matter without having been available to spend, and available to
spend without being big enough to matter.

## Why this question, and why now (selection rationale)

- Prevalence: fifteen distinct accounts in one source, the largest account
  count of the five questions this source produced on how the bike money
  compares with anything else. Every one of them argues the same side,
  which the brief treats as a hazard and not as evidence (see "The
  one-sided intake", below). This is prevalence inside one thread. The
  brief does not claim it is the prevalence of the argument in Edmonton.
- Civic importance: the argument is made by residents and by a sitting
  councillor in the same captured thread, about a programme council
  approved and a committee revisited three days before the capture.
- Verifiability: the City rates the condition of its own assets on a
  published A-to-F scale and publishes the ratings; it publishes its
  adopted capital budget by profile with its own service labels and its
  own funding sources; it publishes its council minutes and its capital
  budget adjustments. Each claim below turns on one of those.
- Sequence: on 2026-09-03 two published claims comparing the $100 million
  with snow clearing and with roads came off the findings board, one
  because it set four years of capital money against one year of operating
  money and one because it depended on a roads-only category the adopted
  budget does not publish (methodology v1.19). Both dated notes say the
  figures were the arithmetic under an argument the site had not checked.
  This is that argument. It is not a re-run of either claim, and it does
  not restate, replace or repair their findings.

## Who asks this

A resident or a reporter would ask, in these words: "Are Edmonton's roads
actually in bad shape? And when council found $100 million for bike lanes,
could that money have gone on my street instead — and would it have been
enough to matter?"

The three claims are those three parts, in that order.

One thing the public record cannot do, and the brief says so rather than
letting a reader assume otherwise: it cannot tell any particular resident
whether their own street or their own alley is in poor condition. The
City publishes a citywide condition distribution, not a per-street
verdict. Claim 1 answers the citywide question, in the City's own Roads
asset class, and says that is what it is answering.

Nor can anyone show what council would have done with $100 million it
never approved for bike lanes. That counterfactual is not tested. What
the record can show is how much of the money council did approve was
money that could lawfully and financially have gone to road or alley
renewal instead. That is Claim 2, and it is the answerable half of the
opportunity-cost argument the holders are making.

## The basis, fixed before any figure

This section is fixed first and on purpose. The two claims withdrawn on
2026-09-03 failed on their basis, not on their arithmetic: one compared
four years of capital money with one year of operating money, and one
compared against a category the City does not publish. A basis chosen
after the numbers are in view is not a basis. Every rule below binds every
claim in this brief, and none of them may be changed after any figure is
seen.

**B1. Capital money and operating money are never compared with each
other.** They are different budgets, raised differently, approved
separately. The $100 million is capital: it is capital profile
CM-20-0330, "Active Transportation Implementation Acceleration - Approach
3", in the adopted 2023-2026 Capital Budget, at 5,950 / 26,750 / 33,650 /
33,650 in thousands for 2023 to 2026, total 100,000 (Appendix A of that
document). Renewing a road or an alley is capital. Filling a pothole,
mowing a berm and clearing snow are operating. So no ratio in this brief
puts the $100 million against an operating budget, and no verdict in this
brief turns on an operating figure. Operating figures appear only where
this brief names them as context, labelled as operating.

**B2. Both sides of every ratio cover the same years, and a ratio that
does not is not classified.** The period is the 2023-2026 capital budget
cycle, the four years that contain CM-20-0330. A verdict-bearing ratio may
be computed only where the numerator and the denominator both cover
2023-2026. Where the City publishes the relevant figure over a different
or an unstated span, the reviewer reports that figure with the span the
City states, reports the ratio computed on it, labels it as spanning
different periods, and does **not** classify it: it is context, and the
claim's own ladder routes to Not established if no same-period figure
exists. No reviewer may annualise, pro-rate or otherwise rescale a
published multi-year figure to make it match. A restated figure is our
arithmetic presented as the City's, and that is how a basis goes wrong.

**B3. Categories come from the City's own labels.** Where the City
publishes a category, the brief uses the City's category under the City's
name for it. Where the brief has to draw a boundary the City does not
publish, it draws it from a named field on a named document, lists the
membership, and requires reviewers to reproduce the membership item by
item and to report every item whose label differs from the brief's
reading. No category is described by a name the City does not use for it.
Where a City category mixes things this brief separates — a capital
profile with both a growth and a renewal share, say — the brief uses the
City's own published split of it and never apportions one itself.

**B4. Approved is not spent.** Both the $100 million and every renewal
figure in this brief are approved amounts unless a reviewer is explicitly
asked for an actual. Actuals are required as calculations and are labelled
as actuals wherever they appear. No actual is put on either side of a
classified ratio in this brief, because at the as-of date only three of
the four budget years have closed and a three-year actual against a
four-year requirement is the span mismatch B2 forbids.

**B5. Nominal Canadian dollars**, unless a reviewer reports a
constant-dollar restatement as a qualification and names the index.

**B6. Drainage is not assumed to be in the City's budget at all.** Whether
Edmonton's drainage assets are funded from the tax-supported capital
budget that funds bike lanes, or from utility rates under a bylaw, is a
fact the panel establishes and reports; it is not a fact this brief
asserts, and no claim's verdict turns on it. See required calculation 6.

**B7. Where the record cannot answer, the brief says so in the
proposition.** A proposition here is stated in the words a resident would
use and is answered at the level the named documents reach. Where those
differ, the difference is written into the claim's own section, not left
for the reader to discover.

## The one-sided intake

Every one of the fifteen accounts counted on this question argued the same
side. Nobody in the source was captured arguing that Edmonton is keeping
its roads and alleys up. The counter-case therefore has to come out of the
City's record rather than out of a captured opponent, and reviewers are
told plainly: search for evidence against each proposition with the same
diligence you search for evidence for it, and treat the absence of a
captured opponent as an artefact of one thread rather than as a signal
about the answer. Every ladder below can reach Contradicted, and every
ladder below can reach Supported, on published City documents. If one of

## Claims under review

### Claim 1, id: `infra-roads-condition`

**Normalized proposition:** Edmonton's roads are in poor condition.

**Why this reading, and why it is roads.** Eight of the ten accounts
behind the registered basic-services claim named roads, alleys or
potholes; the other two named only snow, or only grass and snow, and
nothing in this claim rests on them. None of the eight gave a figure; they
described what they see. The City rates the condition of its road assets
on a five-point scale and publishes the ratings, so the claim can be
answered on the holders' own subject in the City's own terms.

Alleys are not in this proposition and get no verdict. They are in the
holders' words — "back alleys that desperately need attention" — and they
are in required calculation 2, which makes the panel establish whether the
City publishes a condition rating for alleys or laneways as a class at
all, and report the distribution in full if it does. The reason they carry
no verdict is that no source identified in drafting publishes a computable
condition distribution for alleys, and a proposition the record cannot
meet predetermines its own answer. A renewal schedule is not a condition
rating and may not stand in for one. If the panel finds that the City does
rate alleys, the alley result is published beside this verdict, in full,
and the register gains an alley claim for a later run rather than this one
gaining a second verdict after the fact.

**"Edmonton's roads", fixed here.** The asset class the City's own most
recent condition report publishes under its own name for roads, taken at
the level the report publishes it and not reassembled. Reviewers name that
class exactly as the City names it, state where in the report it sits,
list every sub-class the report nests under it, and report each
sub-class's share as well as the class total. Excluded, under every
reading: bridges and other structures, sidewalks, streetlights, transit
and LRT assets, buildings, parks, and any asset the City does not own.
Reviewers list every rated class they excluded and why.

A reviewer may **not** construct a road category the City does not
publish — by adding sub-classes together into a set the report does not
present, by dropping a sub-class the City includes, or by carving driving
surfaces out of a class that also contains something else. If the City's
own road class contains things a reader would not call a road, that is
reported as a qualification under the City's label; it is not corrected
by hand. Building a roads category the City does not publish is what took
`at-100m-vs-roads` off this site's findings board on 2026-09-03.

**"In poor condition", fixed here.** Let *p* be the share of that asset
set the City's own rating puts at D (poor) or F (very poor).

*Denominator.* **Replacement value carries the verdict**, because that is
how the City publishes its condition distribution. Where the City also
publishes the distribution by length in kilometres or lane-kilometres,
reviewers report the classification on that denominator as the **required
alternative**, because expensive road types carry more weight by value
than by length and the two can differ. Count of rated units is used only
where the City publishes neither value nor length, and where it is used
the reviewer says so in `limitations`. A reviewer may not mix denominators
within the set, and may not compute a combined figure across classes the
City publishes on different denominators; where that is the only option,
report each class separately and route to the fourth band.

**Verdict ladder.**

- **Supported** if *p* is at least 25 per cent.
- **Partially supported** if *p* is at least 10 per cent and below 25 per
  cent: a substantial part of the network is rated poor or very poor, and
  the failing part is the generality — "the roads" as a whole are not.
- **Contradicted** if *p* is below 10 per cent.
- **Not established** if the City has published no D-or-F share for these
  classes on any of the three denominators by the as-of date, so *p*
  cannot be computed at all.

The three numeric bands cover every value of *p* from 0 to 100 per cent
with no overlap and no gap: below 10, from 10 to below 25, and 25 or
above. If nothing is rated D or F, *p* is 0 and the verdict is
Contradicted. If everything is, *p* is 100 and the verdict is Supported.
If the City rates none of these classes, the fourth band takes it, so the
ladder is never applied to an empty set.

*Alternative cutoffs, results required under both:* Supported at 20 per
cent or above; Partially supported from 7.5 per cent to below 20 per cent;
Contradicted below 7.5 per cent. These bands are likewise exhaustive and
disjoint over 0 to 100 per cent.

Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard. A quarter
of the network rated poor or very poor is where "the roads are a mess"
stops being one bad street and starts being the network; a tenth is where
it stops being isolated. Reviewers must additionally report whether the
City publishes a target or a service level for the share of these assets
in D or F condition, and if it does, report the classification against the
City's own target as a qualification, and say how that classification
compares with the classification under the cutoffs fixed here.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The D-and-F share for each rated road class separately, each with its
   denominator and its date.
2. The full A-to-F distribution for the set, as published.
3. The same shares from the previous published rating, and the direction
   of change. This is the nearest the record comes to a trend, and it is
   reported, not judged: no captured wording asserts one, so no verdict is
   given on one.
4. What the City's rating measures and what it does not — whether the
   rating is a surface condition assessment, how often each asset is
   re-rated, and what share of the set was rated in the most recent cycle
   rather than carried forward.

**What this claim does not test.** Any particular street; alleys, which
carry no verdict here; snow clearing; grass and berm mowing; sidewalks;
whether the condition is worse than in other cities; whether the City's
rating method is the right one; whether the condition is getting worse.
The story may not assert any of these.

### Claim 2, id: `infra-bike-money-renewal-eligible`

**Normalized proposition:** At least half of the $100 million council
approved for the bike-lane programme was money it could have spent on
renewing roads and alleys instead, at a time when the City was reporting
an unmet capital renewal requirement.

**Why this reading, and what it replaces.** The captured wordings put the
two things side by side — "the city claims they have a huge infestructure
deficit. . Yet spending 100 miiion dollars on bike lanes" — and leave the
link to the reader. The link a holder means is an opportunity cost: you
had the money and you could have fixed my street with it.

An earlier draft of this brief tested whether council had physically
removed money from a budgeted renewal profile and applied it to the
programme. Framing check 1 found that a bookkeeping test of that kind is
the weak form of this claim, and it is right: a holder could accept
Contradicted on it and go on making exactly the argument they were making,
because they never claimed a transfer occurred. The intake record says as
much. So the verdict now falls on the question a Contradicted answer would
actually settle — whether this was money that could have gone on roads —
and the transfer question becomes required calculation 3, reported in full
without a verdict.

**"Money it could have spent on renewing roads and alleys", fixed here.**
Every dollar of CM-20-0330's approved amount whose funding source, as the
adopted 2023-2026 Capital Budget and the council record identify it, could
have been applied to road or alley renewal instead. General municipal
capital funding — tax-supported debt, pay-as-you-go, and unrestricted
reserve draws — is eligible unless the record says otherwise. A grant or
other restricted source is eligible only if the granting body's own
published terms permit road or alley renewal; reviewers name and link
those terms (source 10) and quote the eligibility wording. Where a source
is identified but its terms cannot be determined from published material,
those dollars are undetermined, not eligible and not ineligible.

Let the $100.0 million approved amount divide into three parts, each in
millions of nominal dollars, summing to 100.0:

- *E*, **eligible**: dollars whose funding source is identified and whose
  terms permitted road or alley renewal use.
- *X*, **ineligible**: dollars whose funding source is identified and
  whose terms did not.
- *U*, **undetermined**: dollars whose funding source the record does not
  identify, or identifies without publishing anything from which
  eligibility can be determined.

**Verdict ladder**, applied in this order:

- **Supported** if *E* is $50.0 million or more. Established regardless of
  *U*, because *E* can only rise as more of the record is read.
- **Partially supported** if *E* is above zero and *E* plus *U* is below
  $50.0 million: some of the money could have gone to renewal, and the
  failing part is the claim's scale — it cannot reach half however the
  undetermined part resolves.
- **Contradicted** if *E* is zero and *U* is zero: every dollar's source
  is identified and none of it could have been spent on road or alley
  renewal.
- **Not established** in every remaining case — that is, where *E* is zero
  and *U* is above zero, or where *E* is above zero, below $50.0 million,
  and *E* plus *U* is $50.0 million or more. In those cases the record
  leaves the classification open in a way the undetermined part could
  change.

Every case falls in exactly one band, because the four conditions are
tested in order and are mutually exclusive. Walking the boundaries: *E* of
exactly $50.0 million is Supported; *E* plus *U* of exactly $50.0 million
is not below $50.0 million and so is Not established; *E* of $49.9 million
with *U* of zero is Partially supported; *E* of zero with *U* of zero and
*X* of $100.0 million is Contradicted; *E* of $100.0 million is Supported;
*U* of $100.0 million is Not established. The ladder is never applied to
an empty set, because CM-20-0330's approved amount is a fixed $100.0
million and the three parts always sum to it.

*Alternative cutoffs, results required under both:* Supported at *E* of
$25.0 million or more; Partially supported where *E* is above zero and *E*
plus *U* is below $25.0 million; Contradicted where *E* and *U* are both
zero; Not established in every remaining case. Exhaustive and disjoint on
the same argument.

Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard. Half the
programme is where "that money could have fixed the roads" describes the
programme rather than a corner of it.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The funding sources for CM-20-0330 as the adopted capital budget and
   the council record state them, dollar by dollar, with the document and
   page for each, and the eligibility wording quoted for every restricted
   source.
2. Every motion moved in the December 2022 deliberations to reduce,
   redirect or remove CM-20-0330, with its mover, its amount and its
   disposition, from the minutes. A sitting councillor states in the
   captured source that two such motions were brought and not supported;
   the minutes either show that or do not, and either way it is reported.
3. Whether the City was reporting an unmet capital renewal requirement
   over the 2023-2026 cycle at the time of approval and at the as-of date,
   with the figure and the City's own wording for it. This is the second
   half of the proposition and is reported as fact; it is established in
   required calculation 4 and is not itself classified here.
4. Every capital budget adjustment to the as-of date that changed
   CM-20-0330's approved amount, with its date, its amount and its stated
   source or destination, in both directions.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether council should have funded the
programme; whether the money would have been better spent on renewal;
whether council's priorities are the right ones; what council would in
fact have approved in the programme's absence; whether any transfer
occurred, which is a required calculation and not a verdict; the west-end
recreation centre, which is a registered claim under this question and is
not in this brief. The story may not assert any of these.

### Claim 3, id: `infra-hundred-million-vs-shortfall`

**Normalized proposition:** The $100 million approved for bike lanes
equalled a serious share of the infrastructure renewal shortfall Edmonton
itself reports.

**Why this reading, and why the whole-city denominator.** Two people put
the bike money next to the City's own claim of a deficit, and the force of
that juxtaposition is that the money is big enough to have mattered. That
is a magnitude claim, so the proposition carries the magnitude and a
threshold rather than being neutralised into "the City spent money while
having a deficit", which nobody disputes and which would tell a reader
nothing.

The captured wording is "a huge infestructure deficit", not a
roads-and-alleys one. An earlier draft made the narrower roads-and-alleys
shortfall the primary denominator and said in as many words that it did so
because it gave the holders' argument its best available footing. Framing
check 1 found that reasoning to be a leak — an opponent need not accept a
denominator chosen because it favours the claim — and it is right. The
denominator is now the figure the holders' own words invoke, the City's
whole-infrastructure renewal shortfall. Any roads-and-alleys shortfall the
City publishes is the **required alternative**, classified beside it, and
may not replace it.

The word is "equalled", not "would have covered". Whether the money could
in fact have been moved is Claim 2's question, not this one's.

**"The infrastructure renewal shortfall Edmonton itself reports", fixed
here.** *S* is the City's own published figure, in nominal dollars, for
the renewal it has not funded across its assets — the unfunded or unmet
capital renewal requirement, the renewal gap, or whatever the City's own
term for it is — **covering the 2023-2026 budget cycle**, from the most
recent City publication carrying such a figure by the as-of date. Sources
5, 4, 2 and the budget deliberation reports, in that order of likelihood:
the adopted 2023-2026 Capital Budget is the first place to look, because a
renewal requirement set against funded renewal for that cycle would be on
the same four years as the numerator. It is the City's figure on the
City's basis; no reviewer constructs one, apportions one, or rescales one.

Both figures are capital. Neither is compared with an operating budget.
Under B2, a shortfall the City publishes over a different or an unstated
span is reported with the City's span and its ratio, labelled as spanning
different periods, and is **not** classified; if no same-period figure
exists, the claim routes to Not established, which is a finding about what
the City publishes.

**Thresholds.** Let *r* = 100 divided by *S*, both in millions of nominal
dollars.

- **Supported** if *r* is 0.25 or more.
- **Partially supported** if *r* is at least 0.05 and below 0.25: the
  money is a real fraction of the shortfall, and the failing part is
  "serious share".
- **Contradicted** if *S* is zero or negative — the City reports no
  unfunded renewal, so there is none for the money to be a share of — or
  if *S* is above zero and *r* is below 0.05.
- **Not established** if the City has published no unfunded renewal figure
  covering 2023-2026 by the as-of date.

Every case falls in exactly one band. Where a figure exists and *S* is
above zero, *r* is above zero and the three numeric bands split the
positive reals at 0.05 and 0.25 with no overlap and no gap. *S* zero or
negative is named explicitly rather than left to divide by zero. Where no
same-period figure exists at all the fourth band takes it, so the ladder
is never applied to an empty set. The all-one-way cases resolve: an *S* at
or below $400 million gives *r* of 0.25 or more and Supported; an *S* in
the tens of billions gives *r* near zero and Contradicted.

*Alternative cutoffs, results required under both:* Supported at *r* of
0.15 or more; Partially supported from 0.03 to below 0.15; Contradicted at
*S* zero or negative, or *r* below 0.03. Exhaustive and disjoint on the
same argument.

Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard. A quarter
is where a sum is a serious share of a shortfall in ordinary speech; a
twentieth is where it stops being a rounding error. Reviewers report
whether the City itself publishes any benchmark for what share of its
renewal requirement it funds, and if so, report against it as a
qualification, and say how that compares with the classification under the
cutoffs fixed here.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. *r* against any roads-and-alleys renewal shortfall the City publishes
   covering 2023-2026 — the required alternative — with the figure, its
   date, its stated period and its stated basis, and its own
   classification. If the City publishes no such figure, say so plainly;
   that is a finding about the record.
2. *S* itself in dollars, with the exact wording the City uses for it and
   the period it covers, so the reader sees what the number is before
   seeing the ratio.
3. What one $100 million buys in renewal terms, if and only if the City
   publishes a unit cost — kilometres of road renewed, or neighbourhoods
   completed, per dollar — with the City's own figure and no
   extrapolation beyond it.
4. CM-20-0330's approved amount as a share of the whole adopted 2023-2026
   capital budget, and of the renewal funded in that budget.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether the shortfall is real, well
estimated or well reported; whether the City's deficit figure is
calculated correctly; whether the money could have been moved, which is
Claim 2; whether cancelling the programme was possible; whether roads are
a better use of the money. The story may not assert any of these.

## Stakes

Each verdict is written out below for what it would mean to a holder of
the claim and to an opponent of it.

**Claim 1, road condition.**

- **Supported.** A holder learns the City's own ratings confirm what they
  see, and anyone answering them has to answer the ratings rather than the
  tone. An opponent must concede that the complaint describes the network
  and cannot dismiss it as a few bad streets.
- **Partially supported.** A holder learns a substantial minority of the
  network is rated poor or very poor — a real problem, and not "the roads
  are a mess". An opponent must concede the problem is real at that scale
  while resisting the generality.
- **Contradicted.** A holder must give up the claim that the network is in
  poor condition on the City's own measure, and is left with a complaint
  about particular streets that this record cannot settle. An opponent
  gains the City's own ratings as an answer, and nothing more: the ratings
  do not say the roads are good enough.
- **Not established.** Neither side can appeal to a published citywide
  condition rating, because there is not one a resident can check. That is
  news about the City's reporting, and it costs the opponent the
  reassurance the ratings were supposed to supply.

**Claim 2, whether the money could have gone to roads.**

- **Supported.** A holder learns a material amount of the bike-lane
  funding was available for road or alley renewal and keeps the factual
  premise of the priority argument. An opponent must concede council faced
  a real allocative choice, while remaining free to defend the choice it
  made.
- **Partially supported.** A holder learns some of the funding was
  available for renewal but less than half of it. An opponent must concede
  limited fungibility but can reject the claim that most of the programme
  competed with road money.
- **Contradicted.** A holder must abandon the premise that this money
  could have fixed their street — it was money that existed for this
  purpose or for nothing. An opponent gains a direct answer to the
  opportunity-cost argument rather than the bookkeeping non-answer that a
  transfer test would have produced.
- **Not established.** Neither side can say the public record settles
  where this money could have gone. The holder lacks evidence for the
  opportunity-cost premise; the opponent lacks evidence the money was
  unavailable; and the finding is that Edmonton does not publish enough
  about how it funds a $100 million programme to say.

**Claim 3, whether $100 million was a serious share of the shortfall.**

- **Supported.** A holder learns the sum is large against the City's own
  reported shortfall, and the City cannot answer this argument by calling
  the money small. An opponent must argue the merits of the programme
  rather than its size.
- **Partially supported.** A holder learns the money is a real but partial
  fraction of the shortfall. An opponent can say the programme was never
  going to close the gap, without saying it was trivial.
- **Contradicted.** A holder must accept that every dollar of it would
  move the reported shortfall by less than a twentieth, which takes the
  force out of the juxtaposition and moves the deficit argument somewhere
  else entirely — an unwelcome finding, and the most useful one this
  question could produce for the people who raised it. An opponent gains a
  scale answer and no defence of the programme itself.
- **Not established.** Neither side can cite a City shortfall figure on
  the same four years as the approval. The holder loses the deficit half
  of the juxtaposition; the opponent is left with a City that claims a
  deficit it does not quantify over its own budget cycle, which is itself
  worth publishing.

**All three claims, definition sensitivity.** Each verdict applies under
the primary definition named in its own section. Where a required
alternative produces a different classification, the finding is
definition-sensitive, the story says so beside the verdict, and neither
side may cite the verdict word without the qualification.

## Scope

Geography: the City of Edmonton's own assets, budgets and council record.
No comparator city is required; all three claims are claims about
Edmonton's documents. A reviewer who uses a comparator must say what it
establishes and what it cannot.

Time: as fixed above.

Out of scope, and the story may not claim any of it: the condition of any
particular street or alley; alley condition as a verdict; snow clearing,
which is its own registered question; grass and berm mowing, for which no
City-published condition or service measure was identified; sidewalks;
property taxes and tax increases, which are their own registered question;
the west-end recreation centre; the streetlight programme's net cost;
drainage damage to homeowners; whether drainage is well run or well
funded; whether roads or alleys are getting worse; whether bike lanes are
worth building; whether council should have preferred road renewal;
whether council deliberately neglected anything; whether the provincial
legislation expected in fall 2026 should pass; whether any councillor
voted well; the motives of anyone who voted.

   at the as-of date.

   that difference as a shortfall, gap or unfunded requirement. If the

5. Actual expenditure on CM-20-0330 by year for 2023, 2024 and 2025 from
