# Review brief: Is Edmonton letting roads, alleys and drainage go while it funds bike lanes?

Status: DRAFTED 2026-09-03 by Stew under D-0019, framing check pending,
NOT frozen. Methodology: v1.19. Register question `infrastructure-deficit`
(intake/register.yaml), triage GO from both readers
(reviews/intake/yegscoop-2026-08-26/triage-stories.md). Intake record:
intake.md in this directory, which reviewers do not receive.

## Story

Fifteen people in one captured thread made the same argument in different
words: that Edmonton's roads and back alleys are falling apart, that the
City says it cannot afford to fix them, and that it found $100 million for
bike lanes anyway. This brief tests that argument. Topics: transportation,
bike-lanes, city-finances.

Claims in this question, reviewed and reported separately:

- `infra-roads-alleys-condition`: whether Edmonton's roads and back alleys
  are in poor condition on the City's own condition ratings.
- `infra-bike-money-from-renewal`: whether council paid for the
  $100 million bike-lane program by cutting money it had budgeted to renew
  roads and alleys.
- `infra-hundred-million-vs-shortfall`: whether $100 million is large
  enough to have covered a serious share of what the City says it is short
  of for keeping roads and alleys in repair.

Each claim carries its own verdict, panel agreement and evidence-basis
label. A finding on one is not a finding on another. In particular, a
finding on the third is not a finding on the second: money can be big
enough to matter without having been taken from anywhere.

## Why this question, and why now (selection rationale)

- Prevalence: fifteen distinct people in one source, the largest account
  count of the five questions this source produced on how the bike money
  compares with anything else. Every one of them argues the same side,
  which the brief treats as a hazard and not as evidence (see "The
  one-sided intake", below).
- Civic importance: the argument is the standing case against the program
  in Edmonton's 2026 argument about streets, and it is made by a sitting
  councillor as well as by residents.
- Verifiability: the City rates the condition of its own assets on a
  published A-to-F scale and publishes the ratings; it publishes its
  adopted capital budget by profile with its own service labels; it
  publishes its council minutes and its capital budget adjustments. Each
  claim below turns on one of those.
- Sequence: on 2026-09-03 two published claims comparing the $100 million
  with snow clearing and with roads came off the findings board, one
  because it set four years of capital money against one year of operating
  money and one because it depended on a roads-only category the adopted
  budget does not publish (methodology v1.19). Both dated notes say the
  figures were the arithmetic under an argument the site had not checked.
  This is that argument. It is not a re-run of either claim, and it does
  not restate, replace or repair their findings.

## Who asks this

A resident would ask, in these words: "My street is a mess and the alley
is worse. The City says it hasn't got the money. So how did it find
$100 million for bike lanes?"

That question has three parts, and the record answers each at a different
level. Is my street actually in bad shape, or does it just feel that way?
Did the bike money come out of the money for my street? And if council had
spent it on my street instead, would it have fixed anything? The three
claims are those three parts, in that order.

One part of the question the public record cannot answer at the level
people ask it, and the brief says so rather than pretending otherwise.
Nobody can show what council would have done with $100 million it never
approved for bike lanes. What the record can show is whether the approval
reduced any budgeted renewal, and whether the money could have gone there
under the terms it was raised on. The second claim tests the first of
those and reports the second beside it.

## The basis, fixed before any figure

This section is fixed first and on purpose. The two claims withdrawn on
2026-09-03 failed on their basis, not on their arithmetic: one compared
four years of capital money with one year of operating money, and one
compared against a category the City does not publish. A basis chosen
after the numbers are in view is not a basis. Every rule below binds every
claim in this brief, and none of them may be changed after any figure is
seen.

**B1. Capital money and operating money are never compared with each
other.** They are different budgets, raised differently, approved
separately. The $100 million is capital: it is capital profile
CM-20-0330, "Active Transportation Implementation Acceleration - Approach
3", in the adopted 2023-2026 Capital Budget, at 5,950 / 26,750 / 33,650 /
33,650 in thousands for 2023 to 2026, total 100,000 (Appendix A of that
document). Renewing a road or an alley is capital. Filling a pothole,
mowing a berm and clearing snow are operating. So no ratio in this brief
puts the $100 million against an operating budget, and no verdict in this
brief turns on an operating figure. Operating figures appear only where
this brief names them as context, labelled as operating.

**B2. Both sides of every ratio cover the same number of years.** The
period is the 2023-2026 capital budget cycle, the four years that contain
CM-20-0330. Where the City publishes a figure over a different span, the
reviewer reports the span the City states and the classification computed
on it, and states the mismatch. No reviewer may annualise, pro-rate or
otherwise rescale a published multi-year figure to make it match: a
restated figure is our arithmetic presented as the City's, and that is how
a basis goes wrong.

**B3. Categories come from the City's own labels.** Where the City
publishes a category, the brief uses the City's category under the City's
name for it. Where the brief has to draw a boundary the City does not
publish, it draws it from a named field on a named document, lists the
membership, and requires reviewers to reproduce the membership item by
item and to report every item whose label differs from the brief's
reading. No category is described by a name the City does not use for it.

**B4. Approved is not spent.** Both the $100 million and every renewal
figure in this brief are approved amounts unless a reviewer is explicitly
asked for an actual. Actuals are required as calculations and are labelled
as actuals wherever they appear.

**B5. Nominal Canadian dollars**, unless a reviewer reports a
constant-dollar restatement as a qualification and names the index.

**B6. Drainage is not assumed to be in the City's budget at all.** The
question names drainage. Whether Edmonton's drainage assets are funded
from the tax-supported budget that funds bike lanes, or from utility rates
under a bylaw, is a fact the panel establishes and reports; it is not a
fact this brief asserts, and no claim's verdict turns on it. See required
calculation 5.

**B7. Where the record cannot answer, the brief says so in the
proposition.** A proposition here is stated in the words a resident would
use and is answered at the level the named documents reach. Where those
differ, the difference is written into the claim's own section, not left
for the reader to discover.

## The one-sided intake

Every one of the fifteen people counted on this question argued the same
side. Nobody in the source was captured arguing that Edmonton is keeping
its roads and alleys up. The counter-case therefore has to come out of the
City's record rather than out of a captured opponent, and reviewers are
told plainly: search for evidence against each proposition with the same
diligence you search for evidence for it, and treat the absence of a
captured opponent as an artefact of one thread rather than as a signal
about the answer. Every ladder below can reach Contradicted on published
City documents. If one of them cannot, that is a defect in this brief and
should be reported as a MATERIAL FRAMING CONCERN.

## Claims under review

### Claim 1, id: `infra-roads-alleys-condition`

**Normalized proposition:** Edmonton's roads and back alleys are in poor
condition.

**Why this reading.** Ten people argued this and none of them gave a
figure; they described what they see. The City rates the condition of the
same assets on a five-point scale and publishes the ratings, so the claim
can be answered on the holders' own subject in the City's own terms. The
registered claim's wording also covers snow clearing and grass mowing;
those are not in this proposition, for the reasons in the scope section,
and no part of this verdict speaks to them.

**One assertion, one asset set.** Roads and alleys are treated as one set,
because the holders named them together and because a verdict has to come
out as one number. The per-class shares are required as qualifications, so
a reader can see whether one class carries the result.

**"Edmonton's roads and back alleys", fixed here.** The City's own rated
asset classes covering paved driving surfaces the City owns: roadways
(arterial, collector and local) and alleys or laneways, as the City's most
recent published condition rating names them. Excluded, under every
reading: bridges and other structures, sidewalks, streetlights, transit
and LRT assets, buildings, parks, and any asset the City does not own.
Reviewers list the classes they included with the City's own name for
each, and list every rated class they excluded and why.

**"In poor condition", fixed here.** Let *p* be the share of that asset
set the City's own rating puts at D (poor) or F (very poor).

*Denominator, in this priority order, and reviewers state which they
used:* (a) replacement value, if the City publishes the D and F shares by
replacement value; else (b) length, in kilometres or lane-kilometres; else
(c) count of rated units. A reviewer may not mix denominators between
classes; if the City publishes roadways by one and alleys by another,
report each class separately, use the priority order on the class that
covers the larger share of the set, and record the mismatch in
`limitations`.

**Verdict ladder.**

- **Supported** if *p* is at least 25 per cent.
- **Partially supported** if *p* is at least 10 per cent and below 25 per
  cent: a substantial part of the network is rated poor or very poor, and
  the failing part is the generality — "the roads" as a whole are not.
- **Contradicted** if *p* is below 10 per cent.
- **Not established** if the City has published no D-or-F share for these
  classes on any of the three denominators by the as-of date, so *p*
  cannot be computed at all.

The three numeric bands cover every value of *p* from 0 to 100 per cent
with no overlap and no gap: below 10, from 10 to below 25, and 25 or
above. If nothing is rated D or F, *p* is 0 and the verdict is
Contradicted. If everything is, *p* is 100 and the verdict is Supported.
If the City rates none of these classes, the fourth band takes it, so the
ladder is never applied to an empty set.

*Alternative cutoffs, results required under both:* Supported at 20 per
cent or above; Partially supported from 7.5 per cent to below 20 per cent;
Contradicted below 7.5 per cent. These bands are likewise exhaustive and
disjoint over 0 to 100 per cent.

Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard. A quarter
of the network rated poor or very poor is where "the roads are a mess"
stops being one bad street and starts being the network; a tenth is where
it stops being isolated. Reviewers must additionally report whether the
City publishes a target or a service level for the share of these assets
in D or F condition, and if it does, report the classification against the
City's own target as a qualification. If such a target exists it is a
better standard than either cutoff set, and the story will say so beside
the verdict.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The D-and-F share for roadways and for alleys separately, each with its
   denominator and its date.
2. The full A-to-F distribution for the set, as published.
3. The same shares from the previous published rating, and the direction
   of change. This is the nearest the record comes to "letting them go",
   and it is reported, not judged: no captured wording asserts a trend, so
   no verdict is given on one.
4. What the City's rating measures and what it does not — whether the
   rating is a surface condition assessment, how often each asset is
   re-rated, and what share of the set was rated in the most recent cycle
   rather than carried forward.

**What this claim does not test.** Snow clearing; grass and berm mowing;
sidewalks; whether the condition is worse than in other cities; whether
the City's rating method is the right one; whether any particular street
is in poor condition. The story may not assert any of these.

### Claim 2, id: `infra-bike-money-from-renewal`

**Normalized proposition:** Council paid for the $100 million bike-lane
programme by cutting money it had budgeted to renew roads and alleys.

**Why this reading, and what it is not.** The captured wordings put the
two things side by side — "the city claims they have a huge infestructure
deficit. . Yet spending 100 miiion dollars on bike lanes" — and leave the
link to the reader. The link a holder means is that the basics went short
because the bike lanes did not. Nobody can test what council would have
done with money it never approved, and a proposition about that would be a
definition the record cannot meet. What the council record does hold is
where the money came from and what, if anything, was reduced to provide
it. That is what this claim tests, and the claim says so in the words a
reader would use: was it taken out of the road money.

A Contradicted verdict here means the money was not taken out of budgeted
renewal. It does not mean the money could not have been spent on renewal
instead. That second question is answered in qualification 1, which
reports the funding instrument and whether its terms permitted renewal
use, and the story must carry that qualification wherever it carries this
verdict.

**"Money it had budgeted to renew roads and alleys", fixed here.** Every
capital profile in Appendix A of the adopted 2023-2026 Capital Budget
(source 5) whose profile sheet carries Service Category "Roads" or Service
Category "Neighbourhood Renewal", plus every profile in that document
classified under a drainage service if any exists. Reviewers list every
profile they treated as inside this set, with its identifier, its
2023-2026 amount and its Appendix A page, and report every profile whose
sheet label differs from this rule. Reviewers also report, as a fact about
the document, whether the adopted 2023-2026 Capital Budget contains any
drainage profile at all.

**"Paid for by cutting", fixed here with thresholds.** Two quantities,
both from the council record:

- *D*, in millions of nominal dollars: the total that the council record
  shows was removed from the approved amount of a profile inside the set
  above and applied to CM-20-0330, across the December 2022 budget
  deliberations and every capital budget adjustment adopted from
  2023-01-01 to the as-of date.
- *U*, in millions of nominal dollars: the part of CM-20-0330's
  $100 million approved amount for which the council record identifies no
  funding source.

**Verdict ladder.**

- **Supported** if *D* is $50.0 million or more.
- **Partially supported** if *D* is above zero and below $50.0 million:
  council did take budgeted renewal money for the programme, and the
  failing part is that most of the programme was not paid for that way.
- **Contradicted** if *D* is zero and *U* is $10.0 million or less.
- **Not established** if *D* is zero and *U* is above $10.0 million.

Every case falls in exactly one band. Where *D* is above zero the first
two bands split it at $50.0 million with no overlap and no gap. Where *D*
is zero the last two split *U* at $10.0 million, and *U* cannot exceed
$100 million, so no value is left unclassified. If the capital budget
contains no profile carrying either Service Category label, *D* is zero by
construction and the verdict falls to the *U* test, which is still
computable, so the ladder is not vacuous on an empty set. The all-one-way
cases resolve: *D* of $100 million is Supported, and *D* of zero with a
fully identified funding source is Contradicted.

*Alternative cutoffs, results required under both:* Supported at *D* of
$25.0 million or more; Partially supported above zero and below
$25.0 million; Contradicted at *D* zero with *U* of $20.0 million or less;
Not established at *D* zero with *U* above $20.0 million. Exhaustive and
disjoint on the same argument.

Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard. Half the
programme is where "paid for by cutting renewal" is a fair description of
how it was funded rather than a description of one amendment inside it.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The funding instrument for CM-20-0330 as the council record states it
   — tax-supported debt, pay-as-you-go, reserve draw, grant or other — and
   whether the terms of that instrument permitted the same money to be
   applied to road or alley renewal. This is the qualification that
   answers what the verdict cannot.
2. Every motion moved in the December 2022 deliberations to reduce,
   redirect or remove CM-20-0330, with its mover, its amount and its
   disposition, from the minutes. A sitting councillor states in the
   captured source that two such motions were brought and not supported;
   the minutes either show that or do not, and either way it is reported.
3. The total approved 2023-2026 amount of the renewal set defined above,
   and CM-20-0330 as a percentage of it. Both capital, both the same four
   years.
4. Every capital budget adjustment to the as-of date that changed
   CM-20-0330's approved amount, with its date, its amount and its stated
   source or destination.
5. Whether any adjustment moved money the other way, out of CM-20-0330
   and into a profile in the renewal set.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether council should have funded the
programme; whether the money would have been better spent on renewal;
whether council's priorities are the right ones; what council would have
approved in the programme's absence; the west-end recreation centre, which
is a registered claim under this question and is not in this brief. The
story may not assert any of these.

### Claim 3, id: `infra-hundred-million-vs-shortfall`

**Normalized proposition:** The $100 million approved for bike lanes would
have covered a serious share of what Edmonton says it is short of to keep
its roads and alleys in repair.

**Why this reading.** Two people put the bike money next to the City's own
claim of a deficit, and the force of that juxtaposition is that the money
is big enough to have mattered. That is a magnitude claim, so the
proposition carries the magnitude and a threshold rather than being
neutralised into "the City spent money while having a deficit", which
nobody disputes and which would tell a reader nothing.

**"What Edmonton says it is short of", fixed here.** *S* is the City's own
published unfunded or unmet renewal requirement for the roadway and alley
asset classes, over a period the City itself states, taken from the most
recent City publication carrying such a figure by the as-of date (sources
1, 2, 4 and the capital budget deliberation reports). It is the City's
figure on the City's basis; no reviewer constructs one.

*Why the roads-and-alleys figure and not the whole-city one.* The holders'
own words are about the infrastructure deficit as a whole, which is a
larger denominator and would make the claim harder to support. This brief
uses the narrower, roads-and-alleys shortfall as the primary denominator
because the question is about roads and alleys and because it gives the
holders' argument its best available footing. Choosing the denominator
that favours the claim is a choice both a supporter and an opponent can
accept before the result is known. The City's whole-asset funding gap is
the **required alternative** and is reported beside it with its own
classification.

Both figures are capital. Neither is compared with an operating budget.
Where the City states *S* over a span other than 2023-2026, the reviewer
reports the span as the City states it and the ratio computed on it, and
does not rescale (B2).

**Thresholds.** Let *r* = 100 divided by *S*, both in millions of nominal
dollars.

- **Supported** if *r* is 0.25 or more.
- **Partially supported** if *r* is at least 0.05 and below 0.25: the
  money is a real fraction of the shortfall, and the failing part is
  "serious share".
- **Contradicted** if *S* is zero or negative — the City reports no
  shortfall for these classes, so there is none for the money to cover —
  or if *S* is above zero and *r* is below 0.05.
- **Not established** if the City has published no unfunded renewal figure
  for these classes, over any stated period, by the as-of date.

Every case falls in exactly one band. Where a figure exists and *S* is
above zero, *r* is above zero and the three numeric bands split the
positive reals at 0.05 and 0.25 with no overlap and no gap. *S* zero or
negative is named explicitly rather than left to divide by zero. Where no
figure exists at all the fourth band takes it, so the ladder is never
applied to an empty set. The all-one-way cases resolve: an *S* at or below
$100 million gives *r* of 1 or more and Supported; an *S* in the tens of
billions gives *r* near zero and Contradicted.

*Alternative cutoffs, results required under both:* Supported at *r* of
0.15 or more; Partially supported from 0.03 to below 0.15; Contradicted at
*S* zero or negative, or *r* below 0.03. Exhaustive and disjoint on the
same argument.

Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard. A quarter
is where a sum is a serious share of a shortfall in ordinary speech; a
twentieth is where it stops being a rounding error. Reviewers report
whether the City itself publishes any benchmark for what share of its
renewal requirement it funds, and if so, report against it as a
qualification.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. *r* against the City's whole-asset funding gap, the required
   alternative, with the gap figure, its date, its stated period and its
   stated basis.
2. *S* itself in dollars, with the exact wording the City uses for it, so
   the reader sees what the number is before seeing the ratio.
3. What one $100 million buys in renewal terms, if and only if the City
   publishes a unit cost — kilometres of road renewed, or neighbourhoods
   completed, per dollar — with the City's own figure and no
   extrapolation beyond it.
4. CM-20-0330's approved amount as a share of the whole adopted 2023-2026
   capital budget, and of the renewal set defined in Claim 2.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether the shortfall is real, well
estimated or well reported; whether the City's deficit figure is
calculated correctly; whether cancelling the programme was possible;
whether roads are a better use of the money. The story may not assert any
of these.

## Stakes

**Claim 1.** Supported would mean the ten people who said the roads are a
mess were describing something the City's own ratings confirm, and anyone
answering them would have to answer the ratings rather than the tone.
Contradicted would mean the City's own ratings put the great majority of
its roads and alleys at fair or better, and the complaint is not a
description of the network. Partially supported would mean a substantial
minority of the network is rated poor or very poor, which is a real
problem and is not "the roads are a mess". Not established would mean
Edmonton does not publish a condition rating a resident can check against,
which would be news about the City's reporting. Each is reachable on
published ratings and each changes something for one side.

**Claim 2.** Supported would mean the bike programme was in fact funded by
cutting budgeted renewal, and the people making this argument were right
about the mechanism, not just about the mood. Contradicted would mean the
money came from somewhere that took nothing from renewal, and the
juxtaposition that fifteen people made does not describe what council did
— though the qualification would still tell them whether the money could
have gone to renewal instead, which is the argument they would fall back
on and are entitled to. Partially supported would mean some renewal money
did go to it and most of the programme did not come from there. Not
established would mean the council record does not show where $100 million
came from, which would be a finding about Edmonton's budget transparency
and not a finding about bike lanes. Each is reachable on minutes and
budget documents.

**Claim 3.** Supported would mean cancelling the programme would have
covered a serious share of the roads-and-alleys shortfall, and the City
cannot answer this argument by calling the sum small. Contradicted would
mean that even spending every dollar of it on roads and alleys would move
the shortfall by less than a twentieth, which takes the force out of the
juxtaposition the holders make and puts the deficit argument somewhere
else entirely — a finding that would be unwelcome to the people who raised
the claim and useful to them. Partially supported would mean the money is
a real but partial fraction. Not established would mean the City claims a
deficit it does not quantify for the assets people complain about, which
is itself worth publishing. Each is reachable and each would surprise
someone.

**All three claims, definition sensitivity.** Each verdict applies under
the primary definition named in its own section. Where a required
alternative produces a different classification, the finding is
definition-sensitive, the story says so beside the verdict, and neither
side may cite the verdict word without the qualification.

## Scope

Geography: the City of Edmonton's own assets, budgets and council record.
No comparator city is required; all three claims are claims about
Edmonton's documents. A reviewer who uses a comparator must say what it
establishes and what it cannot.

Time: as fixed above.

Out of scope, and the story may not claim any of it: snow clearing, which
is its own registered question; grass and berm mowing, for which no
City-published condition or service measure was identified; sidewalks;
property taxes and tax increases, which are their own registered question;
the west-end recreation centre; the streetlight programme's net cost;
drainage damage to homeowners; whether bike lanes are worth building;
whether the provincial legislation expected in fall 2026 should pass;
whether any councillor voted well; the motives of anyone who voted.

## Required calculations

Reported by every reviewer whatever the verdicts, so the story can answer
the reader rather than only classify three propositions.
