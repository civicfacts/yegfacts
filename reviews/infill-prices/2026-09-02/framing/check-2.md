## 2. Does the proposition test what the post asserts?

Finding.

The story says it will review the price relationship and affordability separately, but Claim 2 folds the Claim 1 price relationship into the affordability proposition:

> Replacement housing on teardown-infill lots costs more to buy or rent than the housing it replaced and does not meet a predeclared income-based affordability threshold.

This makes an affordability finding depend again on the relative-price claim. A replacement could be unaffordable under the declared income test while costing less than the demolished dwelling. It could also cost much more while passing the income test. The captured post uses the price gap as its affordability argument. It does not state this compound test.

Replace Claim 2 with two separately verdict-bearing propositions:

> **Claim 2a:** Among replacement dwellings on linked teardown-infill lots, more than 50 percent exceed the declared 30-percent shelter-cost-to-income threshold.

> **Claim 2b:** Among replacement dwellings on linked teardown-infill lots, more than 50 percent do not meet the City of Edmonton affordable-housing definition in force on the freeze date.

Keep the replacement-to-demolished price relationship in Claim 1. Report disagreements among the three claims in synthesis rather than joining them into one proposition.

## 3. Is it the strongest fair reading?

Finding.

The brief says both:

> Do not designate one as the claim's intended measure unless captured source material establishes that intent.

> The proposition above declares per-unit first sale price as its test measure for the sake of testability.

The second sentence does what the first forbids. Reporting other measures prominently does not give them equal force when only one determines the verdict. The captured wording compares one demolished house with one replacement house. It does not privilege a per-unit calculation for multi-unit projects.

Replace the verdict measure with:

> For projects in which one dwelling was demolished, compare each separately saleable replacement dwelling's first arm's-length sale price with the demolished dwelling's last arm's-length sale price. Report a dwelling-level share and, separately, a lot-level distribution of total replacement price. Report projects that demolished or replaced multiple dwellings as a separate series under a stated matching rule. No per-square-foot, per-bedroom, assessed-value, asking-price, aggregate-title, or rent measure substitutes for the sale-price proposition.

This preserves both parts of the concern. Added unit count cannot erase a high purchase price, and a high lot total cannot erase a lower per-dwelling price.

## 4. Operationalization and its alternatives

Finding.

The revision adequately handles alternatives for geography, five- versus ten-year windows, permit versus completion cohorts, nominal versus CPI adjustment, CPI versus the new-housing index, sale versus assessment series, down payment, and external comparators. These verdict-sensitive choices remain defective.

**Denominator**

The proposition speaks about “replacement dwellings,” while the threshold and verdict use “matched projects”:

> “Usually” means more than 50 percent of matched projects show the higher-price direction

A project with two replacement dwellings can have one above and one below the old-house price. Counting dwellings, lots, or projects can change the finding.

Replace with:

> Predeclare one verdict denominator. If it is replacement dwellings, count every separately saleable replacement dwelling and state how several dwellings from one lot are weighted. Report the lot-level share as a sensitivity. Define a project's direction before calculating any project-level share.

**Admissible price series**

Claim 1 specifies first-sale price, but the hierarchy substitutes assessed values, asking prices, aggregate sales, and survey rents:

> use the first available measure in this order

Those measures can change the finding and cannot be pooled into a first-sale proposition.

Replace with:

> Only matched arm's-length sale prices enter Claim 1's verdict series. Report assessed values, asking prices, aggregate-title sales, property-level rents, and survey rents as separate series. A case without the required sale prices is missing from the verdict series, not filled from another measure.

**Tenure changes**

Claim 2 says “costs more to buy or rent,” but no common calculation exists for an owner-occupied dwelling replaced by rental housing or a rental replaced by ownership. Purchase price and monthly rent are not comparable.

Replace with:

> Compare sale price with sale price and contract rent with prior contract rent. Report tenure-changing projects separately and do not assign them a higher-cost direction without a predeclared common shelter-cost calculation.

**Fire and safety demolitions**

The brief excludes them without tying that exclusion to the captured claim:

> Exclusion: pairs where the demolition was for fire or safety order

Including those cases is a reasonable alternative and can materially lower the old-house value.

Replace with:

> Include all otherwise eligible residential teardowns in the primary result. Report a sensitivity excluding fire- and safety-order demolitions, or justify before review why the claim's population excludes involuntary demolitions.

**Assessment timing**

The “assessment year nearest the demolition permit date” can be after demolition and may no longer value the standing dwelling. The last assessment before demolition is a reasonable alternative and can change the ratio.

Replace with:

> Use the last assessment that valued the dwelling while it was standing. Report the assessment nearest the permit date only as a sensitivity when it is a different year.

**Affordability population**

The brief classifies every dwelling but never defines when the matched set fails either test. All dwellings, more than half, the median dwelling, and a bedroom-weighted share can produce different findings.

Replace with:

> For each affordability proposition, “usually” means more than 50 percent of replacement dwellings fail the test. Report the numerator, denominator, tenure, and bedroom-count distribution. Also report the lot-level share and results by tenure and bedroom count.

**Income denominator and financing**

City median household income is one reasonable benchmark. Income by household type or income band is another and can change classification, especially when bedroom counts differ. A 30-year amortization is also a reasonable alternative to the fixed 25 years and can move cases across the threshold.

Replace with:

> Report the 30-percent test at city median household income and by available household-income band or household type, matched to bedroom count where the source permits. Report 25- and 30-year amortizations, or justify why the longer amortization is unavailable to the purchaser and dwelling class being tested.

**Missing utilities**

The brief permits classification with utilities excluded:

> report the test with utilities excluded and label it as excluded

That understates shelter cost and can change a threshold result.

Replace with:

> If a required utility input is unavailable, the complete shelter-cost test for that dwelling is Not established. A calculation excluding utilities may be shown as a lower-bound sensitivity but cannot classify the dwelling for the verdict.

**Verdict mapping**

The current rules overlap:

> **Partially supported:** exactly one conjunct is established and the other is identifiably not

> **Contradicted:** the replacement housing does not cost more on the matched set, or it meets the threshold under both tests.

If the price conjunct holds and both affordability tests pass, both rules apply. The same overlap occurs when affordability fails and the price conjunct does not hold. The disagreement rule also fails to say what happens when the price conjunct is contradicted or not established.

Replace the compound mapping by splitting Claim 2 as proposed under check 2. Each proposition can then use the methodology directly:

- Supported when the declared majority threshold is affirmatively established.
- Contradicted when the opposite majority is affirmatively established.
- Not established when the usable evidence establishes neither.
- Partially supported only when a stated proposition contains a meaningful part that the evidence establishes while the rest overreaches.

## 7. Scope traps

Finding.

The brief both excludes the causal question and tells reviewers to report causal evidence:

> Where causal evidence exists, report it separately, label it as causal, and state that it does not bear on the verdict.

> Out of scope: ... the causal question of what infill does to housing costs city-wide

Research that cannot bear on either verdict belongs in a separate story.

Replace the first sentence with:
