# Response to framing check 1

Brief: reviews/infill-prices/2026-09-02/brief.md, Revision 1 after framing
check 1. Every numbered finding in framing/check-1.md is answered below in
the checker's order.

## 1a. Four forms presented alike when only one was captured

Changed to the checker's structure:

> **Captured form:** One founder-supplied quotation, possibly a composite
> paraphrase. Its platform, author, URL and original context were not
> captured.
>
> **Prevalence:** Unknown from this intake. It contains one
> founder-supplied formulation.
>
> **Site-written formulations, not captured posts.** The sentences below
> were written by the site as editorial interpretations of how the claim
> might be put. They were not observed anywhere, they do not establish how
> the claim circulates, and reviewers must not treat them as source
> material:

All six site-written sentences, including the three that had been attached
to Claim 2, are listed there and nowhere else.

## 1b. Unsupported prevalence in the selection rationale

Changed to the checker's wording:

> - Prevalence: the founder supplied this as a recurring response in
>   Edmonton infill discussions. Because no source sample was captured,
>   this brief cannot establish how prevalent or representative the wording
>   is, and reviewers should not treat prevalence as established.

## 2a. Claim 1 expanded into a citywide "typically" claim with a mature-neighbourhood restriction

Changed to the checker's proposition, with the ratio reporting folded in as
the second half of the same claim rather than a separate id:

> **Normalized proposition:** Among Edmonton lots where an existing
> dwelling is demolished for infill, replacement dwellings usually have a
> higher first sale price per unit than the last arm's-length sale price of
> the demolished dwelling, measured in constant dollars. Report alongside
> the verdict the median replacement-to-demolished price ratio and the
> distribution of that ratio, so the size of the gap is reported and not
> only its direction.

"Typically" no longer appears in the brief. The mature-neighbourhood
restriction is no longer assumed:

> The mature-neighbourhood restriction is not assumed and is not part of
> the proposition. Report two sets and give both results: the city-wide set
> of linked demolition-and-replacement lots, and the mature-neighbourhood
> subset of that same set.

## 2b. Claim 2 was a causal claim with a descriptive operationalization

Changed to the checker's descriptive proposition:

> **Normalized proposition (descriptive):** Replacement housing on
> teardown-infill lots costs more to buy or rent than the housing it
> replaced and does not meet a predeclared income-based affordability
> threshold.

The causal version is named and excluded, with the reason:

> Whether infill "makes" housing more affordable is a causal proposition.
> Testing it would require a defined no-infill counterfactual, added
> supply, time lags and filtering over a specified period, and the price
> comparisons in this brief cannot establish any of that. No such causal
> claim was captured in the intake in a form that fixes a counterfactual or
> a period.

## 3. Per-unit price designated as "the figure the claim is about"

Changed. The old sentence is deleted. The replacement carries the checker's
list:

> Report per matched pair: per-unit price, total price per dwelling, total
> price for all replacement dwellings on the lot, bedroom count, tenure,
> and the total replacement-unit count. Do not designate one as the claim's
> intended measure unless captured source material establishes that intent.

One qualification, stated openly rather than quietly: the proposition the
checker supplied itself names per-unit first sale price, so the brief
declares that measure as the test measure for Claim 1 and says why:

> The proposition above declares per-unit first sale price as its test
> measure for the sake of testability, predeclared before review; that
> declaration is not a finding about what the speaker meant, and the other
> measures are reported alongside with equal prominence.

Replacement affordability and the market-wide causal effect are now
separate: the first is Claim 2, the second is excluded by name.

## 4a. Unit of analysis

Changed. All applicable measures are required (per unit, per dwelling, per
lot total, bedroom count, tenure, replacement-unit count), reported per
matched pair, with none substituting for another.

## 4b. Population

Changed. Claim 1 is restricted to linked demolition-and-replacement lots:

> Restrict this claim to linked demolition-and-replacement lots: a lot with
> a demolition permit for an existing dwelling and an identifiable
> replacement dwelling or dwellings built on the same lot. Infill that is
> not a teardown ... is not part of this claim's population; if it is
> examined at all, report it separately and label it.

## 4c. Geography

Changed. Both boundaries are reported (city-wide set and
mature-neighbourhood subset), the City instrument is identified by the
freeze date, and the boundary in force at the permit date is addressed:

> Mature neighbourhoods are delimited by the City's own instrument;
> identify the zoning bylaw or mature-neighbourhood policy instrument and
> the official consolidation in force on the freeze date recorded in
> run.yaml ... Where the applicable boundary differed at the date a permit
> was issued, say so and report which boundary was used.

## 4d. "Typically" is not a prevalence threshold

Changed to the checker's threshold:

> "Usually" means more than 50 percent of matched projects show the
> higher-price direction stated in the proposition. Report that share with
> its numerator and denominator. Report separately the median ratio, the
> full distribution (at minimum the quartiles and the range), the count,
> and the number and character of unmatched or excluded cases. A median
> ratio is not by itself evidence that most projects follow the pattern,
> and neither figure substitutes for the other.

## 4e. Denominator and matching left open

Changed. A predeclared block fixes frame, match key, inclusion, exclusion,
completion lag and missing data, including:

> Missing data: report the count and share of the frame lost at each step
> (no match, no price, no old-house value) and describe how the lost cases
> differ from the retained cases on any observable attribute.

## 4f. Old-house value series

Changed. Two series are required, separately, and selection after the fact
is forbidden:

> 1. the last arm's-length sale price of the demolished dwelling before
>    demolition, with its date;
> 2. the contemporaneous City assessed total value for the assessment year
>    nearest the demolition permit date, with its year.
>
> ... Do not select whichever series is available after seeing results;
> report which pairs have one series, which have both, and the result under
> each.

## 4g. Timing

Changed, with the index named:

> Report every comparison in nominal dollars and in constant dollars. For
> the constant-dollar comparison, deflate both sides to the most recent
> complete calendar year in the window using the Statistics Canada Consumer
> Price Index, all-items, Edmonton census metropolitan area, and state the
> base year. Report as a declared sensitivity the same comparison adjusted
> with the Statistics Canada New Housing Price Index for the Edmonton
> census metropolitan area, labelled as a market-index adjustment ...

## 4h. New-housing price measure

Changed. A hierarchy is predeclared per tenure (freehold single, freehold
multi-unit on one title, condominium, rental), reviewers record which level
each case fell to, unmatched cases are reported, and measures are never
mixed within one figure.

## 4i. Affordability measures were relative-price only

Changed. Both income-based tests are required and reported separately: the
30 percent of before-tax median household income shelter-cost test, and the
City of Edmonton's own affordable-housing definition in force on the freeze
date recorded in run.yaml. The assumptions the checker listed are fixed in
the brief rather than left to reviewers: income source (Statistics Canada
census median total before-tax household income for the City of Edmonton,
plus any later Edmonton CMA estimate), down payment (20 percent, with the
regulatory minimum as a declared sensitivity), rate (Bank of Canada
five-year conventional mortgage rate for the month of the price),
amortization (25 years), property taxes, and utilities. The brief also
states what counts as shelter cost for ownership and for rental.

## 4j. Comparators combined

Changed. Each comparator is listed separately with the instruction not to
merge them, and the suburban comparator carries the checker's caution
against treating it as the relevant alternative.

## 4k. Price per square foot or bedroom

Changed:

> Price per square foot and price per bedroom are supplementary measures.
> Report them where the record supports them, never as substitutes for
> total purchase price or rent.

## 4l. As-of date and window

Changed. A primary cohort and one declared sensitivity are fixed before
review:

> Primary cohort: demolition permits issued in the ten complete calendar
> years ending with the most recent complete calendar year before the
> freeze date recorded in run.yaml, matched to replacements recorded up to
> that freeze date. Declared sensitivity: the five complete calendar years
> ending with the same year.

The completion-cohort alternative is also required where the data supports
it, which addresses the construction-lag point.

## 4m. Transferred evidence

Changed to the checker's position:

> Another city's evidence cannot answer these Edmonton-specific
> propositions ... Edmonton-specific missing evidence stays recorded as
> missing. Do not substitute comparator evidence for an Edmonton figure the
> record does not contain.

## 4n. No verdict rule for Claim 2

Changed. The brief now predeclares the mapping from the two conjuncts to
the four verdict words (Supported, Partially supported, Contradicted, Not
established) and closes the subset loophole the checker named:

> A favourable result on a supplementary comparator (price per square foot,
> price per bedroom, or a comparison with new construction elsewhere in the
> region) is not the "meaningful part" that justifies Partially supported.
> The verdict rests on the two conjuncts above.

## 5. Leaked expected findings (five quoted sentences)

All five are replaced with the checker's neutral wording:

- "a modest older house bought and demolished, something much more
  expensive built on the lot" becomes "The circulating claim describes an
  older house being demolished and replaced by housing alleged to be much
  more expensive."
- "since a teardown purchase is largely a purchase of the lot" becomes
  "Where available, report total assessed value, land value and improvement
  value separately."
- "since that is the alternative a buyer is choosing between" becomes
  "Report regional new-construction comparisons as a separate comparator.
  Do not assume that it represents the relevant alternative for every
  buyer."
- "A single teardown replaced by one expensive house neither supports nor
  contradicts a proposition about infill in general" becomes "Report what
  individual cases establish, label them as cases, and do not infer
  population frequency from them."
- "Search for redevelopments that do not fit the pattern as diligently as
  for those that do" becomes "Apply the same documented sampling and
  matching rules to every eligible redevelopment, and report the complete
  resulting distribution."

## 6. Checkability

Changed. Claim 1's population, matching rules, price measures and frequency
threshold are now fixed, as section 4 requires. Claim 2 is replaced with
the descriptive proposition and two fixed income-based thresholds, and the
causal proposition is named as not tested. The brief also states that the
quoted $350,000 and $1,000,000 figures are not cutoffs and are not
themselves checkable without an address or source:

> The two round numbers in the captured example are not cutoffs and are not
> the test.

## 7a. Scope trap: tenant displacement excluded

Changed. Displacement and prior tenure are back in scope as evidence:

> Where the record supports it, report for the demolished dwelling: whether
> it was owner occupied or rented at the time of the demolition permit, the
> number of dwelling units it contained, the rent charged, and any recorded
> notice to tenants or municipal tracking of tenant displacement.

## 7b. Scope trap: causal dependencies ruled away

Changed by narrowing the proposition, which is the option the checker
offered. Claim 2 is descriptive, and added supply, time lags, filtering and
the no-infill counterfactual are named as the things a causal claim would
require and as the reason the causal claim is not tested here. The causal
question is listed under Out of scope by name, so it is excluded openly
rather than by relabelling.

## 7c. Scope trap: mature-neighbourhood restriction not established

Changed. See 2a and 4c: the restriction is dropped from the proposition,
the city-wide set is the primary set, the mature-neighbourhood subset is
reported alongside, and the regional new-construction comparison is a
labelled separate comparator rather than part of the teardown proposition.

## Findings kept as drafted

None. One finding, 3, is met with a qualification stated in the entry
above: per-unit first sale price remains the declared test measure for
Claim 1 because the checker's own replacement proposition names it, but the
brief says plainly that this is a predeclaration for testability and not a
finding about the speaker's intent, and it requires every other measure to
be reported alongside.
