### 4. Operationalization and its alternatives

Finding.

- **Unit of analysis.** The brief chooses the lot, then privileges per-unit price. Reasonable alternatives are the dwelling, bedroom, household accommodated, or entire project. Multi-unit redevelopment can look less expensive per unit but more expensive per lot, or add capacity despite higher unit prices. Require all applicable measures.

- **Population.** "Infill redevelopment" includes more than teardowns, while the captured claim concerns demolition and replacement. Restrict Claim 1 to linked demolition-and-replacement lots. Test broader infill separately.

- **Geography.** The brief uses the City's mature-neighbourhood definition. Alternatives include all established neighbourhoods, all teardown permits citywide, or boundaries in force when each permit was issued. These choices can change the denominator. State why the selected geography represents the post or report more than one boundary.

- **"Typically."** "A central tendency" is not a prevalence threshold. A median price ratio does not show that most projects follow the pattern. Define "usually" as more than 50 percent of matched projects and separately report the median ratio, distribution, and unmatched cases.

- **Denominator and matching.** "An identified set" leaves sample construction open. All demolition permits, only permits linked to completed replacements, and only properties with recorded sales produce different samples. Predeclare inclusion, exclusion, matching, completion-lag, and missing-data rules.

- **Old-house value.** Assessed value, last sale price, demolition-year estimate, land value, and improvement value answer different questions. Require separate results for arm's-length sale price and contemporaneous assessed total value where available. Do not let reviewers select whichever series is available after seeing results.

- **Timing.** The old dwelling's last sale and the replacement's first sale may be years apart. Nominal values, inflation-adjusted values, and market-index-adjusted values can change the gap. Require nominal and constant-dollar comparisons, with dates and index specified.

- **New-housing price.** Asking price, first sale, assessed value, aggregate duplex sale, condominium-unit sale, and rent are not interchangeable. Predeclare a hierarchy for each tenure and report unmatched cases.

- **Affordability.** The five listed measures are relative-price measures, not standard income-based affordability measures. Add shelter-cost-to-income measures, with a declared household income, down payment, mortgage rate, amortization, taxes, utilities, and the applicable threshold. Reasonable thresholds include 30 percent of before-tax household income and locally defined affordable-housing standards. Report both if they classify units differently.

- **Comparators.** Same-neighbourhood housing, existing citywide housing, new suburban housing, and a no-infill counterfactual answer different questions. The claim does not establish that new suburban construction is the buyer's alternative. Label each comparator and do not combine them into one finding.

- **Price per square foot or bedroom.** These measures can make a high total price appear comparatively low. Report them as supplementary measures, never substitutes for total purchase price or rent.

- **As-of date and window.** The brief selects 2026-09-02, the most recent complete year, at least five years, and also "the longest period the data supports." Alternatives include permit cohorts, completion cohorts, a fixed five-year window, and the period since a zoning change. Construction lags can reverse apparent trends. Fix the primary cohort and window before review, then require sensitivity results for another reasonable window.

- **Transferred evidence.** Another city's evidence cannot answer an Edmonton-specific proposition. It may supply context or test a mechanism, but Edmonton-specific missing evidence must remain missing.

- **Verdict rule.** Claim 2 supplies several measures that can disagree but no rule connecting them to the reviewer vocabulary. Predeclare which proposition each measure tests. Do not let a reviewer treat any favourable subset as a "meaningful part" for Partially supported.
