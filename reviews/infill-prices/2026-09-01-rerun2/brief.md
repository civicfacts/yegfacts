## Story

The circulating claim describes an older house being demolished and
replaced by housing alleged to be much more expensive. Two separate things
are being asserted. One is a price relationship on lots where a dwelling
was demolished and replaced. The other is whether that replacement housing
is affordable on an income-based test. This story separates the two and
tests each on its own terms. Topic: housing-development.

Claims in this story, reviewed and reported separately:

- `ip-teardown-price-gap`: the price relationship on linked
  demolition-and-replacement lots.
- `ip-infill-affordable`: whether replacement housing on those lots fails
  the predeclared 30 percent shelter-cost-to-income test.

Each claim carries its own finding, panel agreement and evidence-basis
label. Reviewer confidence appears only beside the reviewer who assigned
it. The price relationship stays in Claim 1 and is not part of the
affordability proposition. Where the two findings disagree, report the
disagreement in synthesis; do not join them into one proposition.

## Why this story (selection rationale)

- Prevalence: the founder supplied one possibly composite formulation from
  one Edmonton infill thread. No sample of other posts was captured, so
  this brief cannot establish the claim's prevalence or whether this
  wording represents how it circulates.
- Civic importance: mature-neighbourhood infill rules and the zoning bylaw
  renewal are live council business, and affordability is argued over on
  several sides.
- Verifiability: property assessments, demolition and building permits,
  market association reports and CMHC series are published, each with known
  limits, so the answer turns on being explicit about which measure is used
  and what it can establish.

## Unit of analysis, fixed for every claim

The unit of analysis in this brief is the replacement dwelling unit, counted
once. A replacement dwelling unit is each self-contained dwelling unit in the
replacement housing on the lot, counted once, whether it is separately titled
or held with other dwelling units on one title. A single-title multi-unit
building, for example a purpose-built rental fourplex, is four replacement
dwelling units and not one. Both propositions are about the price and the
affordability of the housing people live in, so the dwelling unit is the unit
they are stated in and the unit they are counted in.

Claim 1's verdict denominator is every replacement dwelling unit so defined.
Claim 2's verdict denominator is the separately titled replacement dwellings
among them, each counted once, for the reason fixed in Claim 2's
operationalization below; the dwelling units it excludes are counted and
reported beside its verdict.

**How a replacement dwelling unit is valued, fixed here and used by both
claims.**

1. **A separately titled dwelling**, that is, the only dwelling unit on its
   title. Its value is its own total assessed value, or its own sale price
   where a sale series is in use. This is the "total value per dwelling"
   measure, and in this brief that language applies to separately titled
   dwellings and to no other case.
2. **A dwelling unit inside a single-title multi-unit building.** Its value
   is the building-average assessed value per dwelling unit, that is, the
   building's total assessed value divided by the number of dwelling units
   the building contains. The building average is the rule for single-title
   buildings, and the undivided building total is never compared with the
   demolished dwelling. For a single-title multi-unit building, this
   calculation assigns the same building-average value to every dwelling
   unit. It does not estimate the distinct market value of any particular
   unit. A dwelling unit in such a building is excluded from Claim 2's
   verdict denominator and is reported as a separate count, under the rule
   fixed in Claim 2's operationalization below.

For every replacement dwelling unit, state which of these two cases it falls
in, the number of dwelling units on its title, and, where a division was
made, the building total that was divided.

The demolished dwelling's value is not affected by this rule. It stays that
dwelling's total assessed value on the roll named below, undivided.

The matched lot pair remains the structure that links the evidence: one lot
on which one existing dwelling was demolished, paired with the replacement
dwelling or dwellings built on that same lot. Every replacement dwelling
unit in a verdict denominator comes from a matched lot pair, and every
figure states the lot it belongs to, so that clustering by lot can be seen.

Two lot-level rules are reported as named sensitivities and never carry a
verdict:

1. **The "every replacement" rule:** the share of matched lot pairs on
   which every replacement dwelling unit falls in the claimed direction.
2. **The "at least one replacement" rule:** the share of matched lot pairs
   on which at least one replacement dwelling unit falls in the claimed
   direction.

Report both lot-level shares alongside every unit-level verdict share, each
with its numerator and denominator, and report how many replacement dwelling
units stood on each lot and how they were distributed across titles. Neither
lot rule may displace the unit-level result: a rule under which one
lower-priced dwelling unit removes the other replacement dwelling units on
that lot from the verdict does not test the proposition as written.

Lots on which more than one dwelling was demolished do not enter a verdict
denominator. Report them as a separate series, stating for each the number
of dwellings demolished, their values, the replacement dwellings and their
prices, and state how many such lots the frame contained.

These rules are fixed before any share is calculated and may not be changed
after the results are seen.

## Claims under review

### Claim 1, id: ip-teardown-price-gap

**Normalized proposition:** Among replacement dwelling units on Edmonton
lots where one existing dwelling was demolished for infill, more than 50
percent have a higher assessed value on their first full assessment roll
after completion than the demolished dwelling's total assessed value on the
assessment roll in force in the year before demolition, where the
replacement dwelling unit's assessed value is its own total assessed value
when it is a separately titled dwelling and the building-average assessed
value per dwelling unit, the building's total assessed value divided by its
number of dwelling units, when it sits in a single-title multi-unit
building, both sides taken in the City's market-value assessment basis and
compared in constant dollars. Count every replacement dwelling unit once.
Every otherwise eligible replacement dwelling unit enters this comparison
regardless of the tenure of the demolished or the replacement housing.
Report lot-level shares under the "every replacement" and "at least one
replacement" rules as declared sensitivities. Report alongside the
verdict the median replacement-to-demolished assessed-value ratio and the
distribution of that ratio, so the size of the gap is reported and not only
its direction.

The alleged $350,000 to $1,000,000 example cannot be verified as a specific
case, because its source and its property were not captured. It is context
only and it receives no verdict, and the findings on the claims in this
brief must not be presented as verification of it. The two round numbers
are not cutoffs and are not the test. Do not treat $350,000 and $1,000,000
as thresholds; report the actual distributions on both sides of the
comparison.

**Verdict measure, fixed here.** Compare the demolished dwelling's total
assessed value with each replacement dwelling unit's assessed value, taken
on the two rules fixed in the unit-of-analysis section, and report for every
matched lot pair which rule supplied each figure:

1. **A separately titled replacement dwelling.** Its total value per
   dwelling, that is, its whole total assessed value on its first full
   assessment roll after completion, undivided.
2. **A replacement dwelling unit inside a single-title multi-unit
   building.** The building-average assessed value per dwelling unit: the
   building's total assessed value on its first full assessment roll after
   completion divided by the number of dwelling units the building
   contains. The building average is the rule for single-title buildings.
   This calculation assigns the same building-average value to every
   dwelling unit in that building. It does not estimate the distinct market
   value of any particular unit.

Claim 1 is Supported only when the majority threshold below is met on
replacement dwelling unit values taken on those two rules, each unit counted
once. The value of the dwelling unit a household occupies is the closest
predeclared measure to the quotation's reference to the price of a
replacement house that values both sides of the pair at comparable moments,
the demolished dwelling in the year before it came down and the replacement
in its first full year standing, so that the comparison is not also carrying
the demolished dwelling's holding-period appreciation. It is also the
measure that compares like with like: an undivided multi-unit building total
set against one demolished house measures how much housing was built as much
as what that housing costs. This is an operational choice, not a verified
statement of the original speaker's intent.

Report alongside the verdict, for every matched lot pair and never as a
verdict measure, the undivided total assessed value of each replacement
title, the number of dwelling units on that title, and the total assessed
value of all replacement housing on the lot, each with its own share, median
ratio and distribution where a share is reported. State in the
interpretation notes whenever an undivided title total points in a different
direction from the building-average values per dwelling unit it contains.
Neither an undivided title total nor a lot total carries the verdict: an
added unit count does not erase a high replacement dwelling unit value, and
a high lot total does not erase a lower replacement dwelling unit value.

No per-square-foot, per-bedroom, sale-price, asking-price, undivided
single-title building total or rent measure substitutes for the
assessed-value comparison in Claim 1's verdict.

**Population.** Restrict this claim to linked demolition-and-replacement
lots: a lot with a demolition permit for an existing dwelling and an
identifiable replacement dwelling or dwellings built on the same lot.
Infill that is not a teardown (for example redevelopment of a vacant or
non-residential parcel) is not part of this claim's population; if it is
examined at all, report it separately and label it.

**Geography.** The mature-neighbourhood restriction is not assumed and is
not part of the proposition. The city-wide set of linked
demolition-and-replacement lots is the primary set. Report two sets and give
both results: that city-wide primary set, and the mature-neighbourhood

definition and state the consolidation date and the version reviewed.

No mature-neighbourhood instrument is in force on the freeze date. The

The City Plan's "redeveloping area" is a different boundary covering a

numerator and denominator, and never substitute it for the frozen historical

**The majority threshold.** Claim 1 asks whether more than 50 percent of
replacement dwelling units show the higher-value direction on the

separately titled dwelling and the building-average assessed value per
dwelling unit for a dwelling unit in a single-title multi-unit building,

**Sampling, matching and missing data, predeclared.** Fix these rules
before looking at results and report any departure from them:

- Frame: all residential demolition permits issued in the window below, on
  land within the present-day boundary of Edmonton.
- Match: a demolition permit is matched to a replacement when a building

  address after the demolition permit date. State the matching key used

- Inclusion: matched pairs where both a demolished-dwelling assessed value

- Missing data: report the count and share of the frame lost at each step

**Old-house value, predeclared.** Report two separate series and never
average them:

1. the City assessed total value of the demolished dwelling taken from the

2. the last arm's-length sale price of the demolished dwelling before

Where the assessment separates land from improvement, report total assessed

named above, report that year's figure as a labelled sensitivity. Do not
select whichever series is available after seeing results; report which
pairs have one series, which have both, and the result under each.

**Replacement assessed value, predeclared.** A separately titled
replacement dwelling's assessed value is its total assessed value on its
first full assessment roll after completion. A replacement dwelling unit
inside a single-title multi-unit building takes as its value the
building-average assessed value per dwelling unit: that building's total
assessed value on its first full assessment roll after completion divided by
the number of dwelling units the building contains, with the building total
and the unit count both stated. That figure assigns the same
building-average value to every dwelling unit in the building and does not
estimate the distinct market value of any particular unit. The assessment year
is stated in either case. This is the verdict series. Both assessment years,
the demolished dwelling's and the replacement's, are stated for every
pair.

**Admissible verdict series, predeclared.** Only matched assessed values
enter Claim 1's verdict series: the demolished dwelling's total assessed
value on the assessment roll in force in the year before demolition, and
each replacement dwelling unit's assessed value on the first full assessment
roll after completion, which is that dwelling's total assessed value where
it is separately titled and the building-average assessed value per dwelling
unit, the building's total assessed value divided by its number of dwelling
units, where the unit sits in a single-title multi-unit building. An
undivided single-title building total is not an admissible verdict figure;
it is reported alongside. Tenure does not affect admissibility: every
otherwise eligible replacement dwelling unit enters this series whatever the

dollars with the index and base year named under Timing below. A matched lot
pair that lacks either of those assessed values, or a single-title building

**The sale-to-sale sensitivity, predeclared.** The comparison of the

single-title multi-unit building has no sale price of its own; such units do
not enter this sensitivity, their count is reported, and the sale of the

Report it with its own share, numerator, denominator, median ratio and

Claim 2's threshold test is not changed by any of this. Claim 2 is a

Report each of the following as its own labelled series, never pooled into

For every case, record which series supplied each figure and how many cases
each series covered. Asking price, first sale price, assessed value,

**Tenure changes, predeclared.** Claim 1 compares assessed value with
assessed value for every otherwise eligible replacement dwelling unit,

### Claim 2, id: ip-infill-affordable

**Normalized proposition (descriptive):** Among separately titled
replacement dwellings in the matched set, more than 50 percent would require
shelter costs equal to or greater than 30 percent of before-tax income for a
hypothetical household earning the declared City median income that
purchased the dwelling at its first arm's-length sale price after
completion, or at its total assessed value on its first full assessment roll
after completion where no sale is recorded, under the 25-year amortization
and the other primary financing assumptions below.

This is a model of purchase affordability for the median household. It is
not the actual cost borne by the dwelling's occupant, and no result under it
says what any particular household paid. Tenure is not used anywhere in this
test, because no published source establishes tenure at the dwelling level
for the declared cohort. A separately titled replacement dwelling therefore
enters the test whether it is owner occupied, rented or unlet, and the test
asks only what it would cost the modelled household to buy it.

Dwelling units in single-title multi-unit buildings are excluded from Claim
2's verdict denominator. They are reported as a separate count, with the
building-average assessed value per dwelling unit for each, and the report
states that the affordability of renting one of those units is not tested
here, because CMHC average-rent cells do not state whether the rent includes
utilities and no published source gives contract rents for the declared
cohort. That is a limitation of the record, not a finding about those units.
Reviewers still return one Claim 2 verdict, on the separately titled
replacement dwellings alone.

The household is a modelled household, not the household that bought or
rented the dwelling. The standard 30 percent measure compares one
household's shelter costs with that household's own income; this brief
cannot do that, because the record does not carry purchaser incomes, so the
proposition says what it tests. The 30-year amortization, other household
incomes and other household types are named sensitivities, reported with
their own results and labelled, and none of them carries the verdict.

The City of Edmonton's own affordable-housing definition is not tested in
this story. That definition concerns housing receiving government financial
assistance or operating under a government agreement, and the City's
guidebook says it does not apply to market home ownership, so it is not a
second reading of a captured objection to market replacement prices.
Qualification as City-defined affordable housing needs its own captured
source and its own brief. Do not classify replacement dwellings against it
here.

### Operationalization for Claim 2

**The price relationship is not part of the affordability claim.** A
replacement dwelling can exceed an affordability threshold while costing
less than the dwelling it replaced, and it can cost far more than the
dwelling it replaced while passing the threshold. The comparison with what
was demolished is Claim 1, and it is verdict-bearing there and nowhere
else. Report what the affordability test and the price comparison each
found; where they disagree, report the disagreement in synthesis and do not
join them into one proposition.

**The causal claim is not tested here, and why.** Whether infill "makes"
housing more affordable is a causal proposition. Testing it would require a
defined no-infill counterfactual, added supply, time lags and filtering
over a specified period, and the price comparisons in this brief cannot
establish any of that. No such causal claim was captured in the intake in a
form that fixes a counterfactual or a period. This brief therefore tests
the descriptive propositions above and does not test, and no verdict in it
may be read as testing, whether infill causes housing to be more or less
affordable. The causal effect of infill on city-wide housing costs is out
of scope for this review. Do not research or report it here. Test it only
in a separate brief with a declared counterfactual and period.

**Population, geography and unit.** The same linked
demolition-and-replacement lots as Claim 1, and the same city-wide primary
set and the same frozen historical mature-neighbourhood subset reported
separately. Claim 2's verdict denominator is narrower than Claim 1's: it is
the separately titled replacement dwellings in that matched set, each
counted once. A dwelling unit inside a single-title multi-unit building is
reported as a separate count, with its building-average assessed value per
dwelling unit, and never enters Claim 2's verdict denominator.

**The test.** Apply the shelter-cost-to-income test to every separately
titled replacement dwelling in the matched set. A dwelling meets the
affordability threshold when monthly shelter cost is less than 30 percent of
the modelled household's monthly before-tax income. A dwelling at 30 percent
or more does not meet it. This is Statistics Canada's classification in
force on 2026-09-01, quoted rather than paraphrased.

There is one basis and it is the purchase basis. It is fixed here and is not
chosen after seeing results:

1. **A separately titled replacement dwelling**, that is, the only dwelling
   unit on its title. The purchase basis, on its own total value per
   dwelling: model the purchase at its first arm's-length sale price after
   completion where one exists, otherwise at its total assessed value on its
   first full assessment roll after completion. State which of the two
   supplied the price and state its year. Owner shelter cost is calculated
   under the assumptions fixed below. Tenure plays no part in this: the
   dwelling is tested on the purchase model whether it is owner occupied,
   rented or unlet.
2. **A dwelling unit inside a single-title multi-unit building.** Excluded
   from the verdict denominator. Report it in a separate count with its
   building-average assessed value per dwelling unit, name that figure a
   building average that does not estimate the distinct market value of any
   particular unit, and state that the affordability of renting it is not
   tested here, because CMHC average-rent cells do not state whether the
   rent includes utilities and no published source gives contract rents for
   the declared cohort. No household is modelled as buying a whole
   single-title multi-unit building, and a building-average assessed value
   per dwelling unit is never used as a purchase price for a dwelling unit
   that cannot be purchased separately.

State for every dwelling in the verdict denominator the price used, which of
the two series supplied it and its year, and report the counts under each
series separately as well as in the verdict share. Where neither a first
arm's-length sale price nor a value on a first full assessment roll can be
established for a separately titled replacement dwelling, that dwelling is
unclassified under the bounds rule below; do not substitute a figure from
another series for it. Actual property-level rents, where published, are
reported only as context in the limitations below, and never as a Claim 2
measure.

**Affordability denominator and the majority threshold, predeclared.** The
verdict denominator is separately titled replacement dwellings, each counted
once, and the majority threshold is more than 50 percent of that
denominator. Report the numerator and the denominator for that share, and
report beside it the count of dwelling units in single-title multi-unit
buildings that this denominator excludes, with their share of all
replacement dwelling units in the matched set and their building-average
assessed values per dwelling unit. Report alongside the verdict share the
two lot-level sensitivities from the unit-of-analysis section, restricted to
separately titled replacement dwellings, and report every share broken down
by bedroom count, together with the bedroom-count distribution of the tested
set. A separately titled replacement dwelling for which no price can be
established under the rule above is not counted as meeting or as failing the
test; its count and share of the denominator are stated, and it is carried
into the bounds rule under the verdict mapping below. Fix these rules before
any share is calculated.

**Assumptions for the shelter-cost-to-income test, fixed in this brief.**
Do not vary them after seeing results; report any input that could not be
obtained as missing evidence.

- Income source: Statistics Canada census median total before-tax household
  income for the City of Edmonton, from the most recent census available on
  the freeze date, with the census year stated. Where Statistics Canada
  publishes a later median household income estimate for the Edmonton
  census metropolitan area, report the test under that figure as well and
  label which figure produced which result. The declared City median
  household income is the primary case and it alone carries the Claim 2
  verdict. Report the test by each available household-income band or
  household type, matched to bedroom count where the source permits, as
  named sensitivities, labelling which income figure produced which
  result.
- Down payment: 20 percent of purchase price. Declared sensitivity: the
  regulatory minimum in force on 2026-09-01. For a price of $500,000 or
  less, 5 percent. For a price above $500,000 but below $1.5 million, 5
  percent on the first $500,000 and 10 percent on the remainder. For a
  price of $1.5 million or more, 20 percent. Include mortgage default
  insurance in the financed amount only where the mortgage is eligible for
  it, and state the applicable eligibility rule.
- Mortgage rate: the Bank of Canada five-year conventional mortgage rate
  for the month in which the price is dated, stated with that date.
- Amortization: the 25-year amortization is the primary case and it alone
  carries the Claim 2 verdict. Report the 30-year amortization as a named
  sensitivity and label which produced which result. Where a 30-year
  amortization is not available to the purchaser and dwelling class being
  tested, state the rule that makes it unavailable, with its source and
  date. Where the primary case cannot be computed for a dwelling, that
  dwelling is unclassified under the bounds rule; do not substitute the
  sensitivity for it.
- Property taxes: the City of Edmonton published residential tax rate for
  the price year applied to the dwelling's assessed value, stated with the
  rate, its source and its year. Where the City publishes the annual
  property tax levy for the subject property in that year, report that levy
  beside the rate-based figure as a labelled alternative; the rate-based
  figure is the one that enters the verdict calculation.
- Utilities: a fixed monthly allowance, the same figure for every dwelling.
  Take it from Statistics Canada Table 11-10-0222-01, Household spending by
  household type, Alberta, for the most recent year available on 2026-09-01,
  summing the categories water, fuel and electricity for the principal
  accommodation, and divide the annual total by twelve. State the table, the
  reference year used and why it is the most recent available on the freeze
  date, the categories summed, the annual total and the monthly figure it
  produced. The allowance does not vary by dwelling, so it is a modelled
  cost and not any dwelling's actual utility bill; say so wherever it is
  reported. Report as a declared sensitivity the same test with the utility
  allowance removed, with its own share, numerator and denominator, and
  state whether it changes the verdict.
- Owner shelter cost, the only shelter cost this test uses, is the monthly
  mortgage principal and interest payment on the financed amount, plus
  monthly property tax at the published City residential rate for the price
  year, plus the monthly utility allowance above, plus the condominium fee
  where the dwelling is a condominium and its fee is published in the
  listing or the assessment record. Where the dwelling is a condominium and
  no fee is published, state that the condominium fee is not included, and
  report the count of dwellings in that position. State each component
  separately.

**Limitations of the Claim 2 test, predeclared.** Report every one of these
beside the Claim 2 verdict:

- The test is a model of purchase affordability for a hypothetical household
  earning the declared City median income. It is not the actual cost borne
  by any dwelling's occupant, and no result in it says what a particular
  household paid.
- Tenure is not used. No published source establishes tenure at the dwelling
  level for the declared cohort, so a separately titled replacement dwelling
  is tested on the purchase model whether it is owner occupied, rented or
  unlet.
- Renting is not tested. The affordability of renting a replacement dwelling
  unit is outside this test, because CMHC average-rent cells do not state
  whether the rent includes utilities and no published source gives contract
  rents for the declared cohort. Where actual property-level rents are
  published for a replacement, report them here as context, with their
  source and year, and state that they carry no verdict and entered no
  calculation.
- Dwelling units inside single-title multi-unit buildings are outside the
  verdict denominator. Their count, their share of all replacement dwelling
  units in the matched set and their building-average assessed values per
  dwelling unit are reported, and their absence from the verdict is a
  limitation of the record, not a finding about those units.
- The utility figure is a fixed provincial average and not any dwelling's
  bill. The declared sensitivity without utilities is reported beside the
  verdict.

**Verdict mapping, predeclared.** Each of the two claims carries its own
verdict and uses the methodology's four verdict words directly. There is no
compound rule and no conjunct, and neither claim's verdict depends on the
other claim's result.

Unclassified cases are handled by bounds. Calculate lower and upper bounds
over the complete verdict denominator, which is replacement dwelling units
for Claim 1 and separately titled replacement dwellings for Claim 2. The lower bound treats every
unclassified case as not satisfying the claimed direction; the upper bound
treats every unclassified case as satisfying it. Report the unclassified
count and share separately.

- **Supported:** the lower bound exceeds 50 percent of that claim's verdict
  denominator.
- **Contradicted:** the usable evidence affirmatively establishes that 50
  percent or fewer of the verdict denominator satisfy the proposition, that
  is, the upper bound is 50 percent or less.
- **Not established:** the usable evidence establishes neither.
- **Partially supported:** only where the stated proposition contains a
  meaningful part that the evidence establishes while the rest overreaches.
  Name which part fails and on what evidence, as prompts/reviewer.md
  requires.

A favourable result on a supplementary comparator (price per square foot,
price per bedroom, or a comparison with new construction elsewhere in the
region) is not the "meaningful part" that justifies Partially supported.
For Claim 1, an undivided title total or a lot total that differs from the
result on replacement dwelling unit values is reported as the qualification
on the verdict, not as a "meaningful part" that changes it, and so is a
tenure change between the demolished dwelling and the replacement. For
Claim 2, the exclusion of dwelling units inside single-title multi-unit
buildings, the fixed utility allowance and the result without utilities are
disclosed as qualifications on the verdict and are not a "meaningful part"
that changes it.

**Comparators, reported separately and never combined.** Each of the
following answers a different question. Label each one and do not merge
them into a single finding:

- the price or rent of the replacement against the housing it replaced on
  the same lot (this is Claim 1);
- the replacement against the median or typical price in the same
  neighbourhood in the same year;
- the replacement against new construction elsewhere in the region in the
  same year, including new suburban single-detached and new suburban row or
  townhouse product. Report regional new-construction comparisons as a
  separate comparator. Do not assume that it represents the relevant
  alternative for every buyer;
- rents against neighbourhood rents for units of the same bedroom count
  (CMHC rental market survey), reported where actual property-level rents
  are published. This comparator is context, it carries no verdict, and no
  figure in it enters the Claim 2 calculation.

Where one of these measures does not exist in the public record, say so and
record it as missing evidence rather than substituting a proxy without
labelling it a proxy. Reporting a subset of the measures without saying
which ones were unavailable is not acceptable.

**Prior tenure and displacement, in scope as evidence.** Where the record
supports it, report for the demolished dwelling: whether it was owner
occupied or rented at the time of the demolition permit, the number of
dwelling units it contained, the rent charged, and any recorded notice to
tenants or municipal tracking of tenant displacement. Report these as
evidence about what was lost on the lot, with their sources and years, and
record them as missing evidence where the record does not support them.
This brief does not ask reviewers to estimate a displacement count that the
record does not contain.

**Transferred evidence.** Another city's evidence cannot answer these
Edmonton-specific propositions. It may supply context or illustrate a
mechanism, and if used it carries the transferability statement the
methodology requires (why relevant, how it differs, what can and cannot be
inferred), but Edmonton-specific missing evidence stays recorded as
missing. Do not substitute comparator evidence for an Edmonton figure the
record does not contain.

## Scope

Geography: land within the present-day boundary of Edmonton. Both the
city-wide set of linked demolition-and-replacement lots and the
mature-neighbourhood subset are reported. Regional and suburban figures are
in scope only as the separately labelled comparator named above.

Time: the primary cohort and the declared sensitivity window above.

In scope as evidence: prior tenure, occupancy, prior rent and prior unit
count of the demolished dwelling, and recorded tenant displacement.

Out of scope: whether infill should be permitted or encouraged; design and
neighbourhood-character disputes; construction quality; property tax
effects beyond their use as an input to the shelter-cost test; the causal
question of what infill does to housing costs city-wide; and whether
Edmonton was planned around low-density housing (see the
low-density-history story).

## Required calculations

- Every price or assessed-value comparison states its source, its year,
  whether the figures are nominal or inflation adjusted (and if adjusted,
  the index and base year), and which figure it is: a separately titled
  dwelling's total value per dwelling, a building-average assessed value per
  dwelling unit from a single-title multi-unit building, an undivided title
  total, or a lot total.
- Every replacement dwelling unit value states which of the two valuation
  rules produced it, and for a dwelling unit in a single-title multi-unit
  building names the figure a building-average assessed value per dwelling
  unit and states the building total that was divided and the number of
  dwelling units it was divided by. That figure assigns the same
  building-average value to every dwelling unit in the building and does not
  estimate the distinct market value of any particular unit.
- Assessed values and sale prices are reported as separate series and never
  combined into one average. Claim 1's verdict series is the assessed-value
  pair; the sale-to-sale comparison is reported as a named sensitivity,
  stating for every pair the gap in years between the two sale dates and
  whether the pair falls inside or outside the sensitivity's headline figure
  under the ten-year rule.
- Every median, share or average states its count, its numerator, its
  denominator and its geography, and states whether the denominator is
  replacement dwelling units (the verdict denominator) or matched lot pairs
  (a sensitivity).
- Every step of the sampling and matching pipeline states the count
  entering and the count leaving, and the reason for the loss.
- Every shelter-cost calculation covers a separately titled replacement
  dwelling on the purchase model, and states each of the fixed assumptions
  above and the figure used for it, giving the first arm's-length sale price
  where used and otherwise that dwelling's total assessed value on its first
  full assessment roll, with its year. State the components separately:
  mortgage principal and interest, property tax at the published City
  residential rate, the monthly utility allowance, and the condominium fee
  where published or the statement that no condominium fee is included.
  Report the same calculation without the utility allowance as the declared
  sensitivity. Do not use a fractional assessed value as a purchase price
  for a dwelling unit that cannot be purchased separately, and do not report
  a shelter cost for a dwelling unit inside a single-title multi-unit
  building.
- Amounts are Canadian dollars unless stated otherwise; no silent
  conversions.

## Reviewer instructions of special note

- Apply the same documented sampling and matching rules to every eligible
  redevelopment, and report the complete resulting distribution.
- Report what individual cases establish, label them as cases, and do not
  infer population frequency from them.
- Where available, report total assessed value, land value and improvement
  value separately.
- Do not join datasets that were not built to be joined without stating
  what the join assumed and what it could get wrong.
- Keep the two claims separate. A finding about the price relationship on
  redeveloped lots is not by itself a finding about the affordability
  threshold, and the reverse.
- Report the city-wide set and the mature-neighbourhood subset for every
  claim.
- State the unit behind every figure. Claim 1's verdict share is a share of
  replacement dwelling units, each self-contained dwelling unit counted once
  whether separately titled or held on one title. Claim 2's verdict share is
  a share of separately titled replacement dwellings only, with the excluded
  single-title dwelling units counted and reported beside it. Report the two
  lot-level sensitivities alongside each verdict share and never in place of
  it.
- Where the public record cannot answer a measure, record it as missing
  evidence and name who likely holds it.
- Record every source URL; you have no repo access, so cite fully.
- If any definition, measure, window or predeclared rule here changes what
  the honest answer is, record a MATERIAL FRAMING CONCERN per
  prompts/reviewer.md and answer the claims as posed alongside it.
