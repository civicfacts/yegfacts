Recorded 2026-09-01 by Stew under D-0019 (delegated briefs).

## Raw claim

> "AFFORDABLE!?! Have you looked at the prices of these houses?! A
> $350,000 house is torn down and a $1,000,000 house goes up in its
> place!!"

## Provenance

- Supplied by the founder on 2026-09-01 from an Edmonton social-media
  thread about infill, replying to a statement that infill makes
  housing more affordable. Platform, author and URL were not captured;
  the wording is as the founder pasted it and may be a composite
  paraphrase rather than a single verbatim post.
- No other circulating forms were captured. The "Circulating forms" in
  the brief beyond this one are the site's own paraphrases of how the
  claim is usually put, not captured posts.

## What was left out and why

Nothing. The post makes two checkable assertions: the price of the
demolished house versus its replacement, and the implication that infill
is therefore not affordable. Both go to the panel as separate claims.

## Second rerun

Round one of `reviews/infill-prices/2026-09-02-rerun` produced a MATERIAL
FRAMING CONCERN on both claims from the Gemini 3.1 Pro seat, recorded
verbatim in `framing/panel-concern.md` and in substance below: the v2 brief's
verdict unit, the "separately saleable replacement dwelling", makes a
single-title multi-unit building such as a purpose-built rental fourplex one
dwelling, so Claim 1 compared a whole building's assessed value with one
demolished house and Claim 2 modelled a median-income household buying the
whole building.

> Comparing the assessed value of an entire multi-unit building to a single
> demolished bungalow mathematically guarantees a massive price increase,
> ignoring the per-unit price.

> Because purpose-built rental buildings are sold on a single title, the
> brief models a single median-income household buying an entire apartment
> building to live in, which predictably fails the 30% shelter-cost test.
