# Response to framing check 1

Check: framing/check-1.md, verdict REVISE, run 2026-09-01. Editor's
decision: adopt every replacement wording in the check as written, and
carry each one through the sections of the brief that depended on the
wording it replaced. The brief in this directory is now Revision 1 after
framing check 1; its status line says so.

One entry per finding. Each entry quotes the wording now in brief.md.

## Finding 1. Claim 1 contained a contradictory tenure exclusion

Adopted as written. The tenure section no longer removes a tenure-change
pair from Claim 1's verdict denominator. It now opens:

> **Tenure changes, predeclared.** Claim 1 compares assessed value with
> assessed value for every otherwise eligible replacement dwelling unit,
> regardless of the tenure of the demolished or replacement housing. A
> tenure change is reported as a qualification and does not remove the
> dwelling unit from Claim 1's verdict denominator. Where both sides were
> rented and comparable contract rents exist, report the rent-to-rent
> comparison as a separate non-verdict series.

The exclusion sentence ("do not count it in a verdict denominator") is
gone. What remains of the old paragraph is the reporting duty and the
prohibition on setting an assessed value against a rent:

> Report as a qualification, with its count, every matched lot pair in
> which the demolished dwelling and the replacement are of different
> tenure, for example an owner-occupied dwelling replaced by rental
> housing or a rental replaced by ownership, and report the verdict share
> with those pairs identified so that their effect on it can be seen.

The rent-to-rent comparison is now named a non-verdict series in its own
paragraph and "never entering a verdict denominator".

Carried through: Claim 1's normalized proposition now adds "Every
otherwise eligible replacement dwelling unit enters this comparison
regardless of the tenure of the demolished or the replacement housing",
and the admissible verdict series adds "Tenure does not affect
admissibility: every otherwise eligible replacement dwelling unit enters
this series whatever the tenure of the demolished or the replacement
housing, and a tenure change is reported as a qualification on the
verdict." The verdict mapping now lists a tenure change with the
undivided title total and the lot total as a qualification on the
verdict rather than a "meaningful part" that changes it.

## Finding 2. Claim 2's detailed rule contradicted its normalized proposition

Adopted as written. Claim 2's operationalization now opens the basis
rules with the check's paragraph:

> Select the shelter-cost basis by tenure, not title. Test every rented
> dwelling unit on rent plus utilities. Use the purchase basis only where
> the modelled household could purchase the separately saleable dwelling
> represented by the price. A unit that is neither rented nor separately
> purchasable is unclassified unless a published source provides a valid
> occupancy-cost basis.

The numbered rule is rebuilt on tenure. It is now a rented dwelling unit
"whatever its title" on the rental basis; a dwelling unit "that is not
rented and is separately purchasable" on the purchase basis; and a
dwelling unit "that is neither rented nor separately purchasable, for
example an owner-occupied or unlet dwelling unit inside a single-title
multi-unit building", which is:

> Unclassified, unless a published source supplies a valid occupancy-cost
> basis for it, in which case state that source, its year and the basis
> it supplies.

Carried through: the normalized proposition now tests each unit on "the
price basis its tenure selects"; the unit-of-analysis valuation rule now
says a single-title unit's "price basis for Claim 2 is selected by tenure
and not by title"; the passage in Claim 1 that summarized Claim 2 now
says "Claim 2 selects its shelter-cost basis by tenure and not by title"
and that "A building-average assessed value per dwelling unit is never
used as a purchase price"; and Claim 2's population section says the
basis is selected by tenure "not by the valuation rules that fix Claim
1's assessed-value comparison". Because the rule now turns on tenure,
the brief also states that where a unit's tenure "cannot be established
from a published source, that dwelling unit is unclassified under the
bounds rule below; do not assign it a basis by title instead", and the
affordability denominator carries every unit "unclassified under the
price-basis rules above" into the bounds.

## Finding 3. A building average was called each unit's assessed value

Adopted as written. Every place the brief divided a single-title
building's total by its unit count now names the result a
building-average assessed value per dwelling unit, and the check's added
sentence appears with the definition:

> For a single-title multi-unit building, this calculation assigns the
> same building-average value to every dwelling unit. It does not
> estimate the distinct market value of any particular unit.

The unit-of-analysis rule now reads:

> **A dwelling unit inside a single-title multi-unit building.** Its
> value is the building-average assessed value per dwelling unit, that
> is, the building's total assessed value divided by the number of
> dwelling units the building contains.

Carried through with the same name and the same disclaimer: Claim 1's
normalized proposition, Claim 1's verdict measure, the majority
threshold, the replacement-assessed-value rule, the admissible verdict
series, the interpretation-note rule on undivided title totals, the
"Other measures reported alongside" list, and two required calculations,
one of which now requires the reviewer to name the figure "a
building-average assessed value per dwelling unit" and to state that it
"does not estimate the distinct market value of any particular unit".
The term "per-unit value" no longer names this figure anywhere in the
brief.

## Finding 4. Claim 2 required unpublished property-level rents

Adopted as written, in the form the check's own audit settles: no
published source supplies unit-level tenure, contract rent and utility
inclusion for this cohort, so the primary measure is the disclosed model
rather than actual property rents. Claim 2 now states:

> The rent in that rental basis is a modelled rent, not the unit's actual
> contract rent. No published source supplies unit-level tenure, contract
> rent and utility inclusion for the declared cohort, so the single
> primary Claim 2 measure for a rented dwelling unit is a disclosed
> model: the same-year CMHC Rental Market Survey average rent cell for
> that unit's bedroom count and zone, stated as an estimate of typical
> rent for that bedroom count and zone rather than the replacement unit's
> actual contract rent. Actual property-level rents, where published, are
> reported as qualifications on that measure and never displace it.
> Reviewers still return one Claim 2 verdict.

The rental basis in the numbered rule now uses that cell, and requires
the reviewer to "State the cell used, its year, its bedroom count and its
zone, and state that the figure is an estimate of typical rent and not
the unit's actual contract rent". Where the bedroom count or zone cannot
be established, or the cell is suppressed or unpublished, the unit is
unclassified, and no assessed value is substituted for it.

The utilities rule follows the same cell:

> For a rented dwelling unit, take utility inclusion from the same CMHC
> Rental Market Survey cell that supplied the modelled rent, which states
> for that cell whether the rent includes utilities, and add the cost of
> any utility that cell states is not included from a named published
> utility cost series with its source and year. Where that cell does not
> state whether utilities are included, that dwelling unit is
> unclassified, as under the rule immediately below.

Carried through: the labelled CMHC series in Claim 1 is now marked as
"the series that supplies Claim 2's modelled rent for a rented dwelling
unit"; the verdict mapping discloses the modelled rent, and any published
property-level rent that differs from it, as qualifications on the Claim
2 verdict; and property-level contract rents stay in the brief as
qualifications reported beside the modelled result.

## Finding 5. The required-calculations section named the wrong purchase input

Adopted as written. The shelter-cost calculation bullet now requires:

> giving the first arm's-length sale price where used and otherwise the
> separately titled dwelling's total assessed value in the first case,
> and the CMHC modelled rent cell with its year, bedroom count and zone
> together with the utilities figure in the second. Do not use a
> fractional assessed value as a purchase price for a dwelling unit that
> cannot be purchased separately.

The same bullet now requires the calculation to state "the tenure that
selected the basis", and makes the components follow tenure: "rent and
utilities for a rented dwelling unit; mortgage principal and interest,
property taxes, utilities and condominium fees where applicable for an
owner."

The assumption that set out the components is rewritten on the same
rule, with the Statistics Canada definition named as its ground:

> Shelter-cost components follow tenure, not title, as they do in
> Statistics Canada's shelter-cost definition. Rental shelter cost is the
> modelled rent plus any utilities that rent does not include. Ownership
> shelter cost is mortgage principal and interest plus property taxes
> plus utilities, plus condominium fees where applicable, stated
> separately.
