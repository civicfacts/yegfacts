# Response to framing check 2

Check: framing/check-2.md, verdict REVISE, run 2026-09-01. The check
raised two findings against Claim 2 and proposed dropping the claim from
this brief. The editor's decision is to keep Claim 2 in a narrower form
rather than drop it, and to remove from it every input the check showed
is not published. The brief in this directory is now Revision 2 after
framing check 2; its status line says so.

One entry per finding. Each entry quotes the wording now in brief.md.

## Finding 1. The tenure rule does not keep Claim 2 checkable

Accepted on the facts, resolved by removing tenure from the test rather
than by dropping the claim. No published source establishes tenure at the
dwelling level for this cohort: Edmonton's ownership data are aggregated
to neighbourhood level, and Alberta title records establish ownership and
title structure, not whether a dwelling is rented, owner occupied or
unlet. A test that selects a basis by tenure therefore cannot be run, and
a bounds rule that records unknown tenure measures the gap in the record
rather than affordability.

The tenure-selected basis, the three-way basis rule and the "tenure
cannot be established" unclassified rule are gone. Claim 2's normalized
proposition now reads:

> Among separately titled replacement dwellings in the matched set, more
> than 50 percent would require shelter costs equal to or greater than 30
> percent of before-tax income for a hypothetical household earning the
> declared City median income that purchased the dwelling at its first
> arm's-length sale price after completion, or at its total assessed
> value on its first full assessment roll after completion where no sale
> is recorded, under the 25-year amortization and the other primary
> financing assumptions below.

and states what the model is and is not:

> This is a model of purchase affordability for the median household. It
> is not the actual cost borne by the dwelling's occupant, and no result
> under it says what any particular household paid. Tenure is not used
> anywhere in this test, because no published source establishes tenure
> at the dwelling level for the declared cohort. A separately titled
> replacement dwelling therefore enters the test whether it is owner
> occupied, rented or unlet, and the test asks only what it would cost
> the modelled household to buy it.

The test section now carries one basis:

> There is one basis and it is the purchase basis.

Carried through: the unit-of-analysis section now separates the two
denominators, Claim 1's cross-reference to Claim 2 describes a purchase
model on separately titled dwellings, Claim 2's population section fixes
the narrower denominator, the affordability denominator and majority
threshold are stated on separately titled replacement dwellings, the
bounds rule names each claim's denominator, and the required-calculations
bullet on shelter cost no longer mentions tenure or a rental branch.

## Finding 2. The claimed CMHC utility field does not exist

Accepted. CMHC publishes Edmonton average-rent cells by bedroom count and
geography, and its methodology says those rents are not adjusted for
utility inclusion. The cell cannot supply the inclusion statement the
previous revision required, so the rental branch cannot be computed at
all. The rental branch is removed rather than repaired, and the units it
covered are removed from the verdict denominator rather than carried as
unclassified cases.

The exclusion and its reason are stated in the proposition:

> Dwelling units in single-title multi-unit buildings are excluded from
> Claim 2's verdict denominator. They are reported as a separate count,
> with the building-average assessed value per dwelling unit for each,
> and the report states that the affordability of renting one of those
> units is not tested here, because CMHC average-rent cells do not state
> whether the rent includes utilities and no published source gives
> contract rents for the declared cohort. That is a limitation of the
> record, not a finding about those units.

The CMHC modelled rent is no longer a Claim 2 measure anywhere. The
labelled CMHC series in Claim 1 now says it "carries no verdict in either
claim and supplies no input to Claim 2", and actual property rents
survive only as context:

> Actual property-level rents, where published, are reported only as
> context in the limitations below, and never as a Claim 2 measure.

Utilities for the owner calculation no longer depend on any rent cell.
They are a fixed allowance from a named published table:

> Utilities: a fixed monthly allowance, the same figure for every
> dwelling. Take it from Statistics Canada Table 11-10-0222-01, Household
> spending by household type, Alberta, for the most recent year available
> on 2026-09-01, summing the categories water, fuel and electricity for
> the principal accommodation, and divide the annual total by twelve.

with the calculation without utilities declared in advance as a
sensitivity, and the components of owner shelter cost fixed:

> Owner shelter cost, the only shelter cost this test uses, is the
> monthly mortgage principal and interest payment on the financed amount,
> plus monthly property tax at the published City residential rate for
> the price year, plus the monthly utility allowance above, plus the
> condominium fee where the dwelling is a condominium and its fee is
> published in the listing or the assessment record. Where the dwelling
> is a condominium and no fee is published, state that the condominium
> fee is not included, and report the count of dwellings in that
> position.

A new predeclared limitations block collects all of this beside the Claim
2 verdict: the model is not any occupant's cost, tenure is not used,
renting is not tested, the excluded single-title units are counted and
reported, and the utility figure is a provincial average rather than any
dwelling's bill.

## Editor's decision

The check's proposed replacement, that Claim 2 not be sent to reviewers
under this brief, would drop the claim because a tenure-selected test and
a rental branch cannot be run. Both of those are the brief's own
constructions, not the post's. What the captured post objects to is that
the replacement house is unaffordable to buy: "AFFORDABLE!?! Have you
looked at the prices of these houses?!" followed by a purchase price.
That objection is checkable for separately titled replacement dwellings
from sources the check itself audited as published: first arm's-length
sale prices and first full assessment roll values from City assessment
and title data, the census median household income for the City,
published mortgage rates and down-payment rules, the published City
residential tax rate, and a named Statistics Canada spending table for
utilities. Nothing in that chain requires knowing who lives in the
dwelling or on what terms.

Dropping the claim would answer a question about the record with silence
on the question the post asks. Keeping it in this narrower form answers
the purchase question where the record supports it and says plainly where
it does not: renting is not tested, and dwelling units in single-title
multi-unit buildings sit outside the verdict with their count and their
building-average values reported beside it. A reader is told what was
tested, on how many dwellings, and what share of the replacement housing
that leaves unexamined. That is a smaller claim than the previous
revision attempted and a defensible one, where the previous revision
promised an affordability verdict on every replacement dwelling unit and
could not deliver it.

The narrowing is a real cost and it is disclosed as one. Purpose-built
rental replacements are exactly the units where the affordability
argument is most contested, and this brief now says nothing about them.
That is a gap in the published record, and it belongs in the limitations
where a later brief can pick it up if a contract-rent source appears.
