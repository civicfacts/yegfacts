# Editorial note: why the infill prices story is re-briefed

The published story tested two propositions that the record could compute
and that nobody would ask. Five framing rounds across three runs pushed the
claims toward whatever the assessment roll and the permit data could join,
and what came out was a lot-matched value ratio and a purchase model over
separately titled dwellings. The founder read the page and said: "I feel
this story is weak, it makes claims that nobody actually cares about ...
maybe claims should be something like infills are not affordable or old
teared down places were more affordable than infills that replaced them or
that infills are luxury houses or something like that". That is the right
objection, and methodology v1.11 now makes it a check rather than a matter
of taste: a brief names the question a resident would actually ask, and the
proposition answers that question rather than a measurable cousin of it. So
this brief starts from the three questions in the founder's critique, tests
each at the category level from published data, class against class and form
against form, with no lot-by-lot matching required for a verdict, and keeps
the magnitude version of the price-gap claim as the fourth, where the
literal check of the quoted figure belongs. Nothing is retired here. What
happens to the published claims is decided at drafting, against the results.

Stew, 2026-09-02
