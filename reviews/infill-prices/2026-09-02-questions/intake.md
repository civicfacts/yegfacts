## Re-brief around the questions people ask

Recorded 2026-09-02 by Stew.

The founder read the published story and said, verbatim:

> "I feel this story is weak, it makes claims that nobody actually cares
> about ... maybe claims should be something like infills are not
> affordable or old teared down places were more affordable than infills
> that replaced them or that infills are luxury houses or something like
> that"

The editor accepts the critique. The published story's two claims were
shaped by what the record could compute, a lot-matched value ratio and a
purchase model on separately titled dwellings, and both were stated in
units nobody would ask a question in. Methodology v1.11 adds the check
that names that failure: a brief must state the question a resident or a
reporter would actually ask, and the proposition must answer it rather
than a measurable cousin of it, and where the record cannot answer at the
level people ask, the brief says so and tests the nearest level the record
does answer.

The three questions in the founder's critique, in the founder's order:

1. Were the old places that were torn down more affordable than the infill
   that replaced them?
2. Is new infill luxury housing?
3. Can an ordinary Edmonton household afford new infill?

The editor's decision: one story, four claims, every one answerable at the
category level from published data, with no lot-by-lot matching required
for a verdict. Three of the four are the questions above. The fourth is
the magnitude version of `ip-teardown-price-gap`, carried over from
`reviews/infill-prices/2026-09-02-magnitude/brief.md` unchanged, so the
literal check of the quoted figure stays on the record beside the three
category-level claims. That claim keeps its id and its lot-matched
operationalization, and the magnitude re-run's result replaces the
published finding on it as a verdict change or an updated entry.

The published finding on `ip-infill-affordable` stands until this run
replaces it. This brief does not carry that id: the median-income question
is briefed here as `ip-infill-affordable-median-household`, on
category-level published data rather than on a matched cohort. Nothing is
retired in this intake or in this brief. What happens to the published
`ip-infill-affordable` claim is decided at drafting, in the story file,
against this run's results.

The raw claim, its provenance and the limits on it are unchanged from the
intake above. No new circulating form was captured for this re-brief. The
founder's critique quoted above is an editorial instruction from the
founder, not a captured circulating post, and it is labelled as such
wherever the brief uses its wording.
