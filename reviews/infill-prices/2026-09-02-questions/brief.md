# Review brief: Infill teardown prices, re-briefed around the questions people ask

Status: PROVISIONAL, re-brief of the published story around the questions
people ask (founder critique of 2026-09-02); framing check pending; frozen
before round 1. Methodology: v1.11

## Story

The circulating claim describes an older house being demolished and replaced
by housing alleged to be much more expensive, in an argument about whether
infill makes housing more affordable. The published run split that objection
into two claims, both stated in the units the record happened to publish. The
founder read the published page and said the story makes claims nobody cares
about. This brief re-states the story as the questions people actually ask
about infill prices, and fixes each one so that a reviewer can answer it at
the category level from published data. Topic: housing-development.

Claims in this story, reviewed and reported separately. Each carries its own
finding, panel agreement and evidence-basis label, and no claim's verdict
depends on another claim's result. Reviewer confidence appears only beside
the reviewer who assigned it.

- `ip-teardown-more-affordable`: were the houses being demolished for infill
  more affordable than the housing that replaced them.
- `ip-infill-luxury`: is new infill housing luxury housing.
- `ip-infill-affordable-median-household`: can a median-income Edmonton
  household afford new infill housing.
- `ip-teardown-price-gap`: the magnitude of the price relationship on linked
  demolition-and-replacement lots. Carried over unchanged from
  `reviews/infill-prices/2026-09-02-magnitude/brief.md`, which was frozen on
  2026-09-02 on framing check 2 FRAME OK.

The first three claims are new ids and are answered at the category level:
class against class, form against form, with no lot-by-lot matching required
for a verdict. The fourth is the lot-matched claim and keeps the id the
published finding carries, `ip-teardown-price-gap`, so its result replaces
that finding as a verdict change or an updated entry rather than standing
beside it as a second claim.

This brief retires nothing. The published `ip-infill-affordable` claim is not
re-run under its own id here and its published finding stands; what happens
to it is decided at drafting, in the story file, against this run's results.

**Why the story is re-briefed.** The published propositions were shaped by
what the record could compute rather than by what anybody asks. A lot-matched
ratio of assessed values and a purchase model over separately titled
replacement dwellings are both measurable, and neither is a question a
resident types. Methodology v1.11 makes that a framing defect in its own
right: the brief must name the question people ask, and where the record
cannot answer at the level they ask it, the brief says so and tests the
nearest level the record does answer, in words a reader would use. Every
claim below therefore begins from the question and works back to the record.
The intake record in `intake.md` sets out the founder's critique and the
editor's decision in full.

## Why this story (selection rationale)

- Prevalence: the founder supplied one possibly composite formulation from
  one Edmonton infill thread, and, separately, an editorial critique of the
  published story. No sample of other posts was captured, so this brief
  cannot establish the claim's prevalence or whether this wording represents
  how it circulates.
- Civic importance: mature-neighbourhood infill rules and the zoning bylaw
  renewal are live council business, and affordability is argued over on
  several sides.
- Verifiability: property assessments, demolition and building permits,
  market association reports, the City's own affordability monitoring and
  CMHC series are published, each with known limits, so the answer turns on
  being explicit about which measure is used and what it can establish.

## The level this brief tests, fixed for the first three claims

The first three claims are stated and tested at the category level. The
comparison is a class of housing against a class of housing, not one lot
against itself. No claim among them requires a demolition permit to be
matched to a replacement, and no verdict among them may rest on a matched
lot pair.

This is a deliberate choice and its reason is stated so a reader can weigh
it. The questions people ask are about classes of housing: the old houses
being knocked down, the new infill going up, what an ordinary household can
buy. The published record supports class-level medians by dwelling form, and
the earlier runs on this story established that it does not support a
complete lot-matched price series. Testing at the category level is
therefore the nearest level the record answers, and each proposition below
names that level in the words a reader would use.

What the category level cannot do is stated with each claim. A class-level
comparison says what the typical old house in the area was worth and what
the typical new unit sells for. It does not say what happened on any
particular lot, and no finding on the first three claims may be reported as
if it did. The lot-level question is claim 4 and it is verdict-bearing there
and nowhere else.

**Dwelling form, fixed here.** Where a claim reports "by form", the forms are
the ones the source publishes, named as the source names them, at minimum
single-detached, semi-detached, row or townhouse, and apartment. State for
every figure which form it belongs to, which source published it, and the
form label that source used. Do not pool forms into one figure and do not
drop a form the source publishes.

**Constant dollars, fixed here.** Where a claim compares two figures dated in
different years, report the comparison in nominal dollars and in constant
dollars, and the constant-dollar figures carry the verdict. Convert with the
Statistics Canada Consumer Price Index, all-items, Edmonton census
metropolitan area, monthly series, with July 2025 as the base period, using
July of the year the figure is dated for an annual assessment roll, which is
that roll's legislated valuation date, and the month of publication for a
market figure. Declared alternative: the annual-average CPI for the figure's
year with 2025 as the base year. Report the result under both and label which
is which. Source: Statistics Canada Table 18-10-0004-01 (YF-EV-0108); the
City's valuation-date rule is YF-EV-0111.

**Bounds on missing figures, fixed here.** Where a figure a claim's verdict
turns on is not published for the declared period, it is not filled in from
another series and it is not estimated. State what is missing, name who
likely holds it, and where the missing figure leaves a threshold
undetermined, the verdict on that claim is Not established and the reviewer
states which threshold was undetermined and why.

## Claims under review

### Claim 1, id: ip-teardown-more-affordable

**Circulating forms for this claim.**

Founder's phrasing, an editorial instruction and not a captured post:

> "old teared down places were more affordable than infills that replaced
> them"

Site-written formulations, not captured posts, listed so a reviewer can see
the register the claim is made in:

- "The house that came down was the affordable one."
- "Replacing one affordable house with expensive units is not
  affordability."

**The question a resident asks.** Were the old houses being knocked down for
infill cheaper than the new housing that went up in their place?

**Normalized proposition:** The houses being demolished for infill in
Edmonton were more affordable than the housing that replaces them: in
Edmonton's mature neighbourhoods, the typical older house of the kind being
demolished was worth less than the price of a typical newly built infill
dwelling, in every dwelling form the new housing takes.

**What this proposition is and is not.** It is a comparison of two classes,
the older houses standing in the area against the new units built in the
city, each at its median. It is not a statement about any lot, any address or
any matched pair, and a finding on it may not be reported as one. It does not
say that any particular demolished house was affordable to any particular
household; that is claim 3's question, on claim 3's method. "More affordable"
here means lower priced, and the proposition says so in its own words rather
than resting on an undefined term.

**Operationalization, predeclared.**

*The old side, the class being demolished.* The City's own infill reporting
establishes what is coming down: 387 single detached houses in the 2023
report and a net loss of 294 single detached houses in the 2024 report, with
demolitions of other forms in single figures (YF-EV-0048, YF-EV-0101). The
demolished class is therefore older detached houses in the
mature-neighbourhood area. Compute its median total assessed value from the
City's published assessment data: the historical rolls dataset, which
publishes account, assessment year, address, legal description, year built,
lot size and total assessed value (YF-EV-0041, YF-EV-0099), joined by account
to the current-year roll for assessment class (YF-EV-0040, YF-EV-0098). Use
the most recent complete roll available on the freeze date, state which roll
year that is and state its legislated valuation date.

- **"Older", primary: year built before 1970.** Declared alternative: year
  built before 1980. Report the median under each figure, labelling which is
  the primary and which the alternative. Report the count of accounts behind
  each median, the year-built distribution of the set, and the median under
  each figure for the city as a whole as well as for the area, so a reader
  can see what the area restriction did. Where the City publishes its own
  year-built cutoff for the mature area, quote it, state its source and say
  which of the two figures it sits nearer to; that is a reporting
  requirement, not a third variant, and it does not carry the verdict.
- **The area.** The mature-neighbourhood area is delimited by a frozen
  historical boundary: the area covered by the City of Edmonton's Mature
  Neighbourhood Overlay as it stood on 2023-12-31, the day before Zoning
  Bylaw 20001 retired it, as published in the last consolidation of Zoning
  Bylaw 12800 (YF-EV-0113, YF-EV-0102). Quote the definition and state the
  consolidation date and the version reviewed. No mature-neighbourhood
  instrument is in force on the freeze date; the overlay was retired when
  Zoning Bylaw 20001 took effect on 2024-01-01 (YF-EV-0100, YF-EV-0051), so
  this is a historical boundary frozen here for this review and is not to be
  described as an instrument in force. Report the City Plan "redeveloping
  area" figures as a named sensitivity with their own median and count, and
  never substitute that boundary for the frozen one.
- **Dwelling form on the old side, and what the record carries.** The open
  assessment record publishes assessment class, not dwelling form. State
  which published field identified detached form. Where no published field
  does, compute the median over all residential improved accounts in the area
  meeting the year-built cutoff, label the figure the older residential stock
  rather than older detached houses, say so wherever it is reported, and
  record the detached breakdown as missing evidence, naming City of Edmonton
  Assessment and Taxation as the likely holder. That labelled figure carries
  the verdict where the detached breakdown cannot be computed, because it is
  the nearest level the published record answers.
- **What an assessed value is.** A mass-appraisal estimate of market value on
  the roll's legislated valuation date, not a transaction price
  (YF-EV-0047, YF-EV-0111). Say so wherever the figure is reported. The open
  record publishes no land-and-improvement split and no sale prices
  (YF-EV-0041), so neither is available for this comparison.

*The new side, the class being built.* The per-unit median price of newly
built dwellings in the City of Edmonton, by form, from CMHC absorbed unit
prices (YF-EV-0105 for the City of Edmonton census subdivision, YF-EV-0058
for the historical series including the published price percentiles). Take
the median absorbed price for each form the source publishes, for the most
recent complete calendar year before the freeze date; declared alternative:
the median over the three most recent complete calendar years. Report under
both and label which is which. State for each form the count of absorbed
units behind the figure. Where the captured table's type selector does not
publish a form, for example where it covers single- and semi-detached only,
state which forms are missing, record them as missing evidence naming CMHC as
the holder, and report the verdict on the forms the source does publish while
stating which forms it could not cover.

*What the new-side figure is and is not.* CMHC absorbed prices are prices for
new dwellings across the city. They are not restricted to teardown
replacements, they are not lot-matched, and they include new construction
that replaced nothing. Say so wherever they are reported. This is the
category-level comparison the proposition is stated in, and it is not a
substitute for the matched series claim 4 asks for.

**Verdict mapping, predeclared.** Compare, in constant dollars, each form's
new-unit median with the older-house median under the primary "older"
definition.

- **Supported:** every form the source publishes has a new-unit median above
  the older-house median.
- **Partially supported:** at least one form is above it and at least one
  form is at or below it. Name every form on each side, with its figure.
- **Contradicted:** no form's new-unit median is above the older-house
  median.
- **Not established:** the medians the comparison needs are not published for
  the declared period, or a missing figure leaves the comparison
  undetermined. State which figure was missing and who holds it.

The comparison is run form by form and each form can fall on either side of
the older-house median, so no outcome is foreclosed by the construction.
Report the full set of comparisons, each with both medians, both counts,
both dates and the constant-dollar basis, under the primary and the
alternative "older" definition and under the primary and the alternative
new-side window, labelling every result. Where the primary and the
alternative disagree on any form, say so plainly; the primary carries the
verdict.

**Stakes: what each verdict would mean.**

- **Supported** would mean that whatever else is true about infill, every
  form of new unit going up is priced above the typical older house standing
  in the mature-neighbourhood area, so the area's cheapest ownership stock is
  the stock being removed. To a holder of the claim, that is the objection
  established as a pattern about classes of housing rather than an anecdote
  about one street. To an opponent, it removes the argument that infill
  replaces old houses with cheaper, smaller units at the bottom of the
  market: on this reading no form does.
- **Contradicted** would mean new infill of every form, including new
  detached, is priced at or below the typical older house in the area, so the
  new housing is the cheaper housing on the record's own medians. To a
  holder, the central factual premise of the objection is lost. To an
  opponent, it is direct evidence that redevelopment is adding units priced
  below the stock it replaces.
- **Partially supported** would mean the answer depends on what is built:
  some forms are priced above the older stock and some at or below it, and
  the reviewer names which. To a holder, the objection holds for the forms
  named above the line and fails for the others, so "the old house was the
  affordable one" cannot be said of infill in general. To an opponent, the
  forms above the line are established and cannot be dismissed.
- **Not established** would mean the published medians the comparison needs
  are not there for the declared period. To a holder, the claim remains
  unproven and this review cannot be cited as confirmation. To an opponent,
  it remains unrefuted and missing evidence cannot be cited as showing the
  new units are cheap. It is not a finding that the two classes are priced
  alike.

**Published sources a reviewer computes from.** City of Edmonton property
assessment data, historical and current-year (YF-EV-0041, YF-EV-0099,
YF-EV-0040, YF-EV-0098); the City's residential assessment methodology for
what an assessed value means and its valuation date (YF-EV-0111,
YF-EV-0047); the City's infill reporting for what is being demolished
(YF-EV-0048, YF-EV-0101); Zoning Bylaw 12800 section 814 and its December
2023 consolidation for the frozen boundary (YF-EV-0113, YF-EV-0102); CMHC
absorbed unit prices (YF-EV-0105, YF-EV-0058); Statistics Canada CPI
(YF-EV-0108). Record every source URL; you have no repo access, so cite
fully.

### Claim 2, id: ip-infill-luxury

**Circulating forms for this claim.**

Founder's phrasing, an editorial instruction and not a captured post:

> "infills are luxury houses"

Site-written formulations, not captured posts:

- "It is all million dollar infill."
- "They knock down a $400k bungalow and put up two $900k skinnies."

**The question a resident asks.** Is the new infill going up in my
neighbourhood luxury housing, or is it ordinary housing at an ordinary price?

**Normalized proposition:** New infill housing in Edmonton is luxury housing:
the median price of a newly built dwelling in Edmonton is priced above the
top tenth of the resale housing market, in every dwelling form the new
housing takes.

**"Luxury" has no official definition and the line below is this brief's.**
No Canadian statistical agency, no assessor and no real estate association
publishes a price above which a home is luxury. The word is a judgement about
where a home sits relative to the rest of the market, so this brief fixes the
line in exactly those terms, with one alternative, and requires both readings
to be reported. A reader may disagree with the line; they can see the other
reading beside it and the distribution behind both.

- **Primary line: above the top decile of the resale market.** A form counts
  as luxury when its new-unit median price is above the 90th percentile of
  residential resale sale prices in the Greater Edmonton Area, all forms
  pooled, for the declared period.
- **Declared alternative: above the top quartile**, that is, above the 75th
  percentile of the same distribution.

Report every result under both lines, labelled. The primary line carries the
verdict, because reviewers return one verdict per claim; the alternative is
reported as a qualification and never as a second verdict.

**Operationalization, predeclared.**

*The resale distribution.* REALTORS Association of Edmonton published market
statistics for the Greater Edmonton Area (YF-EV-0104 for the July 2026
release, YF-EV-0056 for the December 2025 and year-end release). Use the most
recent complete calendar year before the freeze date; declared alternative:
the most recent published month before the freeze date. Report under both and
label which is which. State the geography the Association reports on, which
is the Greater Edmonton Area and not the City alone, wherever a figure is
used.

*What to do when the percentile is not published.* The captured Association
releases report averages and activity by dwelling type. If no published
Association source carries a price distribution from which the 90th and 75th
percentiles can be read for the declared period, say so, record the
distribution as missing evidence with the REALTORS Association of Edmonton
named as the holder, and return Not established on this claim under the
primary line. Do not construct the line from averages, do not substitute a
benchmark price for a percentile, and do not import a distribution from
another market. State which published table, if any, supplied each
percentile, and quote the price ranges it publishes.

*The new-unit medians.* The same CMHC absorbed unit price medians by form as
claim 1 (YF-EV-0105, YF-EV-0058), on the same primary window and the same
declared alternative, with the same statement of which forms the source
covers and which it does not. Report each form's median with its count and
its year.

*Comparability, stated plainly.* A new-unit absorbed price is a price for a
newly built dwelling across the City of Edmonton; a resale percentile is a
price from the Greater Edmonton Area resale market, which includes older
housing and housing outside the city. The two are different populations on
different geographies, and the comparison is a statement about where new
units sit against the market at large. Say so wherever the comparison is
reported. Report as a named sensitivity the same comparison against the
Association's reported price level for the same dwelling type rather than the
pooled distribution, with its own figures, labelled, and never carrying the
verdict.

**Verdict mapping, predeclared.** In constant dollars, on the primary line
and the primary window.

- **Supported:** every form the source publishes has a new-unit median above
  the top decile of the resale market.
- **Partially supported:** at least one form is above the line and at least
  one form is at or below it. Name every form on each side, with its figure
  and the percentile it was measured against.
- **Contradicted:** no form's new-unit median is above the line.
- **Not established:** the percentile or the medians the comparison needs are
  not published for the declared period. State which figure was missing and
  who holds it.

**Stakes: what each verdict would mean.**

- **Supported** would mean that a new infill unit of any form costs more than
  nine out of ten homes that change hands in the region, which is what a
  reader means by luxury. To a holder of the claim, the word is vindicated on
  a stated line rather than as rhetoric. To an opponent, the argument that
  infill delivers ordinary market housing at ordinary prices fails on every
  form.
- **Contradicted** would mean no form of new infill reaches even the top
  tenth of the market, so on the brief's line none of it is luxury housing.
  To a holder, the word is not carried by the price record and the objection
  has to be made in other terms, on size, design or neighbourhood change. To
  an opponent, it is the evidence that new units sit inside the ordinary
  range of the market.
- **Partially supported** would mean some forms clear the line and others do
  not, and the reviewer names which. To a holder, the word holds for the
  forms named and not for infill as a class. To an opponent, the forms above
  the line are established and cannot be waved away as anecdote.
- **Not established** would mean the resale distribution the line needs is
  not public for the declared period. To a holder, the claim remains
  unproven and cannot be cited as confirmed. To an opponent, it remains
  unrefuted. It is not a finding that new units are ordinary.

**Published sources a reviewer computes from.** REALTORS Association of
Edmonton market statistics (YF-EV-0104, YF-EV-0056); CMHC absorbed unit
prices (YF-EV-0105, YF-EV-0058); Statistics Canada CPI (YF-EV-0108). Record
every source URL; you have no repo access, so cite fully.

### Claim 3, id: ip-infill-affordable-median-household

**Circulating forms for this claim.**

Founder's phrasing, an editorial instruction and not a captured post:

> "infills are not affordable"

Captured form, the sentence the objection was made against, quoted here as
context and carrying no verdict:

> "AFFORDABLE!?! Have you looked at the prices of these houses?!"

Site-written formulations, not captured posts:

- "Nothing being built here is affordable for anyone."
- "Infill was supposed to make housing affordable and it did the opposite."

**The question a resident asks.** Can a household on a normal Edmonton income
afford to buy the new infill being built?

**Normalized proposition:** A household at Edmonton's median income cannot
afford newly built infill housing: on the City of Edmonton's own market
housing affordability method, the median price of a newly built dwelling in
Edmonton is out of reach for a median-income Edmonton household, in every
dwelling form the new housing takes.

**Operationalization, predeclared.**

*The primary reading is the City's own method, applied to new-unit prices.*
The City publishes an affordability analysis that already answers this
question by housing form for benchmark market housing: its 2024 Monitoring
Market Housing Affordability Report reports, by form, whether a median-income
Edmonton household can afford the benchmark, and it states the median
household income it used (YF-EV-0049). Reviewers must:

1. Quote the City's affordability criterion as the report states it, rather
   than paraphrase it, including its 30 percent shelter-cost test, its
   qualification assumptions and the income figure it uses, each with the
   page or section it appears on and the year it applies to.
2. Report the City's own published result by form, on the City's own
   benchmark prices, as the nearest published statement on the record. State
   the benchmark price the City used for each form and the report's year.
   This is reported, not computed, and it is not the verdict figure.
3. Apply that same criterion, unchanged, to the CMHC new-unit median absorbed
   price for each form (YF-EV-0105, YF-EV-0058), on the same primary window
   and declared alternative as claim 1. This is the primary reading and it
   carries the verdict, because the claim is about new infill and the City's
   benchmark is a market-wide benchmark rather than a new-construction price.
   State every input, every assumption and the arithmetic for each form.

Where the City's report does not state an input the criterion needs, state
which input, record it as missing evidence with the City of Edmonton Urban
Planning and Economy named as the holder, and say what the missing input does
to the calculation.

*The declared alternative reading: the brief's own shelter-cost model.*
Report, as a labelled alternative that does not carry the verdict, the same
question under the shelter-cost-to-income model frozen for the published run
in `reviews/infill-prices/2026-09-01-rerun2/brief.md`, applied to the same
new-unit median price per form rather than to any dwelling. A form is
affordable under it when modelled monthly shelter cost is less than 30
percent of the modelled household's monthly before-tax income, on Statistics
Canada's shelter-cost-to-income classification in force on the freeze date,
quoted rather than paraphrased (YF-EV-0106, YF-EV-0110). Its inputs, fixed
here and not to be varied after seeing results:

- Income: the City's declared median household income for Edmonton, the same
  figure the City's own report uses, stated with its year and source
  (YF-EV-0049). Where Statistics Canada publishes a later median income
  estimate on a different unit or geography, report it beside the figure and
  label the difference in unit and geography (YF-EV-0059); it does not
  replace the primary income figure.
- Down payment: 20 percent of the price.
- Mortgage rate: the Bank of Canada posted five-year conventional mortgage
  rate for the month the price is dated, stated with that date
  (YF-EV-0038, YF-EV-0092). The Bank discontinued the monthly chartered-bank
  publication on 2019-10-01 and kept the weekly posted series (YF-EV-0091),
  so take the simple average of the weekly posted observations in that month
  and state the observations used.
- Amortization: 25 years.
- Property taxes: the City of Edmonton published residential tax rate for the
  price year applied to the price, stated with the rate, its source and its
  year (YF-EV-0046).
- Utilities: a fixed monthly allowance, the same figure for every form, from
  Statistics Canada Table 11-10-0222-01, household spending, for the most
  recent year available on the freeze date, summing water, fuel and
  electricity for the principal accommodation and dividing the annual total
  by twelve (YF-EV-0107). State the table, the reference year, why it is the
  most recent available, the categories summed, the annual total and the
  monthly figure. It is a modelled average cost and not any dwelling's bill;
  say so wherever it is reported.
- Condominium fees are not included, because none is published for a
  category-level median price. State that they are excluded and say which
  forms the exclusion affects.
- Owner shelter cost is the monthly mortgage principal and interest on the
  financed amount, plus monthly property tax, plus the monthly utility
  allowance. State each component separately.

*What this test is and is not.* It is a model of purchase affordability for a
hypothetical household at the declared median income, buying at a
category-level median price. It is not the cost borne by any household, no
result under it says what anyone paid, and it says nothing about renting. It
does not use tenure anywhere. Renting a new infill unit is not tested,
because CMHC rent cells do not state whether the rent includes utilities and
no published source carries contract rents for new infill units (YF-EV-0039);
that is a limitation of the record and not a finding about renters. The
City's own affordable-housing definitions, which concern housing receiving
government assistance and expressly exclude market home ownership
(YF-EV-0050), are not applied here and are not a second reading of this
claim.

*The causal claim is not tested here, and why.* Whether infill "makes"
housing more affordable is a causal proposition. Testing it would require a
defined no-infill counterfactual, added supply, time lags and filtering over
a specified period, and none of the price comparisons in this brief can
establish any of that. No such causal claim was captured in the intake in a
form that fixes a counterfactual or a period. No verdict in this brief may be
read as testing it. Do not research or report it here.

**Verdict mapping, predeclared.** On the primary reading, the City's

**The question a resident asks.** When a house gets knocked down for infill,

**Unit of analysis, fixed for this claim.** The unit of analysis is the
replacement dwelling unit, counted once. A replacement dwelling unit is each
self-contained dwelling unit in the replacement housing on the lot, counted
once, whether it is separately titled or held with other dwelling units on
one title. A single-title multi-unit building, for example a purpose-built
rental fourplex, is four replacement dwelling units and not one. The
proposition is about the price of the housing people live in, so the dwelling
unit is the unit it is stated in and the unit it is counted in. A
self-contained dwelling unit is counted once and is never fractional; a
denominator containing fractional dwelling units does not conform to this
brief.

The verdict denominator is every replacement dwelling unit so defined.

**How a replacement dwelling unit is valued, fixed here.**

1. **A separately titled dwelling**, that is, the only dwelling unit on its
   title. Its value is its own total assessed value, or its own sale price
   where a sale series is in use. This is the "total value per dwelling"
   measure, and in this brief that language applies to separately titled
   dwellings and to no other case.
2. **A dwelling unit inside a single-title multi-unit building.** Its value
   is the building-average assessed value per dwelling unit, that is, the
   building's total assessed value divided by the number of dwelling units
   the building contains. The building average is the rule for single-title
   buildings, and the undivided building total is never compared with the
   demolished house. For a single-title multi-unit building, this
   calculation assigns the same building-average value to every dwelling
   unit. It does not estimate the distinct market value of any particular
   unit.

For every replacement dwelling unit, state which of these two cases it falls
in, the number of dwelling units on its title, and, where a division was
made, the building total that was divided.

The demolished house's value is not affected by this rule. It stays that
dwelling's total assessed value on the roll named below, undivided.

The matched lot pair remains the structure that links the evidence: one lot
on which one existing dwelling was demolished, paired with the replacement
dwelling or dwellings built on that same lot. Every replacement dwelling
unit in the verdict denominator comes from a matched lot pair, and every

**Normalized proposition:** On Edmonton lots where one house was demolished
for infill, the housing built in its place is typically worth about three
times the house that came down: the median ratio of the replacement dwelling
unit's assessed value to the demolished house's assessed value, in constant
dollars, is at least 2.5, and more than half of matched replacement dwelling
units are worth at least double the demolished house.

**The ratio, defined.** For each matched replacement dwelling unit, the
ratio is that unit's assessed value divided by the demolished house's total
assessed value on the same lot, both sides taken in the City's market-value
assessment basis and both converted to constant dollars with the index and
base period named under Timing below. The replacement dwelling unit's
assessed value is its own total assessed value on its first full assessment
roll after completion where it is a separately titled dwelling, and the
building-average assessed value per dwelling unit, the building's total
assessed value on that roll divided by its number of dwelling units, where
it sits in a single-title multi-unit building. The demolished house's
assessed value is its total assessed value on the assessment roll in force
in the year before demolition, undivided. Count every replacement dwelling
unit once. Every otherwise eligible replacement dwelling unit enters this
comparison regardless of the tenure of the demolished or the replacement
housing.

**The claim is a generalisation and is tested as one.** The word "typically"
in the proposition is doing real work and it is not a hedge. The captured
quotation offers one example, a $350,000 house and a $1,000,000
replacement, as evidence for a pattern, and the pattern is the claim. One
example cannot establish a pattern, and this brief does not ask reviewers to
verify that example. It asks what the matched population does. Both
predeclared thresholds below are statements about the population: a median
across matched replacement dwelling units, and a share of them. A result on
a handful of cases, however striking, does not answer either.

**The two predeclared thresholds, fixed before any result is seen.**

1. **The magnitude threshold, primary: the median ratio is at least 2.5.**
   Declared alternative: at least 2.0. Report the median ratio, and report
   the result against each of the two figures, labelling which is the
   primary and which the alternative.
2. **The majority threshold, primary: more than 50 percent of matched
   replacement dwelling units have a ratio of at least 2.0**, that is, are
   worth at least double the house they replaced. Declared alternative: a
   ratio of at least 1.5. Report the share with its numerator and
   denominator against each of the two figures, labelling which is the
   primary and which the alternative.

**Why these figures and not others.** The circulating quotation names
$350,000 and $1,000,000, which is a ratio of about 2.9. The claim as put is
"about triple". A fair-minded holder of the view would accept at least 2.5
as a faithful reading of "about triple", since a median of 2.5 or more on a
population is a stronger statement than the single example implies is
routine, and would not accept a threshold set at or near 1.0, which is the
truism the published proposition tested. An opponent of the claim would
accept that 2.0 is not "triple": a population median of 2.0 is a doubling,
which is a real and large difference but not the thing the quotation
asserts. The primary magnitude threshold is therefore set at 2.5, between
the two, and the alternative at 2.0 so that a reader can see the result
under the reading most favourable to the claim's holder that an opponent
would still recognise as a distinct claim. The majority threshold is set at
2.0 rather than at 2.5 because it asks a different question, not how large
the typical gap is but how widely a large gap is shared, and a doubling is
the plainest reading of "much more expensive" that both sides would
recognise as more than the truism. Its alternative, 1.5, is the weakest
reading under which the claim still says anything beyond direction. All four
figures are judgements. They are fixed here, before any result is consulted,
and may not be changed after the results are seen.

**The $350,000 and $1,000,000 figures are not cutoffs.** The alleged
$350,000 to $1,000,000 example cannot be verified as a specific case,
because its source and its property were not captured. It is context only
and it receives no verdict, and the finding on this claim must not be
presented as verification of it. The two round numbers are not cutoffs and
are not the test. Do not treat $350,000 and $1,000,000 as thresholds; the
test is the ratio thresholds fixed above, applied to the matched population,
and reviewers report the actual distributions on both sides of the
comparison.

**Stakes: what each verdict would mean.** Stated so that no verdict on this
claim is empty, and so that the reader can see in advance what is at stake in
each.

- **Supported** would mean the typical Edmonton teardown lot replaces a
  house with housing worth at least two and a half times as much per
  dwelling unit, and that most replacement dwelling units are worth at least
  double the house that came down. To a holder of the claim, that is the
  claim confirmed as a pattern and not as an anecdote. To an opponent, it
  would mean the argument that per-unit infill values are moderate, that
  what looks like a price jump is mostly the added unit count on the lot,
  does not hold on the matched population.
- **Contradicted** would mean the typical replacement dwelling unit is worth
  less than one and a half times the house it replaced and that fewer than
  half exceed even that, so the "$350,000 to $1,000,000" picture is not what
  the city-wide record shows. To a holder of the claim, that is the central
  fact of the objection lost, whatever else remains true about
  affordability. To an opponent, it is the evidence that per-unit
  replacement values sit far closer to the demolished stock than the
  circulating figures suggest. This verdict is reachable on the
  measure fixed here: a per-unit ratio can fall at or below 1.0 as readily
  as above it, because a lot can be redeveloped into several dwelling units
  each worth less than the single house removed, and nothing in the
  operationalization forecloses either outcome.
- **Partially supported** would mean the magnitude and the spread come apart:
  either a high typical ratio that most units do not share, or a widely
  shared doubling that falls short of "about triple". To a holder, only one
  material part of the claim survives, so they must give up either
  "typically about triple" or the claim that a doubling is widespread. To an
  opponent, one material part is established and cannot be dismissed, while
  the other is not established.
- **Not established** would mean the public record does not carry the
  matched, unit-level series the proposition is stated in, or that the
  bounds on the missing cases straddle a threshold. To a holder, the claim
  remains unproven and this review cannot be cited as confirmation. To an
  opponent, the claim remains unrefuted and missing evidence cannot be cited
  as showing that replacement values are moderate. Both sides would need a
  sufficiently complete matched series to change that result. It is not a
  finding that the ratio is small.

## What no claim in this brief tests

- **The causal question.** Whether infill causes housing to be more or less
  affordable city-wide is out of scope for every claim here, for the reason
  set out under claim 3. No verdict in this brief may be read as answering
  it. Test it only in a separate brief with a declared counterfactual and
  period.
- **The City's affordable-housing definitions.** They concern housing
  receiving government assistance and expressly exclude market home
  ownership (YF-EV-0050), so market infill falls outside them by
  construction. Do not classify infill against them here.
- **Renting.** No claim here tests what it costs to rent a new infill unit.
  CMHC rent cells do not state whether the rent includes utilities and no
  published source carries contract rents for new infill units (YF-EV-0039).
  Report published rents as context where they exist, labelled, carrying no
  verdict.
- **Anything about a particular lot, from claims 1 to 3.** Those three are
  category-level claims. The lot-level question is claim 4.

## Scope

Geography: land within the present-day boundary of Edmonton for the
assessment and permit series and for CMHC City of Edmonton figures; the
Greater Edmonton Area for REALTORS Association figures, labelled as such
wherever used; the Edmonton census metropolitan area for the price indices,
labelled as such. State the geography of every figure.

Time: the windows declared in each claim, all closing on the freeze date
2026-09-02.

In scope as evidence: what the City reports about what is demolished and
what is built; prior tenure, occupancy, prior rent and prior unit count of
demolished houses, and recorded tenant displacement.

Out of scope: whether infill should be permitted or encouraged; design and
neighbourhood-character disputes; construction quality; property tax
effects; the causal question above; and whether Edmonton was planned around
low-density housing (see the low-density-history story).
