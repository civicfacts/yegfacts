## Story

The circulating claim describes an older house being demolished and
replaced by housing alleged to be much more expensive. Two separate things
are being asserted. One is a price relationship on lots where a dwelling
was demolished and replaced. The other is whether that replacement housing
is affordable on an income-based test. This story separates the two and
tests each on its own terms. Topic: housing-development.

Claims in this story, reviewed and reported separately:

- `ip-teardown-price-gap`: the price relationship on linked
  demolition-and-replacement lots.
- `ip-infill-affordable`: whether replacement housing on those lots fails
  the predeclared 30 percent shelter-cost-to-income test.

Each claim carries its own finding, panel agreement and evidence-basis
label. Reviewer confidence appears only beside the reviewer who assigned
it. The price relationship stays in Claim 1 and is not part of the
affordability proposition. Where the two findings disagree, report the
disagreement in synthesis; do not join them into one proposition.

## Why this story (selection rationale)

- Prevalence: the founder supplied one possibly composite formulation from
  one Edmonton infill thread. No sample of other posts was captured, so
  this brief cannot establish the claim's prevalence or whether this
  wording represents how it circulates.
- Civic importance: mature-neighbourhood infill rules and the zoning bylaw
  renewal are live council business, and affordability is argued over on
  several sides.
- Verifiability: property assessments, demolition and building permits,
  market association reports and CMHC series are published, each with known
  limits, so the answer turns on being explicit about which measure is used
  and what it can establish.

## Unit of analysis, fixed for every claim

The verdict denominator for every claim in this brief is separately
saleable replacement dwellings, each counted once. Both propositions are
about the price and the affordability of replacement houses, so the
dwelling is the unit they are stated in and the unit they are counted in.

The matched lot pair remains the structure that links the evidence: one lot
on which one existing dwelling was demolished, paired with the replacement
dwelling or dwellings built on that same lot. Every replacement dwelling in
a verdict denominator comes from a matched lot pair, and every figure
states the lot it belongs to, so that clustering by lot can be seen.

Two lot-level rules are reported as named sensitivities and never carry a
verdict:

1. **The "every replacement" rule:** the share of matched lot pairs on
   which every separately saleable replacement dwelling falls in the
   claimed direction.
2. **The "at least one replacement" rule:** the share of matched lot pairs
   on which at least one separately saleable replacement dwelling falls in
   the claimed direction.

Report both lot-level shares alongside every dwelling-level verdict share,
each with its numerator and denominator, and report how many separately
saleable replacement dwellings stood on each lot. Neither lot rule may
displace the dwelling-level result: a rule under which one lower-priced
dwelling removes the other replacement dwellings on that lot from the
verdict does not test the proposition as written.

Lots on which more than one dwelling was demolished do not enter a verdict
denominator. Report them as a separate series, stating for each the number
of dwellings demolished, their values, the replacement dwellings and their
prices, and state how many such lots the frame contained.

These rules are fixed before any share is calculated and may not be changed
after the results are seen.

## Claims under review

### Claim 1, id: ip-teardown-price-gap

**Normalized proposition:** Among separately saleable replacement
dwellings on Edmonton lots where one existing dwelling was demolished for
infill, more than 50 percent have a higher total assessed value on their
first full assessment roll after completion than the demolished dwelling's
total assessed value on the assessment roll in force in the year before
demolition, both taken in the City's market-value assessment basis and
compared in constant dollars. Count every separately saleable replacement
dwelling once. Report lot-level shares under the "every replacement" and "at
least one replacement" rules as declared sensitivities. Report alongside the
verdict the median replacement-to-demolished assessed-value ratio and the
distribution of that ratio, so the size of the gap is reported and not only
its direction.

The alleged $350,000 to $1,000,000 example cannot be verified as a specific
case, because its source and its property were not captured. It is context
only and it receives no verdict, and the findings on the claims in this
brief must not be presented as verification of it. The two round numbers
are not cutoffs and are not the test. Do not treat $350,000 and $1,000,000
as thresholds; report the actual distributions on both sides of the
comparison.

**Verdict measure, fixed here.** Compare the demolished dwelling's assessed
value with the replacement's assessed value on two bases, and report both
for every matched lot pair:

1. **Total assessed value per dwelling.** The whole total assessed value of
   each separately saleable replacement dwelling on its first full
   assessment roll after completion, undivided.
2. **Per unit.** The same assessed value divided by the number of dwelling
   units the replacement dwelling contains, where it contains more than
   one.

Claim 1 is Supported only when the majority threshold below is met on the
total-assessed-value-per-dwelling basis. Total assessed value per separately
saleable dwelling is the closest predeclared measure to the quotation's
reference to the price of a replacement house that values both sides of the
pair at comparable moments, the demolished dwelling in the year before it
came down and the replacement in its first full year standing, so that the
comparison is not also carrying the demolished dwelling's holding-period
appreciation. This is an operational choice, not a verified statement of the
original speaker's intent. The per-unit result is reported alongside as the
qualification on the finding, with its own share, median ratio and
distribution, and it is stated in the interpretation notes whenever the two
bases point in different directions. A per-unit result does not carry the
verdict on its own, and neither does a lot total: an added unit count does
not erase a high assessed value, and a high lot total does not erase a lower
per-dwelling value.

No per-square-foot, per-bedroom, sale-price, asking-price, aggregate-title
or rent measure substitutes for the assessed-value comparison in Claim 1's
verdict.

**Population.** Restrict this claim to linked demolition-and-replacement
lots: a lot with a demolition permit for an existing dwelling and an
identifiable replacement dwelling or dwellings built on the same lot.
Infill that is not a teardown (for example redevelopment of a vacant or
non-residential parcel) is not part of this claim's population; if it is
examined at all, report it separately and label it.

**Geography.** The mature-neighbourhood restriction is not assumed and is
not part of the proposition. The city-wide set of linked
demolition-and-replacement lots is the primary set. Report two sets and give
both results: that city-wide primary set, and the mature-neighbourhood
subset of that same set.

The mature-neighbourhood subset is delimited by a frozen historical
boundary: the area covered by the City of Edmonton's Mature Neighbourhood
Overlay as it stood on 2023-12-31, the day before Zoning Bylaw 20001 retired
it, as published in the last consolidation of Zoning Bylaw 12800. Quote the
definition and state the consolidation date and the version reviewed.

No mature-neighbourhood instrument is in force on the freeze date. The
Mature Neighbourhood Overlay was retired when Zoning Bylaw 20001 took effect
on 2024-01-01, so the boundary fixed above is a historical boundary, frozen
here for this review, and is not to be described as an instrument in force.
The City Plan's "redeveloping area" is a different boundary covering a
different area. Report it as a named sensitivity, with its own share,
numerator and denominator, and never substitute it for the frozen historical
boundary. Where the applicable boundary differed at the date a permit was
issued, say so and report which boundary was used.

**The majority threshold.** Claim 1 asks whether more than 50 percent of
separately saleable replacement dwellings show the higher-value direction
on the total-assessed-value-per-dwelling basis, each such dwelling counted
once. Report that share with its numerator and denominator. Report
separately the
median ratio, the full distribution (at minimum the quartiles and the
range), the count, and the number and character of unmatched or excluded
cases. A median ratio is not by itself evidence that most replacement
dwellings follow the pattern, and neither figure substitutes for the other.
Report the two lot-level sensitivities named in the unit-of-analysis
section alongside this share.

**Sampling, matching and missing data, predeclared.** Fix these rules
before looking at results and report any departure from them:

- Frame: all residential demolition permits issued in the window below, on
  land within the present-day boundary of Edmonton.
- Match: a demolition permit is matched to a replacement when a building
  permit or completed dwelling is recorded at the same parcel identifier or
  address after the demolition permit date. State the matching key used
  (parcel identifier preferred, address as fallback) and the number matched
  on each key.
- Inclusion: matched pairs where both a demolished-dwelling assessed value
  and a replacement assessed value are recorded on the declared series
  below.
- Exclusion: pairs where the replacement is non-residential. Report the
  excluded count and reason. Fire-order and safety-order demolitions are
  not excluded. Include every otherwise eligible residential teardown in
  the primary result, and report as a declared sensitivity the same result
  with fire-order and safety-order demolitions removed, with their count
  and the effect on the demolished-dwelling values.
- Completion lag: replacements not yet completed or not yet sold at the end
  of the window stay in the frame and are reported as unmatched, with their
  count. Do not drop them silently.
- Missing data: report the count and share of the frame lost at each step
  (no match, no replacement value, no old-house value) and describe how the
  lost cases differ from the retained cases on any observable attribute.

**Old-house value, predeclared.** Report two separate series and never
average them:

1. the City assessed total value of the demolished dwelling taken from the
   assessment roll in force in the year before the demolition, which is the
   last roll that valued the dwelling while it was standing, with that year
   stated. This is the verdict series;
2. the last arm's-length sale price of the demolished dwelling before
   demolition, with its date. This is the demolished side of the
   sale-to-sale sensitivity below and never enters the verdict.

Where the assessment separates land from improvement, report total assessed
value, land value and improvement value separately. Where the assessment
year nearest the demolition permit date is a different year from the roll
named above, report that year's figure as a labelled sensitivity. Do not
select whichever series is available after seeing results; report which
pairs have one series, which have both, and the result under each.

**Replacement assessed value, predeclared.** The replacement dwelling's
assessed value is its total assessed value on its first full assessment roll
after completion, and that assessment year is stated. This is the verdict
series. Both assessment years, the demolished dwelling's and the
replacement's, are stated for every pair.

**Admissible verdict series, predeclared.** Only matched assessed values
enter Claim 1's verdict series: the demolished dwelling's total assessed
value on the assessment roll in force in the year before demolition, and the
total assessed value of each separately saleable replacement dwelling on its
first full assessment roll after completion. Both sides are taken in the
City's market-value assessment basis, and both are compared in constant
dollars with the index and base year named under Timing below. A matched lot
pair that lacks either of those assessed values is missing from the verdict
series. It is not filled in from another measure. Report its absence in the
missing-data counts.

**The sale-to-sale sensitivity, predeclared.** The comparison of the
demolished dwelling's last arm's-length sale price before demolition with
the first arm's-length sale price of each separately saleable replacement
dwelling after completion is a named sensitivity. Report it with its own
share, numerator, denominator, median ratio and distribution, labelled as a
sensitivity, and never let it carry the verdict. For every pair in it,
report the date of each sale and the gap in years between the two sale
dates. Pairs whose gap exceeds ten years are excluded from the sensitivity's
headline figure, because across a longer holding period that comparison
measures the demolished dwelling's own appreciation as much as the
redevelopment. Report the headline figure, the count and character of the
pairs excluded by the ten-year rule, and the result with those pairs
included, each labelled.

Claim 2's threshold test is not changed by any of this. It uses the
replacement dwelling's first arm's-length sale price where one exists, and
otherwise that dwelling's first full assessment after completion.

Report each of the following as its own labelled series, never pooled into
the verdict series and never mixed within one reported figure:

- arm's-length sale prices, on the timing rules above, with the date of
  each sale stated, as the sale-to-sale sensitivity above;
- listing or asking prices, labelled as asking;
- aggregate sale prices where a multi-unit replacement was sold on one
  title without separate unit titles, with the unit count stated and
  labelled as an aggregate;
- property-level contract rents at first occupancy, where recorded;
- CMHC rental market survey rents for the same bedroom count, year and
  neighbourhood or zone, labelled as a survey figure rather than a
  property-level figure.

For every case, record which series supplied each figure and how many cases
each series covered. Asking price, first sale price, assessed value,
aggregate multi-unit sale price, per-unit condominium sale price and rent
are not interchangeable.

**Tenure changes, predeclared.** Compare assessed value with assessed value
and contract rent with prior contract rent. A matched lot pair in which the
demolished dwelling and the replacement are of different tenure, for
example an owner-occupied dwelling replaced by rental housing or a rental
replaced by ownership, is reported as a separate series with its count. Do
not assign such a pair a higher-cost or lower-cost direction and do not
count it in a verdict denominator: an assessed value and a monthly rent are
not comparable figures, and this brief predeclares no common shelter-cost
calculation that would make them so. Where both the demolished dwelling and
the replacement were rental, compare the replacement's contract rent at
first occupancy with the prior dwelling's recorded rent, and report that
comparison as its own series with its own share, median ratio and count,
separate from the assessed-value verdict series.

**Other measures reported alongside.** Report per matched lot pair: total
assessed value per dwelling, per-unit assessed value, total assessed value
for all replacement dwellings on the lot, bedroom count, tenure, and the
total replacement-unit count. The verdict rests on the two bases named
above and on no other measure.

Price per square foot and price per bedroom are supplementary measures.
Report them where the record supports them, never as substitutes for total
assessed value or rent.

**Timing, predeclared.** Report every comparison in nominal dollars and in
constant dollars. For an annual assessment roll, treat July 1 of the
preceding calendar year as the value date, which is the City's valuation
date for that roll. The primary constant-dollar calculation uses the
Statistics Canada Consumer Price Index, all-items, Edmonton census
metropolitan area, monthly series, for that July, with July 2025 as the
base period. Report as the declared alternative the same comparison using
the annual-average CPI for the valuation year with 2025 as the base year,
with its own numerator and denominator, and state whether it changes any
dwelling's direction or the verdict share. Report as a further declared
sensitivity the same comparison adjusted with the Statistics Canada New
Housing Price Index for the Edmonton census metropolitan area, on the same
July value dates, labelled as a market-index adjustment rather than a
general price adjustment. State the date of each figure used and the gap
in years between the two sides of every pair.

**Window and cohort, predeclared.** Primary cohort: demolition permits
issued in the ten complete calendar years ending with the most recent
complete calendar year before the freeze date, 2026-09-01, matched to
replacements recorded up to that date. Declared
sensitivity: the five complete calendar years ending with the same year.
Report both. Where the data supports it, also report the cohort defined by
replacement completion year rather than demolition permit year, and say
whether the two cohort definitions change the result. Do not blend a
partial year into an annual figure.

**Datasets to look for.** City of Edmonton property assessment data on the
open data portal; demolition permits and building permits on the same
portal; any City infill or mature-neighbourhood monitoring reporting;
REALTORS Association of Edmonton monthly and annual reports for sale prices
by dwelling type; CMHC Housing Market Information for starts, completions
and absorbed prices by dwelling type; CMHC rental market survey for rents;
Statistics Canada price indices for the adjustments named above. Where two
datasets cannot be joined lot to lot, say so: a comparison of citywide
averages answers a different question than the one this claim asks, and
must be labelled as such if it is used at all.

### Claim 2, id: ip-infill-affordable

**Normalized proposition (descriptive):** Among separately saleable
replacement dwellings in the matched set, more than 50 percent would
require shelter costs equal to or greater than 30 percent of before-tax
income for a hypothetical household earning the declared City median
income, under the 25-year amortization and the other primary assumptions
below.

The household is a modelled household, not the household that bought or
rented the dwelling. The standard 30 percent measure compares one
household's shelter costs with that household's own income; this brief
cannot do that, because the record does not carry purchaser incomes, so the
proposition says what it tests. The 30-year amortization, other household
incomes and other household types are named sensitivities, reported with
their own results and labelled, and none of them carries the verdict.

The City of Edmonton's own affordable-housing definition is not tested in
this story. That definition concerns housing receiving government financial
assistance or operating under a government agreement, and the City's
guidebook says it does not apply to market home ownership, so it is not a
second reading of a captured objection to market replacement prices.
Qualification as City-defined affordable housing needs its own captured
source and its own brief. Do not classify replacement dwellings against it
here.

### Operationalization for Claim 2

**The price relationship is not part of the affordability claim.** A
replacement dwelling can exceed an affordability threshold while costing
less than the dwelling it replaced, and it can cost far more than the
dwelling it replaced while passing the threshold. The comparison with what
was demolished is Claim 1, and it is verdict-bearing there and nowhere
else. Report what the affordability test and the price comparison each
found; where they disagree, report the disagreement in synthesis and do not
join them into one proposition.

**The causal claim is not tested here, and why.** Whether infill "makes"
housing more affordable is a causal proposition. Testing it would require a
defined no-infill counterfactual, added supply, time lags and filtering
over a specified period, and the price comparisons in this brief cannot
establish any of that. No such causal claim was captured in the intake in a
form that fixes a counterfactual or a period. This brief therefore tests
the descriptive propositions above and does not test, and no verdict in it
may be read as testing, whether infill causes housing to be more or less
affordable. The causal effect of infill on city-wide housing costs is out
of scope for this review. Do not research or report it here. Test it only
in a separate brief with a declared counterfactual and period.

**Population, geography and unit.** The same linked
demolition-and-replacement lots as Claim 1, the same separately saleable
replacement dwellings as the verdict denominator, and the same city-wide
primary set and the same frozen historical mature-neighbourhood subset
reported separately.

**The test.** Apply the shelter-cost-to-income test to every separately
saleable replacement dwelling in the matched set. A dwelling meets the
affordability threshold when monthly shelter cost is less than 30 percent
of the modelled household's monthly before-tax income. A dwelling at 30
percent or more does not meet it. This is Statistics Canada's
classification in force on 2026-09-01, quoted rather than paraphrased.

**Affordability denominator and the majority threshold, predeclared.** The
verdict denominator is separately saleable replacement dwellings, each
counted once, and the majority threshold is more than 50 percent of that
denominator. Report the numerator and the denominator for that share.
Report alongside it the two lot-level sensitivities from the
unit-of-analysis section, and report every share broken down by tenure and
by bedroom count, together with the tenure and bedroom-count distribution
of the matched set. A dwelling whose test result is Not established under
the utilities rule below is unclassified: it is not counted as meeting or
as failing the test, its count and share of the denominator are stated, and
it is carried into the bounds rule under the verdict mapping below. Fix
these rules before any share is calculated.

**Assumptions for the shelter-cost-to-income test, fixed in this brief.**
Do not vary them after seeing results; report any input that could not be
obtained as missing evidence.

- Income source: Statistics Canada census median total before-tax household
  income for the City of Edmonton, from the most recent census available on
  the freeze date, with the census year stated. Where Statistics Canada
  publishes a later median household income estimate for the Edmonton
  census metropolitan area, report the test under that figure as well and
  label which figure produced which result. The declared City median
  household income is the primary case and it alone carries the Claim 2
  verdict. Report the test by each available household-income band or
  household type, matched to bedroom count where the source permits, as
  named sensitivities, labelling which income figure produced which
  result.
- Down payment: 20 percent of purchase price. Declared sensitivity: the
  regulatory minimum in force on 2026-09-01. For a price of $500,000 or
  less, 5 percent. For a price above $500,000 but below $1.5 million, 5
  percent on the first $500,000 and 10 percent on the remainder. For a
  price of $1.5 million or more, 20 percent. Include mortgage default
  insurance in the financed amount only where the mortgage is eligible for
  it, and state the applicable eligibility rule.
- Mortgage rate: the Bank of Canada five-year conventional mortgage rate
  for the month in which the price is dated, stated with that date.
- Amortization: the 25-year amortization is the primary case and it alone
  carries the Claim 2 verdict. Report the 30-year amortization as a named
  sensitivity and label which produced which result. Where a 30-year
  amortization is not available to the purchaser and dwelling class being
  tested, state the rule that makes it unavailable, with its source and
  date. Where the primary case cannot be computed for a dwelling, that
  dwelling is unclassified under the bounds rule; do not substitute the
  sensitivity for it.
- Property taxes: the City of Edmonton annual property tax levy for the
  subject property in the price year where published; otherwise the City's
  residential tax rate for that year applied to the assessed value. State
  which was used for each case.
- Utilities: heat, water and electricity. For rentals, follow the CMHC
  rental market survey convention and state whether the reported rent
  includes heat. For ownership, use a named published utility cost series
  with its source and year. Where a required utility input is unavailable
  for a dwelling, the complete shelter-cost test for that dwelling is Not
  established: report the calculation that excludes utilities as a labelled
  lower-bound sensitivity, and do not use it to classify that dwelling for
  the affordability verdict. That dwelling is unclassified and enters the
  bounds rule below. Do not substitute an unsourced allowance.
- Ownership shelter cost is mortgage principal and interest plus property
  taxes plus utilities, plus condominium fees where applicable, stated
  separately. Rental shelter cost is contract rent plus any utilities not
  included in rent.

**Verdict mapping, predeclared.** Each of the two claims carries its own
verdict and uses the methodology's four verdict words directly. There is no
compound rule and no conjunct, and neither claim's verdict depends on the
other claim's result.

Unclassified cases are handled by bounds. Calculate lower and upper bounds
over the complete verdict denominator. The lower bound treats every
unclassified case as not satisfying the claimed direction; the upper bound
treats every unclassified case as satisfying it. Report the unclassified
count and share separately.

- **Supported:** the lower bound exceeds 50 percent of that claim's verdict
  denominator.
- **Contradicted:** the usable evidence affirmatively establishes that 50
  percent or fewer of the verdict denominator satisfy the proposition, that
  is, the upper bound is 50 percent or less.
- **Not established:** the usable evidence establishes neither.
- **Partially supported:** only where the stated proposition contains a
  meaningful part that the evidence establishes while the rest overreaches.
  Name which part fails and on what evidence, as prompts/reviewer.md
  requires.

A favourable result on a supplementary comparator (price per square foot,
price per bedroom, or a comparison with new construction elsewhere in the
region) is not the "meaningful part" that justifies Partially supported.
For Claim 1, a per-unit result that differs from the result on the
total-assessed-value-per-dwelling basis is reported as the qualification on
the verdict, not as a "meaningful part" that changes it.

**Comparators, reported separately and never combined.** Each of the
following answers a different question. Label each one and do not merge
them into a single finding:

- the price or rent of the replacement against the housing it replaced on
  the same lot (this is Claim 1);
- the replacement against the median or typical price in the same
  neighbourhood in the same year;
- the replacement against new construction elsewhere in the region in the
  same year, including new suburban single-detached and new suburban row or
  townhouse product. Report regional new-construction comparisons as a
  separate comparator. Do not assume that it represents the relevant
  alternative for every buyer;
- rents against neighbourhood rents for units of the same bedroom count
  (CMHC rental market survey).

Where one of these measures does not exist in the public record, say so and
record it as missing evidence rather than substituting a proxy without
labelling it a proxy. Reporting a subset of the measures without saying
which ones were unavailable is not acceptable.

**Prior tenure and displacement, in scope as evidence.** Where the record
supports it, report for the demolished dwelling: whether it was owner
occupied or rented at the time of the demolition permit, the number of
dwelling units it contained, the rent charged, and any recorded notice to
tenants or municipal tracking of tenant displacement. Report these as
evidence about what was lost on the lot, with their sources and years, and
record them as missing evidence where the record does not support them.
This brief does not ask reviewers to estimate a displacement count that the
record does not contain.

**Transferred evidence.** Another city's evidence cannot answer these
Edmonton-specific propositions. It may supply context or illustrate a
mechanism, and if used it carries the transferability statement the
methodology requires (why relevant, how it differs, what can and cannot be
inferred), but Edmonton-specific missing evidence stays recorded as
missing. Do not substitute comparator evidence for an Edmonton figure the
record does not contain.

## Scope

Geography: land within the present-day boundary of Edmonton. Both the
city-wide set of linked demolition-and-replacement lots and the
mature-neighbourhood subset are reported. Regional and suburban figures are
in scope only as the separately labelled comparator named above.

Time: the primary cohort and the declared sensitivity window above.

In scope as evidence: prior tenure, occupancy, prior rent and prior unit
count of the demolished dwelling, and recorded tenant displacement.

Out of scope: whether infill should be permitted or encouraged; design and
neighbourhood-character disputes; construction quality; property tax
effects beyond their use as an input to the shelter-cost test; the causal
question of what infill does to housing costs city-wide; and whether
Edmonton was planned around low-density housing (see the
low-density-history story).

## Required calculations

- Every price or assessed-value comparison states its source, its year,
  whether the figures are nominal or inflation adjusted (and if adjusted,
  the index and base year), and whether the figure is per lot or per unit.
- Assessed values and sale prices are reported as separate series and never
  combined into one average. Claim 1's verdict series is the assessed-value
  pair; the sale-to-sale comparison is reported as a named sensitivity,
  stating for every pair the gap in years between the two sale dates and
  whether the pair falls inside or outside the sensitivity's headline figure
  under the ten-year rule.
- Every median, share or average states its count, its numerator, its
  denominator and its geography, and states whether the denominator is
  separately saleable replacement dwellings (the verdict denominator) or
  matched lot pairs (a sensitivity).
- Every step of the sampling and matching pipeline states the count
  entering and the count leaving, and the reason for the loss.
- Every shelter-cost calculation states each of the fixed assumptions above
  and the figure used for it.
- Amounts are Canadian dollars unless stated otherwise; no silent
  conversions.

## Reviewer instructions of special note

- Apply the same documented sampling and matching rules to every eligible
  redevelopment, and report the complete resulting distribution.
- Report what individual cases establish, label them as cases, and do not
  infer population frequency from them.
- Where available, report total assessed value, land value and improvement
  value separately.
- Do not join datasets that were not built to be joined without stating
  what the join assumed and what it could get wrong.
- Keep the two claims separate. A finding about the price relationship on
  redeveloped lots is not by itself a finding about the affordability
  threshold, and the reverse.
