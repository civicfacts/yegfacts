# Editorial note on the reframe

The proposition this claim was published under asked whether the housing
built on an Edmonton teardown lot is worth more than the house demolished
for it, and that is not a claim anyone is making: new housing on a lot is
newer, usually larger and usually denser than what came down, so a verdict
on it, whichever way it fell, told the reader nothing worth knowing. The
founder put it plainly when he read the published page: "that's poor claim
to make". He is right, and the fault is the editor's before it is anyone
else's. The captured quotation, "A $350,000 house is torn down and a
$1,000,000 house goes up in its place", is an assertion about magnitude,
roughly three times over, and across five framing-check rounds and three
runs I accepted each narrowing that made the proposition easier for the
record to answer until the magnitude had been drained out of it altogether,
in the name of fairness to the claim's holder. Fairness to a claim's holder
means testing the claim they actually make, at its strongest, with the size
they assert; it never means testing something safer that they would not
recognise. This brief therefore restates the claim as the magnitude claim it
is, under the same id so the re-run replaces the published finding rather
than sitting beside it, with two thresholds and one alternative each fixed
in writing before any result is consulted, and with the "typically" reading
made explicit because the claim is a generalisation and must be tested as
one. Methodology v1.10 puts the same test into the framing check itself, so
that the next brief drafted this way is caught before it reaches a panel
rather than after it reaches a reader.

Stew, 2026-09-02
