# Review brief: Infill teardown prices, magnitude re-run

Status: FROZEN 2026-09-02 on framing check 2 FRAME OK (framing/check-2.md),
the first brief through the v1.10 stakes test. Magnitude re-run of the
published claim (see the story's 2026-09-02 changelog entry).
Methodology: v1.10

## Story

The circulating claim describes an older house being demolished and replaced
by housing alleged to be much more expensive. The published run split that
objection into two claims and tested each on its own terms. This brief
re-runs one of them, the price relationship, under a proposition that
carries the magnitude the circulating quotation asserts rather than only its
direction. Topic: housing-development.

Claims in this story, reviewed and reported separately:

- `ip-teardown-price-gap`: the price relationship on linked
  demolition-and-replacement lots. This is the only claim in this brief.
- `ip-infill-affordable`: whether replacement housing on those lots fails
  the predeclared 30 percent shelter-cost-to-income test. It is not re-run
  here and no reviewer returns a verdict on it in this run. Its published
  finding stands.

The claim in this brief carries the same id the published finding carries,
`ip-teardown-price-gap`, so this run's result replaces that finding as a
verdict change or an updated entry rather than standing beside it as a
second claim. The claim carries its own finding, panel agreement and
evidence-basis label. Reviewer confidence appears only beside the reviewer
who assigned it. The affordability question is not part of this
proposition and no figure from it enters this claim's verdict.

**Why this claim is being re-run.** The published proposition asked whether
the housing built on a teardown lot is worth more than the house that came
down. No informed person disputes that, so a verdict on it, whichever way it
fell, tells a reader almost nothing. The captured quotation is a claim about
magnitude, not direction. This brief tests the magnitude. Methodology v1.10
records the change to the framing check that this run is the first to
operate under. The intake record in `intake.md` sets out the founder's
objection and the editor's position in full.

## Why this story (selection rationale)

- Prevalence: the founder supplied one possibly composite formulation from
  one Edmonton infill thread. No sample of other posts was captured, so
  this brief cannot establish the claim's prevalence or whether this
  wording represents how it circulates.
- Civic importance: mature-neighbourhood infill rules and the zoning bylaw
  renewal are live council business, and affordability is argued over on
  several sides.
- Verifiability: property assessments, demolition and building permits,
  market association reports and CMHC series are published, each with known
  limits, so the answer turns on being explicit about which measure is used
  and what it can establish.

## Unit of analysis, fixed for this claim

The unit of analysis in this brief is the replacement dwelling unit, counted
once. A replacement dwelling unit is each self-contained dwelling unit in the
replacement housing on the lot, counted once, whether it is separately titled
or held with other dwelling units on one title. A single-title multi-unit
building, for example a purpose-built rental fourplex, is four replacement
dwelling units and not one. The proposition is about the price of the housing
people live in, so the dwelling unit is the unit it is stated in and the unit
it is counted in. A self-contained dwelling unit is counted once and is never
fractional; a denominator containing fractional dwelling units does not
conform to this brief.

The verdict denominator is every replacement dwelling unit so defined.

**How a replacement dwelling unit is valued, fixed here.**

1. **A separately titled dwelling**, that is, the only dwelling unit on its
   title. Its value is its own total assessed value, or its own sale price
   where a sale series is in use. This is the "total value per dwelling"
   measure, and in this brief that language applies to separately titled
   dwellings and to no other case.
2. **A dwelling unit inside a single-title multi-unit building.** Its value
   is the building-average assessed value per dwelling unit, that is, the
   building's total assessed value divided by the number of dwelling units
   the building contains. The building average is the rule for single-title
   buildings, and the undivided building total is never compared with the
   demolished house. For a single-title multi-unit building, this
   calculation assigns the same building-average value to every dwelling
   unit. It does not estimate the distinct market value of any particular
   unit.

For every replacement dwelling unit, state which of these two cases it falls
in, the number of dwelling units on its title, and, where a division was
made, the building total that was divided.

The demolished house's value is not affected by this rule. It stays that
dwelling's total assessed value on the roll named below, undivided.

The matched lot pair remains the structure that links the evidence: one lot
on which one existing dwelling was demolished, paired with the replacement
dwelling or dwellings built on that same lot. Every replacement dwelling
unit in the verdict denominator comes from a matched lot pair, and every
figure states the lot it belongs to, so that clustering by lot can be seen.

Two lot-level rules are reported as named sensitivities and never carry a
verdict:

1. **The "every replacement" rule:** the share of matched lot pairs on
   which every replacement dwelling unit falls in the claimed direction and
   meets the claimed magnitude.
2. **The "at least one replacement" rule:** the share of matched lot pairs
   on which at least one replacement dwelling unit does.

Report both lot-level shares alongside every unit-level verdict figure, each
with its numerator and denominator, and report how many replacement dwelling
units stood on each lot and how they were distributed across titles. Neither
lot rule may displace the unit-level result: a rule under which one
lower-priced dwelling unit removes the other replacement dwelling units on
that lot from the verdict does not test the proposition as written.

Lots on which more than one dwelling was demolished do not enter the verdict
denominator. Report them as a separate series, stating for each the number
of dwellings demolished, their values, the replacement dwellings and their
prices, and state how many such lots the frame contained.

These rules are fixed before any figure is calculated and may not be changed
after the results are seen.

## Claim under review

### Claim 1, id: ip-teardown-price-gap

**Normalized proposition:** On Edmonton lots where one house was demolished
for infill, the housing built in its place is typically worth about three
times the house that came down: the median ratio of the replacement dwelling
unit's assessed value to the demolished house's assessed value, in constant
dollars, is at least 2.5, and more than half of matched replacement dwelling
units are worth at least double the demolished house.

**The ratio, defined.** For each matched replacement dwelling unit, the
ratio is that unit's assessed value divided by the demolished house's total
assessed value on the same lot, both sides taken in the City's market-value
assessment basis and both converted to constant dollars with the index and
base period named under Timing below. The replacement dwelling unit's
assessed value is its own total assessed value on its first full assessment
roll after completion where it is a separately titled dwelling, and the
building-average assessed value per dwelling unit, the building's total
assessed value on that roll divided by its number of dwelling units, where
it sits in a single-title multi-unit building. The demolished house's
assessed value is its total assessed value on the assessment roll in force
in the year before demolition, undivided. Count every replacement dwelling
unit once. Every otherwise eligible replacement dwelling unit enters this
comparison regardless of the tenure of the demolished or the replacement
housing.

**The claim is a generalisation and is tested as one.** The word "typically"
in the proposition is doing real work and it is not a hedge. The captured
quotation offers one example, a $350,000 house and a $1,000,000
replacement, as evidence for a pattern, and the pattern is the claim. One
example cannot establish a pattern, and this brief does not ask reviewers to
verify that example. It asks what the matched population does. Both
predeclared thresholds below are statements about the population: a median
across matched replacement dwelling units, and a share of them. A result on
a handful of cases, however striking, does not answer either.

**The two predeclared thresholds, fixed before any result is seen.**

1. **The magnitude threshold, primary: the median ratio is at least 2.5.**
   Declared alternative: at least 2.0. Report the median ratio, and report
   the result against each of the two figures, labelling which is the
   primary and which the alternative.
2. **The majority threshold, primary: more than 50 percent of matched
   replacement dwelling units have a ratio of at least 2.0**, that is, are
   worth at least double the house they replaced. Declared alternative: a
   ratio of at least 1.5. Report the share with its numerator and
   denominator against each of the two figures, labelling which is the
   primary and which the alternative.

**Why these figures and not others.** The circulating quotation names
$350,000 and $1,000,000, which is a ratio of about 2.9. The claim as put is
"about triple". A fair-minded holder of the view would accept at least 2.5
as a faithful reading of "about triple", since a median of 2.5 or more on a
population is a stronger statement than the single example implies is
routine, and would not accept a threshold set at or near 1.0, which is the
truism the published proposition tested. An opponent of the claim would
accept that 2.0 is not "triple": a population median of 2.0 is a doubling,
which is a real and large difference but not the thing the quotation
asserts. The primary magnitude threshold is therefore set at 2.5, between
the two, and the alternative at 2.0 so that a reader can see the result
under the reading most favourable to the claim's holder that an opponent
would still recognise as a distinct claim. The majority threshold is set at
2.0 rather than at 2.5 because it asks a different question, not how large
the typical gap is but how widely a large gap is shared, and a doubling is
the plainest reading of "much more expensive" that both sides would
recognise as more than the truism. Its alternative, 1.5, is the weakest
reading under which the claim still says anything beyond direction. All four
figures are judgements. They are fixed here, before any result is consulted,
and may not be changed after the results are seen.

**The $350,000 and $1,000,000 figures are not cutoffs.** The alleged
$350,000 to $1,000,000 example cannot be verified as a specific case,
because its source and its property were not captured. It is context only
and it receives no verdict, and the finding on this claim must not be
presented as verification of it. The two round numbers are not cutoffs and
are not the test. Do not treat $350,000 and $1,000,000 as thresholds; the
test is the ratio thresholds fixed above, applied to the matched population,
and reviewers report the actual distributions on both sides of the
comparison.

**Verdict mapping for this claim, predeclared.** The primary measure is the
ratio series defined above, on the primary thresholds, over the city-wide
primary set.

- **Supported:** both primary thresholds are met on the primary measure,
  that is, the median ratio is at least 2.5 and more than 50 percent of
  matched replacement dwelling units have a ratio of at least 2.0.
- **Partially supported:** exactly one of the two primary thresholds is met.
  Name which one is met and which one fails, and on what evidence, as
  prompts/reviewer.md requires.
- **Contradicted:** the median ratio is below 1.5 and fewer than half of
  matched replacement dwelling units have a ratio of at least 1.5.
- **Not established:** any other combination of results, and any case in
  which the bounds rule below leaves the result undetermined.

**Unclassified cases and the bounds rule, predeclared.** Calculate bounds
over the complete verdict denominator, which is every matched replacement
dwelling unit, including those that cannot be classified because a required
value is missing.

- For the majority threshold, the lower bound treats every unclassified unit
  as failing the threshold and the upper bound treats every unclassified
  unit as meeting it.
- For the median ratio, the lower bound is the median computed with every
  unclassified unit assigned a ratio below the lowest observed ratio, and
  the upper bound is the median computed with every unclassified unit
  assigned a ratio above the highest observed ratio.
- Report the unclassified count and share separately, and describe how the
  unclassified cases differ from the classified ones on any observable
  attribute.
- A threshold counts as met only where the lower bound meets it, and counts
  as failed only where the upper bound fails it. Where a bound straddles a
  threshold, that threshold is undetermined and the verdict is Not
  established. State which threshold was undetermined and why.

**Report alongside the verdict.** The full distribution of the ratio, at
minimum the deciles, the quartiles, the median, the count and the range, on
the primary measure and city-wide. Report the same distribution for the
mature-neighbourhood subset. Report the two lot-level sensitivities named in
the unit-of-analysis section, each with its numerator and denominator.
Report the demolished-house value distribution and the replacement
dwelling-unit value distribution separately, each with its median,
quartiles, range and count, so that a reader can see both sides of the ratio
and not only the ratio. A median ratio is not by itself evidence that most
replacement dwelling units follow the pattern, and a majority share is not
by itself evidence of magnitude; neither figure substitutes for the other,
which is why the proposition carries both and the verdict mapping turns on
both.

**Verdict measure, fixed here.** Compare the demolished house's total
assessed value with each replacement dwelling unit's assessed value, taken
on the two rules fixed in the unit-of-analysis section, and report for every
matched lot pair which rule supplied each figure:

1. **A separately titled replacement dwelling.** Its total value per
   dwelling, that is, its whole total assessed value on its first full
   assessment roll after completion, undivided.
2. **A replacement dwelling unit inside a single-title multi-unit
   building.** The building-average assessed value per dwelling unit: the
   building's total assessed value on its first full assessment roll after
   completion divided by the number of dwelling units the building
   contains. The building average is the rule for single-title buildings.
   This calculation assigns the same building-average value to every
   dwelling unit in that building. It does not estimate the distinct market
   value of any particular unit.

The value of the dwelling unit a household occupies is the closest
predeclared measure to the quotation's reference to the price of a
replacement house that values both sides of the pair at comparable moments,
the demolished house in the year before it came down and the replacement in
its first full year standing, so that the comparison is not also carrying
the demolished house's holding-period appreciation. It is also the measure
that compares like with like: an undivided multi-unit building total set
against one demolished house measures how much housing was built as much as
what that housing costs. This is an operational choice, not a verified
statement of the original speaker's intent.

Report alongside the verdict, for every matched lot pair and never as a
verdict measure, the undivided total assessed value of each replacement
title, the number of dwelling units on that title, and the total assessed
value of all replacement housing on the lot, each with its own median ratio
and distribution and, where a share is reported, its own share. State in the
interpretation notes whenever an undivided title total points in a different
direction or a different magnitude from the building-average values per
dwelling unit it contains. Neither an undivided title total nor a lot total
carries the verdict: an added unit count does not erase a high replacement
dwelling unit value, and a high lot total does not erase a lower replacement
dwelling unit value.

No per-square-foot, per-bedroom, sale-price, asking-price, undivided
single-title building total or rent measure substitutes for the
assessed-value comparison in this claim's verdict.

**Population.** Restrict this claim to linked demolition-and-replacement
lots: a lot with a demolition permit for an existing dwelling and an
identifiable replacement dwelling or dwellings built on the same lot.
Infill that is not a teardown (for example redevelopment of a vacant or
non-residential parcel) is not part of this claim's population; if it is
examined at all, report it separately and label it.

**Geography.** The mature-neighbourhood restriction is not assumed and is
not part of the proposition. The city-wide set of linked
demolition-and-replacement lots is the primary set. Report two sets and give
both results: that city-wide primary set, and the mature-neighbourhood
subset of that same set.

The mature-neighbourhood subset is delimited by a frozen historical
boundary: the area covered by the City of Edmonton's Mature Neighbourhood
Overlay as it stood on 2023-12-31, the day before Zoning Bylaw 20001 retired
it, as published in the last consolidation of Zoning Bylaw 12800. Quote the
definition and state the consolidation date and the version reviewed.

No mature-neighbourhood instrument is in force on the freeze date. The
Mature Neighbourhood Overlay was retired when Zoning Bylaw 20001 took effect
on 2024-01-01, so the boundary fixed above is a historical boundary, frozen
here for this review, and is not to be described as an instrument in force.
The City Plan's "redeveloping area" is a different boundary covering a
different area. Report it as a named sensitivity, with its own median ratio,
share, numerator and denominator, and never substitute it for the frozen
historical boundary. Where the applicable boundary differed at the date a
permit was issued, say so and report which boundary was used.

**Sampling, matching and missing data, predeclared.** Fix these rules
before looking at results and report any departure from them:

- Frame: all residential demolition permits issued in the window below, on
  land within the present-day boundary of Edmonton.
- Match: a demolition permit is matched to a replacement when a building
  permit or completed dwelling is recorded at the same parcel identifier or
  address after the demolition permit date. State the matching key used
  (parcel identifier preferred, address as fallback) and the number matched
  on each key.
- Inclusion: matched pairs where both a demolished-house assessed value
  and a replacement assessed value are recorded on the declared series
  below.
- Exclusion: pairs where the replacement is non-residential. Report the
  excluded count and reason. Fire-order and safety-order demolitions are
  not excluded. Include every otherwise eligible residential teardown in
  the primary result, and report as a declared sensitivity the same result
  with fire-order and safety-order demolitions removed, with their count
  and the effect on the demolished-house values.
- Completion lag: replacements not yet completed or not yet sold at the end
  of the window stay in the frame and are reported as unmatched, with their
  count. Do not drop them silently.
- Missing data: report the count and share of the frame lost at each step
  (no match, no replacement value, no old-house value) and describe how the
  lost cases differ from the retained cases on any observable attribute.

**Old-house value, predeclared.** Report two separate series and never
average them:

1. the City assessed total value of the demolished house taken from the
   assessment roll in force in the year before the demolition, which is the
   last roll that valued the dwelling while it was standing, with that year
   stated. This is the verdict series;
2. the last arm's-length sale price of the demolished house before
   demolition, with its date. This is the demolished side of the
   sale-to-sale sensitivity below and never enters the verdict.

Where the assessment separates land from improvement, report total assessed
value, land value and improvement value separately. Where the assessment
year nearest the demolition permit date is a different year from the roll
named above, report that year's figure as a labelled sensitivity. Do not
select whichever series is available after seeing results; report which
pairs have one series, which have both, and the result under each.

**Replacement assessed value, predeclared.** A separately titled
replacement dwelling's assessed value is its total assessed value on its
first full assessment roll after completion. A replacement dwelling unit
inside a single-title multi-unit building takes as its value the
building-average assessed value per dwelling unit: that building's total
assessed value on its first full assessment roll after completion divided by
the number of dwelling units the building contains, with the building total
and the unit count both stated. That figure assigns the same
building-average value to every dwelling unit in the building and does not
estimate the distinct market value of any particular unit. The assessment
year is stated in either case. This is the verdict series. Both assessment
years, the demolished house's and the replacement's, are stated for every
pair.

**Admissible verdict series, predeclared.** Only matched assessed values
enter this claim's verdict series: the demolished house's total assessed
value on the assessment roll in force in the year before demolition, and
each replacement dwelling unit's assessed value on the first full assessment
roll after completion, which is that dwelling's total assessed value where
it is separately titled and the building-average assessed value per dwelling
unit, the building's total assessed value divided by its number of dwelling
units, where the unit sits in a single-title multi-unit building. An
undivided single-title building total is not an admissible verdict figure;
it is reported alongside. Tenure does not affect admissibility: every
otherwise eligible replacement dwelling unit enters this series whatever the
tenure of the demolished or the replacement housing, and a tenure change is
reported as a qualification on the verdict. Both sides are taken in the
City's market-value assessment basis, and both are compared in constant
dollars with the index and base year named under Timing below. A matched lot
pair that lacks either of those assessed values, or a single-title building
whose dwelling unit count cannot be established, is unclassified: it is
missing from the ratio series, it is not filled in from another measure, and
it is carried into the bounds rule above. Report its absence in the
missing-data counts.

**The sale-to-sale sensitivity, predeclared.** The comparison of the
demolished house's last arm's-length sale price before demolition with
the first arm's-length sale price of each separately titled replacement
dwelling after completion is a named sensitivity. A dwelling unit inside a
single-title multi-unit building has no sale price of its own; such units do
not enter this sensitivity, their count is reported, and the sale of the
whole building is reported only as the labelled aggregate series below.
Report it with its own median ratio, distribution, share, numerator and
denominator against the same thresholds, labelled as a sensitivity, and
never let it carry the verdict. For every pair in it, report the date of each
sale and the gap in years between the two sale dates. Pairs whose gap
exceeds ten years are excluded from the sensitivity's headline figure,
because across a longer holding period that comparison measures the
demolished house's own appreciation as much as the redevelopment. Report the
headline figure, the count and character of the pairs excluded by the
ten-year rule, and the result with those pairs included, each labelled.

Report each of the following as its own labelled series, never pooled into
the verdict series and never mixed within one reported figure:

- arm's-length sale prices, on the timing rules above, with the date of
  each sale stated, as the sale-to-sale sensitivity above;
- listing or asking prices, labelled as asking;
- aggregate sale prices where a multi-unit replacement was sold on one
  title without separate unit titles, with the unit count stated and
  labelled as an aggregate;
- property-level contract rents at first occupancy, where recorded;
- CMHC rental market survey rents for the same bedroom count, year and
  neighbourhood or zone, labelled as a survey figure rather than a
  property-level figure. It carries no verdict and supplies no input to the
  ratio series.

For every case, record which series supplied each figure and how many cases
each series covered. Asking price, first sale price, assessed value,
aggregate multi-unit sale price, per-unit condominium sale price and rent
are not interchangeable.

**Tenure changes, predeclared.** This claim compares assessed value with
assessed value for every otherwise eligible replacement dwelling unit,
regardless of the tenure of the demolished or replacement housing. A tenure
change is reported as a qualification and does not remove the dwelling unit
from the verdict denominator. Where both sides were rented and comparable
contract rents exist, report the rent-to-rent comparison as a separate
non-verdict series.

The replacement side of an assessed-value comparison is always a replacement
dwelling unit value: the total value per dwelling for a separately titled
dwelling, and the building-average assessed value per dwelling unit for a
dwelling unit inside a single-title multi-unit building. This holds whatever
the tenure of the replacement, so a purpose-built rental building enters an
assessed-value comparison per dwelling unit and never as an undivided
building total.

Report as a qualification, with its count, every matched lot pair in which
the demolished house and the replacement are of different tenure, for
example an owner-occupied dwelling replaced by rental housing or a rental
replaced by ownership, and report the verdict figures with those pairs
identified so that their effect on the median ratio and the majority share
can be seen. Do not set an assessed value against a monthly rent: they are
not comparable figures, and this brief predeclares no common shelter-cost
calculation that would make them so. No rent figure enters the verdict
series.

The rent-to-rent series is that separate non-verdict series. Where both the
demolished house and the replacement were rented and comparable contract
rents exist, compare the replacement's contract rent at first occupancy,
stated per dwelling unit, with the prior dwelling's recorded rent, and
report that comparison with its own median ratio, share and count, separate
from the assessed-value verdict series and never entering the verdict
denominator. The same replacement dwelling unit valuation rules apply inside
every separately reported tenure series.

**Other measures reported alongside.** Report per matched lot pair: total
assessed value per separately titled dwelling, the building-average assessed
value per dwelling unit for each dwelling unit in a single-title multi-unit
building, the undivided total assessed value of each replacement title with
its dwelling unit count, total assessed value for all replacement housing on
the lot, bedroom count, tenure, contract rent where a unit is rented, and
the total replacement dwelling unit count. The verdict rests on the
replacement dwelling unit values fixed above and on no other measure.

Price per square foot and price per bedroom are supplementary measures.
Report them where the record supports them, never as substitutes for total
assessed value or rent.

**Timing, predeclared.** Report every comparison in nominal dollars and in
constant dollars. The constant-dollar figures carry the verdict, because a
ratio taken across a gap of years in nominal dollars is carrying general
inflation as well as the redevelopment. For an annual assessment roll, treat
July 1 of the preceding calendar year as the value date, which is the City's
valuation date for that roll. The primary constant-dollar calculation uses
the Statistics Canada Consumer Price Index, all-items, Edmonton census
metropolitan area, monthly series, for that July, with July 2025 as the base
period. Report as the declared alternative the same comparison using the
annual-average CPI for the valuation year with 2025 as the base year, with
its own median ratio, share, numerator and denominator, and state whether it
changes any dwelling's position against either threshold, the median ratio
or the verdict. Report as a further declared sensitivity the same comparison
adjusted with the Statistics Canada New Housing Price Index for the Edmonton
census metropolitan area, on the same July value dates, labelled as a
market-index adjustment rather than a general price adjustment. State the
date of each figure used and the gap in years between the two sides of every
pair.

**Window and cohort, predeclared.** Primary cohort: demolition permits
issued in the ten complete calendar years ending with the most recent
complete calendar year before the freeze date, 2026-09-02, matched to
replacements recorded up to that date. Declared sensitivity: the five
complete calendar years ending with the same year. Report both. Where the
data supports it, also report the cohort defined by replacement completion
year rather than demolition permit year, and say whether the two cohort
definitions change the result. Report the median ratio and the majority
share by demolition permit year, so that a reader can see whether the
magnitude moves with the product mix over the window. Do not blend a partial
year into an annual figure.

**Datasets to look for.** City of Edmonton property assessment data on the
open data portal; demolition permits and building permits on the same
portal; any City infill or mature-neighbourhood monitoring reporting;
REALTORS Association of Edmonton monthly and annual reports for sale prices
by dwelling type; CMHC Housing Market Information for starts, completions
and absorbed prices by dwelling type; CMHC rental market survey for rents;
Statistics Canada price indices for the adjustments named above. Where two
datasets cannot be joined lot to lot, say so: a comparison of citywide
averages answers a different question than the one this claim asks, and
must be labelled as such if it is used at all.

**What the affordability claim is not.** Whether replacement housing is
affordable on an income-based test is a separate claim,
`ip-infill-affordable`, with its own published finding. It is not re-run
here, no verdict on it is returned in this run, and no figure from it enters
this claim's verdict. A replacement dwelling can exceed an affordability
threshold while costing less than the dwelling it replaced, and it can cost
far more than the dwelling it replaced while passing the threshold.

**The causal claim is not tested here, and why.** Whether infill "makes"
housing more affordable is a causal proposition. Testing it would require a
defined no-infill counterfactual, added supply, time lags and filtering over
a specified period, and the price comparisons in this brief cannot establish
any of that. No such causal claim was captured in the intake in a form that
fixes a counterfactual or a period. This brief therefore tests the
descriptive proposition above and does not test, and no verdict in it may be
read as testing, whether infill causes housing to be more or less
affordable. The causal effect of infill on city-wide housing costs is out of
scope for this review. Do not research or report it here. Test it only in a
separate brief with a declared counterfactual and period.

**What Partially supported is and is not.** Partially supported here means
exactly one of the two primary thresholds was met, and the reviewer names
which. A favourable result on a supplementary comparator (price per square
foot, price per bedroom, or a comparison with new construction elsewhere in
the region) is not a "meaningful part" that justifies it. Nor is a result on
an undivided title total or a lot total that differs from the result on
replacement dwelling unit values: that is reported as the qualification on
the verdict. Nor is a tenure change between the demolished house and the
replacement. Nor is a result on a declared alternative threshold where the
primary threshold fails; the alternatives are reported so the reader can see
them, and they do not carry the verdict.

**Comparators, reported separately and never combined.** Each of the
following answers a different question. Label each one and do not merge
them into a single finding:

- the price of the replacement against the housing it replaced on the same
  lot (this is the claim);
- the replacement against the median or typical price in the same
  neighbourhood in the same year;
- the replacement against new construction elsewhere in the region in the
  same year, including new suburban single-detached and new suburban row or
  townhouse product. Report regional new-construction comparisons as a
  separate comparator. Do not assume that it represents the relevant
  alternative for every buyer;
- rents against neighbourhood rents for units of the same bedroom count
  (CMHC rental market survey), reported where actual property-level rents
  are published. This comparator is context and it carries no verdict.

Where one of these measures does not exist in the public record, say so and
record it as missing evidence rather than substituting a proxy without
labelling it a proxy. Reporting a subset of the measures without saying
which ones were unavailable is not acceptable.

**Prior tenure and displacement, in scope as evidence.** Where the record
supports it, report for the demolished house: whether it was owner occupied
or rented at the time of the demolition permit, the number of dwelling units
it contained, the rent charged, and any recorded notice to tenants or
municipal tracking of tenant displacement. Report these as evidence about
what was lost on the lot, with their sources and years, and record them as
missing evidence where the record does not support them. This brief does not
ask reviewers to estimate a displacement count that the record does not
contain.

**Transferred evidence.** Another city's evidence cannot answer this
Edmonton-specific proposition. It may supply context or illustrate a
mechanism, and if used it carries the transferability statement the
methodology requires (why relevant, how it differs, what can and cannot be
inferred), but Edmonton-specific missing evidence stays recorded as
missing. Do not substitute comparator evidence for an Edmonton figure the
record does not contain.

## What each verdict would mean

Stated here so that no verdict on this claim is empty, and so that the
reader can see in advance what is at stake in each.

- **Supported** would mean the typical Edmonton teardown lot replaces a
  house with housing worth at least two and a half times as much per
  dwelling unit, and that most replacement dwelling units are worth at least
  double the house that came down. To a holder of the claim, that is the
  claim confirmed as a pattern and not as an anecdote. To an opponent, it
  would mean the argument that per-unit infill values are moderate, that
  what looks like a price jump is mostly the added unit count on the lot,
  does not hold on the matched population.
- **Contradicted** would mean the typical replacement dwelling unit is worth
  less than one and a half times the house it replaced and that fewer than
  half exceed even that, so the "$350,000 to $1,000,000" picture is not what
  the city-wide record shows. To a holder of the claim, that is the central
  fact of the objection lost, whatever else remains true about
  affordability. To an opponent, it is the evidence that per-unit
  replacement values sit far closer to the demolished stock than the
  circulating figures suggest. This verdict is reachable on the
  measure fixed here: a per-unit ratio can fall at or below 1.0 as readily
  as above it, because a lot can be redeveloped into several dwelling units
  each worth less than the single house removed, and nothing in the
  operationalization forecloses either outcome.
- **Partially supported** would mean the magnitude and the spread come apart:
  either a high typical ratio that most units do not share, or a widely
  shared doubling that falls short of "about triple". To a holder, only one
  material part of the claim survives, so they must give up either
  "typically about triple" or the claim that a doubling is widespread. To an
  opponent, one material part is established and cannot be dismissed, while
  the other is not established.
- **Not established** would mean the public record does not carry the
  matched, unit-level series the proposition is stated in, or that the
  bounds on the missing cases straddle a threshold. To a holder, the claim
  remains unproven and this review cannot be cited as confirmation. To an
  opponent, the claim remains unrefuted and missing evidence cannot be cited
  as showing that replacement values are moderate. Both sides would need a
  sufficiently complete matched series to change that result. It is not a
  finding that the ratio is small.

## Scope

Geography: land within the present-day boundary of Edmonton. Both the
city-wide set of linked demolition-and-replacement lots and the
mature-neighbourhood subset are reported. Regional and suburban figures are
in scope only as the separately labelled comparator named above.

Time: the primary cohort and the declared sensitivity window above.

In scope as evidence: prior tenure, occupancy, prior rent and prior unit
count of the demolished house, and recorded tenant displacement.

Out of scope: whether infill should be permitted or encouraged; design and
neighbourhood-character disputes; construction quality; property tax
effects; the affordability test in `ip-infill-affordable`, which is not
re-run here; the causal question of what infill does to housing costs
city-wide; and whether Edmonton was planned around low-density housing (see
the low-density-history story).

## Required calculations

- Every price or assessed-value comparison states its source, its year,
  whether the figures are nominal or inflation adjusted (and if adjusted,
  the index and base year), and which figure it is: a separately titled
  dwelling's total value per dwelling, a building-average assessed value per
  dwelling unit from a single-title multi-unit building, an undivided title
  total, or a lot total.
- Every replacement dwelling unit value states which of the two valuation
  rules produced it, and for a dwelling unit in a single-title multi-unit
  building names the figure a building-average assessed value per dwelling
  unit and states the building total that was divided and the number of
  dwelling units it was divided by. That figure assigns the same
  building-average value to every dwelling unit in the building and does not
  estimate the distinct market value of any particular unit.
- Every ratio states the two values it was computed from, the year and
  valuation date of each, the index and base period used to put them in
  constant dollars, and the gap in years between the two sides.
- The ratio distribution is reported at minimum as deciles, quartiles,
  median, count and range, city-wide and for the mature-neighbourhood
  subset, and by demolition permit year.
- Both predeclared thresholds are reported under the primary figure and
  under the declared alternative, each labelled, with the numerator and
  denominator of every share.
- The bounds under the unclassified-case rule are reported for both
  thresholds, with the unclassified count and share.
- Assessed values and sale prices are reported as separate series and never
  combined into one average. The verdict series is the assessed-value pair;
  the sale-to-sale comparison is reported as a named sensitivity, stating
  for every pair the gap in years between the two sale dates and whether the
  pair falls inside or outside the sensitivity's headline figure under the
  ten-year rule.
- Every median, share or average states its count, its numerator, its
  denominator and its geography, and states whether the denominator is
  replacement dwelling units (the verdict denominator) or matched lot pairs
  (a sensitivity). A denominator of replacement dwelling units is a whole
  number.
- Every step of the sampling and matching pipeline states the count
  entering and the count leaving, and the reason for the loss.
- Amounts are Canadian dollars unless stated otherwise; no silent
  conversions.
