## The question

Ten people in one Facebook thread, writing in the days after the
Infrastructure Committee met on the bike-lane programme on 2026-08-26, made
three factual claims between them about what bike lanes do to street
parking and who pays for that parking. Eight say installing a bike lane
takes away on-street parking. One says a business could not use its street
for customer parking all summer. One, answering the rest, says free parking
is itself a subsidy paid by taxpayers.

One claim is under review:

- `bike-lanes-remove-street-parking`: whether, on most of the on-street
  routes of the City's current bike-lane programme, installing the route
  permanently removes on-street parking along at least one side of the
  street for at least one full block face.

It carries its own verdict, panel agreement and evidence-basis label.
Reviewer confidence appears only beside the reviewer who gave it.

Two claims are not sent to the panel, because the record identified at
intake cannot reach both verdict directions on either: one person's account
of an unnamed street, and a claim both sides of the thread agree with. They
are parked on the register, each with the condition that would reopen it;
see "Parked at framing". The second half of the question, what on-street
parking costs the City, is parked with the second of them, and this brief
says so rather than test a cousin of it.

Topics: transportation, bike-lanes, city-finances.

## Who asks this

A resident whose street is on the map asks: if the bike lane comes to my
street, do I lose the parking in front of my house? A resident who has
watched the argument asks: do these bike lanes actually take street parking
away, or is that a few streets being talked up? A reporter asks: how many
of the new routes removed parking, where, and how much?

The City does not publish a count of stalls removed by route, so the record
cannot answer "how much" in stalls. It does publish, for every route in the
programme, a statement of what parking changes the route makes: which side
of which street, between which cross streets, permanently or near
intersections only, or no change. For a named street, the City's route
entry may identify the side and segment where parking is planned to
change; it cannot establish who used a space or that construction matched
the plan. Across the programme, this brief asks how often the City records
a qualifying removal on an on-street route. Those are two different
answers, and the programme verdict is only the second. The route entries
and linked maps are the published instruments for the first, where they
give enough location detail, and the two streets the commenters named are
reported that way.

A different resident asks: what does all that free street parking cost the
City, and who pays for it? The record identified at intake does not answer
that at the level anyone asks it, and the brief says so under "Parked at
framing" rather than test a cousin.

## Terms fixed in this brief

**A route.** One row of the route table. Required alternative unit: rows
that the table itself describes as parts of one route, by saying the row
"is part of" a longer named route, are merged into one; reviewers report the
counts and S under both units and name every row they merged.

**An on-street route.** A route whose Facility Type(s) cell names a
protected bike lane of any kind (one-way, two-way or contraflow), a local
street bikeway, or an on-street shared pathway. A route whose facility type
is "Shared Pathway" alone, which the table uses for a pathway off the
roadway, is not an on-street route under the primary rule. A route whose
facility type reads "N/A" or "To be confirmed" is undetermined and is not
an on-street route. Required alternative denominator: every route with a
determined parking entry, shared pathways included, because the opponent of
the claim would say the programme is mostly pathways that touch no parking.

**A determined parking entry.** A Parking Change(s) cell that states what
the route does to parking: a removal, a change, or that there is or will be
no change. A cell that reads "N/A" or "To be confirmed", or that says the
parking impacts of any part of the route are to be confirmed, is
undetermined. An undetermined route is outside both the numerator and the
denominator and is listed with its cell quoted.

**Removes on-street parking, primary reading.** The Parking Change(s) cell
states that parking is or will be permanently removed along one or both
sides of a named street for at least one full block face, from one cross
street to the next, and the cell or the linked route map establishes both
ends of that run. Wording such as "permanent removal of parking on the east
side from 119 Avenue to 124 Avenue" meets it, because the run spans whole
blocks between named cross streets; "both sides of the roadway from 76
Avenue to the alley south of 82 Avenue" meets it, because the run from 76
Avenue to 81 Avenue is whole blocks even though it ends at an alley. A run
from a cross street to the next alley, such as "from 86 Street to nearby
east and west alleys", is part of a block and does not meet it. A cell
that states only removal "near intersections" or "at intersections",
removal described as "minor", a "short section" or "short segments", a
stated number of stalls at a corner, or removal in front of a single named
building or address does not meet it on its own; nor does a cell that
converts angle parking to parallel parking or moves parking without
removing it. Do not infer a full block from an unspecified "short
section"; where neither the cell nor the linked map establishes both
ends, the route does not meet the primary reading and reviewers say so.
Where a cell contains both kinds of statement it meets the reading if any
one statement in it does.

**Removes on-street parking, required alternative reading.** Any permanent
removal of any on-street parking the cell states, including removal for
part of a block, near intersections, short sections, a few stalls and
single addresses. Reviewers report the classification of every route under
both readings. The primary is the stricter because the holders describe
losing the parking on their street, "parking from in front of someone's
house", and a corner clearance for sightlines is not that; the alternative
is reported because a holder who lost two stalls at the corner did lose
parking.

**The share S, and the completeness rule.** For each denominator being
reported, let D be eligible routes with determined parking entries, R the
number meeting the removal reading, and U the routes that could be eligible
but cannot be classified because their facility type or parking entry is
undetermined. Report R, D and U. If D + U is zero, return Not established.
Otherwise calculate a lower bound R/(D + U) and an upper bound
(R + U)/(D + U). If those bounds fall in different verdict bands, return
Not established; otherwise use their common band. Apply this rule under
every required alternative. S is written for R/D and is reported beside
the two bounds; the bounds, not S, decide the band. Reviewers transcribe
every row of the table, classify each, and show the arithmetic.

**Open-or-underway routes, required alternative set.** The same
calculation over routes whose Status cell reads open for use, under
construction or starting construction, so that routes still at the
planning stage are left out. The primary set includes every route with a
determined entry whatever its status, because the holders in Holyrood and
Idylwylde were describing routes then listed for construction and the
City's entry for a planned route is its statement of what the route will
do. Reviewers report S for this set beside the primary set. It excludes
routes that have not started, but parking changes on routes still under
construction remain the City's stated plans, not completed outcomes.

## Claims under review

### The claim under review, id: `bike-lanes-remove-street-parking`

**Normalized proposition:** On most of the on-street routes of the City's
Active Transportation Network Expansion Program, as the City listed them on
2026-08-31, installing the route permanently removes on-street parking
along at least one side of the street for at least one full block face,
from one cross street to the next.

**Reader-facing question:** Do Edmonton's new bike routes take away the
street parking on the streets they run along?

**Why this reading.** Eight people say bike lanes take street parking, in
general terms; two of them offer a place as the example, and every one of
them offers the loss as what a bike lane does, not as a story about one
street. So the generalisation is the claim, and it is tested as one, over
the programme the thread is about, with the two named places checked as
stated and reported as qualifications. "Takes away" is read as permanent
removal of parking along the street, because "taking away my street
parking" and "parking from in front of someone's house" describe losing
the parking one used, and a corner clearance is reported under the
alternative reading rather than counted as that loss. "From residents and
businesses" in the register's proposition is carried as a qualification,
because the City's cell says which side of which street and not who parked
there; reviewers report what the City's text says fronts each removal where
it says anything. The proposition names the programme and the date because
the record is a dated table of that programme's routes.

**What this verdict establishes, stated beside it.** This proposition
checks the City's stated parking changes across this programme's routes.
It does not establish whether a particular resident or business lost
usable parking, how much curb space was lost, or whether the work occurred
as planned. The named streets are reported separately. The published short
answer carries these limits beside the verdict, not only in the
qualifications below.

**The instrument.** The route table on the primary snapshot, the live page
on the alternative date, and the bulletins and maps linked from each row
where a cell needs resolving.

**Order of classification.** Apply the Not established conditions first. A
share band applies only when none of them holds, and it applies to the
lower and upper bounds under the completeness rule: the band is the one
both bounds fall in. This order also applies under every alternative.

**Verdict ladder.**

- **Supported** if S is greater than one half: on most on-street routes
  with a determined entry, the City states a permanent block-length
  removal.
- **Partially supported** if S is at least one fifth and at most one half:
  the City states such a removal on a substantial share of routes, but not
  a majority.
- **Contradicted** if S is less than one fifth, including zero: the City's
  own record shows most on-street routes leaving block-length parking in
  place, which affirmatively points against the generalisation.
- **Not established** if the primary snapshot cannot be read, or the table
  on it carries no Parking Change(s) column, or D + U is zero, or the lower
  and upper bounds under the completeness rule fall in different bands.

Alternative bands, results required under both: Supported if S is at least
two thirds, Partially supported if S is at least one third and less than
two thirds, Contradicted if S is less than one third. Neither set comes
from a pre-existing standard: the primary reads "most" as a simple
majority and treats one route in five as too few to call a pattern; the
alternative asks for a clear majority before crediting the generalisation
and credits a pattern at one in three.

**Required alternatives, each reported with its R, D, U, S, bounds and
band while every other choice stays primary:** the alternative snapshot
(2026-10-01); the alternative unit (merged rows); the alternative
denominator (shared pathways included); the alternative removal reading
(any removal); the open-or-underway set; and the alternative bands. Each
alternative's band is its bounds' common band, or Not established where
they differ. Where any alternative changes the band, the finding is
definition-sensitive, the story says so beside the verdict, and neither
side can fairly cite the verdict without that qualification.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The full route table transcribed from the primary snapshot: for every
   row, the route name, facility type, year, status, the Parking Change(s)
   cell quoted in full, and its classification under each definition
   above, with undetermined routes listed and their cells quoted.
2. The two named examples checked as stated. For 132 Avenue: that its bike
   lanes were built under the 132 Avenue Collector Renewal and not under
   the programme; what the What We Decided Report and the final design
   plans say about parking along the avenue, in the City's words, section
   by section where they differ; and whether the City's record states that
   parking was removed, moved or kept. For Holyrood and Idylwylde: each
   programme route the table lists in those neighbourhoods, its status and
   year on both snapshots, and its Parking Change(s) cell quoted.
3. For each route that meets the primary reading, what the City's cell,
   bulletin or map says fronts the removed parking (homes, an apartment
   building, a church, businesses, a school), quoted, or "not stated".
4. Routes the table shows cancelled, deferred or sent back for redesign
   with parking named in the City's reason, with the entry each carried on
   the 2026-07-07 capture, so a reader sees what was planned and what was
   withdrawn.
5. Any route whose cell states that parking is added, converted from one
   form to another, or expressly kept on one side, quoted.
6. Every difference between the primary snapshot and the live page on the
   alternative date that bears on a classification.
7. Whether any City record identified by the reviewer counts the on-street
   stalls a route removed: the project page, the route bulletins and fact
   sheets, and report IS03688 of 2026-08-26 with its attachments. An
   absence is bounded: say what was searched and as of when.
8. The City's own grouping of the programme's remaining routes in
   Attachment 5 to report IS03688, which separates routes whose plans
   "do not affect travel lanes or parking" from those that do, as a
   cross-check on the classification, quoted by version. The attachment is
   served at
   `https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=<id>`,
   which answers scripts and AI tools with a browser check, as DocumentId
   304032 (original), DocumentId 304031 (REPLACEMENT, Version 1) and
   DocumentId 304030 (REPLACEMENT, Version 2); the report itself is
   DocumentId 304024. The grouping joins travel lanes and parking in one
   phrase and does not by itself classify a route under this brief.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether the removals were right, whether
residents can park elsewhere, how many stalls were lost, what the parking

## Stakes

What each verdict would change, for the person making the claim and for
the person arguing against it. Stated without any view of which way it
comes out, and no wider than the rules above establish. Stakes are stated
only for the claim under review; for the two parked claims the record

**Bike lanes take the parking.** Supported would establish that the City

Contradicted would defeat that majority claim; a holder could still point
to documented losses on particular streets, including the named examples.
Partially supported would establish qualifying removals on a substantial
share, but no more than half, of eligible routes. Not established would
leave the programme-wide frequency unresolved.

**Definition sensitivity.** The verdict applies under the primary snapshot,
unit, denominator, removal reading, route set and bands fixed above. Where
a required alternative produces a different band, the finding is
definition-sensitive, the story says so beside the verdict, and neither
side can fairly cite the verdict without that qualification.

## Reviewer instructions of special note

- **The City's cell is the record.** A Parking Change(s) cell is the City's
  statement of what the route does to parking; it establishes what the
  City says, and for a route not yet built it establishes what the City
  plans, which the finding says in those words. It does not establish how
  many stalls were lost or what was built on the ground.
- **Transcribe before you count.** Every row goes in the table with its
  cell quoted, so a reader can check every classification; a share with no
  table under it is not a finding.

  the finding says the City states it will remove parking, not that it
  has. The open-or-underway alternative shows the effect of excluding
  routes that have not started; it is not a measure of completed removals.
