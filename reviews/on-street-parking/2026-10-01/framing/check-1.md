Verdict: REVISE

This is framing check 1. The route table is a plausible instrument for checking what the City *planned* to do. The brief is not yet fair about what its route-level verdict would establish for residents, and its rules can produce a verdict despite unclassified routes.

1. **Provenance — [framing].** The intake identifies the captured comments, their context, and the 621-record limit. The brief nevertheless says, “Nobody chose it.” Grouping and triage did select this question, and the uncaptured portion cannot be assessed for representativeness. Replace that sentence with: “Whole-source extraction produced this question through grouping and two-reader triage. Both readers returned GO. The capture contains 621 accessible records; we cannot tell whether the comments the platform did not expose framed the issue differently.” The brief is otherwise clear that one thread does not represent Edmonton.

2. **Proposition versus post — [framing].** “On most of the on-street routes ... permanently removes ... parking ... for at least a block” is narrower than “takes away on-street parking from residents and businesses.” It drops smaller removals and actual access for residents or businesses, and substitutes programme route frequency for what happens on an affected street. Replace the explanation of the proposition with: “This proposition checks the City’s stated parking changes across this programme’s routes. It does not establish whether a particular resident or business lost usable parking, how much curb space was lost, or whether the work occurred as planned. The named streets are reported separately.” Keep those limits beside any verdict, not only in the detailed qualifications.

3. **Strongest fair reading — [framing].** The brief says “at least a block,” but defines that as “at least one block face or part of one between a cross street and an alley.” Part of a block is not a block. This is a choice about the *size* of loss worth testing, not a mechanical correction. Replace the primary reading with a consistent, measurable length rule. If the intended rule is a full block face, say “from one cross street to the next” and require the City cell or linked route map to establish both ends. Keep any shorter permanent removal in the required alternative. The City’s [route table and linked route material](https://www.edmonton.ca/projects_plans/roads/active-transportation-network-improvements-project) are plausible sources for that check; do not infer a full block from an unspecified “short section.”

4. **Operationalization and alternatives.**
   - **[defect] Unclassified remainder.** The rule excludes undetermined routes and permits Supported when every *determined* route qualifies, even if enough unclassified routes could put the programme in another band. Replace the “share S” and Not established rules with: “For each denominator being reported, let D be eligible routes with determined parking entries, R the number meeting the removal reading, and U the routes that could be eligible but cannot be classified because their facility type or parking entry is undetermined. Report R, D and U. If D + U is zero, return Not established. Otherwise calculate a lower bound R/(D + U) and an upper bound (R + U)/(D + U). If those bounds fall in different verdict bands, return Not established; otherwise use their common band. Apply this rule under every required alternative.” This supplies the missing completeness rule without assuming what the unknown entries say.
   - **[defect] Half is not a simple majority.** The brief calls its primary “most” rule “a simple majority” but says “Supported if S is at least one half.” Replace that band with “Supported if S is greater than one half,” and replace the adjacent band with “Partially supported if S is at least one fifth and at most one half.” Leave the lower band unchanged.
   - **[framing] The dated alternative is not reproducible as instructed.** The brief calls it “the live page as it stood on 2026-10-01,” gives a hash, then tells reviewers to read the live page later. A hash alone does not give reviewers the October 1 bytes. Replace the instruction with a retrievable, fixed October 1 copy, or call this “the live page on the reviewer’s retrieval date” and stop treating it as an October 1 comparison. I confirmed the live [City table, column names and facility definitions](https://www.edmonton.ca/projects_plans/roads/active-transportation-network-improvements-project), the separate [132 Avenue renewal record](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/on_your_streets/132-avenue-renewal), [capital profile CM-20-0330](https://www.gov.edmonton.ab.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2023-2026CapitalBudget.pdf), and the [Curbside Management Strategy](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/assets/PDF/Curbside-Management-Strategy.pdf). The web access available for this check did **not** retrieve the precise August 31 or July 7 Wayback captures, their stated hashes, or the meeting portal files. That is a verification limit, not proof they do not exist; give the panel a reproducible way to inspect the fixed records before freezing the brief.

   The brief does supply reasonable alternatives for row versus merged-route units, on-street versus all-route denominators, block-length versus any removal, planned versus construction-stage routes, dates, and verdict bands. No alternative to those alternatives is needed.

5. **Expected finding — OK.** I found no sentence directing reviewers to a verdict on the route claim. The ladder describes outcomes conditionally.

6. **Checkability — OK.** The route-table proposition is factual. The brief excludes whether parking loss is fair or wasteful and parks the unnamed business account rather than pretending a project record can be matched to it. The [City strategy](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/assets/PDF/Curbside-Management-Strategy.pdf) confirms that “subsidy” is a City policy term, but it does not supply the magnitude asked for in this intake.

7. **Scope traps — [framing].** “132 Avenue is an example, not a programme route” is a sound accounting rule, but a programme-only verdict must not be presented as settling the general effect of Edmonton bike lanes when a holder explicitly offered 132 Avenue as an example. Replace the scope statement with: “The verdict covers only the Active Transportation Network Expansion Program’s on-street routes. The 132 Avenue example receives a separate account; neither that example nor this programme share establishes the frequency of parking loss across all Edmonton bike-lane projects.”

8. **Stakes — [framing].** “Supported would establish that on most ... routes ... [there is] a permanent, block-length removal” would surprise an opponent of a programme-wide pattern. It might not satisfy the holder who says parking in front of their home is gone, because a route can qualify on one block while retaining parking elsewhere. Conversely, “Contradicted” would defeat the programme-wide frequency claim but would not answer a holder on an affected block. Replace the stakes paragraph with: “Supported would establish that the City recorded qualifying removals on a majority of eligible programme routes; an opponent could no longer describe them as isolated programme exceptions. It would not establish loss at every home or business on those routes. Contradicted would defeat that majority claim; a holder could still point to documented losses on particular streets, including the named examples. Partially supported would establish a minority of qualifying routes, and Not established would leave the programme-wide frequency unresolved.” This makes both directions consequential without claiming more than the instrument measures.

9. **Who asks this — [framing].** “If the bike lane comes to my street, do I lose the parking in front of my house?” is a real resident’s question. A majority-of-route-rows calculation does not answer it. Replace the passage beginning “So the brief tests the question at the level the record answers” with: “For a named street, the City’s route entry may identify the side and segment where parking is planned to change; it cannot establish who used a space or that construction matched the plan. Across the programme, this brief asks how often the City records a qualifying removal on an on-street route. Those are two different answers, and the programme verdict is only the second.” The route entries and linked maps are the published instruments for the first, where they give enough location detail.

A holder would welcome the named-street reporting but could reasonably object that a route-share verdict discounts a loss in front of their home. An opponent would welcome a programme-wide test but could reasonably object if undetermined routes are omitted while the result is described as a programme-wide pattern. The revisions above make both limits visible and prevent the unknown remainder from deciding the verdict.
