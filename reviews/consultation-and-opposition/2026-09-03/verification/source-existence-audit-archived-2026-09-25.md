### Route-set and status cross-check

The **14 project names in the frozen brief match the 14 item names** in all three supplied Attachment 5 texts. The **13 “continue” names also match across all three**. Thus the arithmetic of the two lists is 14 + 13 = **27 listed projects**. The lists describe an administration proposal; they do not by themselves establish the committee’s decision.

The version wording matters:

- Original Attachment 5, p. 2, says the 14 **“would be removed from the scope”** for 2026 and 2027 if Council approved the change.
- The replacement labelled “Version 1,” p. 2, says those projects **“may be revisited using alternate design approaches or other future initiatives.”**
- The replacement labelled “Version 2,” p. 1, says the remaining routes would be evaluated for trade-offs, alternative designs and budget implications, with a report in Q1 2027; its 14-project introduction says they **“may be re-evaluated.”**

Accordingly, the brief’s statement that the *route lists* are identical is **verified**. Any statement that the three attachments share the same heading or proposed treatment is a **mismatch**. In particular, the brief’s defining heading occurs in the original and one replacement, but not in the other replacement. The 14-route boundary is a **published list selected by the editor** as set S; the City does not label it “set S” or define the editor’s tested population.

## Definitions and unresolved dependencies

The City’s documents supply **route lists, status categories and descriptions of its engagement approach**. The brief’s set name **S**, construction-start cutoff rule, alternative decision-point cutoff, three tests for qualifying route engagement, open-to-everyone alternative, E/N/U buckets, verdict thresholds and “rare” ratio are **editor-chosen definitions**. They are not established as City definitions by IS03688 or its attachments. The brief’s attribution of a distinction to the *project page specifically* cannot be confirmed against an immutable September 3 page; the dated report independently uses focused and targeted engagement language.

The report and attachments are internally dated **August 26, 2026**, before the main cutoff, and the Phase 2 report and three dated bulletins predate both cutoffs. An internal document date does not, by itself, prove the exact day a particular eScribe version became publicly available. No later publication or current mutable page was used to establish historical wording.

**Essential dependencies remain unverified:** the mapping and order of the two replacement Attachment 5 DocumentIds; public availability and version state of the nine eScribe files by the applicable cutoff; the official August 26 committee decision; each set-S route’s construction status after Attachment 3’s August 10 snapshot; and the historical project-page route table needed for all neighbourhood mappings.
