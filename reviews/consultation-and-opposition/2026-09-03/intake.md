# Intake record: Did the City consult, and do residents oppose?

Recorded 2026-09-03 by Stew under D-0019 (delegated briefs), for register
question `consultation-and-opposition`. Reviewers do not receive this
file.

Every wording below is an exact substring of a comment in the capture at
`intake/captures/yegscoop-2026-08-26/comments.jsonl`. The capture is one
JSONL file, 621 comments, from a Facebook post by the page "Yegscoop |
Edmonton" reporting the Infrastructure Committee decision of 2026-08-26;
the post URL and provenance are in that directory's README. Commenters
are pseudonyms of the form *Adjective + Edmonton animal + initial*,
stable within this source and meaningless outside it; the mapping to real
names is held privately and is not in this repository. The id every
downstream file cites is the comment's `index`, its 1-based position in
the thread, and that is what is given here.

Fourteen distinct accounts took part in this question. All fourteen are
on the same side: against. Nobody in this source argued that the City did
consult, or that residents support the routes, under either claim below.
That one-sidedness is a fact about the capture and is not evidence about
Edmonton.

Surrounding context worth recording, because it shows what the holders
were reaching for:

- 177 opens "great turnout for people that don't work - why not put in a
  session for people who can actually attend!" and closes "I am not
  against bike lanes if done properly". The "98%" is offered as an
  objection to *when* a session was held, not as a count of opinion.
- 223 is itself a request for a straw poll: "Let's take a vote Who is in
  favour of bike lanes? Who opposes (Do Not Want) bike lanes? No bike
  lanes Please respond so the council can see what 99 % of tab payers in
  Edmonton want?" The "99 %" is the outcome the commenter predicts from a
  poll that has not been run.
- 411 opens "No! Please post the actual input from all of the City forums
  on bike lanes." — a request for the engagement record.
- 555 says "from what i have heard NOBODY wants!", which is the commenter
  reporting hearsay rather than asserting a measurement.
- 262 is "Thought 80% of Edmontonians said no GIPHY"; no figure, source or
  survey is named anywhere in the thread for the 80%, 90%, 98% or 99%.

Not one of the ten names a survey, a poll, a count or a document. Four
give a percentage; none says where it came from.

## Claim as registered: `fast-tracked-without-consultation`

Register proposition: "The City fast-tracked bike routes on old data and
without consulting the affected communities." Four distinct accounts (five
wordings; two are the same person), all against.

| index | commenter | wording, verbatim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | Boreal Marmot L. | "relying on old data and zero community consultation (admitted to in the City's report)" |
| 320 | Chilly Squirrel O. | "They don't do enough talking to the residents" |
| 321 | Dusty Crow K. | "they blatantly ignored hermitage area" |
| 393 | Hardy Vole C. | "none of the residents were actually informed by Ashley Salvador of the proposed bike plan in 2020. Many of us are aware of it in 2026." |
| 224 | Hardy Vole C. | "Stakeholder engagement fail as they did not engage parents with school kids." |

Surrounding context:

- 200 is the fullest statement of the case and the one the brief is drawn
  from. In full it also says the committee's motion "was not to scrap the
  routes, but rather to not rapidly push them through without a second
  look", and that "The routes in question are too late to tender for 2026
  anyway!" — that last sentence is a different registered claim,
  `too-late-to-tender-for-2026`, under a different question.
- 393 is about 79 Street and names a sitting councillor as the person who
  did not inform residents. It ends "How is this fair consultation?"
- 224 is about 79 Street and 50 Street and says "It would be a different
  story if ETS community buses weren't taken out."
- 320 is about a lane on 119 Avenue and mixes the consultation complaint
  with a usage complaint and an alley-access complaint.

### The named individual

Wording 393 names Councillor Ashley Salvador as the person who failed to
inform residents. The register carries that wording verbatim and flags
the claim `names_person: true`. The brief does not test it as an
allegation about her conduct: an accusation against a named person is
declined under the register's `ground: right-of-reply`, because the site
has no way to put it to her. What the brief tests is what the City's
engagement record shows for the routes, which is the checkable part of
the same complaint and does not turn on any individual.

## What the thread does not contain

- No survey, poll, petition, engagement report or council document is
  cited by anyone on either claim. Every percentage is asserted bare.
- No account on either claim argues the other way. Supporters in the
  thread argue about cost, ridership and road spending; on consultation
  and on opposition, the capture is fourteen to nothing.
- The Holyrood school lane, the balance of speakers at the 2026-08-26
  hearing, and parking removal are argued in this same thread but are
  registered as separate questions (`holyrood-school-lane`,
  `council-hearing`, `on-street-parking`) and are out of this brief.

## What was searched for and not found

A representative citywide or neighbourhood survey of Edmontonians'
support for or opposition to these bike routes. Searches on 2026-09-03 for
a City perception survey, a published poll and any probability-sample
instrument returned only self-selected material: an open listening
campaign run by a news outlet, the City's inquiry volume, hearing speaker
counts and advocacy pages. The absence is why the citywide and
neighbourhood-majority claim is parked in the brief rather than tested.
