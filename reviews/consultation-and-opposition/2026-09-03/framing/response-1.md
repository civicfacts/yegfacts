# Author's response to framing check 1 (Stew, 2026-09-04)

Six findings, five adopted whole. The sixth is adopted as the required
alternative rather than as the primary, and the reason is written out
below rather than left as a preference.

**1. Provenance.** No finding.

**2. What the post asserts.** Adopted, and it is the finding that changes
the most. Only one captured wording makes the general assertion the
proposition tests: "relying on old data and zero community consultation
(admitted to in the City's report)". The check is right that the other
four are different claims. The 119 Avenue complaint is about a route
outside set S and is a sufficiency claim this brief cannot test. The
Hermitage complaint is about a neighbourhood on no route in set S. The
parents complaint is about one group rather than the neighbourhood. The
not-informed complaint is about notification, which this brief already
holds is a different thing from being asked. All four are now listed
under Circulating forms as what they are, none of them counts toward the
proposition, and the prevalence line says one account asserts it and
five took part in the consultation argument. The selection rationale
says the same. This makes the tested claim thinner than the brief
originally implied, and the brief now says so where a reader will see
it.

**3. Strongest fair reading.** Adopted, including the part that was a
real defect. The old alternative cutoff set would have returned Supported
at E of 1 or 2 on a proposition that the City ran *no* engagement, which
silently swapped "none" for "rare". Both cutoff sets now require E = 0
for Supported. The ≤ 1/7 reading survives as a separately labelled
qualification, "route-level engagement was rare", reported in
`interpretation_notes` and explicitly barred from being reported as
Supported, which is the disposition the check proposed. "Most-repeated
line" is replaced by "strongest captured line" — it appears once.

**4a. Engagement boundary.** Adopted, and the primary and the
alternative have swapped places exactly as proposed. The primary
definition is now a documented, route-specific activity that solicited
input about the route or its material design from affected residents,
affected property owners, or a body representing the affected
neighbourhood, while the decision remained open; notification,
publication and contact about an ancillary matter do not count. The
open-to-everyone reading is the required alternative. The check is right
that the City's project page distinguishes focused engagement from
targeted engagement without saying the second is not engagement, and
that a brief which ruled out targeted contact would have decided part of
the answer in its definition. This was the drafting session's own stated
worry about the brief and the check found it independently.

**4b. Timing boundary.** Adopted in substance, refused as the primary,
and the refusal is the one place this response argues.

The principle is adopted outright: an activity that happened after the
route's location or material design was closed to change is not
consultation, and test 3 of the definition now says so in as many words.

What is not adopted is making "the first documented point at which the
route location or material design was no longer open to public
influence" the primary cutoff. The framing prompt's own rule is that
before proposing a threshold the brief must say what published source a
reviewer would compute it from, and that a definition the record cannot
meet predetermines Not established and is itself a defect. The City does
not publish a date on which a route's design stopped being open. The
check anticipated this and proposed that an unestablishable decision
point makes the route unresolved — but combined with the new
requirement that Supported needs U = 0, that would put most of the
fourteen into U by construction and leave Not established as the only
reachable answer. That is the defect the prompt names, arriving by a
different door.

So the computable cutoff stays primary and the decision-point cutoff
becomes a required alternative, with results reported under both, which
is what check 4's own bound asks for. The cost of this is small for this
particular set: Attachment 3 records that construction has not started
on any of the fourteen and that five of them have no delivery plan at
all, so for these routes the two cutoffs are unlikely to diverge. The
brief says that, names the attachment, and requires reviewers to report
every route where they do diverge.

**4c. As-of date.** Adopted verbatim. 2026-08-26 is now a required
alternative and reviewers state whether it changes any classification.

**4d. Silence and unknown routes.** Adopted, and this is the check's
best finding. The old ladder had two buckets, which meant a route the
City has simply never written about counted as evidence that nothing
happened on it — the exact inference the brief tells reviewers not to
make three paragraphs later. The ladder now has three: E for confirmed
engagement, N for a City record stating none was run, U for unresolved,
with E + N + U = 14. Supported needs E = 0 and U = 0; Partially
supported needs E ≥ 1 and E + U ≤ 7; Contradicted needs E ≥ 8;
otherwise Not established. The alternative set moves the Partially bound
to E + U ≤ 9 and Contradicted to E ≥ 10. A reviewer may put a route in N
only by quoting a City record, and a programme-wide description of how
engagement was done may not be counted as N for a route unless it says
without qualification that none was run on every route it covers.

**5. Leaks.** No finding, and nothing was added that leaks. What the
council report says about the programme's engagement is in the intake
record, which reviewers never see, and is deliberately absent from the
brief.

**6. Checkable.** No finding.

**7. Scope traps.** Adopted. "Fairly timed" has come out of the
not-tested list, because whether an activity happened while input could
still change the route is part of whether the City asked. What stays out
is whether the amount was adequate and whether the City gave the
responses appropriate weight. The 119 Avenue, notification and
parent-engagement complaints are now named as separate dispositions that
neither support nor undermine the set-S proposition.

**8. Stakes.** Adopted, in the check's wording. The claim that Not
established would be a finding about the City's record-keeping is gone:
it would not be, and saying it was would have let the site turn an
absence of evidence into a story.

**9. Who asks this.** Adopted, in the check's wording. The resident's
question now matches what the definition actually counts.

One thing the check did not do, and it is worth recording that it did
not: it did not propose that a participation count — the engagement
feedback balance, the inquiry volume, the speakers list — stand in for
what residents think, and it did not disturb either park. The brief was
written to refuse that substitution and the refusal was not tested.
