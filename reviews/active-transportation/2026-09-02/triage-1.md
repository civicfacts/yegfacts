## Claim A: "Edmonton spends $100 million a year on bike lanes"

1. **Who asks this, in their words.** "Does Edmonton really spend $100 million every year on bike lanes?" This is a real resident and reporter question.

2. **How it circulates, and where the wording came from.** The $100 million figure has circulated since council's December 2022 budget decision. It appears in news coverage and on a petition page that reports 6,989 signatures. The issue returned to the news in 2026. The captured public wording says "$100 million on bike lanes," not "$100 million a year." Searches found no independent use of the annual wording. That part remains the founder and design assistant's `editor` hypothesis. [Common Sense Edmonton](https://www.commonsenseedmonton.com/stop_the_bike_lane_boondoggle), [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/9343735/edmonton-bike-plan-100-million-reaction), [Taproot Edmonton](https://edmonton.taproot.news/news/2026/09/01/bike-routes-cancelled-or-put-off-despite-council-direction)

3. **Can the public record answer it, at the level people ask it?** Yes. A reviewer can use capital profile CM-20-0330, council's budget minutes, quarterly capital updates and the City's budget FAQ. The review must distinguish the approved capital total, actual expenditures by year, debt servicing, operating costs, and infrastructure that is not a bike lane. The records publish both the four-year allocation and annual figures, so the resident's question can be answered directly. [Capital profile CM-20-0330](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=183567), [council budget record](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/Meeting.aspx?Agenda=PostMinutes&Id=215a3a89-60c7-4eff-8533-cf94079fabd2&Item=446&Tab=attachments&lang=English), [City budget FAQ](https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/budget_finances/frequently-asked-questions)

4. **Which verdicts are possible, and who each would surprise.** **Supported** would require annual spending near $100 million and would surprise defenders of the program. **Partially supported** could establish the $100 million figure while finding that "a year" or "bike lanes" overstates its period or scope. That would surprise people on both sides who treat the whole phrase as either plainly true or wholly invented. **Not established** would apply if budget authorizations cannot be reconciled with actual spending, surprising readers expecting an easy budget check. **Contradicted** would apply if the records affirmatively show a materially different annual amount, surprising people repeating the annual claim.

5. **Who is affected, and what it costs.** The claim affects taxpayers, drivers and people who walk, cycle or roll, including residents who cannot drive or afford a car. It bears directly on budget choices and the current dispute over municipal bike-lane authority. This is one numeric claim. It needs careful budget classification but no lot linkage or comparator cities. YEGFacts has not checked it before.

`Triage: GO` because residents ask the question, published financial records can answer it directly, and several possible verdicts would upset settled claims in the debate.

## Claim B: "The active-transportation network has reduced congestion"

1. **Who asks this, in their words.** "Have Edmonton's new bike lanes actually reduced traffic congestion?" This is a real question.

2. **How it circulates, and where the wording came from.** Since at least 2023, City pages and reports have said active transportation "reduces" or "will help manage" congestion. In 2026, provincial statements and public comments alleged the reverse. No captured source says Edmonton's network "has reduced congestion" as a measured result. That achieved-outcome wording remains an `editor` hypothesis, but it fairly identifies a factual dispute now circulating in Edmonton. [City project page](https://www.edmonton.ca/projects_plans/roads/active-transportation-network-improvements-project), [CHAT News](https://chatnewstoday.ca/2026/07/15/alberta-government-creates-bike-lane-complaint-line-ahead-of-fall-legislation/), [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12035727/edmonton-proposes-changes-bike-routes-community-pushback-provincial-legislation/)

3. **Can the public record answer it, at the level people ask it?** Not yet at the citywide causal level. Edmonton publishes route dates, bike counts, household travel patterns and some vehicle counts. Its older Downtown Bike Network evaluation also used before-and-after monitoring. The current sources do not join the 2024-to-2027 expansion to comparable before-and-after vehicle volumes or travel times with a credible basis for attributing changes to the network. A narrower question, "What happened to vehicle volumes and travel times on measured streets after particular routes opened?", would still matter to readers, but the current public record does not cover enough of the expansion to support a network-wide answer. [Bike-network monitoring page](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/cycling_walking/bike-maps-and-routes), [traffic-volume records](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/traffic_reports/traffic-volumes-turning-movements), [household travel survey](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/traffic_reports/household-travel-survey), [2018 Downtown Bike Network report](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/assets/The_Downtown_Bike_Network_Interim_Report.pdf)

4. **Which verdicts are possible, and who each would surprise.** **Supported** would surprise opponents who say bike lanes worsen congestion. **Partially supported** could find reductions on some routes or at some times while the claimed network-wide effect fails, surprising both camps. **Not established** would reject both sides' present certainty. **Contradicted** would surprise the City and network supporters by showing that the expansion increased congestion. The claim has genuine losers whichever direction a sound answer takes.

5. **Who is affected, and what it costs.** Drivers, cyclists, transit riders, businesses, emergency-service users and residents without access to a car are affected. Route approvals and proposed provincial legislation may turn on the answer. This is one causal claim, but it requires route-level dates, pre/post traffic volumes, travel times, displacement checks and comparison locations. Comparator cities cannot establish Edmonton's outcome. No YEGFacts panel has checked it.

`Triage: PARK` until Edmonton publishes a sufficiently complete evaluation of the current expansion, or route-level dates and comparable pre/post traffic data become available for an independent causal analysis.

`Story: PARK` as a two-claim package because Claim A can proceed now, but Claim B lacks the current-network evidence needed for a reader-level answer.
