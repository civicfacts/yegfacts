# Editor decision on the triage (2026-09-02)

The triage read (triage-1.md) returned GO on `at-100m-a-year`, PARK on
`at-congestion-reduced`, and PARK on the story as a package, because
the public record does not join the 2024 to 2027 expansion to
before-and-after travel times with a basis for attributing the change
to the network, so a network-wide causal answer is not available.

Decision: the story proceeds with both claims. Reasons, on the record:

1. Both claims are on the launch slate that was pre-registered on
   2026-08-31 before any panel ran (D-0008 in the board record;
   docs/DESIGN.md section 7). The slate exists so the site cannot trim
   its claims to the answers they produce, and parking a slate claim
   because the answer is likely to be "nobody has measured it" is that
   trimming in another form. Changing the slate is the founder's call,
   not the editor's, and he has not made it.
2. The reader's own answer to question 4 says a Not established finding
   "would reject both sides' present certainty". The City asserts the
   benefit on its project page and in two council documents; the
   Province asserts the reverse ahead of legislation. A published
   finding that neither side holds an Edmonton measurement is a finding
   residents can use this autumn, not an empty verdict, and the brief's
   verdict rule already makes Not established the honest answer when no
   measurement exists.
3. The reader's answer to question 3 names the level the record does
   hold: what happened to vehicle volumes and travel times on measured
   streets after particular routes opened, and the Downtown Bike
   Network's before-and-after monitoring. Per the framing check's ninth
   test, the brief is revised so the proposition is stated at that
   level, the streets where the lanes were built, in a reader's words,
   with the citywide reading reported as a qualification. That is what
   the reader said would still matter to readers.

The triage prompt lets the editor overrule a NO in writing; this is the
same act on a PARK, recorded here with the reader's report beside it.
The named PARK condition (a City evaluation of the current expansion,
or route-level dates joined to comparable before-and-after traffic
data) is carried into the brief as evidence the reviewers must look
for, and into the story's missing-evidence list if they do not find it.

Stew, 2026-09-02

## Addendum, later on 2026-09-02: the overrule is withdrawn

Framing check 1 (framing/check-1.md), from the same vendor but a fresh
session with the draft brief in front of it, reached the triage reader's
conclusion independently: on the named record no Edmonton instrument can
carry Supported or Contradicted on the congestion claim, so a brief that
tests it predetermines Not established, which the checkability limit
(methodology v1.9) treats as a framing defect. Two different reads
against one editor's preference is the end of it. The overrule above is
withdrawn; `at-congestion-reduced` is PARK on the register with the
reader's condition, and the story proceeds on the $100 million figure
alone.

What the brief did not get to keep, and why it matters for the launch
bar (D-0008 in the board record): the slate registered this claim, and
parking it means the story carries fewer claims than pre-registered. The
charter's own test is that a claim gets checked "or the site says plainly
why it cannot be"; a public PARK with its condition on /considered is
that statement. Whether it satisfies the launch bar is the founder's
call, recorded as an open question in the board record.

The same check also found that no captured source makes the slate's
other claim in its registered form, "$100 million a year"; the brief was
re-framed around the comparisons holders actually make with the figure,
and the registered wording is listed on the register as PARK with that
reason.
