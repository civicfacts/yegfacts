## 2. Does the proposition test what the post asserts?: FINDING

### Claim A

The captured claim concerns the $100 million expansion program. The proposition changes it into total City spending across the program, other capital projects, maintenance equipment, parking, wayfinding and operating expenses:

> "all City of Edmonton spending on cycling and active-transportation infrastructure"

That is broader than the captured forms. It could approach $100 million in one year for reasons unrelated to what the quoted speakers meant.

If the annual claim is retained after obtaining provenance, replace the proposition with:

> "During the 2023-2026 program, the City of Edmonton's actual spending on CM-20-0330 averaged about $100 million per completed fiscal year."

Report other cycling-specific City spending separately. Do not use it to decide a claim drawn from CM-20-0330.

### Claim B

The City's captured assertion concerns active transportation generally. The proposition narrows it to streets carrying routes, then narrows it again to whichever streets happen to have measurements:

> "Where the record holds measurements for some streets and none for most, the verdict is about the streets measured."

That drains the generality from the claim. A result on one evaluated street is not a verdict on "Edmonton's bike lanes."

The strongest fair proposition would be:

> "Edmonton's built active-transportation network has reduced motor-vehicle delay in Edmonton compared with what delay would have been without the network."

If no published source can plausibly establish either side of that causal proposition, park it. Do not substitute a measured-streets proposition and present its verdict as answering the general claim.

## 3. Is it the strongest fair reading?: FINDING

Claim A is weakened by using the highest single year:

> "The verdict-bearing figure is the highest single year of bike-lane spending"

One year at $75 million could produce Supported even if every other year were far lower. "$100 million a year" ordinarily describes a recurring rate, not a peak.

Replace with:

> "The primary annual rate is total actual expenditure during completed fiscal years divided by the number of those years. Report each year's actual expenditure as well. The highest year is a qualification, not the verdict-bearing statistic."

The City publishes year-end cumulative actuals, so annual expenditure is plausibly computable by differencing successive year-end reports. For example, the profile appears in the [2023 year-end capital report](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=215340) and [2024 year-end capital report](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=253138).

Claim B's measured-streets qualification also weakens a network-wide generalization. It cannot carry the original verdict.

## 4. Operationalization and alternatives: FINDING

The cutoff alternatives satisfy the methodology's bounded threshold rule. The underlying statistic and evidence rules do not.

### Claim A

- **Actual expenditure versus approved budget.** These can change the verdict. The brief currently allows 2026's approved ceiling to carry Supported even though it says, correctly, that approved budget is not spending. Replace with:

- **CM-20-0330 versus all City cycling expenditures.** These can change the result. Use CM-20-0330 as primary because that is the program attached to the circulating $100 million figure. Require an alternative result under the broader boundary.
- **Nominal versus constant dollars.** Inflation adjustment could matter near a cutoff. Nominal dollars are the better primary reading because the circulating figure is nominal, but the brief must say that constant dollars are an alternative and whether they change the classification.

The named program and budget instruments exist. The [City project page](https://www.edmonton.ca/projects_plans/roads/active-transportation-network-improvements-project), [capital profile CM-20-0330](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=183567), and [2023-2026 Capital Budget](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2023-2026CapitalBudget.pdf) describe a multi-year $100 million commitment, with annual approved allocations. ### Claim B

The following choices remain undefined or unjustified:

- **Measurement window.** "Any date up to the as-of date" permits unmatched seasons, construction periods and unequal follow-up. A reasonable alternative is matched periods for one year before and one year after opening. This could change the finding.
- **Congestion measure.** Average speed can fall because of a changed speed limit or traffic-calming design without congestion increasing. Use delay relative to free flow or a travel-time index as primary. The US Federal Highway Administration identifies travel time as the basis for meaningful congestion measures and defines delay against ideal or free-flow travel time. [FHWA congestion measures](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/congestion_report/chapter2.htm)
- **Causal standard.** Attribution merely asserted by the publisher is not enough. A reasonable alternative is an evaluation with a stated counterfactual, such as matched control corridors or a defensible interrupted time-series design. This choice can change every verdict.
- **Material change.** Any numerical fall currently qualifies as reduction. Specify a confidence interval or predeclared practical threshold, with one alternative, so measurement noise cannot decide the verdict.
- **Aggregation.** One street can currently establish Contradicted, while Supported requires no measured street to show the reverse. Specify route weighting, evidence-conflict rules and minimum network coverage.
- **Street boundary.** Letting each evaluation name affected parallel and crossing streets creates an evidence-selected boundary. Predeclare how far diversion effects extend.
- **Route denominator.** "Count of routes built" is undefined. Specify whether a route means a named project, corridor, segment or kilometre.

The named data sources do not exist in the form the brief describes:

TomTom says longer historical analysis from 2018 is available through Area Analytics, not that the public Edmonton page supplies a complete 2017-2025 series. [TomTom data availability](https://www.tomtom.com/downloads/traffic-index/)

[Statistics Canada Edmonton commuting data](https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/as-sa/fogs-spg/Page.cfm?Dguid=2021S0503835&Lang=E&topic=13)

The proposition answers only a narrower, evidence-selected street question. Replace the public question with:
