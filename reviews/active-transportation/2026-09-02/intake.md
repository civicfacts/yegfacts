# Intake record: Active transportation investment

Recorded 2026-09-02 by Stew under D-0019 (delegated briefs). Fourth story
of the pre-registered launch slate (docs/DESIGN.md section 7, registered
2026-08-31 before any panel ran). Topics: transportation, city-finances.

## Pre-registered claims

The slate fixed two claims for this story, in this wording, on
2026-08-31:

> Edmonton spends $100 million a year on bike lanes; the
> active-transportation network has reduced congestion.

Both were written by the founder and the assistant that drafted the
design document as their reading of how the two sides of Edmonton's
bike-lane argument put their case. Neither was captured from a post at
the time. Under the candidate register's origin labels both are
`editor` wording; the register itself does not repeat the slate, so this
record is where their provenance lives.

The brief has to say which reading carries the verdict and report the

4. Taproot Edmonton, 2026-09-01, above, paraphrasing a source: if bike

Opponents, in their own words:

5. Alberta's Minister of Transportation, quoted by CHAT News on

What was not captured: a verbatim instance of the pre-registered wording
"has reduced congestion" as an achieved outcome. The City's wording is
general or prospective ("reduces", "will also help manage"). The
opponents' wording asserts the reverse effect. Both sides put the claim
in Edmonton's argument as a matter of fact, which is why it is on the
slate.

## Context on 2026-09-02

- Council approved the $100 million on 2022-12-09 as part of the
  2023-2026 capital budget (vote reported as 9 to 4).
- On 2026-08-26 the Infrastructure Committee heard report IS03688, in
  which administration recommended a "modified approach" that would keep
  13 routes and re-evaluate 14 that take parking or travel lanes; the
  committee did not adopt it and the program stands as approved. More
  than 60 people registered to speak.
- On 2026-07-07 council directed the 50 Street route to proceed under an
  alternative design that keeps parking and travel lanes, funded from
  CM-20-0330; Taproot reports the redesign at $4.9 million against an
  original $2.4 million.
- The Province of Alberta opened a bike-lane complaint portal on
  2026-07-15 and has said legislation affecting municipal bike-lane
  authority under the Traffic Safety Act is coming in fall 2026.

## What was left out and why

The slate's claim wording is the claim. Whether the network is worth
its cost, whether the Province should legislate, whether the modified
approach was wise, and whether specific routes should keep or lose
parking are policy questions and are not tested. Ridership (whether
people use the lanes) is a separate factual question that is not on the
slate for this story; it enters this brief only where a congestion
measurement depends on it.
