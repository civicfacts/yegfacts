## Story

Since council's budget vote of 2022-12-09, "$100 million" and "bike
lanes" have travelled together in Edmonton's argument about streets, and
the figure is almost always stated as a comparison: against what the City
spends clearing snow, against what it spends on roads. This brief tests
the two comparisons that were captured verbatim, one from each side.
Topics: transportation, city-finances.

Claims in this story, reviewed and reported separately:

- `at-100m-vs-snow`: whether the $100 million approved for bike lanes is
  almost double the City's annual snow-clearing budget.
- `at-100m-vs-roads`: whether roads get $1.8 billion in the same capital
  budget, about eighteen times the $100 million for bike lanes.

Each claim carries its own verdict, panel agreement and evidence-basis
label. Reviewer confidence appears only beside the reviewer who gave it.
A finding on one claim is not a finding on the other.

**What the slate registered, and what changed.** The launch slate
registered this story's first claim as "Edmonton spends $100 million a
year on bike lanes" and its second as "the active-transportation network
has reduced congestion". The intake captured no source making the annual
claim, and the framing check found that the record holds no Edmonton
instrument that could establish the congestion claim in either
direction. Both registered wordings are listed on the public register
(intake/register.yaml) as PARK with those reasons, and the story page
will say so. The claims below are the ones holders were actually
captured making with the same figure.

## Why this story (selection rationale)

- Prevalence: the "$100 million" figure is in news headlines from
  2022-12-09 onward, in a councillor's floor comparison, in an advocacy
  campaign page, and again in the coverage of the 2026-08-26
  Infrastructure Committee meeting and the Province's bike-lane
  legislation. The two comparisons under review are captured verbatim
  (intake.md).
- Civic importance: the Infrastructure Committee heard on 2026-08-26 a
  recommendation to re-evaluate 14 of the program's remaining routes and
  declined it; the Province has said it will legislate on municipal
  bike-lane authority this fall. Both sides argue from the size of the
  figure.
- Verifiability: the City publishes its capital budget by profile and by
  year, its operating budget by program, year-end capital reports and
  program updates to council. Both comparisons turn on published budget
  lines.

## Who asks this

A resident or a reporter would ask: "Is $100 million for bike lanes a
lot? Compared with what the City spends on roads, or on clearing snow?"
The two propositions answer that question in the terms the two sides
themselves chose. Behind it sits a second question, "is that $100
million a year, or over the whole budget, and what has actually been
spent?", which the required calculations answer whichever way the
verdicts fall, so the reader gets the figures and not only two verdict
words.

## Claims under review

### Claim A, id: at-100m-vs-snow

**Normalized proposition:** The $100 million approved for bike lanes is
almost double the City's annual snow-clearing budget.

**Why this reading.** The proposition is the holder's own comparison. The
holder set the period on one side (a program total) and on the other (a
year's budget); the proposition keeps both as stated, because a
fair-minded holder would say that is what they said. Whether the
comparison is a fair one is the first thing the qualification below
reports, under both readings, so the reader sees the like-for-like
figures whatever the verdict.

**"The $100 million approved for bike lanes", fixed here.** The approved
budget of capital profile CM-20-0330, "Active Transportation
Implementation Acceleration - Approach 3", as approved by council in the
2023-2026 capital budget in December 2022. Reviewers confirm the amount,
the years it spans and its per-year allocation from the capital profile
sheet, the approved capital budget document and the council minutes of
the December 2022 deliberations, and report any later amendment to the
profile with its date. No other cycling spending enters this figure: the
circulating number is the program.

**"The City's annual snow-clearing budget", fixed here to a named
cell.** The first run showed that "the approved 2022 gross budget in
force" names no single figure: the record holds the program's base
budget as adopted, the same budget as amended in-year, and a net figure,
and they straddle a cutoff. So the primary denominator is one printed
cell:

- **Primary:** the City of Edmonton, *Approved 2019-2022 Operating
  Budget*
  (https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/assets/APPROVED_2019-2022_OPERATING_BUDGET.pdf),
  Branch: Parks & Roads Services, "Approved 2019 - 2022 Budget - Program
  Summary", Program Name: Snow and Ice Control, row "Expenditure &
  Transfers", column "2022 Budget", in $000s. This is the program's
  approved gross expenditure budget for 2022 as adopted, the figure a
  reader opening the budget document finds as the year's snow-clearing
  budget. Reviewers quote the cell as printed and the page it is on.
- **Alternative A, required:** that cell plus every council amendment
  to the Snow and Ice Control budget for 2022 in force on 2022-12-12,
  each amendment named with its meeting date, motion and amount from the
  council minutes (the seats in the first run cited a one-time increase
  approved 2022-07-04; reviewers establish the full list, with the
  source of each). This is the budget as amended in-year.
- **Alternative B, required:** the same row and column for "2023
  Budget" in the City's *2023-2026 Operating Budget and Plans*
  (https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2023-2026OperatingBudget.pdf),
  Parks and Roads Services, Snow and Ice Control program summary, as
  approved on 2022-12-16, including the December 2022 council amendments
  to it (the enhanced snow standards amendment among them), each named.

Compute the ratio and the classification under all three; the primary
carries the verdict and the two alternatives are reported beside it in
`interpretation_notes`. Also report, as context and not as a
denominator: the row "Total Net Operating Requirement" for 2022 (the net
figure), and 2022 actual expenditure where published, because an actual
is not a budget. Where a printed figure is in thousands, say so.

**"Almost double", fixed here with thresholds.** The verdict figure is
the ratio of the approved $100 million to the primary denominator cell
above, in nominal dollars. The classifications under Alternatives A and
B are required and reported beside it. The cutoffs below are unchanged
from the first frozen brief.

- **Supported** if the ratio is at least 1.8.
- **Partially supported** if the ratio is at least 1.5 and below 1.8:
  the figure is well above the snow budget, and the failing part is the
  word "double".
- **Contradicted** if the ratio is below 1.5.
- **Not established** if the City did not publish a 2022 snow and ice
  control budget in a form from which the ratio can be computed.

Alternative cutoffs, results required under both: Supported at 1.7 or
above; Partially supported from 1.25 to below 1.7; Contradicted below
1.25. Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing standard; 1.8
is where "almost double" stops being a stretch in ordinary speech, and
1.5 is where the figure is "half again" rather than "double". No cutoff
may be changed after the figures are seen.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The like-for-like comparison, under both of its readings: (a) the
   per-year approved allocation of CM-20-0330 for 2023 against the 2023
   snow budget, and (b) the whole $100 million against four years of
   snow-clearing budget (2023 approved, then the approved budgets for
   2024, 2025 and 2026 as published). State each ratio.
2. The same ratios on actual expenditure where published: CM-20-0330
   actual spending by year against snow and ice control actual
   expenditure by year, for 2023, 2024 and 2025.
3. The ratio on the net tax-levy figure for the primary year, where the
   City publishes one.
4. Constant-dollar restatement, if any reviewer applies one, with the
   index used and whether it changes the classification.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether the comparison is a good
argument; whether $100 million is too much or too little; the affordable
housing comparison on the same page (reported as context only if a
reviewer finds the figure the page compared against; it is not a claim
in this brief).

### Claim B, id: at-100m-vs-roads

**Normalized proposition:** In the City's 2023-2026 capital budget,
roads get $1.8 billion, about eighteen times the $100 million for bike
lanes.

**Why this reading, and the "180".** The quotation gives three numbers:
$100 million, $1.8 billion and "180 times as much". The third does not
follow from the first two, which are eighteen times apart. The
proposition tests the two dollar figures the councillor gave and the
ratio they imply, because that is the substance of the claim, that roads
get an order of magnitude more, and a fair-minded holder would say the
figures are what they meant. The brief does not test "180 times" as a
separate claim. Reviewers report the discrepancy in
`interpretation_notes` as a fact about the quotation, and note whether
the figure appears as "180" or "18" in any other record of the same
statement (council meeting minutes or video, other coverage).

**"Roads", fixed here.** The City's adopted 2023-2026 Capital Budget
(https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/2023-2026CapitalBudget.pdf)
publishes no roads-only total. Its Table 8, "Adopted Capital Budget by
Service", gives one combined line, "Active Pathways and Roads Service",
$1,794.0 million for 2023-2026, which includes active pathways. So the
primary roads-only boundary is fixed by the City's own label on each
capital profile sheet in that document, and the combined line is the
required alternative.

*Primary boundary (roads-only).* The sum of the 2023-2026 approved
amounts (Appendix A of the same document, in thousands) of every capital
profile whose sheet carries Service Category "Roads" or Service Category
"Neighbourhood Renewal", with these predeclared exceptions: excluded
from "Roads", 21-20-2100 (170 Street Pedestrian Bridge) and 14-66-2570
(Parking Control Technology), because neither carries vehicle traffic;
included although their sheets carry no Service Category, the three
vehicle-carrying bridge profiles 23-24-0300 (High Level Bridge
Rehabilitation), 21-24-9301 (Latta Bridge Replacement) and 21-24-9302
(Glenora Bridge Renewal). Every included profile is included in full;
no mixed profile is apportioned unless the City itself publishes the
roadway share. The editor's reading of the resulting membership, for
reviewers to verify against the sheets and correct on the record, not to
take on trust: Roads, 18-66-6503, 19-22-9006 (both lines), 21-20-9301,
21-20-9302, 21-40-9031, 21-40-9032, 22-22-9700, 22-22-9701, CM-20-2020,
CM-22-0000, CM-24-0000, CM-26-0000, CM-66-2194, CM-66-2525, CM-66-2585,
CM-66-2596, CM-99-0060, CM-99-9600; Neighbourhood Renewal, 20-40-9017,
20-40-9019, 21-40-9023, 21-40-9024, 21-40-9025, 21-40-9026, 21-40-9027,
22-40-9028, 22-40-9029, CM-25-0000, CM-27-0000; plus the three bridges.
Reviewers list every profile they summed with its amount and page, and
state any profile whose sheet's label differs from this reading.

*Required alternative.* Table 8's "Active Pathways and Roads Service"
total, $1,794.0 million, identified by its full published name, with
CM-20-0330 and the other active-pathway funding it contains left
visible. It is never called a roads total; a result on it establishes
the combined service allocation, not a roads-only one.

*Widest and narrowest readings, qualifications.* Widest: the primary
boundary plus the streetscape and pedestrian-realm profiles the City
lists under the same service (103 Avenue Streetscape, 103A Avenue
Pedway, Jasper Avenue New Vision Phase 2, Chinatown Infrastructure
Improvements, Missing and Enhanced Sidewalk Connections, the
Edmonton-Strathcona County Pedestrian Bridge). Narrowest: Service
Category "Roads" profiles only, without Neighbourhood Renewal and
without the three bridges. Excluded under every reading: transit, LRT,
bus, active-transportation profiles including CM-20-0330, and fleet.
Reviewers report the classification under the primary boundary and the
required alternative, and the totals under the widest and narrowest
readings.

**"$1.8 billion, about eighteen times", fixed here with thresholds.**
The verdict figure is the approved roads-only total for 2023-2026 under
the primary boundary, in nominal dollars, and its ratio to $100 million.
The classification under the required alternative (the combined service
line) is reported beside it.

- **Supported** if the roads total is at least $1.5 billion (fifteen
  times or more).
- **Partially supported** if it is at least $1.0 billion and below
  $1.5 billion: roads get an order of magnitude more, and the failing
  part is the figure.
- **Contradicted** if it is below $1.0 billion.
- **Not established** if the City's published capital budget does not
  let a roads total be computed on any reasonable reading.

Alternative cutoffs, results required under both: Supported at
$1.62 billion or above (nine tenths of the stated figure); Partially
supported from $0.9 billion to below $1.62 billion; Contradicted below
$0.9 billion. Neither set is drawn from an identified pre-existing
standard; five-sixths of a stated figure is "about" that figure in
ordinary speech, and a figure below one thousand million is a different
order of claim from $1.8 billion. No cutoff may be changed after the
figures are seen.

**Qualifications reported, never carrying the verdict:**

1. The classification under the required alternative, and the totals
   under the widest and narrowest readings, each with its ratio to
   $100 million.
2. The roads total as amended to the as-of date (supplemental capital
   budget adjustments, including the 2024 increase council approved to
   the capital budget), with dates.
3. Actual roads capital expenditure by year for 2023, 2024 and 2025 where
   the City's year-end capital reports publish it, against CM-20-0330
   actual expenditure by year.
4. The share of the whole 2023-2026 capital budget, and of its
   transportation portion, that CM-20-0330 represents, on approved
   figures.

**What this claim does not test.** Whether roads should get more or
less; whether the ratio is the right way to think about either budget;
operating spending on roads (maintenance, snow clearing), which is not
what the councillor compared.

## Stakes

**Claim A.** Supported would mean the opponents' comparison holds as
they made it, and supporters would have to answer it on its merits
rather than on its arithmetic, with the like-for-like figures beside it
for them to argue from. Contradicted would mean the arithmetic does not
hold even on the holder's own terms, and the campaign page's comparison
would have to be dropped. Partially supported would mean the figure is
well above a year of snow clearing but "double" overstates it. Not
established would mean the City's own budget documents do not let a
resident check a comparison the City's critics make, which would be news
in itself. Each verdict is reachable on the budget documents and each
changes something for one side.

**Claim B.** Supported would mean the supporters' proportion argument
holds on the City's own labels: the City has committed to roads
something like eighteen times what it has committed to bike lanes, and
opponents could not call the bike-lane figure outsized without answering
that. Contradicted would mean the roads figure is much smaller than
stated once "roads" is read on the City's own classification, and the
councillor's comparison would not stand. Partially supported would mean
roads get an order of magnitude more but not $1.8 billion. Not
established would mean the City's published budget does not let anyone
compute a roads total, which would itself be a finding. Each verdict is
reachable and each would surprise someone.

**Both claims, definition sensitivity.** Each verdict applies under the
named primary denominator (Claim A: the printed 2022 gross expenditure
cell)
or boundary (Claim B: the roads-only profile set). If the required
alternative produces a different classification, the finding is
definition-sensitive, the story says so beside the verdict, and neither
side can fairly cite the verdict without that qualification. A result
on the "Active Pathways and Roads Service" line establishes the
combined service allocation, not a roads-only allocation.

## Scope

Geography: the City of Edmonton's own budgets. No comparator city is
required for either claim; both are claims about Edmonton's documents.

Time: as fixed above.

Out of scope: policy questions (whether the program should continue,
whether the Province should legislate, whether any route should keep or
lose parking); whether bike lanes affect congestion, safety, parking or
business (the congestion claim is parked on the register with the reason);
ridership; the value or cost-effectiveness of any spending; provincial
actions after the as-of date; the affordable-housing comparison on the
advocacy page.

## Required calculations

Shared by both claims, so the story can answer the reader's second
question:

- Capital profile CM-20-0330: approved budget by year for 2023 to 2026
  as approved in December 2022 (state whether printed in thousands);
  every amendment with its date and amount; actual expenditure by year
  for 2023, 2024 and 2025 from the City's year-end capital reports (the
  2023 and 2024 year-end reports and the September 2025 capital
  performance report are known to exist; reviewers locate the 2025
  year-end report if published by the as-of date), computed by
  differencing cumulative actuals where the City reports cumulatively,
  with the arithmetic shown; total spent to the latest published date;
  approved and actual averages per year, labelled as such. Approved and
  projected amounts are never described as spending.

Claim A:

- The primary cell, Alternative A (with its list of amendments) and
  Alternative B, each with source and page; the ratio and classification
  under each; the net figure and 2022 actual as context; the
  like-for-like ratios in the qualifications, each with numerator,
  denominator and source.

Claim B:

- The roads-only total under the primary boundary, listing every
  profile summed with its 2023-2026 amount and Appendix A page, and any
  label that differs from the editor's reading; the classification
  under the required alternative (Table 8 line, full name); the totals
  under the widest and narrowest readings; each ratio to $100 million;
  the as-amended total with dates; the shares in qualification 4.

All figures in nominal Canadian dollars unless a constant-dollar
restatement is reported as a qualification with its index named.

## Reviewer instructions of special note

- Quote operative figures from the City's own documents (capital profile
  sheets, the approved capital and operating budget documents, council
  minutes and amendments, year-end capital reports), and name the
  document, its report number or title, its page and its date. A news
  figure is a lead to the document behind it, never the source of a
  figure in a verdict.
- Keep approved from spent. A funded profile is not money out the door.
  The verdict figures in both claims are approved amounts because that is
  what both holders compared; the actuals are required so the story can
  say what has been spent.
- Keep the program from the network. CM-20-0330 is the figure; bike
  infrastructure funded elsewhere is context in the required
  calculations, not part of any verdict.
- Keep the combined service line from a roads total. "Active Pathways
  and Roads Service" is reported under that name only.
- Report one verdict per claim under the primary readings and cutoffs
  fixed here, and every alternative reading's result in
  `interpretation_notes` or `limitations`. The schema carries one
  verdict per claim.
- Record every source URL in full; you have no repo access. Where a
  council document is reached through the City's meeting portal, give
  the meeting agenda page as well as the attachment URL.
- If a definition, cutoff or date in this brief changes what the honest
  answer is, record a MATERIAL FRAMING CONCERN per prompts/reviewer.md
  and answer the claims as posed alongside it.
