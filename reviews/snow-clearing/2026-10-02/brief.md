## The question

Five people in one Facebook thread, writing in the days after the
Infrastructure Committee met on the bike-lane programme on 2026-08-26, made
five factual claims between them about how the City clears snow. One
commenter says bike lanes are plowed before streets. Two others mention
winter clearing but do not assert an order. One says Whyte Avenue becomes
very narrow and dangerous in winter for want of snow removal. One says the
plows windrow the snow into the parking spaces left beside the bike lanes.
One, answering a commenter who expects the City to ask for more snow money,
says administration has already put two snow removal proposals to council
that both need more spending, and that council will soon debate bringing
back calcium chloride as an anti-icer on the roads.

One claim is under review:

- `two-snow-removal-proposals-need-more-money`: whether, by the day the
  comment was written, Administration had placed before City Council or
  one of its committees, on a published agenda, at least two distinct
  proposals to change snow and ice control service, each stating new
  spending to deliver it.

It carries its own verdict, panel agreement and evidence-basis label.
Reviewer confidence appears only beside the reviewer who gave it.

The question as registered asks how Edmonton clears snow from its streets
and bike lanes. The one graded question in this brief is narrower: has
Administration already asked council for more snow clearing money, and
how many times? The brief does not promise a verdict about clearing
streets and bike lanes, and the question's page leads with the four parks
below, not with the finding.

Four claims are not sent to the panel, because the record identified
cannot reach both verdict directions on any of them: that the City plows
bike lanes before the streets, which the published standard's deadlines
and priorities do not establish and no record of actual clearing order
can be compared; that Whyte Avenue becomes "very narrow and dangerous" in
winter; that the plows windrow snow into the parking spaces that remain
beside bike lanes; and the forecast that council will soon debate bringing
back calcium chloride. Each is parked on the register with the condition
that would reopen it; see "Parked at framing". The City's standard and
the meeting record are given there as background, not as a verdict on
any of them.

## Terms fixed in this brief

**A proposal.** A report, attachment, option or service package authored
by City Administration and listed on the published agenda of City Council
or a committee of Council, that proposes a change to snow and ice control
service levels, operations or programs. A Council or committee motion
asking Administration to bring options back is not a proposal; the report
that answers it is. For the primary count, count each separately
presented service package once. A decision or attachment within that
package does not add a proposal. Under the required alternative, count
separately costed options. A committee report that forwards the same
document to Council, or a later version of the same document, counts once
under either unit.

**Put to the body.** A proposal is put to Council or a committee of
Council on the date the published agenda of a meeting of that body first
lists the report, attachment or package carrying it, whether or not the
meeting has yet been held. The publication date is read from the agenda
page, from an archive capture of it, or from the minutes of the Agenda
Review Committee that settled it; where none fixes the date, the meeting
date is the latest date the agenda can have been published, and the
reviewer says which it used. Required alternative, **considered by the
body**: the minutes of a Council or committee meeting held by the date in
question record the item carrying the proposal as dealt with: a
presentation, a speaker, questions answered, or a motion under it. Under
the alternative, a proposal listed on a published agenda for a meeting
after that date is "on the agenda" and not yet considered. Both are
reported, the primary carrying the verdict.

**States new spending.** The proposal's text states an incremental
operating or capital cost for the City to deliver it, as a dollar figure
or a stated number of positions with a cost. Required alternative, the
stricter reading: the text also establishes that the cost exceeds an
identified approved budget, by stating the approved figure and the
increase against it; reviewers do not infer a budget baseline the
documents omit. A proposal whose text states that it can be delivered
within existing resources does not meet either. A proposal whose text
states no cost either way is undetermined.

**Council or a committee of Council.** City Council is the body of
thirteen; the Community and Public Services Committee and the other
standing committees are committees of Council whose members are members
of Council. The holder says "council" for both. The primary body is
Council or any of its committees, and the required alternative is City
Council alone; reviewers report separately what a committee did and what
Council did, and never describe a committee meeting as a Council meeting.

## Claim under review

### The claim under review, id: `two-snow-removal-proposals-need-more-money`

**Normalized proposition:** Between 2025-11-18 and 2026-08-27,
Administration placed before City Council or a committee of Council, on a
published agenda, at least two distinct proposals to change snow and ice
control service, each of whose text states new spending to deliver it.

**Reader-facing question:** Has the City's administration already asked
council for more snow clearing money, and how many times?

**Why this reading.** The holder answers a commenter who expects the City
to ask for snow money by saying the asks already exist: "2 different
proposals", from administration, "to council", and "both will require
spending increases". The count is part of the claim and is carried as a
floor: at least two, because a holder who found three would not say "that
is not what I meant", and the exact count is reported. "To council" is
read as Council or a committee of Council, because the standing
committees are where Administration's reports are first heard and the
holder, like the other commenters in this thread, says "council" for
both; Council alone is the required alternative body. "Already put" is
read as placed before a body on a published agenda by the comment date,
because to put a proposal to a body is to submit it, and Administration
has done that once the body's published agenda lists the proposal,
whether or not the body has yet dealt with it; the stricter reading,
considered by a body by that date, under which an ask on the agenda for
a later meeting is not yet heard, is the required alternative timing rule.
"Proposals" is defined so that a councillor's motion asking for options
is not one and Administration's answer to it is, with two units, each
separately presented service package counted once (primary) and
separately costed options (alternative), both reported. "Require spending
increases" is read off the proposal's own text as new spending stated,
with the stricter reading, that the text shows the cost exceeding an
identified approved budget, reported beside it.

**What this verdict establishes, stated beside it.** This proposition
checks what Administration's published documents proposed, what cost they
stated, when they were first placed before a body on a published agenda
and when a body first dealt with them. It does not establish
whether Council agreed, whether the money was spent, or whether the
proposals were wise.

meeting in the reference period that list a snow and ice control report,
attachment or service package by Administration, found through the
portal's calendar and named by meeting id, with the date each agenda was
published; the minutes of those meetings, for the alternative timing rule
