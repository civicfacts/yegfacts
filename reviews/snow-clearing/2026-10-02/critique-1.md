## 1. Ten-second test

The title, question and standfirst read: "What the records show about
Edmonton's snow clearing and its costs" / "How does Edmonton clear snow from
its streets and bike lanes?" / "Nobody can tell from the City records we
found whether Edmonton plows bike lanes before streets."

What a resident would say back: "The City doesn't know, or won't say, how
it plows." That is wrong in two ways. The City publishes clear deadlines by
type of road and bike lane, and the page reports them. And the page's one
finding, about the extra-money proposals, is not in the first screen or in
the TL;DR as a plain statement.

## Required (misleads)

### R1. The standfirst answers one parked claim instead of the page's question, and hides what the record does show

Quoted: "Nobody can tell from the City records we found whether Edmonton
plows bike lanes before streets."

Why it misleads: the question is *how* Edmonton clears snow. The page
answers part of that from the City's own procedure (YF-EV-0281): "The
winter priority bike network ... is to be at bare pavement within 24 hours
after a snowfall ends. So are some freeways, main roads and business
districts. Residential streets are to be brought to a five-centimetre
snowpack within ten days." Leading with "Nobody can tell" makes the City's
snow clearing sound unknowable. It also makes a parked claim the headline
of the page. The editor's plain-speech change log says the standfirst "now
answers only the question people asked, whether the City plows bike lanes
first". That is the Facebook argument's question, not this page's question.
The standfirst should answer the page's question and then bound the hot
claim.

On whether it is the right lead: it is not. Bounding the bike-lanes-first
claim belongs in the standfirst, but as the second half, after what the
record shows. The page itself says "A reader can compare the deadlines.
This page draws no conclusion from them." So the standfirst can give the
deadlines without ruling on which comes first.

Exact fix: "Edmonton's rules give its main bike lanes and some main roads a
day to be cleared after snow stops, and residential streets ten days, but
no record shows which crews actually clear first."

### R2. The TL;DR never states the finding, and leads with what Council did not do

Quoted, bullets 1 to 5:
- "Council's recorded budget amendments in December 2025 named neither of
  the two snow packages on its fall agenda."
- "A committee agenda published before the comment listed administration's
  package for snow and other City work."
- "If you count only proposals a council meeting had dealt with by August
  27, the claim is only partly right unless parking-ban towing counts as
  snow work."
- "If snow proposals had to show their increase over the City's approved
  snow budget, the two-proposal claim would be wrong."
- "If the comment meant exactly two snow proposals, it is only partly right
  because we found three on the agendas we checked."

Why it misleads:
- **The answer is missing.** No bullet says what was found: administration
  had put at least two, and we found three, proposals for more snow money on
  council or committee agendas by August 27. Three bullets give the
  exceptions to an answer the reader has not been given. A resident on
  either side of the argument comes away with "it depends", which is weaker
  than the Supported finding.
- **Bullet 1 comes first and stands without context.** It reads as "council
  turned the snow money down", which the minutes do not show. They show
  only that no amendment named the packages.
- **Bullet 2 depends on unexplained terms.** "The comment" has no
  antecedent, and the bullet depends on "the two snow packages" from bullet
  1. §12: "Stand alone."
- **These are method readings in the ten-second layer.** §12 puts method
  in claim detail.

Exact fix: replace the TL;DR with these four bullets.
1. "By late August 2026, City administration had put three proposals for more snow-clearing money on council and committee agendas."
2. "Two were unfunded packages in the fall 2025 budget, and council's budget changes in December did not add either one."
3. "The third, a larger package that also covers street sweeping and other work, is due to come back in the 2027 to 2030 budget."
4. "Whether that matches the claim of two proposals depends on how you count, and the page shows how each reading changes the answer."

Keep the three "If…" sentences where they already are, in "When the
answer changes" and the limitations. Also check bullet 1 against the
gate's wording. "Three ... on agendas" is the reviewers' final count, and
the page says the total "could be higher". If the editor wants that bound
in the bullet, write "at least three".

### R3. The finding's answer names one exception cryptically and leaves out the one that matters most

Quoted: "Yes. By August 27, 2026, published council and committee agendas
listed at least two snow proposals from administration seeking more money,
though none says what the snow budget already is, and we found three, not
two."

Why it misleads:
- **The first exception is cryptic.** "None says what the snow budget
  already is" is the strict budget reading. A resident cannot tell that it
  is an exception, or that under it the claim would be wrong.
- **The agenda-versus-meeting reading is missing.** The answer rests on
  counting an agenda listing as "put to council". That is the reading most
  readers would dispute ("put to council" can mean council dealt with it),
  and it is not mentioned.
- **The share card cuts the caveat in half.** It ends at "though none
  says…".

Exact fix (one sentence, no colon or semicolon):
"Yes, counting a proposal once it is on a published agenda, administration had at least two snow proposals seeking more money before council or a committee by August 27, 2026, and we found three."
The budget-baseline and meeting readings then stay in "When the answer
changes", which the answer could link to.

The title is "What the records show about Edmonton's snow clearing and its
costs". It is fine, because the finding is about costs. With R1's
standfirst, the title, the question and the answer will all line up. No
change is needed if R1 is made.
