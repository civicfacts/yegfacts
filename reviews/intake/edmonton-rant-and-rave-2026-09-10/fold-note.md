folded onto it. The other stays where it is. The test is the one the merge

## Road condition: folded

The second source's claim was "Edmonton's roads are in poor condition despite
being funded through taxes." It rested on one comment: "Taxes also pay for the
roads .... Oh wait those suck too!"

The first source's claim `basic-services-in-poor-condition` already holds
wordings about roads alone, such as "Roads are a complete mess" and "Fix the
roads. They're horrendous!" The road-condition records that settle those would
settle this comment too. That roads are paid for through taxes is the
comment's premise, not the thing it disputes, and the register already holds
that as a claim of its own under `who-pays-for-roads`.

So the comment's wording now sits on `basic-services-in-poor-condition`, under
its commenter's pseudonym and this source. The claim now counts 11 people
instead of 10, its question `infrastructure-deficit` counts 16 instead of 15,
and the claim lists the Sonnet seat beside Luna. The `road-condition` question
had no other claim, so it was removed. Its address and the claim's address now
redirect to the older question and claim.

The `infrastructure-deficit` brief was parked on 2026-09-03, and its run
record says new intake evidence would reopen it. This fold adds one wording.
It does not decide whether that wording is the new evidence the run record
describes.

## Four-year tax plan: kept apart

`planned-tax-increases` says Edmonton plans to raise taxes substantially over
the next four years. The `property-taxes` question holds two claims. One
resident's taxes rose $1,500 in three years, and another resident pays $6,000
a year. Those are past amounts on individual tax notices. The four-year claim
is about a budget plan for future years. The records that settle one say
nothing about the other, so they are not the same claim, and
`four-year-tax-plan` stays a question of its own.
