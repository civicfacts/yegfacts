# Public-exposure audit — 2026-09-03 (incident: a withheld claim's slug was published)

Incident record under `methodology/exposure-audit.md`. The intake run
`reviews/intake/yegscoop-2026-08-26/` withheld one claim under the standing
right-of-reply rule and then published, in its own run artifacts, the descriptive
identifier and the wording that the rule exists to withhold. This record says
what was published, what was taken out on 2026-09-03, what was deliberately left
alone, and what remains exposed and cannot be taken back by an edit.

Nothing in this record names the person, quotes the accusation, or repeats the
identifier. It is called the withheld right-of-reply claim throughout, and the
register carries it as `withheld-yegscoop-2026-08-26-1`.

## 1. What happened

The register entry was written correctly. It carries the neutral id, the
outcome and the reason, and no proposition and no wording — that was the point
of the decline, and `/considered` renders it that way.

The run artifacts were not cleaned to match. They had been written before the
register entry existed, and they identified the claim by a four-word descriptive
slug that named the person and summarised what the comment accused them of. The
proposition drafted from the comment was in them too, and so was the commenter's
sentence. Eleven tracked files in the run directory carried one or more of the
three.

The run's own `README.md` then told readers the opposite. It said the wording and
the comments were held in the private board record so the decision stayed
auditable, and added that the id is neutral because a slug is published as surely
as a paragraph is. The second sentence is the exact principle the directory
around it was breaking. A false assurance in a governance record is worse than
the leak it covers, because it stops anyone looking.

`scripts/exposure-audit.ts` did not catch any of it. Its fail classes matched
credential formats, private evidence paths, republication rights and local paths.
None of them knew that some text is withheld by decision rather than by format.

## 2. What was changed

All under `reviews/intake/yegscoop-2026-08-26/`.

- The descriptive slug was replaced with the register's neutral id in
  `triage.md`, `triage.json`, `groups.json`, `merged.json`, `merged.raw.txt`,
  `triage-sol.raw.txt` and `triage-gemini.raw.txt`.
- The proposition was replaced with a dated, marked placeholder pointing at the
  register entry, in `triage.md`, `groups.json`, `groups.raw.txt`, `merged.json`,
  `merged.raw.txt` and `triage-stories-sol.raw.txt`.
- The commenter's sentence was replaced with the same kind of placeholder in
  `merged.json`, `merged.raw.txt`, `extract-flash.json` and
  `extract-flash.raw.txt`. Leaving it beside a placeholder that said the wording
  had been withheld would have been the same false assurance again.
- Six `*.raw.txt` files are a model seat's verbatim output, and rewriting one
  silently would destroy what it is for. Each redacted one now opens with a
  notice giving the date, the class of text removed, and where the unredacted
  text lives, so a reader can tell a redaction from an edit.
- `README.md` was rewritten to say all of the above, including the part that is
  not fixed.

The scope was wider than the survey that opened this. The slug was in the seven
files that survey named. The proposition and the commenter's sentence reached
four more — `groups.raw.txt`, `triage-stories-sol.raw.txt`, `extract-flash.json`
and `extract-flash.raw.txt` — which only turned up by searching for the strings
themselves rather than for the slug that summarised them.

## 3. What was deliberately not changed

`intake/captures/yegscoop-2026-08-26/comments.jsonl` holds the source comment
verbatim at index 362 and was not touched. The capture is kept verbatim under
D-0026, and whether a right-of-reply decline should also redact its capture is a
separate question that has not been decided. It is now an explicit, named
exemption in `intake/withheld-fingerprints.yaml` rather than an unexamined gap,
and the audit prints it on every run.

## 4. What is still exposed

The slug, the proposition and the wording are in this repository's git history,
in every commit up to and including `cb73b39`. Redacting the working tree does
not remove them, and anyone who clones the repository has them. Rewriting the
history of a public repository is a much larger decision — it breaks every
existing clone and every commit link on the site and in the board record — and it
has not been taken. This record exists partly so that decision can be taken on
the facts rather than on an assumption that the edit was enough.

The exposure is reduced, not undone.

## 5. The check that would have caught it

`scripts/exposure-audit.ts` gains a fail class, WITHHELD LEAK:

- the proposition, wording or descriptive slug of any claim declined with
  `ground: right-of-reply` must not appear in any tracked file; and
- any such claim with no entry in `intake/withheld-fingerprints.yaml` is itself a
  failure, so a future withheld claim cannot pass by being unlisted.

The check cannot hold the strings it looks for — committing them would be the
leak. It matches salted SHA-256 digests of the normalised words instead, listed
in `intake/withheld-fingerprints.yaml` and produced by
`npm run withheld:fingerprint` from text piped in from the board record.
Normalisation collapses case and punctuation, so a hyphenated slug and the same
words in a sentence fingerprint identically; that matters, because the slug was
the leak here and a check that only matched prose would have missed it. Findings
print `file:line` and never the match. Exempt paths are named on every run.

Verified by reintroducing all three strings in a tracked scratch file: the audit
reported three WITHHELD LEAK findings and exited 1. The file was removed.

`tests/withheld-fingerprints.test.ts` pins the matching behaviour against a
made-up sentence, and pins the structural rule against the live register.
