The day after Edmonton's Infrastructure Committee met on the bike-lane
program on August 26, 2026, six people under one Facebook post made
claims about what administration had proposed and what council did with
it. One comment, by Amber Owl J., carries three of the four claims on
this page.

Five of the six said council had not supported the review, had ignored
it, or had passed up a second look. Amber Owl J. wrote that administration
had recommended "freezing 14 future routes to reassess", and that she and
Councillor Karen Principe had twice brought motions to cut the bike-lane
money to $50 million, and lost.
One person said the same seven councillors voted for the program.

Three of the claims are checked against the City's own report and council
minutes, and each gets its own finding. The fourth was set aside and has
none. The checks were made by a panel of three AI models, called the
reviewers below, under rules fixed before they started. Why anyone voted
as they did is not checked. Nor is whether the routes should be built.

### What administration asked for

Report IS03688 went to the Infrastructure Committee on August 26, 2026.
It asked the committee to recommend that City Council approve an updated
approach for the program's remaining routes, set out in the report's
Attachment 5. It gave two reasons. The provincial government was
expected to change cities' authority over bike lanes. And residents were
complaining more about the trade-offs, such as lost parking. It said
the change would not alter the program's capital budget or its
borrowing.

Attachment 5 came in three versions, all on the committee's agenda. The
City labelled the two replacements Version 1 and Version 2. All
three split the same routes into two lists. Thirteen projects would
continue as planned. Fourteen were set apart. The City's route status
table, dated August 10, 2026, describes them this way. Three had a contract
awarded and no work started. Six had been scheduled for 2026, but
installing them that year was "no longer viable". Five had no delivery plan
or schedule. For those five the table does not say in so many words
whether work had started.

What would happen to the 14 is where the versions differ. The original
said that if Council approved the change, they "would be removed from the
scope" of the program for 2026 and 2027, and might be revisited later.
Version 1 dropped that sentence but kept a rule that routes not yet built
"would be evaluated to determine if they should proceed". Version 2 says
the remaining routes "will be evaluated for trade-offs, alternative design
treatments and budget implications", and that administration will report
to Council in the first three months of 2027 for direction. It says the 14 "may
be re-evaluated using alternate design approaches".

So the finding on whether administration recommended a freeze depends on
the version. Under the original it did, and in cross-review all three
reviewers read Version 1 as holding the routes back too. Under Version 2, the latest, it
recommended taking another look and reporting back, and did not say the
routes would wait. The committee's minutes add both replacements without
saying which one members had in front of them, so this check reads
Version 2 and the finding is Partially supported.

The report comes close to a freeze in other places. It says that because
of the weather, construction not yet started is "best postponed to 2027",
though that covers every unstarted route, including those continuing as
planned. The status table says the three tendered routes "may be delayed"
or removed pending the Council report. Read together, these suggest the
14 would wait for Council. Before any research, this check decided that a
freeze needs the plan's own words to say the routes wait. No passage tied
to Version 2 says the 14 must wait. One reviewer called it a close call.

### What the committee did

The Infrastructure Committee has six members, the mayor among them. Seven
other councillors attended. No member moved to adopt the recommendation.

Councillor A. Salvador moved instead that administration keep building
within the program's approved scope, push ahead with routes ready to
tender, and get routes that could not go ahead in 2026 ready to build at
the earliest chance in 2027. Councillor E. Rutherford moved to
take out the part about making the most of the 2026 construction season.
Both were defeated 2 to 2. Salvador and M. Janz voted for the main motion,
and Rutherford and R. Clarke against. On the amendment the votes were the
other way round. Mayor A. Knack and Councillor A. Paquette are recorded on
neither vote, and the minutes do not say why.

So the committee neither adopted the plan nor turned it down. That is why
the finding is Partially supported. The plan was not adopted. What the
minutes do not show is the committee voting it
down or voting to carry on against it, which is what "ignore" and "go
their own route" describe. If "did not support" means only that the plan
was not adopted, the claim holds.

A committee is not City Council. The report asked the committee to send
the plan to Council with a recommendation, and the committee carried no
motion on it. Nothing in the Council
minutes the reviewers read decided it by the cut-off date of September 25.
Those were chosen items from the September minutes, not whole meetings,
and one reviewer's look at the City's open voting record, on September
29, found no Council vote on the report up to September 22. The minutes of Council's
two September public hearings, read here after the panel ran, have no
item on it either.
Salvador gave notice of a motion asking administration to prepare options
for more money to finish the 14 routes in the 2027 to 2030 budget. Council
put it off to its meeting of October 6, after this page's cut-off date.
No Council decision overrode the committee, because the committee made no
decision on the plan to override.

### The two motions to cut the money

Council approved the program in December 2022 at $100 million, by 9 votes
to 4, with Principe and Rice, both councillors then, among the four
against. Rice sat on Council until the October 2025 election.

In November 2023 Principe moved, and Rice seconded, a cut of $50 million
from the program's 2024 to 2026 funding, with less borrowing to match.
Council defeated it 4 to 8. In December 2024 Principe moved, and Rice
seconded, a cut of $33.65 million in each of 2025 and 2026, to defund
the program's planning, design and delivery. Council defeated it 4 to 7.

The first would have left $50 million. The second adds up to $67.3
million, which would have left about $32.7 million, about a third. Both
remainders are calculations made here, against the $100 million approved
in 2022. No reviewer could open the program's budget sheet from either
date. Two cited a September 2024 capital update showing $100 million, which
none could open in cross-review and this site could not archive. Administration's August 2026 report still describes the
program as $100 million approved in the 2023 to 2026 budget.

So the pair did bring two cuts, and both lost. Only the 2023 one
matches the "$50M" in the claim. It is also the only one whose text
says where the money would go, toward less borrowing. The 2024 text names
the borrowing that funded the program but not where the money taken out
would go. Neither text uses the comment's words "core priorities".

Three other recorded motions that one or both of them moved or seconded
were not votes on cutting the program. A separate 2023 cut that Rice moved, which did not name the
program, was withdrawn. In June 2024 the pair moved an amendment on new
funding requests that did not name the program, and it lost 2 to 10. In
September 2025 Principe seconded a motion to
halt construction on bike lanes that had not started until every project
was reviewed. It lost 4 to 9.

### Why the 'same seven' claim is not answered here

One commenter said the same seven councillors voted for the program.
This page does not test that, or what the commenter said about their
intentions, and nothing above should be read as an answer to it.

A pattern like that shows only across all the votes, in both councils, the
one before the October 2025 election and the one after. A single vote, or
a list of who voted on one motion above, does not test it. Several
budget-adjustment meetings in that period are not in our archive yet. Some budget votes bundle this program with many others,
and which way each of those counts depends on documents that are not
archived either. Depending on how those votes are counted, the answer
could come out as no, as partly, or as nobody can tell. So the claim was
set aside before the panel ran. It is listed [among the claims with no finding](#not-checked). It
can be checked again once those minutes and documents are archived and
read.

### What this page does not answer

Nothing here says why any member of council voted as they did, or whether
