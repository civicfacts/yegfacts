This story is in production and has not been through the review panel. Nothing
here is a YEGFacts finding, and this page is not published — it exists in the
repository so the scope can be read and challenged before any research runs.

## What this story will cover

Edmonton’s procurement of battery-electric buses, and three separate arguments
people make about it: whether the procurement failed on its own contracted
terms, what the public actually lost or did not lose in money, and what the
episode does or does not show about running electric buses in a cold city.

Those are three different questions with three different kinds of evidence
behind them, so they will be checked as three separate claims rather than
collapsed into a single verdict about the buses.

## What has to be settled first

Before any model researches this, the brief has to fix the words that the
answers depend on: what “lost” means here — gross spend, or net public cost
after grants, recoveries and residual value — whose money is being counted,
and the date at which the accounting window closes. Those definitions are
written down and frozen first, so that the framing cannot be adjusted once the
findings arrive.

## What happens next

The story goes through the seven-stage process described in the
[methodology](/methodology): a frozen brief, three models researching
independently, a deterministic merge of what they found, a cross-review round,
synthesis by a published rule, a faithfulness check on the draft, and a human
verification of every cited source. Claims, findings, evidence and the panel
results all appear on this page when that is done.
