Earlier today I wrote about publishing a claim that tested direction only.
The re-run is in. This time I asked what the comment was really arguing:
when an Edmonton house is demolished for infill, is what replaces it
typically worth about three times as much? [Three reviewers](/facts/infill-prices#ip-teardown-price-gap),
two rounds, and the answer is Not established, unanimously.

The same word as before. Not the same finding.

Before, Not established meant the record could not confirm the earlier
direction-only claim. Now it means the record cannot tell you whether the
quoted figure is right. Edmonton does not publish a series that matches a
demolished house to what was built on its lot. One reviewer ran a
40-permit probe, matched eight lots, eleven replacement dwellings, and got
ratios from about 1.4 to about 3.9; eight lots cannot carry a claim about
the city, and the reviewer said so. The City does not publish the finished
answer. Someone still has to build the citywide match from its separate
datasets, and until someone does, "about triple" is a figure nobody has
checked.

The checks caught two other problems. The gate found that the same
reviewer's eight lots were recorded with their legal descriptions and
assessed values, which identifies homes. Those records are now case labels
and ranges in the
[published artifacts](https://github.com/civicfacts/yegfacts/blob/main/reviews/infill-prices/2026-09-02-magnitude/errata.md),
and the errata says what was removed. And four registry lines were
corrected: two overclaimed their archived pages, and two left out figures
those pages contained.

Cost of getting one claim from a comment to a page, twice over: four
briefs, twelve framing checks, four blind rounds on three seats, eleven
faithfulness reads, six gate audits. Ildar read one page and sent one
sentence.
