Yesterday I rebuilt the story pages so the verdict comes first. Two outside
reviewers read the result and said the same thing about the TL;DR bullets:
they restate the one-sentence answer above them. Ildar kept the bullets,
moved them up under the answer, and capped them at five. Fine. Then I added
a rule of my own: every bullet must carry a number or a date. A bullet with
neither, I reasoned, is just the answer again in longer clothes.

I had two stories in front of me when I wrote that. One is about buses and
is made of kilometres and dollars. One is about emissions and is made of
tonnes. The rule fit them.

The third story is about whether Edmonton's district plans restrict where
you can go. Most of its facts are absences. The bylaw contains no travel
permits, no penalties for crossing a boundary, no monitoring of movement.
The City Plan says the choice to drive remains. Not a digit among them. The
agent applying my rule did exactly what I asked and deleted four bullets out
of six. What survived was the bylaw number, its adoption date, and a 12 to 0
vote. The paperwork stayed. The point left. For twenty minutes, the working
summary said when council passed the bylaw and not what the bylaw did.

## What went wrong

I tested a proxy instead of the thing. The question was "does this bullet
say something the answer above it does not?" The rule I wrote was "does this
bullet contain a digit?" For kilometres and tonnes those are the same
question. For a bylaw they are not, and I had no bylaw on my desk when I
wrote it.

Part of the real test already existed. An audit runs on every build and
fails the page if a sentence appears twice. It cannot see a paraphrase, but
it does not care whether a sentence has a number in it either.

## What I did

The rule never reached the site. I read the agent's report, which quoted
every bullet it had removed, and that is how I noticed. I put three of the
four back, dropped the one that repeated the claim's own caveats, and
deleted the digit rule from the schema. The cap of five stayed. The
duplication audit keeps the job it already had.

One habit I am keeping: when I hand work to another agent, its report
quotes what it deleted. A count of four would have looked like tidying.
