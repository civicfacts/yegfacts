Yesterday, for about twenty minutes, the summary at the top of
[the district plans story](/facts/fifteen-minute-districts) looked like
this.

**What my rule kept**

- The District Policy is Charter Bylaw 24000, adopted October 2, 2024, a
  statutory plan that guides rezoning and permit decisions.
- Council added the sentence that the plans "shall not restrict freedom of
  movement, association and commerce" by a vote of 12 to 0.

**What it deleted**

- The bylaw contains no travel permits, no penalties for crossing a
  boundary, no monitoring of movement.
- The strongest text anyone could use later is about user charges and
  paying for growth; a real restriction would need new instruments and a
  new Council vote.
- The City Plan says the choice to drive remains.
- The finding is about the restriction claim only, not about whether
  district planning is good policy.

A reader who wanted to know whether the plans fence people in got a filing
date and a vote count. The paperwork stayed. The point left.

## How I got there

The day before, I had rebuilt the story pages so the verdict comes first.
Two outside reviewers said the summary bullets restated the one-sentence
answer above them. Ildar kept the bullets, moved them under the answer, and
capped them at five. Then I added a rule of my own: every bullet must carry
a number or a date. A bullet with neither, I reasoned, is the answer again
in longer clothes.

I had two stories on my desk when I wrote it. One is about buses and is
made of kilometres and dollars. One is about emissions and is made of
tonnes. The rule fit them. The third story is about what a bylaw says, and
most of what it says is what is not there. Not a digit among the facts that
matter. The agent applying my rule did exactly what I asked.

## How I noticed

The agent's report quoted every bullet it removed instead of counting them.
Four lines of a story's substance, sitting under the word "deleted", is a
hard thing to read past. A count of four would have looked like tidying.

## What changed

I put three of the four back, dropped the one that repeated the claim's
own caveats, and deleted the digit rule from the schema. The cap of five
stayed. The rule never reached the site. An audit that already runs on
every build fails a page if a sentence appears twice. It cannot see a
paraphrase, but it does not care whether a sentence has a number in it
either, and it was doing the real job all along.

## What I learned

I tested a proxy instead of the thing. The question was "does this bullet
say something the answer does not?" The rule I wrote was "does this bullet
contain a digit?" For kilometres and tonnes those are the same question.
For a bylaw they are not, and I had no bylaw in front of me when I wrote
it. I am now mildly suspicious of any rule that fits every example I have.
That is usually a fact about my examples.
