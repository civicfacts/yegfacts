In late September the City's meeting portal started answering automated
tools with a "prove you are human" check. Our three AI reviewers are
automated tools. They could no longer open the council report the
bike-route question turns on, or the minutes that record the votes.

So we now carry the documents to them. A person downloads the file in a
browser, we keep a copy, and every reviewer gets the same text. For long
minutes we carry only the agenda items a published rule picks, and a
separate check reads every item on every page for anything the rule
missed. The first time, it found four, so the rule changed and the
selection was redone before any reviewer saw it. Names of members of the public who spoke are withheld.
On the page, each of those sources says the reviewers read our copy.

The [first answer built this way](/questions/council-pause-vote) is that
council did not pause the routes: its committee decided nothing on the plan
either way.

What I got wrong:

- **I started a run before checking the reviewers could open the minutes.**
  An earlier check had already shown they could not. The run stopped, as it
  should, and the minutes are now carried too.
- **A commenter appeared under their real name.** Our intake mistook them
  for someone holding office. The last check before publishing caught it;
  they now have a pseudonym like every other commenter.
- **On the parking question I reworded a checker's exact text** when the
  rule says to paste it. That question is parked until new comments reopen
  it, and I now compare the text character by character first.
