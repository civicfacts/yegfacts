## Triage report

### 1. Who asks this, in their words

“Did the City of Edmonton sign an agreement to make Edmonton a 15-minute city?”

That is a concrete question a resident or reporter could ask. “Agreement” should mean an executed document with identifiable parties, not a bylaw, plan, policy, campaign promise or general membership.

### 2. How it circulates, and where the wording came from

The exact allegation appears once in the supplied Yegscoop Facebook capture. Commenter 21 wrote it verbatim. The post URL is missing, so this is **supplied reader demand**, not fully captured demand and not an editor hypothesis.

I found no independently indexed repetition of the precise “signed an agreement” wording. The broader Edmonton 15-minute-city dispute has circulated since at least February 2023 on Facebook and remained prominent during the 2024 district-plan hearings. [AFP documented the 2023 Facebook circulation](https://factcheck.afp.com/doc.afp.com.338V8C3), and [CityNews reported residents raising related allegations at the May 2024 hearings](https://edmonton.citynews.ca/2024/05/28/edmonton-urban-plan-15-minute-city-conspiracy-theory/). This is one captured instance of a specific allegation inside a larger, established argument.

### 3. Can the public record answer it?

Yes, if the claim is normalized as:

> “Did the City of Edmonton execute an agreement with another organization that commits Edmonton to becoming a 15-minute city, and if so, what document and parties?”

A reviewer would compare the alleged agreement with:

- [The City Plan page](https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/city_vision_and_strategic_plan/city-plan) and [Charter Bylaw 20000](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=61597), to distinguish a council-adopted planning instrument from an external agreement.
- [Council agendas, minutes, reports and attachments](https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/council-committee-meetings), including the City Clerk’s [search instructions](https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/council_committee_meetings/council-committee-meetings-faq).
- The City’s permanent [Agreement Register record series](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/classification-and-retention-schedule.pdf?cb=1764468405), which covers original executed agreements binding the City.
- A formal [access-to-information request](https://www.edmonton.ca/city_government/city_organization/access-and-privacy) if the agreement is not published. The City’s [request form](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/documents/FOIP_Request_Form.pdf?cb=1724282161) expressly lists an agreement as a requestable general record.

That narrower wording preserves what readers care about: whether an outside commitment exists, rather than merely whether Edmonton adopted 15-minute-district planning.

### 4. Which verdicts are possible, and who each would surprise?

- **Supported:** An executed agreement expressly commits Edmonton to becoming a 15-minute city. This would surprise people who consider the allegation invented.
- **Partially supported:** A related agreement exists, but its parties, obligations or purpose do not match the full allegation. This would surprise both the commenter and categorical skeptics.
- **Not established:** Published plans discuss 15-minute districts, but the search does not establish an executed agreement. This would surprise the commenter, though it might leave room for an access request.
- **Contradicted:** A sufficiently scoped Agreement Register search or formal records response affirmatively shows that no such agreement exists. This would most surprise people sharing “Google it, it’s true.”

At least Supported and Contradicted would change what a meaningful part of the audience believes.

### 5. Who is affected, and what it costs

The allegation affects citywide trust in planning, district policy, zoning and transportation decisions. It can also displace specific concerns raised by residents affected by growth, housing, accessibility and transportation changes. The intake identifies no pending vote that turns directly on this claim, but the belief attaches itself to active civic debates.

This is one document-identification claim. It requires no lot-level linkage, comparator cities or quantitative dataset. The work is a council-record and Agreement Register search, possibly followed by an access request. The existing fifteen-minute-districts story tested travel restrictions, not whether an agreement exists, so this proposition has not already been checked.

**Triage: GO. A resident made a concrete claim, permanent City records can test it, and finding either a binding agreement or a comprehensive absence would surprise part of the audience.**

This is an answerability decision, not a verdict on the claim.
