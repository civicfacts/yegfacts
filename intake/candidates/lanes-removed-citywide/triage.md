3. **Can the public record answer it, at the level people ask it?**  
Not literally as written. "All throughout the city" has no period, denominator, or threshold. Edmonton also builds bike facilities through several programs, so the current expansion program is not the whole historical network.

The record can answer a narrower question that readers would still care about: "Among the routes in Edmonton's active-transportation expansion program, how many completed or planned designs permanently remove a motor-vehicle travel lane, and where?"

A reviewer would start with Infrastructure Services report IS03688 through the [August 26 meeting item](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/Meeting.aspx?Agenda=Agenda&Id=55300824-2d70-4b6a-a504-df43ace1c6b4&Item=29&Tab=attachments&lang=English) and [direct report file](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=302682). The [City's expansion-program page](https://www.edmonton.ca/projects_plans/roads/active-transportation-network-improvements-project) is the working route ledger. It lists status, facility type, operational changes, parking changes, maps, and bulletins. Historical examples can be checked against the City's [West Central Bike Routes brochure](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/assets/WestCentralBikeRoutes.pdf), [102 Avenue fact sheet](https://www.edmonton.ca/public-files/assets/document?path=RoadsTraffic/2017_102_ave_bike_route_facts.pdf), [132 Avenue project page](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/on_your_streets/132-avenue-renewal), and [132 Avenue final-design report](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/132AveRenewal_WhatWeDecided.pdf?cb=1755166476).

The check must keep completed and planned routes separate. It must also distinguish permanent travel-lane removal from parking removal, turn restrictions, service-road changes, traffic calming, and temporary construction closures. The report's combined category of routes affecting "travel lanes or parking" cannot supply the answer by itself.

4. **Which verdicts are possible, and who each would surprise.**  
- **Supported** is possible if the defined program shows frequent, geographically dispersed removals made to accommodate bike facilities. That would surprise people arguing that the changes are only traffic calming.
- **Partially supported** is possible if some routes remove lanes but the citywide scale or claimed purpose fails. The interpretation would have to name precisely which part fails. That would challenge both the sweeping claim and blanket denials.
- **Not established** is possible if published designs cannot reliably reconstruct the before condition or separate the bike-lane purpose from other project purposes. That would undercut the certainty shown on both sides.
- **Contradicted** is possible if the defined route set shows only isolated exceptions, or if the cited changes were demonstrably part of separately planned work rather than bike-lane installation. That would surprise the claim's holders.

At least Supported, Partially supported, and Contradicted would impose a real loss on someone. The framing check must define the route universe and what counts as "throughout" before panel review.

5. **Who is affected, and what it costs.**  
Road allocation affects drivers, cyclists, pedestrians, transit riders, nearby residents and businesses, and people who cannot drive or afford a car. Those last groups often receive less attention in a debate framed only around motorists versus cyclists. Current route decisions, the next capital budget, and possible provincial restrictions give the question civic weight.

This is one claim, but it requires a route-level census and record linkage across the program ledger, final maps, bulletins, and selected historical projects. No lot-level work or comparator cities are needed. Dated copies should be saved because project pages and designs change. YEGFacts has checked related spending, congestion, and ridership questions, but not this route-by-route lane-removal count.

**Triage: GO**. Residents are arguing about a concrete use of public road space, and City route records can answer a defined, reader-relevant version once permanent lane removals are separated from parking changes, traffic calming, and construction closures.
