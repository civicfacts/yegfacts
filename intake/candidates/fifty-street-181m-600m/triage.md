1. **Who asks this, in their words.**  
“Did Edmonton spend $181 million to grade-separate about 600 metres of 50 Street, nearly twice the $100 million budgeted for four years of active-transportation expansion?” This is a concrete question a resident or reporter could ask.

2. **How it circulates, and where the wording came from.**  
The exact wording appears once, in a comment on a Yegscoop Facebook thread posted on or after August 27, 2026 and supplied to YEGFacts on September 2. The founder preserved the comment verbatim, but not its URL or a screenshot, so the register correctly calls the origin “supplied.” This is reader wording, not an editor’s hypothesis. Searches found no independently indexed repetition, though Facebook’s limited indexing makes that weak evidence about overall circulation.

3. **Can the public record answer it, at the level people ask it?**  
Yes, with one necessary clarification. The reviewer can use the City’s [50 Street project page](https://www.edmonton.ca/projects_plans/roads/50-street-widening), [2025 Infrastructure Report](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/assets/PDF/IIS-Infrastructure-Report-2025.pdf?cb=1769008586), [2023–2026 capital budget](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/Proposed2023-2026CapitalBudget.pdf?cb=1670957378), and council’s [2022 borrowing amendment](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=154042) and [later budget amendment](https://pub-edmonton.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=150937). The other side of the comparison is covered by the City’s [Active Transportation Network Expansion page](https://www.edmonton.ca/projects_plans/roads/active-transportation-network-improvements-project).

The fair reader-level question is: “Does the City’s $181-million 50 Street project cover roughly 600 metres, and how does that bundled project compare with the $100-million active-transportation program?” The word “road” cannot be accepted without checking scope because the published project also includes railway overpasses, widening, a shared-use path, a sidewalk and related work. That clarification preserves the comparison people care about.

4. **Which verdicts are possible, and who each would surprise.**  
**Supported** is possible if the current records establish the cost, approximate length, four-year active-transportation amount and comparable budget bases. That would surprise residents who treat the $100-million program as uniquely large. **Partially supported** is possible if the two dollar amounts hold but “600 metres of road” misstates the project’s length or bundled scope; that would surprise the commenter and supporters repeating the comparison. **Not established** is possible if the records cannot reconcile the length or accounting bases. **Contradicted** is possible if current profiles show a materially different cost, distance or budget period. The commenter can lose, and so can critics of the active-transportation expenditure.

5. **Who is affected, and what it costs.**  
The comparison bears on how taxpayers judge spending between a freight and commuter corridor and a citywide network for walking, cycling and rolling. It affects drivers delayed at the railway, nearby businesses, active-transportation users and residents who cannot drive. That last group is easily lost in a “roads or bike lanes” argument. The intake identifies a recent committee decision, but not a still-pending vote; the claim may influence later route and capital-budget decisions.

This is one compound claim with several checkable parts. It needs budget-version reconciliation and a defensible measurement of the published project limits, but no lot-level linkage or comparator cities. The $100-million figure has already appeared in YEGFacts’ active-transportation work; this exact 50 Street comparison has not.

**Triage: GO** — A resident made a concrete comparison, and City project and budget records can test both sides, including whether $181 million fairly describes 600 metres of road.
